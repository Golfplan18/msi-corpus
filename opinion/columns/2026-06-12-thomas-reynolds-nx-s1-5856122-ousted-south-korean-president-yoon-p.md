Yoon Suk Yeol manufactured a war to seize power. The Seoul Central District Court found the former South Korean president guilty of aiding an adversary and abusing official power, sentencing him to 30 years in prison. A separate rebellion conviction already gave him life.

The prosecution, led by special prosecutor Cho Eun-suk, argued what the record shows: Yoon ordered drone flights over Pyongyang in October 2024 to provoke an armed North Korean response. The emergency would supply the pretext. The pretext would supply the martial law. The martial law would supply the power. North Korea accused Seoul of flying drones over its capital three times that month to drop propaganda leaflets. Defense Minister Kim Yong Hyun issued a vague denial. The ministry said it could neither confirm nor deny. The court rejected the denial and accepted the record.

Yoon's lawyers advanced the working-bar-recognizable defense. The drone flights were a measured, proportional response to North Korea's campaign of sending thousands of trash-carrying balloons across the border earlier that year. Standard border-security signaling. The opposition's impeachment effort and budget cuts were political grievances the president was legally entitled to counter. Martial law was a constitutional emergency power available when the legislature obstructs the executive.

The court tore that defense apart. It ruled that Yoon deliberately set out to provoke armed North Korean attacks, manufacturing a national emergency to neutralize political opposition. The ruling found the flights harmed South Korea's military interests — exposing capabilities, undermining future operations, prompting the North to strengthen its defenses without strategic necessity.

On December 3, 2024, Yoon delivered a televised address accusing liberal lawmakers of being North Korea-sympathizing anti-state forces. Troops blockaded the National Assembly. Lawmakers broke through the blockade and voted to overturn the measure. The cabinet lifted it six hours later. Yoon was suspended, impeached, arrested in July 2025, and formally removed by the Constitutional Court. The rebellion conviction carried a life sentence — a verdict [both Yoon and the prosecution appealed](/articles/2026-01-13-south-korea-prosecutor-seeks-death-sentence-for-yoon-over-martial-law-rebellion/), the prosecution demanding execution. The 30-year aiding-an-adversary sentence now enters its own appellate track.

Notice the inversion at the heart of the defense. North Korea spent 2024 flying thousands of trash-filled balloons into the South — a sustained low-grade assault — and the moment Yoon's government responded with its own propaganda-leaflet flights, the court branded that response a provocation worthy of decades behind bars. The special prosecutor argued Yoon tried to create a "warlike situation" while plotting to remove political opponents and "monopolize" power. The court accepted that framing. The record supplies the sequence.

That sequence — manufacture threat, declare emergency, seize power — is the textbook autocrat's playbook. The executive invokes national security to neutralize political opposition. The executive cites budget cuts and legislative gridlock as existential threats. The executive deploys security forces to seize the legislature. The legal mechanism is the emergency declaration. The factual premise is the manufactured crisis. The Seoul Central District Court's verdict strips the mechanism from the premise. It treats the executive's stated national-security rationale as evidence of the crime, not as a shield against it.

An honest application of the emergency powers framework requires the threat to exist independently of the executive's provocations. A constitutional posture requires the executive to demonstrate that the security measure was necessary to repel an actual or imminent attack, not to provoke one. The court found Yoon provoked the attack to make the necessity defense available. That is not the application of emergency powers. That is the weaponization of them.

The audit does not require speculation about internal mental states. The record supplies the external conduct. The drone flights in October 2024. The vague denial. The televised address on December 3. The troop deployment to the National Assembly. The six-hour martial law window. The special prosecutor's demand for execution. The court's findings of aiding an adversary and abusing power. The stacked sentences.

Yoon Suk Yeol manufactured a war to seize power. The Seoul Central District Court named the crime and imposed the sentence. The appellate courts now hold the penalty. The page holds the verdict; the verdict holds the warning: constitutions do not survive presidents who treat them as props.
