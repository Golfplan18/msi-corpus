The Trump administration is moving to terminate Temporary Protected Status for Haitian immigrants in the United States. The Supreme Court ruled 6 to 3 in June in favor of the administration's effort to end TPS for Haitians and Syrians. More than 330,000 Haitians in the country are set to lose their legal status and their authorization to work under the order. An estimated 13,000 of them are nursing assistants, caring for 65,000 patients every day.

The U.S. State Department has issued a non-travel advisory for Haiti, citing a national state of emergency. The United Nations has warned that Haiti continues to be plagued by violence. Asylum grant rates under the Trump administration have fallen to less than five percent. There is no direct legal pathway from Temporary Protected Status to a green card.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has argued that TPS recipients should leave the United States and seek other legal immigration statuses. A Department of Homeland Security spokesperson said TPS "is exactly that — temporary" and has "been allowed to function as a de facto amnesty program despite Congress never intending it to be permanent." A White House spokesperson added that TPS "was never intended to be a pathway to permanent status or legal residency."

TPS was first designated for Haitians in 2010.
