Let me show you the trick, because I have built versions of it, and the reader deserves to see the trick before deciding whether to take it.

The title performs a bait-and-switch before a single sentence of the column arrives. It takes the W — the league's most basic unit, the letter that signals victory, the mark the players put on their chest when they win — and turns it into a question mark. The WNBA, the title tells you, does not know what its own victory is. The reader who has not read a word of the column now holds that frame. The reader is permitted, by the title, to think the league is confused about its own product. The body of the column gets to inherit the work the headline already did.
