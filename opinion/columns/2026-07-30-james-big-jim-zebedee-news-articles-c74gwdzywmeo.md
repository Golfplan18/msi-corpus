Trump and Hegseth are killing Iranian civilians and calling it retaliation.

Last night's [heavy strikes on Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets inside Iran](https://mainstreetindependent.com/articles/2026-07-30-u-s-launches-heavy-strikes-on-iran-after-missile-attack-on-jordan-base/) killed three members of a family on Qeshm island — a father, a mother, and their two-year-old child. The child was two years old. U.S. Central Command described the operation as "a powerful response to yesterday's attempted Iranian attacks." The actual consequence was a dead child whose name the Pentagon will never say.

Clausewitz wrote that war is the continuation of politics by other means. What political object is being continued here? The administration cannot name one. "We're going to be hitting them very hard because it's our turn to hit them," the President told reporters. "They asked us not to do it." Asked whether he would always retaliate, he said: "Pretty much." Not strategy. Not deterrence. Not the protection of American interests. Revenge-as-policy, dressed in the vocabulary of military necessity. That is not a war aim. That is a mood. Wars run on moods kill the men and women the moods send into them.

Now, I'm just a simple man, but I sat in an Abrams crew seat in 2003, and I have read enough Clausewitz to know what a war looks like when it is being run as one. This is not what one looks like.

The Iran strike is the latest move in a six-month campaign that has produced more than 600 U.S. service members injured, an Iraqi sovereignty violation after joint U.S.-Saudi strikes killed at least 20 Iraqi Popular Mobilisation Forces members — which the Iraqi presidency condemned as "an unacceptable attack and a blatant violation of Iraq's sovereignty" — and a drone attack on an Egyptian port on Wednesday, the first direct strike on Egyptian territory since the war began. The war has crossed borders and oceans and is now reaching into countries that were bystanders a week ago. Secretary Hegseth sat down this week with Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu to review three framing options: diplomacy, intensified economic pressure, or renewed military action. That is not a strategy. That is a menu of framings the Pentagon can swap depending on which one plays best on cable. The strategic goal is not stated because the strategic goal is operating tempo — the machine runs because the machine runs.

This is what a strategy vacuum looks like when filled with the capacity to inflict violence. The United States has spent six months and hundreds of casualties generating the very thing the military was supposed to prevent: a widening Middle Eastern war with no exit.

The IRGC shares culpability, and I do not equivocate on that. Tuesday's attempted ballistic missile attack on American forces in Jordan was an act of war. The official Iranian statement called it a response to "the acts of aggression of the American child-killing army" — a line Tehran knows will land because it knows what will come next. Iran's IRGC also struck three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz after the vessels "ignored warnings." But the fact that Iran committed an act of war does not establish that the American response was proportionate to its stated military objective, nor does it make a dead child on Qeshm island a necessary cost of self-defense. The proportionality math does not work in any direction that ends with a dead toddler.

Michael Walzer, in *Just and Unjust Wars*, set out the principle of discrimination: military force must be directed at combatants and military objectives, not at civilians. The doctrine of double effect permits civilian casualties only as an unintended side effect of a proportionate attack on a legitimate military target. Even taking the U.S. account at face value — that the strike hit IRGC command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance, maritime capabilities — the question becomes proportionality: was the expected military advantage of a specific strike on a residential street of Qeshm island worth the foreseeable risk? A resident described the electricity cutting out repeatedly and explosions since 3:40 in the morning: "We are helpless." On a target list that ran dozens of sites across a country, the calculus is unlikely to satisfy the standard Walzer's framework requires. CENTCOM did not explain why a residential home in Chah Tangu was on the same target list as military command centers, or whether the expected military advantage of that particular strike outweighed the certainty of residential exposure.

Sun Tzu wrote that the highest form of warfare is to win without fighting, and the next best is to break the enemy's strategy. The current campaign is pursuing neither. It is pursuing the operating tempo of a military-industrial complex that needs kinetic action to justify its budget and a presidential ego that needs to be seen hitting back. The strategy is the rhythm. The rhythm is the strategy.

The next strike produces the next "powerful response." The body count on both sides climbs, and the strategic vocabulary stays the same because the strategic vocabulary is the only thing keeping the American public from asking what the strategy actually is.
