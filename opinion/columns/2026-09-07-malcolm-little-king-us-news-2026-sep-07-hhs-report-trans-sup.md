HHS published a study built to convict transgender people of political violence. Not peer-reviewed, not published in any scientific journal, the report tells the American public that a person who agrees with the statement "People should be supported in expressing their transgender identity" is, by that agreement alone, entangled with something called "left-wing authoritarianism" and a "willingness to justify political violence." The evidence behind that extraordinary claim: an online survey of 1,208 people, a pile of correlations, and not one number released for independent inspection.

This is what a weapon looks like before it is fired. The epidemiologist Gideon Meyerowitz-Katz called it "nakedly political" and "probably the most obviously political study I've come across." Daniel Aaron, a law professor at the University of Utah, called it "quite an abnormal document" that likely violates the federal Information Quality Bulletin — a regulation that requires peer review for influential scientific information from the US government — because it was never peer-reviewed. Craig Konnoth, of the University of Virginia, called it a "broadside attack on the scientific establishment more generally, and trans rights are being used as the vehicle to do this." Three different scholarly registers, one identical verdict: this was never a scientific study. Look at the instrument, and the verdict writes itself. The political-ideology questionnaire, Meyerowitz-Katz said, "essentially measured whether someone was a Democrat." One question asked whether opposition to gay marriage is homophobic. Another asked whether the terrorism focus should shift to the far right — a proposition, Meyerowitz-Katz argued, that any honest person who has read the data would accept, since roughly 75–80 percent of deaths from terrorist attacks in the United States come from the far right. The political-violence questions counted only violence directed at conservative figures and institutions. Leave out every other target of political violence, and the correlation the authors wanted could not fail to appear. The instrument was rigged to convict agreement with facts as agreement with arson.

This is not a badly made study. It is a specimen of two documented techniques. The first, frame-engineered relabeling, is the substitution of a loaded term for the thing it refers to.
