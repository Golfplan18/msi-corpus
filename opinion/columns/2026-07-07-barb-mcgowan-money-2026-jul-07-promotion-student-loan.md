*They called it a phone bill. They sold you a mortgage.*

They sold my kids a phone contract and handed them a mortgage.

That's what this is. The British government ran slideshows comparing student loan repayments to a mobile phone plan. "Just a few pounds a month," they said. "Easy." What they didn't say: we get to change the rules whenever we want, and the debt stays.

The Treasury committee finally called it what it is. Mis-selling. Plan 2 loans went out to English and Welsh students who started university between 2012 and 2023. The committee got 52,000 responses, and more than half of those respondents said they didn't understand what they were signing. One kid called it "a tax on ambition." He wasn't wrong.

Rachel Reeves — the Chancellor — froze the repayment threshold at £29,385 for three more years starting April 2027. Above that line, graduates lose 9% of every pound they earn. Every pay raise gets clipped. The original promise back in 2010 was that the threshold would rise with earnings every year from 2016 onward. They froze it once in 2016, again in 2021, and now again. Every time the working young get a little ahead, the government moves the goalpost.

The YouTube videos didn't mention any of this. The Student Loans Company didn't make it clear. The slideshow compared the monthly bill to your phone contract. A fucking phone contract.

Meg Hillier, who chairs the committee, said patience has run out. She wants the freeze reversed. The government says they're "looking for ways to make it fairer."

"Looking."

Meanwhile the kid who borrowed £50,000 to become a teacher owes £60,000 because interest compounded while she paid. The nurse who borrowed owes more than she borrowed. The young family that did everything right — university, job, kept their head down — gets clipped on every raise for the next twenty-five goddamn years.

This is what they do. Every government, every country, both teams. Promise a deal. Sell the deal. Change the deal after the signature. The young get the bill.

Reverse the freeze, Treasury. Cap the interest. Stop comparing student debt to a phone contract — you know goddamn well that phone gets turned off. This one doesn't.

Eat shit and fix it.
