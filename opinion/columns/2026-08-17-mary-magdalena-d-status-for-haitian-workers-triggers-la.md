Since July 27, your administration has terminated Temporary Protected Status for Haitian immigrants, ending the work authorization of about 350,000 people — nearly half of them in Florida, roughly 93,000 of them in that state's workforce. The [Supreme Court ruling](/articles/2026-07-09-supreme-court-ruling-on-tps-could-deepen-caregiver-shortage/) in June cleared the way; the termination runs across thirteen countries, and the Ukrainians come next, their status expiring in October.

The numbers the court permitted came to rest on specific bodies. Jewish Community Services of South Florida let go of eighteen Haitian TPS holders, some who had served as home health aides for more than twenty years, in an organization that provides home care for about four hundred Holocaust survivors. Two home-care agencies in Miami-Dade and Broward counties shut down after each lost more than eighty workers. Moorings Park in Naples lost seven more, on top of seventeen the year before. Half Shell Raw Bar in Key West terminated six, including the cook who had made the conch chowder and the fritter batter for more than a decade. At ArchCare in New York, administrators tripled sign-on bonuses to six thousand dollars and still could not fill twenty positions. About sixteen thousand cooks and servers fed Florida. About eight thousand stockers and packers kept the shelves stocked. About four thousand nursing assistants dressed the bodies of the dying.

They held the spoon. They held the hand. They held the body that could no longer hold itself. For twenty years, for twenty-odd, for a decade in a Key West kitchen making the conch chowder and the fritter batter that nobody else knew. They held the dying at three in the morning when the family could not get there. They held the child while the mother worked the double shift. They held the home together with their presence. You removed the presence and called it law. The people who [fear the return to the violence they fled](/articles/2026-07-26-haitians-losing-tps-fear-deportation-to-violence-family-separation/) do not go quietly into the night you scheduled for them.

Steven Camarota, research director at the Center for Immigration Studies, called the collapse an opportunity. "Their departure creates real opportunities for less-educated, American-born workers."
