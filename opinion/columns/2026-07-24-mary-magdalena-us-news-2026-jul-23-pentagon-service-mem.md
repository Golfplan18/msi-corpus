Four names on the manifest to Dover. Four bodies on the airplanes. Four boxes on the tarmac. The list shows fourteen.

The Pentagon's official online casualty list for the Iran war now shows fourteen, down from eighteen reported as of Wednesday. Three military officials told the New York Times the four were excluded because their deaths occurred during a period in which President Donald Trump had declared a ceasefire in April. A Pentagon spokesperson attributed the reduced count to "temporary data disruptions."

Four soldiers are the difference. Three were killed in an attack on a base used by U.S. forces in Jordan. The fourth died during a controlled detonation of an Iranian drone at Erbil air base in Iraq.

A separate Names of Fallen list on the Defense Casualty Analysis System website currently includes thirteen service members. The Pentagon issued a press release Wednesday documenting the formal transfer of remains of four fallen service members. Trump attended. Their names do not appear on the public casualty list.

The change came one day after Trump went to Dover Air Force Base for a dignified transfer ceremony for the four. He stood before the boxes. He told reporters the fallen supported his argument that the war is necessary to prevent Iran from developing a nuclear weapon. U.S. intelligence assessed that Iran abandoned its nuclear weapons program in 2003. The administration has spent months [building the case that the war is worth its cost](/articles/2026-06-19-thousands-killed-across-middle-east-as-us-iran-war-ends/).

Trump posted a chart on his social media platform showing the war death toll of eighteen and comparing it to those of previous U.S. wars. He captioned the chart: "These are the real facts. REPORT THEM!" The chart misstated the number of people killed in Iraq and Afghanistan. Trump told reporters: "We were in Vietnam for twenty years, lost thousands and hundreds of thousands of lives. We were in Afghanistan for years. Well, we're in for four months and we — I had a very sad day yesterday. I went to Dover, four great American patriots killed. That's 18… and one is too many, but it's 18. Whereas in Vietnam, we lost 200,000." The actual U.S. military death toll in Vietnam was 58,220.

Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, said the reduced death toll stems from the administration's claim that the war technically ended in June, when Trump signed a temporary ceasefire with Iran. "Let me explain this absurd ruse," Massie wrote. "The Pentagon is pretending there have been two Iran wars separated by a brief cease-fire." He said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth "IS BREAKING THE LAW and must be held accountable" by going more than ninety days without congressional authorization. Democrats have been [pressing Trump to end the war since the first soldiers fell](/articles/2026-07-21-democrats-press-trump-to-end-iran-war-after-three-u-s-troops-killed/).
