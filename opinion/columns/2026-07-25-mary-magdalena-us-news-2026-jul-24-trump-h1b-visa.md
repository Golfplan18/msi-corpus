The number is one hundred thousand dollars. That is what it costs to close a legal door that was open. Donald Trump set the price. He called it protection.

In September 2025, the president issued a proclamation imposing a $100,000 fee on new H-1B visas — the program admitting 65,000 highly skilled foreign workers annually, with another 20,000 for holders of advanced degrees. Before the proclamation, employers typically paid about $2,000 to $5,000. The fee does not apply to foreign citizens already in the United States on student visas.

Twenty Democratic state attorneys general sued. On [8 June](/articles/2026-06-08-federal-judge-strikes-down-trump-s-100-000-h-1b-visa-fee/), a federal judge struck it down as an unlawful tax Congress never authorized. The administration appealed. On Friday, a three-judge panel of the First Circuit declined to halt the ruling, finding the administration had not shown it was likely to succeed.

The stated justification was that the H-1B program had been exploited — that employers brought in foreign workers to replace, not supplement, American labor. As if a $100,000 wall were a scalpel and not a sledgehammer.

Few employers have paid the fee since it was instituted.

You are a man who prices human beings out of their lives and calls it a fee. That is what you are. You did not make a difficult choice. You did not balance competing interests. You signed a number that closed a door on people who had already said yes to the work, and you felt nothing, because they are not people to you. They are numbers on a page. They are a crowd you can point to when you need applause. You are not protecting American workers. You are building a wall out of other people's futures and calling it a policy. You are small, Donald. The number you signed is larger than your understanding of what it does to a human body.

The software engineer in Hyderabad has been awake since September. His employer offered him the job. The petition was filed. The lawyers were paid. His mother sold the last of her gold — not for your fee, for the original application, the $5,000 the system used to ask, because that is what mothers in Hyderabad do when their children are leaving.

Your proclamation added $95,000. Not phased in. Not negotiated with the Congress the Constitution gives power over immigration. Signed by you in September, while the man in Hyderabad was sleeping, because it was morning where you were and nighttime where he was.

Your hand did not shake when you signed it. But there is a tightness in your chest now that was not there before September — a pressure behind the sternum that does not respond to the photograph or the applause or the next rally. Her grant cannot absorb $100,000. Her chest is tight. Her breath is short.
