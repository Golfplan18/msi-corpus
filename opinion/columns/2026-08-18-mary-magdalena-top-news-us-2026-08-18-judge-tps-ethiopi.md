On Tuesday, U.S. District Court Judge Brian E. Murphy denied a request from African Communities Together to postpone the termination of Temporary Protected Status for approximately five thousand Ethiopians living in the United States. The plaintiffs had asked the court to delay the end of the designation while their underlying legal challenge moved through the courts.

TPS, established by Congress, allows nationals of countries experiencing armed conflict, natural disaster, or other humanitarian crisis to live and work legally in the United States on a temporary basis. The Biden administration granted protected status to some Ethiopians for an eighteen-month period beginning in 2020, after war broke out in the Tigray region and spread to other parts of the country. Then-Secretary of Homeland Security Alejandro Mayorkas extended the designation because the war was still going on. Secretary Kristi Noem moved to terminate the designation. The lawyers argued two things: that Noem lacked the authority to terminate TPS — that only the Attorney General holds that power — and that the termination violated their clients' constitutional rights. Murphy rejected both. He ruled the Secretary had the authority. He ruled the Constitution was not offended.

In June, the [Supreme Court ruled that the Trump administration may end TPS for Haitians and Syrians](/articles/2026-07-10-supreme-court-rules-trump-may-end-tps-for-haitians-syrians-affecting-1-3-million/), affecting approximately 1.3 million people. Earlier this month, [Judge Murphy applied that precedent to Haitians](/articles/2026-08-05-judge-murphy-applies-tps-precedent-to-haitians/). His Tuesday decision applies it to Ethiopians.
