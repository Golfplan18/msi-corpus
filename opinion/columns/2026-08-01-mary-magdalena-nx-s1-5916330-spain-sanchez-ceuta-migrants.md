Sixty-seven people drowned this week, Pedro. The sea laid them at the foot of your barrier. The men of Europe did not see bodies. They saw an invasion. Giorgia called it a threat. Donald called it a catastrophe. No one called it a funeral. No one called it a drowning.

Nearly 60,000 people crossed into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta from Morocco this week. At least 67 died, most of them drowning while trying to swim around the border breakwater. The Spanish government mobilized the army and installed a 500-meter floating barrier. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez declared the situation under control and wrote to European allies accusing some of "attacking Spain." Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni temporarily suspended the Schengen free-movement agreement from Spain, calling the event a "threat" and invoking "the threat of terrorism." U.S. President Donald Trump called it an "invasion" and blamed "weak" Spanish laws. Morocco's ambassador to Spain, Karima Benyaich, said the crossing unfolded against the kingdom's wishes; witnesses and analysts described Moroccan border forces as absent or complicit during the critical hours when thousands walked across by foot. The International Crisis Group's Riccardo Fabiani called it a manufactured crisis. VOX's Santiago Abascal called for the immediate militarization of the border and the deportation of every minor who crossed, including children carried through the open gate.
