This month in western Wisconsin's third congressional district, nonpartisan canvassers with GrassRoots Organizing Western Wisconsin — Groww — are going door to door. Forty of them, three days a week, four thousand conversations by November. Their target is Republican Representative Derrick Van Orden, who voted for the administration's reconciliation bill, the one the president calls "big and beautiful." Independent estimates project the bill delivers $1tn in healthcare cuts over the next decade. The cuts take effect in January.

Estimates show more than 276,000 Wisconsinites could lose coverage as the cuts roll out. Wisconsin is one of ten states that never expanded Medicaid under the Affordable Care Act, and the state program BadgerCare covers low-income families. Whether to expand it is now a live question in the open governor's race — and in the congressional campaign between Van Orden and Rebecca Cooke, whose campaign lists "healing our healthcare system" first among her priorities. Two hospitals in western Wisconsin closed permanently in 2024, and researchers have identified others at risk of closure or service reductions. Voter anger over the cost of care was already on open display this summer, when the administration's healthy-eating tour met it head-on.

Asked about the coming cuts, Van Orden said: "There has been no cuts to Medicare and Medicaid. There's not going to be cuts to Medicare and Medicaid. What's going to end, though, is the rampant waste, fraud and abuse present."
