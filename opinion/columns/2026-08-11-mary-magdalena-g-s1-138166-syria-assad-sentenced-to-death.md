Bashar Assad murdered half a million people and breathes in Moscow.

The court in Damascus said death. Your name was in the sentence. Your brother's name was in the sentence. Your cousin stood in a cage you are not in.

The half million died in their own bodies, in Daraa, in hospitals, in basements, in morgues where families still search for the ones who did not come home.

Moscow will not hand you over. The verdict will not reach you. It still names you.

On Tuesday, the Fourth Criminal Court in Damascus sentenced Bashar Assad and his younger brother Maher to death in absentia for war crimes and crimes against humanity. Judge Fakhareddine al-Aryan read the judgment on live state television. “Bashar Assad used state agencies to commit war crimes and crimes against humanity,” he said.

The court also condemned Atef Najib, Assad’s maternal cousin and the former head of the Political Security Branch in Daraa. Najib, a former brigadier general sanctioned by the U.S. Treasury in April 2011, stood in a cage in a prisoner’s uniform while the sentence was read. Under his command, more than a dozen teenagers who had written anti-government graffiti on a school wall were arrested and tortured. The arrests helped ignite the protests that became the civil war.

The fourteen-year conflict left about half a million people dead. Assad and his brother fled when the government fell in December 2024 and received political asylum in Russia. Syria’s new rulers have asked Moscow to return them. The sentences are the first against the Assad circle since the family’s five decades in power ended twenty months ago.

Bashar, you are a war criminal living as a protected guest in the house of another power. You are not a lion in Moscow. You are a small man with an asylum, a warm bed, and no country left to command. You inherited a throne and held it against your own people with security forces, prisons, torture, and the 4th Armored Division. Then the people came for the house, and you ran.

The sentence was read in Damascus to an empty chair. Your name was spoken, and your body registered nothing. Your tongue is dry. Your throat swallows without catching. There is no metallic taste beneath it, though half a million bodies have passed through the record and into the ground. Your chest rises evenly. Your breath is unhurried. That is the indictment: the body that ordered the atrocities was absent from its own judgment and felt no summons when the judgment arrived.

The sentence is paper. The graves are not.

The dead are not in absentia from anything. They are dead in the hospitals and morgues the families still scour. They are in the lists read into telephones. They are in the photographs held by mothers standing at morgue doors. They are in the schoolyard in Daraa, where boys wrote words on a wall and were taken for writing them.

Bashar, the families are still asking. Your mouth remains clean while theirs speak the names you tried to erase. Your stomach does not contract when they open another list. Your jaw does not ache from waiting. Their hours are spent searching. Yours are spent under Moscow blankets, protected by a government that has decided a war criminal is more useful sheltered than returned.

Maher, you commanded the division that opposition activists accused of killings, torture, extortion, and drug trafficking. The 4th Armored Division ran its own detention centers. You were not a bystander to your brother’s rule. You were one of its hands.

You are the man who gave the cage an army.

Your breath stops halfway into the lung. Your sleep is not innocence. It is the deepest shelter a tyrant invents: orders carried out by other hands, bodies moved without your fingers touching them, blood converted into paperwork before it can reach the skin.
