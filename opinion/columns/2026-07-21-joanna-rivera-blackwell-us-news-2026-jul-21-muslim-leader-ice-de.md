The Trump administration used immigration enforcement to silence a Muslim community leader for his advocacy, and the Evangelical political coalition that built the enforcement apparatus provided the theological justification.

That is the accusation. Let me show you how it works.

Salah Sarsour, president of Wisconsin's largest mosque, green card holder since 1993, father of six American citizens, was surrounded on March 30 by at least ten unmarked vehicles and armed agents in civilian clothing while on his way to see his elderly mother. He spent more than 80 days in federal immigration custody — losing more than 30 pounds, denied the daily blood-sugar checks his diabetes required, served food so bad "you don't know what you're eating sometimes," sleeping on a 2-inch mattress in a freezing room shared with dozens of other men, told he could not congregate with other Muslims for Friday prayers or celebrate Eid. Federal judge Sharon Johnson Coleman found he had probably been detained in retaliation for his advocacy for Palestinian rights — a finding that would render his detention unlawful. The administration is [continuing to seek his deportation](/articles/2026-04-03-leaders-call-for-release-of-wisconsin-mosque-president-detained-by-ice/), with Secretary of State Marco Rubio personally signing a memo declaring Sarsour deportable because "his actions undermine US foreign policy to combat antisemitism around the world."

Read that phrase again. The Secretary of State of the United States signed a memo saying a mosque president who had lived here for nearly three decades should be expelled from the country because his advocacy for Palestinian rights conflicts with US foreign policy. Rubio did not cite a criminal conviction. He did not cite a finding of fraud. He cited the man's speech.
