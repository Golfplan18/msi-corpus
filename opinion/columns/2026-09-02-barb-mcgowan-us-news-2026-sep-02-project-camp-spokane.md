*Two plastic totes. One charter from Sesame Street. Thirty-three disasters.*

Read this story. A Spokane mother named Mareesha Backman hands her youngest son to a stranger at a free day camp, two weeks after wildfires forced 60,000 people from their homes and burned 920 structures to ash. She finally gets to see a doctor. Her 7-year-old sits down in a thankfulness circle and tells the counselors he's glad he didn't die. The volunteers are saints. The Sesame Street fairy flew out from New York. The school district opened its buildings. The Red Cross showed up. It is a beautiful thing, and it is bullshit that any of us have to feel grateful for it.

Project:Camp has mounted thirty-three of these pop-up camps in ten states since 2021. Thirty-three disasters in five years. That is not a charity's mission statement. That is a measurement of how broken this country has gotten. The article literally calls it 'education in emergencies' — a best practice borrowed from international humanitarian aid. From refugee camps. We are running refugee-camp logistics in eastern Washington because the richest country in the history of the world cannot be bothered to fund a baseline childcare and disaster-recovery system that exists before the smoke shows up.

The mother said she felt 'lucky.' Lucky her husband works for the school district. Lucky in America, after her house burned. 169 Spokane students lost their homes. 38 staff. Twenty more kids up the road in Nine Mile Falls. The family lived in a hot-box trailer with a dead fridge and no running toilet for nine goddamn days. And the safety net for those kids was two plastic totes flown in from out of state by a nonprofit with no more than a T-shirt budget and a Sesame Street booking.

Who paid for that? Not the senators who took fossil-fuel money and called the climate bill socialism. Not the governors who cut emergency-preparedness budgets. Not the donor class that pocketed the tax cuts while the country burns. They get the photo op. They get to call it a great community. Then they go home and vote to make damn sure the next fire burns just as hot.

Take the flag pin off before you praise the volunteers for doing your goddamn job, you freedom-frying hypocrites. The hug that mother needed should not have had to fly in on a charter from Sesame Street.
