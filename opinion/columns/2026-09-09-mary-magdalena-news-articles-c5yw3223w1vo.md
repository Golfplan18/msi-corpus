At Prime Minister's Questions on 9 September 2026, Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch challenged Prime Minister Andy Burnham on the path to 3% of GDP on defence spending by 2030. Her published plan of 3 September proposed funding the increase in part by saving at least £4 billion through reforms to housing benefit. The Department for Work and Pensions forecasts total benefit spending will reach £408.6 billion in 2030/31, with nearly one million young people not in education, employment, or training. Burnham rejected the housing benefit cut, saying it would "create levels of homelessness in this country that we wouldn't have seen before, hundreds of thousands of children in temporary accommodation." He reaffirmed the NATO 3.5% by 2035 target, with 3% set at the spring spending review, and proposed funding the increase through education, training, and mental health support for the Neets. Badenoch's response in the Commons: "We need to fund our defence, not spend all of our money on welfare." A Conservative Party spokesperson said afterwards that it was "shameful" the prime minister would not cut welfare to fund defence.
