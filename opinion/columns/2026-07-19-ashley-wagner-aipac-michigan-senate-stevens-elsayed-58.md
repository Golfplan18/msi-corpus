AIPAC is starving Michigan families of representation by buying their senator for $30 million.

The Associated Press followed U.S. Rep. Haley Stevens around a July campaign stop in West Michigan and asked about the American Israel Public Affairs Committee — twice. She responded for more than 800 words. She did not say "AIPAC" once. She talked about manufacturing, about taking on Donald Trump, about her electability pitch. Israel came up exactly once, when she said she wanted to see the United States and Israel "working together to rebuild Gaza."

"Anyone who supported me is supporting me because of my dedication to the people of Michigan," she told the AP.

Nobody asked about her dedication. She was asked whether she welcomed the support of an organization that has spent close to $30 million — its largest single-race investment ever, per the AP — backing her against Abdul El-Sayed in the Aug. 4 Democratic Senate primary. She answered the question nobody asked. She did not answer the one that was.

Here is what $30 million looks like from a kitchen table in a house with two kids in daycare and a school-supply list on the fridge.

It is more than 2,500 children's worth of childcare for a year at the national average — enough to fill every licensed daycare slot in a mid-sized Michigan school district. It is more than the Title I allocation for most individual school districts in the state. It is roughly what the federal government owes Michigan's special-education students every year under IDEA — a fifty-year-old promise to fund 40 percent of special-education costs that the federal government has never kept above 18. The $24 billion annual shortfall nationally comes out of every other line in every school budget in the state: the art teacher your school does not have, the counselor covering three buildings instead of one, the reading specialist cut in the last budget round.

I have a spreadsheet on my kitchen table that tracks what my family spends every month. The daycare line alone runs $2,400. The mortgage is more. The student-loan payment is more than it was three years ago because the terms keep changing. I know what those numbers look like from this side. I also know what it looks like when the senator who is supposed to fight for the programs that would change those numbers cannot name the organization paying for her campaign instead.

$30 million flowing into a primary is not a one-time transaction. It is a standing claim on legislative time, and legislative time is finite. Every hour allocated to the donor's agenda is an hour not spent on the family-budget agenda — the childcare-subsidy expansion that died when the Build Back Better Act was negotiated down to nothing, the version that would have saved families with young children $5,000 to $6,500 a year in 32 states. The Pell Grant that covered 80 percent of a public-university education in your parents' generation and now covers 25. The student-loan repayment terms that changed three times in eighteen months for borrowers enrolled in the SAVE plan before a federal court enjoined it. The housing costs that have pushed, per Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies, half of all American renter households into cost-burdened territory, with middle-income renters seeing the fastest increases.

Annie Lowrey writes that poverty in the United States is not an inevitability but a choice. The choice is made in rooms where $30 million determines whose questions get answered. Stevens's 800-word evasion on a single question is not a communication failure. It is the answer.

Michigan families are running their own spreadsheets right now. The daycare invoice that is bigger than the car payment. The school-supply list that has become a second August grocery run. The student-loan payment that tripled when the SAVE injunction hit. The grocery receipt that stays high even though the CPI says inflation is back to the Fed's target. Those families needed a senator whose first commitment was to the line items on their spreadsheets — childcare subsidies, Title I funding, the IDEA shortfall, Pell Grant restoration, housing affordability.

The primary is Aug. 4. By then, AIPAC and its affiliated groups will have spent more on this race than they have ever spent on any race in the country. Haley Stevens will have said a lot of words about a lot of things. She will not have said the word that names the people who wrote the checks.

Michigan families needed a senator who fights for their budgets. They are getting a fundraiser for someone else's.

You're on your own, kid.
