He sleeps tonight in a crib that is not his mother's arms.
She did the job the state required. The state did not do what he needs.
Your budget put him there.

An Associated Press analysis by Moriah Balingit, published this week, found that hundreds of thousands of children sat on childcare assistance waitlists across twenty-three states and the District of Columbia this spring. In three additional states, many eligible families who applied were turned away rather than added to a waitlist.

Indiana's program ran short on funds and began waitlisting families who had qualified for the subsidy. The cases Balingit documented include a medical technician who stopped buying groceries and turned to food banks so she could pay for her infant's care, a mother of two who needs a full-time job but cannot afford full-time childcare, and a mother who completed every state-required step to bring her baby home from foster care only to learn the state could not — or would not — pay for the childcare that would let her do it.

Indiana, hear what your funding has arranged.

The medical technician works in a hospital. She touches sick people for a living. The state of Indiana told her to keep working. The state told her to pay for childcare out of what the state pays her. She did the arithmetic. The arithmetic had no good answer. She chose her infant over her own food. She eats what the food bank gives her. Her infant does not eat food bank formula. Her infant eats what she buys.

There is a metallic taste in your mouth, Indiana, when you read that sentence. It does not leave. It is the taste of the paycheck you arranged her to live on — the one that arrives at the end of every week and is already gone before she gets home.

The caseworker will say it again next month. The caseworker goes home to her own children at five o'clock.
