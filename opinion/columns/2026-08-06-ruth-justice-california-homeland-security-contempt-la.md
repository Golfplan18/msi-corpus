The Department of Homeland Security failed to comply with a court order, and a federal judge fined the government $500 a day until it does.

U.S. District Judge Maame Ewusi-Mensah Frimpong held DHS in contempt over its failure to create digital copies of communications from the personal cell phones of more than 800 immigration agents. The order arose from discovery in litigation over immigration-enforcement operations in the Los Angeles area in 2025.

At a June hearing, DHS attorneys said the agency was processing three phones every two weeks. The plaintiffs estimated that pace would take nearly four years. The government also must reimburse the plaintiffs' attorneys for the legal costs of obtaining the records.

The arithmetic is the verdict. A court ordered the records in January; by June the agency had produced none. Its own attorneys said it was processing three phones every two weeks — a pace the plaintiffs calculated at nearly four years. No private party would be given that clock. An ordinary litigant that let the months pass on a discovery order would stand before the judge explaining why, with sanctions already running, not quietly processing three phones a fortnight and calling it compliance. The department did exactly that until contempt sanctions priced the delay. That is the whole of this dispute, and it is enough: the agency sat under the same order it treated as optional.

The fine is $500 a day. The order still stands.
