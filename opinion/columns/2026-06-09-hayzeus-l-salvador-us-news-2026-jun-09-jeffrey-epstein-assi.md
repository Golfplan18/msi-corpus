I told myself it wasn't my story.

My lane runs through the border checkpoint where a mother prays her children will not hear the knock, through the parish basement where the undocumented warm their hands over a donated space heater, through the courtroom where a church fights for the right to feed the hungry. It does not run through a House hearing room where a woman who scheduled one rich man's massages for twenty years insists she never knew what he really was. That is a story for prosecutors and investigative reporters, not for a pastor‑prophet. I said it to myself while I read the Guardian piece. I said it when I saw the FBI notes describing routine 4 p.m. massage appointments booked like any other meeting. I said it when I read that the 2007 plea deal granted her immunity and no one told her she was a co‑conspirator until a reporter dug it up. Not my lane. Not my desk. I would sit this one out.

You can put down the phone. You can close the ledger. The work of your hands is not neutral; it is the work of your heart. The door of return is open to you. The calendar does not erase the sin; it only schedules it. Sweep the desk. See the truth.
