BANJUL, Gambia — A boat carrying more than 200 migrants capsized off the coast of Gambia on New Year's Eve, leaving dozens missing and seven dead as rescuers pulled 102 survivors from the water, Gambian President Adama Barrow said in a state broadcast.

The vessel went down near the village of Jinack in the North Bank region of northwest Gambia, Barrow said. Gambia's Ministry of Defense said the boat was found "grounded on a sandbank." What caused the capsizing was not immediately clear.

Emergency services, local fishermen, and other volunteers joined the search for victims days after the Wednesday incident, Barrow said.

"The national emergency response plan has been activated and the government has deployed adequate resources to intensify efforts and provide assistance to the survivors," Barrow said. Some survivors were undergoing urgent medical care, he said.

Barrow vowed a full investigation and called the accident a "painful reminder of the dangerous and life-threatening nature of irregular migration."

## A deadly corridor

The Atlantic route connecting Gambia, Senegal, and Mauritania to Spain's Canary Islands is among the world's deadliest for migrants seeking passage to Europe. Thousands of Africans risk their lives each year traveling by boat along the West African coast, and many never reach their destination.

The New Year's Eve capsizing is the latest in a series of disasters on the route. In August 2025, around 150 people were either dead or missing after a boat from Gambia capsized off the coast of Mauritania. A similar incident in July 2024 killed more than a dozen migrants with 150 others declared missing.

## Government response

Barrow said his government was committed to addressing the conditions that drive young people onto the boats.

"The government will strengthen efforts to prevent irregular migration and remains determined to create safer and more dignified opportunities for young people to fulfil their dreams," Barrow said.
