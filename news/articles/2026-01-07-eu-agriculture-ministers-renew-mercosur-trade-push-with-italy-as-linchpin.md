BRUSSELS — European Union agriculture ministers from all 27 member states met in Brussels on Wednesday to advance a long-stalled free trade agreement with five South American nations, with Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni's decision seen as potentially decisive in overcoming French opposition to the deal.

EU trade negotiator Maroš Šefčovič said after the meeting that negotiations would continue Friday, amid reports that a signing ceremony could take place in Paraguay as early as January 12. He called the agreement "the biggest free trade agreement we ever negotiated" and said it would increase EU agrifood exports by up to 50 percent.

The EU-Mercosur agreement covers a market of 780 million people and a quarter of global gross domestic product, and would progressively remove duties on nearly all goods traded between the European Union and the Mercosur nations of Brazil, Argentina, Bolivia, Paraguay and Uruguay.

## Italy's role

Italy is seen as the swing vote. If Meloni joins the deal's supporters, led by Germany, the agreement would pass over the objections of France and Poland, according to the Associated Press. Meloni posted on X on Tuesday that she welcomed European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen's proposal to fast-track financial support for farmers, but she did not commit to backing the deal.

France's opposition derailed the agreement in December, when farmer protests prompted von der Leyen to cancel a planned trip to Brazil where EU officials had hoped to sign the deal after 26 years of negotiations.

French Agriculture Minister Annie Genevard reaffirmed France's opposition Wednesday, saying the agreement threatens sectors including beef, chicken, sugar, ethanol and honey.

"As long as the combat is not over, nothing is lost," Genevard said on French broadcaster France Info. She also said there was no guarantee the deal would survive the European Parliament, adding that Italy "will likely" approve the agreement.

## Safeguards and geopolitics

Šefčovič said recent internal EU negotiations produced new protections, including what he described as "semiautomatic triggering thresholds" that would activate if Mercosur imports are found to be deeply undercutting EU products.

"It's a landmark one. This is the biggest free trade agreement we ever negotiated," Šefčovič said, arguing that EU credibility as a trading partner is among its most valuable assets in the current global environment.

Supporters of the deal say it would offer a geopolitical alternative to both China's export controls and the Trump administration's tariff policies and its recent military operation in Venezuela, which took place about a week before Wednesday's Brussels meeting. The EU has been seeking new trade alliances as commercial tensions with both Washington and Beijing have grown.

French President Emmanuel Macron has led opposition to the deal, with his government arguing it risks fueling far-right political movements in France that have used the agreement as a rallying point. Macron's government has demanded monitoring mechanisms, pesticide restrictions in Mercosur nations, and increased port inspections of imports.

The political divide within Mercosur itself — particularly between Argentina's President Javier Milei and Brazil's President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva — has not prevented South American governments from pressing forward with the alliance. Venezuela was suspended from Mercosur in 2016.
