Japan’s Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi plans to dissolve the lower house of Parliament soon and call a snap election to seek the public’s mandate for her policies, a senior ruling party official said Wednesday.

Shunichi Suzuki, secretary general of the Liberal Democratic Party, told reporters that Takaichi told him and other top officials she intends to dissolve the lower house “soon” after it convenes on Jan. 23, according to the report. Suzuki did not give specific dates for a dissolution or for the snap election, and said Takaichi will explain her plans at a news conference next Monday.

A snap election is seen as an effort to capitalize on Takaichi’s approval ratings, which are described as around 70%, to help the beleaguered governing Liberal Democratic Party gain more seats. Takaichi was elected prime minister in October and became Japan’s first female prime minister, the report said.

The ruling coalition has a slim majority in the lower house — the more powerful of Japan’s two-chamber Parliament — after an election loss in 2024. Opposition lawmakers criticized the plan, saying it would be a selfish move that could delay parliamentary discussion on the budget, which needs to be approved as soon as possible.

Media reports have said Takaichi plans to dissolve the lower house on Jan. 23, the first day of this year’s ordinary session, paving the way for a snap election as early as Feb. 8.

Suzuki said Takaichi wants to seek the public’s mandate for policies that include “proactive” fiscal spending and further accelerating the ongoing military buildup under the coalition with a new partner, the Japan Innovation Party. He said the conservative Japan Innovation Party joined the ruling bloc after the centrist Komeito left due to disagreements over Takaichi’s ideological views and anti-corruption measures.

Takaichi met with Suzuki and other coalition officials Wednesday after talks in Nara with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung, a summit intended to deepen ties between the neighboring countries. The report said she is facing intensifying trade and political tension with China over a remark about Taiwan that angered Beijing days after she took office.

The report said winning the upcoming election would help Takaichi and her governing bloc pass the budget and other legislation more easily. It added that in late December, her Cabinet approved a record 122.3 trillion yen ($770 billion) budget, which needs approval before the fiscal year begins in April to fund measures to fight inflation, support low-income households and projects aimed at supporting economic growth.

Takaichi is known for hawkish and nationalistic views, and the report described her as ultra-conservative on social issues such as gender and sexual diversity. Suzuki said she wants to win back conservative voters who have turned to emerging populist parties in recent major elections.

The Liberal Democratic Party has since handpicked a number of independents to join the governing coalition to reach a slim majority, the report said, but it remains a minority in the upper house.
