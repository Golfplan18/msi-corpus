London police recorded 97 homicides in 2025, down from 109 the previous year and the fewest since 2014, the Metropolitan Police said Monday. The per-capita rate of 1.1 per 100,000 residents is the lowest since comparable records began in 1997. Mayor Sadiq Khan said the figures directly refute claims by U.S. President Donald Trump and others on the political right that crime in Britain's capital is out of control.

The data place London's murder rate below those of Paris, New York, and Berlin on the same per-capita measure, and surface amid documented concern from researchers that misinformation about London crime is shaping decisions by potential visitors.

The Metropolitan Police said London's homicide rate stood at 1.1 per 100,000 residents in 2025 — the lowest since the force began keeping comparable records in 1997. That compares to 1.6 per 100,000 in Paris, 2.8 in New York, and 3.2 in Berlin, the force said.

Mayor Sadiq Khan said the data directly refute a narrative that has spread widely on social media and been amplified by American politicians, including President Donald Trump, portraying London as a city where crime is out of control.

"There are some politicians and commentators who've been spamming our social media with an endless stream of distortions and untruths, painting an image of a dystopian London," Khan told the Associated Press. "And nothing could be further from the truth."

## Khan and Trump

Trump, who has directed personal attacks at Khan for a decade, said in September that crime in London is "through the roof." He called Khan a "stone-cold loser," a "nasty person," and, speaking before the U.N. General Assembly in September, a "terrible, terrible mayor." Trump has also claimed, without documented evidence, that Khan wants to bring Sharia, or Islamic law, to London.

"There are certain politicians, certain commentators who have been using London as a punchbag" to fit their own political agenda, Khan told the AP.

Khan said London remained a world capital for tourism, sports, culture, and education, adding that more Americans came to London to study, work, or invest in 2025 than in any year since records began.

## What drove the decline

City officials said a combination of targeted policing aimed at organized crime and a violence reduction unit focused on keeping young people out of gangs drove down violent crime.

## A fuller picture

The homicide figures do not capture the full range of crime Londoners experience. The Crime Survey for England and Wales, which asks residents about their experiences rather than relying on police reports, found overall crime rose 7% in the year to March 2025 compared with the prior 12 months. The survey found overall crime remains significantly lower than in 2017.

Vijay Pankhania, a London resident walking his dog near City Hall beside the Thames, said minor crimes had become more common in his experience. "It feels like the minor crimes have gone up," he said. "Things like stealing mobile phones from people – I've seen that loads of times around here."

## Misinformation and tourism

Mark J. Hill, a lecturer in cultural computation at King's College London who studies the growth of social media posts about violence in the city, said online narratives about London crime were shaping real-world behavior, including decisions about travel.

"Posts where people are asking if it's safe to visit London might be bots, but are just as likely to be real people who are concerned about coming to London," Hill said. "These narratives are impacting their decisions about where they might go on holiday. … That's, in my opinion, one of the really problematic things about it."

Hill said no straightforward remedy exists. "There is no magic bullet at the moment for making people aware of what is statistically the case and what is misinformation or a misunderstanding of the actual reality," he said.
