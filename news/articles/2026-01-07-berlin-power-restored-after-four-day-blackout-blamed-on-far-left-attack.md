The attack drew federal scrutiny onto a network of far-left groups that German intelligence says has targeted infrastructure in and around Berlin for more than a decade, raising questions about the vulnerability of urban power grids to politically motivated sabotage.

Berlin's power network operator began reconnecting the last tens of thousands of outage-affected households Wednesday, ending what authorities described as the longest blackout the German capital has experienced since World War II.

The four-day outage began Saturday morning when a fire on a bridge carrying high-voltage cables over the Teltow Canal cut power to about 45,000 households and 2,200 businesses in southwest Berlin, affecting an estimated 100,000 people, the Associated Press reported. Restoration proved slow: as of Tuesday, some 25,500 households and 1,200 businesses — concentrated in the prosperous Zehlendorf district — remained without electricity during freezing temperatures.

## A claim of responsibility

Investigators focused on a written statement headlined "Turning off the juice to the rulers," in which a far-left group alleged it had "successfully sabotaged" a gas-fired power plant in the city's Lichterfelde district. The statement said the aim was to target the fossil-fuel energy industry, not to cause the residential outages that followed.

The claimed intent did not match the outcome. Tens of thousands of ordinary Berlin households spent days without heat or light in winter cold as workers repaired the damaged cable infrastructure.

## Intelligence context and prior attacks

Germany's domestic intelligence agency said self-styled "Volcano Groups" have carried out attacks on infrastructure in Berlin and the surrounding state of Brandenburg since 2011. A 2024 attack attributed to the groups struck a pylon supplying a Tesla factory near Berlin and temporarily halted production at the facility.

On Tuesday, Germany's federal prosecutor's office assumed jurisdiction over the case, citing suspicions of anticonstitutional sabotage, membership in a terrorist organization, and arson.
