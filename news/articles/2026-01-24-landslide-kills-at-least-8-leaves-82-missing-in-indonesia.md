A massive landslide triggered by heavy rains killed at least eight people and left 82 others missing in Java Occidental province, Indonesia, early Saturday. The landslide struck the villages of Pasir Langu and Pasir Kuning in Bandung Occidental district around 3 a.m., burying approximately 34 houses as rescue teams worked through thick mud and debris to search for survivors.

Twenty-four residents managed to escape the disaster.

Days of torrential rainfall had caused rivers to overflow before the ground gave way. Mud, rocks, and uprooted trees swept through the mountainside communities, transforming green rice terraces and roads into turbid brown sludge that buried homes and cut off access routes.

## Rescue Efforts Hampered by Weather and Terrain

Unstable soil and continued heavy rains complicated the search and rescue operations, according to Teten Ali Mungku Engkun, director of the Disaster Management Office of Java Occidental. Local authorities assessed damage and deployed emergency response teams immediately after the landslide. Families living within 100 meters of the disaster zone were evacuated due to the risk of additional slides.

Officials warned residents in landslide-prone areas to remain alert and flee immediately if they heard rumbling sounds, noticed ground movement, or felt unsafe conditions.

## Indonesia's Seasonal Vulnerability

Such disasters occur regularly during Indonesia's annual rainy season, when seasonal downpours frequently cause flooding and landslides across the archipelago's more than 17,000 islands. Millions of Indonesians live in mountainous areas or near fertile flood plains, placing them in harm's way when the seasonal rains arrive between October and April.

## A Pattern of Catastrophe

The death toll comes less than two months after catastrophic flooding and landslides devastated Sumatra, Indonesia's largest island, in December. Those disasters killed at least 1,200 people and injured more than 7,000, according to the National Disaster Management Agency.

Similar sequences of deadly weather have struck the country repeatedly. More than 20 people died in Central Java province in January after being swept away by floods and landslides triggered by torrential rains.
