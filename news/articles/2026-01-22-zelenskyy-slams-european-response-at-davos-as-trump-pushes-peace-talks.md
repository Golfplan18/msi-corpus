Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy criticized European allies Thursday at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, saying the continent's response to Russia's invasion has been slow and inadequate.

Zelenskyy invoked the film "Groundhog Day" to describe repeating warnings about European defense year after year. He said Europe "looks lost" and contrasted the continent's approach with more decisive U.S. actions in Venezuela and Iran.

The speech came after an hour-long meeting with President Donald Trump, who called the talks "very good." Zelenskyy said the meeting was "productive and meaningful" and told Trump he wants to make a deal to end the war.

The negotiations come as Ukraine faces critical shortages in personnel and resources. Ukraine's defense ministry reported some 200,000 troop desertions and approximately 2 million Ukrainians avoiding the draft. Zelenskyy also criticized Europe for failing to use frozen Russian assets to finance Ukraine's defense and for not blocking Russia's "shadow fleet" of oil tankers evading international sanctions.

## Zelenskyy's Critique

Addressing the World Economic Forum, Zelenskyy expressed frustration with what he called Europe's slow and fragmented response to Russia's nearly four-year-old invasion. "Europe looks lost," he said, contrasting the continent's cautious approach with what he portrayed as more decisive U.S. actions in Venezuela and Iran.

He repeated the "Groundhog Day" reference he had made at the same forum a year earlier. "Just last year, here in Davos, I ended my speech with the words: Europe needs to know how to defend itself. A year has passed. And nothing has changed. We are still in a situation where I must say the same words again," he said.

Zelenskyy said Ukrainians live in that repeated reality daily in the war itself. "Repeating the same thing for weeks, months and, of course, for years. And yet that is exactly how we live now. It's our life," he said.

## Trump Meeting and Peace Push

Trump arrived in Davos as his administration accelerates efforts to negotiate an end to the war. Speaking to reporters aboard Air Force One as he flew back to Washington, Trump said his meeting with Zelenskyy went well and that the Ukrainian president told him he wants to make a deal.

"I had a good meeting, but I've had numerous good meetings with President Zelenskyy and it doesn't seem to happen," Trump said.

Trump said both Putin and Zelenskyy want to reach a deal and that "everyone's making concessions" to try to end the war. He identified "boundaries" as a key sticking point that has blocked progress during talks over the past six to seven months.

"The main hold-up is the same things that's been holding it up for the last year," Trump said.

Trump noted that he and Zelenskyy discussed how Ukrainians were surviving the cold winter without heat. "It's really tough for the people of Ukraine," Trump said, calling it "amazing" that residents have persevered through relentless Russian strikes on power infrastructure. "It's no way to live," he said.

## Negotiations Underway

Trump's envoys are moving quickly on multiple fronts. Witkoff and Kushner arrived in Moscow late Thursday for talks with Putin that lasted more than three hours, according to the Kremlin. Witkoff said at Davos that one major issue remained unresolved in negotiations but did not specify what it was.

Zelenskyy said the future status of land in eastern Ukraine currently occupied by Russia is an unresolved issue but that peace proposals are "nearly ready." He said postwar security guarantees between the U.S. and Ukraine have been agreed but would require each country's ratification.

Two days of trilateral meetings involving the U.S., Ukraine and Russia are due to begin Friday in the United Arab Emirates.

"Russians have to be ready for compromises because, you know, everybody has to be ready, not only Ukraine, and this is important for us," Zelenskyy said.

## Ukraine's Crisis

These negotiations come against a backdrop of enormous Ukrainian losses. Russia has captured roughly 20 percent of Ukraine since hostilities began in 2014 and launched a full-scale invasion in 2022. But battlefield gains along the approximately 1,000-kilometer front line have been costly for Moscow, and the Russian economy is feeling the consequences of war and international sanctions.

Ukraine faces critical resource constraints. The country is short on both money and personnel for the war effort. Its defense minister last week reported some 200,000 troop desertions and draft-dodging by about 2 million Ukrainians, compounding challenges on the front line.

Despite significantly boosting its own arms manufacturing, Ukraine still depends on Western weaponry. Zelenskyy thanked Trump for providing U.S.-made Patriot air defense systems that can help stop Russian missiles repeatedly hitting Ukraine's power grid, causing hardship for civilians denied light, heating and running water. He said he asked Trump for more of them.

Yet Zelenskyy saved his strongest criticism for Europe. He chided Europe for being slow to act on key decisions and spending too little on defense. He criticized European countries for failing to stop Russia's "shadow fleet" of oil tankers that are breaking international sanctions, and for balking at using frozen Russian assets in Europe to finance Ukraine.

Europe, he said, "still feels more like a geography, history, a tradition, not a real political force, not a great power."

"Some Europeans are really strong, it's true, but many say we must stand strong, and they always want someone else to tell them how long they need to stand strong, preferably until the next election," he said.

While European countries have provided financial, military and humanitarian support for Kyiv, not all members of the 27-nation European Union are helping equally. Ukraine has also been frustrated by political disagreements within Europe over how to deal with Russia, as well as the bloc's at times slow-moving responses.
