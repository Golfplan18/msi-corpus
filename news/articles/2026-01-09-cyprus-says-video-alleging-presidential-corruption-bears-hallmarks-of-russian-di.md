NICOSIA, Cyprus — Cyprus said Friday it had fallen victim to a disinformation attack targeting its government with corruption allegations, with a Cyprus Security Services analysis concluding the video "bears all the hallmarks" of previous Russian campaigns mounted against France, Germany, and the United States.

The eight-and-a-half-minute video, posted on social media Thursday, weaves together snippets of three figures — the president's brother-in-law and director of the presidential office, a former energy minister, and the chief executive of a major construction company — discussing their ties to President Nikos Christodoulides, campaign financing, and securing foreign investments in a manner suggesting influence peddling.

Its most damaging charge is that the Cypriot government would work to block European Union sanctions against Russian oligarchs in exchange for corporate cash. The video also claims Christodoulides exceeded a 1 million euro ($1.16 million) campaign funding cap by taking off-the-books cash donations ahead of his 2023 presidential victory.

Cyprus government spokesman Constantinos Letymbiotis condemned the video as a collection of "lies, deceptions and unfounded claims" intended to besmirch the government's and the country's image.

## Intelligence assessment

An initial Cyprus Security Services analysis obtained by the Associated Press found the video exhibits "the characteristics of organized Russian disinformation campaigns" similar to a 2021 Russian online operation that targeted EU countries, the United States, and Israel. The analysis said the video — through its editing and narration — does not offer "tangible evidence" of the corruption claims it presents.

Such videos are known in intelligence circles as "kompromat," a Soviet-era tactic used to conduct character assassination, blackmail, or politically weaken opponents, the analysis said.

The analysis stopped short of a definitive attribution, stating it did not rule out that "another actor using a similar methodology" may be responsible. Russia did not immediately comment on the allegation.

## Timing and political fallout

The video appeared the day after Cyprus marked its assumption of the rotating EU presidency — a ceremony attended by Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy — timing that government officials said immediately raised suspicion.

Cyprus has a parliamentary election scheduled approximately four months away, amplifying the video's political impact domestically. The leader of the communist-rooted AKEL party called for the resignation of the presidential office director, one of the three men who appeared in the video.

## Geopolitical context

Since his 2023 election, Christodoulides has moved a formerly Kremlin-friendly Cyprus to a solidly pro-Western posture, advocating staunch support for Ukraine in its war against Russia and forging closer diplomatic and military ties with the United States.

The Cyprus incident fits a documented pattern of alleged Russian influence operations across Europe. In September, Moldovan President Maia Sandu alleged that Moscow carried out a "hybrid war" to try to undermine a parliamentary election and derail Moldova's path toward EU membership — an accusation Moscow denied. Two years ago, French officials and cybersecurity experts in Europe and the United States issued reports pointing to disinformation campaigns orchestrated out of Russia and targeting France.
