The dismissal unravels what legal experts call the largest criminal racketeering case ever filed against protest defendants in U.S. history, though five of the 61 defendants still face domestic terrorism charges that Farmer said can likely stand.

ATLANTA — A Fulton County judge on Tuesday dismissed racketeering charges against all 61 defendants in the "Cop City" prosecution, ruling that Georgia Attorney General Chris Carr lacked the legal authority to bring the case under state law without first obtaining permission from Gov. Brian Kemp.

Judge Kevin Farmer's order found that Carr had not followed the required process before securing the 2023 indictments under Georgia's Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations statute. Carr's office, in a statement issued after the ruling, said it would appeal.

"We strongly disagree with this decision and will continue to vigorously pursue this domestic terrorism case to ensure that justice is served," Carr's office said.

As MSI previously reported, the ruling resolves the racketeering charges against defendants in what legal experts call the largest criminal racketeering case ever filed against protest defendants in U.S. history. <a href="/articles/2025-12-31-georgia-judge-dismisses-racketeering-charges-against-61-cop-city-protesters">MSI's earlier coverage</a> detailed the full scope of the 2023 indictment.

## What charges remain

Five of the 61 defendants also face domestic terrorism and first-degree arson charges, stemming from a 2023 night when masked activists burned a police car in downtown Atlanta and threw rocks at a skyscraper housing the Atlanta Police Foundation.

Farmer said Carr also lacked authority to pursue the arson charge against those five defendants, but that the domestic terrorism charge can likely stand. Each defendant on the dismissed racketeering counts had faced up to 20 years in prison.

## Defense reaction

Amanda Clark Palmer, an attorney for one of the defendants, praised the ruling, saying "the prosecution did not follow the law when filing these charges," according to the Atlanta Journal-Constitution.

"We are relieved the dismissal order has been entered but our relief is not complete yet as we wait to see whether the Attorney General will appeal," Clark Palmer said in a statement.

## Background on the case

The "Cop City" prosecution arose from years of opposition to an 85-acre (34-hectare) police and firefighter training facility in South River Forest outside Atlanta that ultimately cost more than $115 million.

The controversy reached a turning point in January 2023, when state troopers conducting a sweep of the forest killed a 26-year-old activist known as "Tortuguita," who authorities said had fired at them while inside a tent near the construction site. A prosecutor later found the troopers' actions "objectively reasonable." Tortuguita's family filed a lawsuit alleging that his hands were in the air when troopers initially fired pepper balls into the tent and that the force used was excessive.

Protests followed the killing. Masked activists at times attacked police vehicles and construction equipment; opponents also pursued civic strategies including packing City Council meetings and mounting a referendum effort that became entangled in the courts.

The 61 defendants charged under RICO faced allegations spanning a broad range of conduct — from throwing Molotov cocktails at police officers to providing food to protesters.

## Political context

Carr, a Republican who is running for governor, had pursued the prosecution. Kemp had previously praised the case as a step against "out-of-state radicals that threaten the safety of our citizens and law enforcement."

Critics had characterized the indictment as a politically motivated effort to suppress the movement against the facility.
