## Mark Cruz, a Klamath Tribes citizen, confirmed to lead Indian Health Service

**Subtype:** fact

- The U.S. Senate confirmed Mark Cruz, a citizen of the Klamath Tribes, as director of the Indian Health Service in a 51-47 vote on Friday.
- Cruz will serve a four-year term leading the federal agency that provides healthcare to some 2.8 million Native American and Alaska Native patients.
- The Indian Health Service director post has sat vacant since late 2025.
- Cruz told the Senate Indian Affairs Committee in July that he would advocate "ferociously" to improve health outcomes in Indian Country.
- The Senate confirmed Cruz alongside more than 70 other nominees of President Donald Trump, one of its last actions before the August recess.

## Indian Health Service director post vacant since late 2025

The vote ends an extended vacancy at the top of the Indian Health Service, which had been operating without a permanent director since late 2025.

Cruz, a citizen of the Klamath Tribes, takes charge of the federal agency responsible for healthcare delivered to some 2.8 million Native American and Alaska Native patients. His confirmation places a tribal citizen atop the system that serves Indian Country's communities.

At his confirmation hearing in July, Cruz told the Senate Indian Affairs Committee he would advocate "ferociously" to improve health outcomes, drawing on the stake he holds in the agency's work. "My family, my friends and my tribal community will live with the consequences of the work I do for decades to come," he said at the hearing.

The Senate approved him by a 51-47 margin, with the vote counted among the chamber's last actions before the August recess. Cruz was confirmed alongside more than 70 other nominees of President Donald Trump, part of a wave of confirmations lawmakers cleared before leaving Washington.
