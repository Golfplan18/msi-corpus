China's commerce ministry on Tuesday banned exports of dual-use goods — products that can serve both civilian and military purposes — to Japan, prohibiting sales to Japanese military users and any other end-users capable of enhancing Japanese military power. The measure drew an immediate and sharp protest from Tokyo, which called it unacceptable and demanded retraction.

The export ban marks a significant escalation in deteriorating China-Japan relations, arriving days after Beijing launched large-scale military drills near Taiwan and amid months of sharpening disputes over the island's status, Japan's accelerating arms buildup, and Tokyo's increasingly explicit commitment to respond militarily to any Chinese action against Taiwan.

## The ban

The Chinese commerce ministry said in its statement that any individual or organization that violates the rule by transferring or providing Chinese-made dual-use products to Japanese groups or individuals would face legal consequences, regardless of where they are located.

The notice did not identify or describe specific items covered by the prohibition. Technology sector products such as drones and navigation systems are among those that can be adapted for military use, the Associated Press reported.

## Japan's response

Masaaki Kanai, the Japanese Foreign Ministry's head of Asia Oceanian affairs, "strongly protested" to Chinese deputy consul Shi Yong, according to a statement from the Japanese ministry. Kanai said a measure exclusively targeting Japan that deviates from international practice is unacceptable and demanded it be retracted.

## Deteriorating relations

Relations between the two governments worsened sharply in recent months. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said late last year that Japan's military could get involved if China were to take action against Taiwan, a self-ruled island that Beijing claims as its sovereign territory.

In December, Japan said Chinese military aircraft locked radar on its fighter jets despite a safe distance between the aircraft. Japan is pressing ahead with efforts to significantly boost its military capabilities, aiming to double its annual arms spending in response to what it describes as China's growing threats.

Last week, China launched large-scale military drills around Taiwan for two days to warn against what it characterized as separatist and external forces. During that period, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi said Japan's current leaders openly challenge China's territorial sovereignty and the postwar international order. Wang also criticized Taiwan's "pro-independence forces."

## Regional framing

At a meeting in Beijing on Monday, Chinese leader Xi Jinping invoked World War II-era history in talks with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung. Xi called on both countries to "join hands to defend the fruits of victory in World War II and safeguard peace and stability in northeast Asia." Japan was defeated in World War II by the United States and its allies.
