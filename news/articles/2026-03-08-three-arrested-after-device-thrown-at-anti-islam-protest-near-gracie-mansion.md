New York City police are investigating after a counterprotester lit and threw a device packed with nuts, bolts and screws at an anti-Islam demonstration near Gracie Mansion on Saturday, Police Commissioner Jessica Tisch said. A second device was handed to the same individual moments later; he dropped it. Three people were arrested in connection with the confrontation, and no injuries were reported.

The incident began when someone from the anti-Islam rally used pepper spray against a counterprotesting group gathered nearby, Tisch said at a news conference. Tensions rose when a counterprotester then lit and threw a device she described as smaller than a football into the protesting crowd of about 20 people.

The device struck a barrier and extinguished itself "a few feet from police officers," Tisch said. The same person then ran, and a second individual handed him another device, which he dropped. Both devices were wrapped in black tape and contained nuts, bolts, screws and a hobby fuse that could be lit, Tisch said. She said it was unclear whether they were functioning devices or a hoax.

All three individuals were arrested and an investigation is underway, Tisch said.

The anti-Islam event, billed as "Stop the Islamic Takeover of New York City," was tied to Jake Lang, a conservative influencer. About 20 people attended the anti-Islam protest while the counterprotest reached roughly 125 people at its peak, Tisch said. Mayor Zohran Mamdani was believed not to be in Gracie Mansion, the official mayoral residence on Manhattan's Upper East Side, at the time, she said.

Lang was previously charged with assaulting an officer with a baseball bat, civil disorder and other crimes in connection with the January 6, 2021, Capitol riot. He later received clemency as part of President Donald Trump's sweeping pardons for January 6 defendants. Lang recently announced he is running for U.S. Senate in Florida and has also protested in Minneapolis during the Trump administration's immigration enforcement operations, according to the Associated Press.
