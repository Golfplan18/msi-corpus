A bereaved father knelt over his 15-year-old son's body in a Gazan hospital in January, unable to accept the boy was dead. "He's sleeping. He'll wake up now," said Yusuf Zawara at Shifa Hospital in Deiр al-Balah. "There's nothing wrong with him. He's fine."

Mohammad Zawara and his 13-year-old cousin were killed in an Israeli strike as they searched for firewood in Gaza, according to hospital officials and family members.

The deaths are part of a broader toll on Gaza's civilian population since a ceasefire began in October. Gaza's Health Ministry reports more than 480 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli fire in the months since, including at least nine children who died of severe cold as winter temperatures drop below freezing.

## Hospital vigil: Father confronts son's death

In the morgue of Shifa Hospital in Deiр al-Balah, dust clung to Yusuf Zawara's hair and blood stained his clothes. He knelt over the body of his 15-year-old son, Mohammad, refusing what lay before him.

"He's sleeping. He'll wake up now," Zawara said. "There's nothing wrong with him. He's fine. I'm just cleaning his face. He's sleepy. He didn't sleep all night because of the bombing."

Mohammad and his 13-year-old cousin, Sulaiman, had been killed in an Israeli airstrike as they searched for firewood, hospital officials said. In Gaza in winter, with no central electricity since the war began, firewood is essential for warmth.

Zawara moved between denial and raw anguish. He addressed his dead son with commands and questions, patting the boy's face and wiping bloody traces with his fingertip.

"Get up!" he said. "They hit you with a missile. You couldn't escape? Run. People, run! Why didn't you run away?"

Then he turned to his cousin's body and shook it. "Sulaiman, get up so we can get some wings and grill them! Get up, get up, get up, my nephew! Come on, get up, why are you dying?!"

## Dueling accounts of the strike

A relative, Arafat al-Zawara, told the Associated Press that the boys "were targeted directly, not through any fault of their own." He said they were in an area Israel's military has designated as safe for Palestinians, approximately 500 meters from the boundary Israel calls the Yellow Line.

Israel's military offered a different account. The military denied that the killed were children and said it had targeted militants who it claimed crossed into the territory and planted explosives, threatening troops. The military did not provide evidence to support the claim.

## Ceasefire's toll on civilians

The deaths are part of a larger toll. Gaza's Health Ministry reports that more than 480 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli fire since a ceasefire began on October 10. The ministry, part of the Hamas-led government, maintains casualty records that U.N. agencies and independent experts consider generally reliable.

Israel disputes the Health Ministry's figures but has not provided its own casualty count.

## Winter's deadly impact

Winter has brought additional casualties. The Health Ministry documented at least nine children who died of severe cold in recent weeks as temperatures dropped below 10 degrees Celsius (50 degrees Fahrenheit) at night and storms moved in from the Mediterranean.
