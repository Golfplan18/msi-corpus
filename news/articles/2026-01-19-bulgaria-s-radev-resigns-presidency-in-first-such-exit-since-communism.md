SOFIA, Bulgaria — Bulgaria's President Rumen Radev announced Monday he is stepping down from the mostly ceremonial post, becoming the first head of state to resign in the country's post-communist history. In a televised address, Radev said he will formally submit his resignation to the Constitutional Court on Tuesday.

Under the Bulgarian constitution, Vice President Iliana Yotova must be sworn in by parliament to hold the post until the end of the presidential mandate.

The resignation deepens a prolonged political crisis in the European Union and NATO member state. Bulgaria is headed toward its eighth parliamentary election since 2021 after repeated failures to form a stable government, and Radev has indicated he may enter electoral politics himself.

"The battle for the future of our homeland lies ahead, and I believe we will face it together with all of you — the worthy, the inspired, and the unyielding! We are ready. We can, and we will succeed!" Radev said in his address.

## Protests and the fall of the governing coalition

Large anti-corruption protests last month forced the resignation of the center-right governing coalition led by the GERB party. Subsequent attempts to form a new government within the current parliament failed.

Radev, a 62-year-old former Air Force general, had thrown his support to the protests in early December. He has been a vocal opponent of GERB leader Boyko Borissov and of Delyan Peevski, a politician and oligarch under U.S. and U.K. sanctions whose MRF New Beginning party has repeatedly backed the GERB-led coalition.

## Possible new political party

Public expectation has mounted that Radev intends to form a new political party. Asked about that prospect, he said there was a need for a party that "unites all democrats — left and right — regardless of where they belong or whether they are politically active at all, because we all need fair elections and democratic, free development."

Radev did not address his plans directly in Monday's address. His second presidential mandate was set to expire in 2026.
