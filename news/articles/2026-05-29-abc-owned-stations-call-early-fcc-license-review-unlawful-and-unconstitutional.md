ABC-owned television stations in eight markets coordinated legal objections on Thursday with the Federal Communications Commission, characterizing the agency's demand for early license renewal applications as unlawful and unconstitutional. The filings represent a collective pushback from Disney-owned network affiliates against a regulatory timeline the stations argue exceeds the commission's statutory authority, forcing broadcasters to navigate a compliance dilemma under threat of regulatory sanction.

The objections were submitted alongside the required renewal paperwork, a procedural step the stations took to avoid violating FCC compliance orders while formally contesting the agency's accelerated review schedule. WABC, the network's flagship station in New York, filed a detailed legal objection stating that the early review sends "a clear warning to every broadcaster in America" and constitutes "an extraordinary demonstration of power and coercion directed at disfavored editorial voices." Stations in seven other major markets echoed the constitutional and arbitrary-agency-action arguments in their own filings.

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr defended the commission's actions in a statement following the coordinated filings. "Broadcast licensees have a unique obligation to operate in the public interest," Carr said, framing the early review as a routine exercise of the agency's statutory oversight mandate rather than a targeted penalty.

The license renewal dispute sits within a broader, increasingly public confrontation between the FCC and ABC's parent company. Under Carr, an ally of President Donald Trump, the commission has opened multiple investigations into the network. The agency has examined ABC's corporate diversity and hiring practices, its editorial decisions surrounding the moderation of a 2024 presidential debate, and the on-air content and guest bookings on its daytime talk program, "The View."

The regulatory scrutiny coincides with repeated public calls from President Trump to remove late-night host Jimmy Kimmel from broadcast television. The administration has targeted Kimmel's monologues and network interviews on multiple occasions, framing the host's political commentary as evidence of broader broadcast industry bias.

Federal broadcast licenses typically run on a multi-year cycle, but the commission maintains the legal authority to initiate early reviews under specific statutory conditions. The stations' joint legal filings argue that no such triggering conditions have been met and that the accelerated timeline functions as a punitive measure intended to chill editorial independence across the Disney-owned affiliates. The FCC has not yet issued a formal administrative response to the constitutional challenges raised by the ABC stations.
