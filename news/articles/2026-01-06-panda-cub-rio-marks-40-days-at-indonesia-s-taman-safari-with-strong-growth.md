CISARUA, Indonesia — Indonesia's Taman Safari conservation park released a video Tuesday documenting the healthy development of giant panda cub Satrio Wiratama — nicknamed Rio — 40 days after his birth at the park in Cisarua, West Java. Veterinarians said the cub's body weight had increased 46% and his body length had grown 95% over the preceding 30 days.

Rio's birth is a milestone for the decade-long conservation partnership between Indonesia and China and advances international efforts to sustain a species with fewer than 1,900 individuals remaining in the wild.

"The panda cub is developing healthily and growing very well. Its body weight has increased by 46% over the past 30 days, while its body length has increased by 95%," said Bongot Huaso Mulia, a veterinarian at Taman Safari who monitors Rio's progress.

The video documents Rio's growth from a tiny pink newborn to a panda with black-and-white fur.

## A long road to birth

Rio was born Nov. 27 to Hu Chun, a 15-year-old female, and Cai Tao. The pair arrived at Taman Safari in 2017 as part of a 10-year conservation partnership between Indonesia and China. The park sits about 70 kilometers (43 miles) from Jakarta.

His conception required years of sustained effort. "So we tried natural mating four times first, followed by four rounds of artificial insemination. It really was not easy," Mulia said. Rio was conceived through the fourth round of artificial insemination.

## Panda diplomacy

China's Ambassador to Indonesia, Wang Lutong, said at the examination: "This is the moment we've been waiting for. After years of hard work, finally we've got real. The baby panda, joining the global panda family."

China's practice of loaning giant pandas to overseas zoos has long been described as "panda diplomacy" — a form of soft power. Giant pandas have significant difficulty reproducing, making each birth notable for conservation efforts. Fewer than 1,900 remain in their only wild habitats, in China's Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu provinces.
