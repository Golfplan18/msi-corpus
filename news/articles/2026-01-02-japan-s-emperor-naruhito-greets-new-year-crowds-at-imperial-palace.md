TOKYO — Emperor Naruhito and Empress Masako waved to thousands of flag-waving well-wishers from the Imperial Palace balcony in Tokyo on Friday, resuming an annual New Year's greeting tradition that had been suspended in 2024 after a deadly earthquake struck Japan's Noto Peninsula.

The emperor used his New Year's statement to call for sustained peace efforts, noting that 2025 had marked 80 years since the end of World War II — a conflict fought in the name of his grandfather, Emperor Hirohito.

"I deeply feel it's important to continue with efforts among people for dialogue, trying to deepen mutual understanding, so we can build a world of peace," Naruhito said in the statement.

The annual appearance is one of the few occasions when the Imperial Palace's usually cloistered grounds in central Tokyo open to the public. Crowds stood in long lines in the cold for each of the five appearances scheduled throughout the day, with some shouting "banzai" — the Japanese expression for "long live." The imperial family remains relatively popular, according to the Associated Press.

Japan's emperors hold no political power under the country's postwar constitution but carry symbolic significance. Naruhito and his family have long been advocates for peace.

In both his statement and remarks on Friday, Naruhito also spoke about recent natural disasters — earthquakes, heavy rainfall, snow, and forest fires.

The 2024 New Year's appearance was canceled after a January 1 earthquake struck the Noto Peninsula, a coastal area in central Japan, killing hundreds of people. The tradition had also been suspended in 2021 and 2022 due to the coronavirus pandemic.

Princess Aiko, the imperial couple's only child, appeared alongside her parents. She is at the center of a national debate over succession rules that currently bar women from inheriting the Chrysanthemum Throne.

Prince Hisahito, Naruhito's nephew, also drew attention. Now taking part in adult imperial events, he is second in line to the throne after his father, the emperor's younger brother. Former Emperor Akihito, who abdicated in 2019, and his wife Michiko were also present at the greeting.
