## Childcare fraud investigation contributed to Walz ending reelection bid

Minnesota voters head to the polls Tuesday in a state primary in which Democrats will decide a competitive U.S. Senate nomination and Republicans will choose nominees they hope will end their 20-year drought in statewide elections, according to an Associated Press preview by Robert Yoon.

The AP described the Senate race as "another face-off between moderate and progressive candidates that could define the party's identity heading into the midterm elections."

The state has been at the center of a political maelstrom in President Donald Trump's second term, according to the AP, as the Republican president has focused much of his ire on Minnesota and its Democratic leaders.

An ongoing fraud investigation into state childcare programs contributed to Democratic Gov. Tim Walz ending his reelection bid, the AP reported. The investigation has been a frequent topic among Republican candidates on the campaign trail.

The Trump administration's immigration crackdown, which resulted in the deaths of two U.S. citizens, has been a defining issue for Democratic candidates, according to the AP.

Tuesday's choices set the field for the general election, and the Senate contest will offer an early measure of the Democratic Party's direction heading into the midterm elections.
