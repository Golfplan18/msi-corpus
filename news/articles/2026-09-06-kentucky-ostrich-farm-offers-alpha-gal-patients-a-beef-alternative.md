## Kentucky ostrich farm offers alpha-gal patients a beef alternative

- Alpha Roost Farms in Danville, Kentucky, raises ostriches whose meat — red in color, high in iron, and similar to beef in look and taste — offers people with alpha-gal syndrome a beef-like meal.
- Williams, a nutritionist and partner at the farm, developed a life-threatening red meat allergy from tick bites.
- A friend approached Williams four years ago with the proposition of starting the farm.
- Alpha-gal syndrome was first linked to a particular species of ticks about 15 years ago, and cases are on the rise.
- Williams said most alpha-gal patients who have tried an ostrich burger are "almost in tears," telling him, "You just don't know how bad I missed this."

"The people that have alpha-gal that I talked to that have tried an ostrich burger, most of them are almost in tears," Williams said. They tell him, "You just don't know how bad I missed this."

The reaction captures what the allergy takes off the table. Even a mouthful of red meat can bring on hives, diarrhea or itchiness. Chicken, turkey, eggs and seafood remain safe to eat, but for people who miss a beef-like meal, those substitutes do not fill the gap.

Ostrich offers something those substitutes lack — meat that is red in color, high in iron, and similar to beef in look and taste.
