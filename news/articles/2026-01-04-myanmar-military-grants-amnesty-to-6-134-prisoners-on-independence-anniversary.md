The release coincided with a disputed monthlong election process that critics said was designed to lend a facade of legitimacy to military rule, while former civilian leader Aung San Suu Kyi — serving a 27-year sentence since the military's 2021 takeover — showed no sign of being among those freed.

Myanmar's military government pardoned 6,134 prisoners Sunday to mark the country's 78th independence anniversary, state television reported, though it remained unclear whether the releases included any of the more than 22,000 political detainees held by the junta.

Senior Gen. Min Aung Hlaing, the head of the military government, issued the pardon, according to state broadcaster MRTV. A separate government statement said 52 foreigners would also be released and deported from Myanmar.

## Political detainees' status uncertain

The Assistance Association for Political Prisoners, an independent organization that tracks arrests and casualties linked to Myanmar's political conflicts, reported that more than 22,000 political detainees were in custody as of last week, among them former civilian leader Aung San Suu Kyi.

There was no indication Sunday that Suu Kyi would be among those freed. She is 80 and is serving a 27-year sentence after being convicted in prosecutions that her supporters have called politically motivated. She was ousted in the military's February 2021 takeover and has been held virtually incommunicado since then. The takeover was met with massive nonviolent resistance, which has since become a widespread armed struggle.

Many political detainees have been held on incitement charges, a catch-all offense widely used to arrest critics of the government and punishable by up to three years in prison.

## Releases under way at Insein Prison

Buses transported prisoners out of Yangon's Insein Prison after 11 a.m. Sunday, where relatives and friends had gathered since morning. No comprehensive list of those freed was available.

Among the first released from Insein Prison, according to the pro-army news outlet Popular News Journal, was Ye Htut, a former army officer who had served as information minister and presidential spokesperson in a previous military-backed government. He was arrested in October 2023 and sentenced the following month to 10 years in prison after being convicted of sedition and incitement for Facebook posts that authorities said spread false or inflammatory news.

Prisoners whose sentences were reduced did not include those convicted of serious offenses such as murder or rape, or those jailed under various security statutes. Released prisoners were warned that any future violation of the law would result in serving the remainder of their original sentence on top of any new punishment.

Prisoner releases on significant dates are common practice in Myanmar. The releases begun Sunday were expected to take several days to complete.

## Election backdrop; U.S. calls for dialogue

The amnesty came as the military government proceeded with a monthlong, three-stage election process that critics said was designed to add a facade of legitimacy to military rule.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in a statement Sunday that the military should cease violence, allow unhindered humanitarian access, release those unjustly detained, and engage in dialogue to pursue a peaceful and long-term resolution of the crisis.

Myanmar marked its 78th anniversary of independence from Britain with a flag-raising ceremony in the capital, Naypyitaw. The country regained independence on Jan. 4, 1948.
