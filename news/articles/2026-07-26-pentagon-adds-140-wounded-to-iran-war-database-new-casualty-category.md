## Bipartisan lawmakers challenge separate accounting for renewed fighting

The Pentagon updated its official war casualty database over the weekend, adding more than 140 wounded service members and introducing a new casualty category labeled "Overseas Operations" for personnel killed and wounded "starting July 7," according to the Defense Casualty Analysis System (DCAS).

The update, first reported by The Guardian, comes days after the Pentagon reduced the recorded death toll from 18 to 14 by removing four soldiers who died after President Donald Trump declared a ceasefire in April. Those four soldiers' names were included in a Pentagon press release announcing the formal transfer of their remains on Wednesday, but they were not on the official online list of service members killed in the Iran war.

According to the updated database, the official toll for the earlier phase of the U.S.-Iran war — known as Operation Epic Fury — now stands at 14 service members killed and more than 400 wounded in action. Combined with the new "Overseas Operations" figures, the Pentagon data show that 18 U.S. troops have been killed and 624 wounded since the U.S. launched its war against Iran on Feb. 28.

The dispute centers on the Trump administration's effort to classify the renewed military campaign, which began on July 6 after the ceasefire collapsed, as separate from Operation Epic Fury. The change is tied to the War Powers Act of 1973, which requires the president to end military operations within 60 days unless Congress authorizes an extension. The Trump administration has argued that the April ceasefire with Iran paused the clock on May 1 for the conflict's initial period.

Analysts and officials have questioned whether casualties from the renewed fighting should be counted separately from Operation Epic Fury, a move that would be unprecedented, according to The Guardian.

Lawmakers from both parties have challenged the administration's interpretation of the War Powers Act timeline. Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) responded to the updated numbers in a post on X, writing: "Let me explain this absurd ruse: The Pentagon is pretending there have been two Iran wars separated by a brief cease-fire."

Massie argued that by going more than 90 days without congressional authorization, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth "IS BREAKING THE LAW and must be held accountable."

The Pentagon's shift became apparent earlier this week when the official online list of service members killed in the ongoing Iran war did not include the four soldiers who died during renewed fighting over the weekend. Their names, however, appeared in a separate Pentagon press release.

The database update adds the latest chapter in a pattern of accounting changes that have drawn scrutiny from lawmakers and transparency advocates. The Pentagon held far fewer press briefings during the Iran war than during previous conflicts, as MSI has reported.
