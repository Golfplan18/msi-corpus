The Department of Homeland Security deployed approximately 2,000 Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents and officers to the Minneapolis–St. Paul area on Tuesday, announcing what it called the largest immigration enforcement operation in the agency's history. Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem accompanied officers during at least one arrest in St. Paul, and federal authorities said 150 people were arrested in Minneapolis the day before.

The operation—focused in part on fraud allegations tied to federal nutrition and pandemic aid programs—drew immediate condemnation from Minnesota Gov. Tim Walz, who called the surge "a war that's being waged against Minnesota" and said the Trump administration had not coordinated with state officials.

"The largest DHS operation ever is happening right now in Minnesota," the department said in a post on X.

## Noem present at arrest

A video posted on X showed Noem wearing a tactical vest and knit cap as agents arrested a man in St. Paul. In the video, she told the handcuffed man: "You will be held accountable for your crimes."

DHS said in a news release that the man was from Ecuador and was wanted in his homeland and in Connecticut on charges including murder and sexual assault.

## State and local officials condemn the surge

Walz, a Democrat who announced Monday that he was ending his campaign for a third term as governor, criticized the deployment at a press conference in Minneapolis on Tuesday.

"You're seeing that we have a ridiculous surge of apparently 2,000 people not coordinating with us, that are for a show of cameras," Walz said.

St. Paul City Council member Molly Coleman, whose district includes a manufacturing plant where agents arrested more than a dozen people in November, said the day stood apart from any prior experience.

"It's incredibly distressing," Coleman said. "What we know happens when ICE comes into a city, it's an enforcement in which every single person is on guard and afraid."

## Operation's composition and scope

Roughly three-quarters of the enforcement personnel were from ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations, which carries out immigration arrests and deportations, according to a person briefed on the operation who was not authorized to speak publicly. The force also included agents from Homeland Security Investigations, ICE's investigative arm, which typically concentrates on fraud and cross-border criminal networks.

HSI agents went door-to-door in the Twin Cities area investigating allegations of fraud, human smuggling, and unlawful employment practices, according to DHS. The operation also included personnel from U.S. Customs and Border Protection, among them Cmdr. Gregory Bovino, whose tactics during prior federal operations in other cities have drawn scrutiny from local officials and civil rights advocates, the person said. Specialized tactical units were also expected to be involved.

The person cautioned that the operation's scope and duration could shift in coming days as it develops.

Immigrant rights groups and elected officials in the Twin Cities reported a sharp increase on Tuesday in sightings of federal agents and enforcement vehicles, notably around St. Paul. Agents' vehicles were reported making traffic stops and appearing outside businesses and apartment buildings.

"We can definitely feel a heavier presence," said Dieu Do, an organizer with the Minnesota Immigrant Rights Action Committee, which dispatches response teams to reports of agents.

Julia Decker, policy director at the Immigrant Law Center of Minnesota, said there had been an increase in sightings of agents in locations such as parking lots.

## Fraud allegations and Somali community

President Donald Trump has repeatedly linked his administration's immigration enforcement in Minnesota to fraud cases involving federal nutrition and pandemic aid programs, many of which have involved defendants with roots in Somalia. The Minneapolis area is home to the largest Somali community in the United States.

Last month, Minneapolis Police Chief Brian O'Hara criticized federal agents for using "questionable methods" following a confrontation between agents and protesters.

Federal authorities began increasing immigration arrests in the Minneapolis area late last year. Noem and FBI Director Kash Patel announced last week that federal agencies were intensifying operations in Minnesota, with an emphasis on fraud investigations.

## Hotel dispute

Hilton said Tuesday it was removing a Minnesota hotel from its systems for "not meeting our standards and values" after the hotel denied service to federal agents. The Hampton Inn Lakeville, located about 20 miles outside Minneapolis, apologized Monday for canceling the reservations of federal agents and said it would work to accommodate them. The hotel did not respond to requests for comment.
