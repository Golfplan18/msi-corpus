Russia launched three Iranian communications satellites into orbit Sunday from the Vostochny launchpad in eastern Russia, Iranian state television reported, marking the second such launch since July and the latest demonstration of deepening ties between Moscow and Tehran.

The three satellites — Paya, Kowsar, and Zafar-2 — were placed into a 500-kilometer (310-mile) orbit, the report said.

The launch comes months after Russia and Iran signed a "strategic partnership" treaty and amid continued U.S. assertions that Iran's satellite program violates U.N. Security Council resolutions.

## Satellite specifications

Paya, at 150 kilograms (330 pounds), is the heaviest satellite Iran has ever deployed into orbit, the Iranian state television report said. Kowsar weighs 35 kilograms (77 pounds); the report did not specify Zafar-2's weight.

The satellites are equipped with cameras capable of up to 3-meter resolution and are intended for applications including water resource management, agriculture, and environmental monitoring, the report said. Each has a projected lifespan of up to five years.

## Second launch this year

Russia launched Iranian satellite Nahid-2 into orbit in July, the first such mission in the current period of close bilateral cooperation.

Russia and Iran formalized their partnership in a "strategic partnership" treaty signed in January. Russia strongly condemned the Israeli and U.S. strikes on Iran during a 12-day air war in June, which killed nearly 1,100 Iranians — including military commanders and nuclear scientists — according to the Associated Press report. Iran launched retaliatory missile barrages that killed 28 people in Israel, the report said.

## U.S. and U.N. positions

The United States has said Iran's satellite launches defy a U.N. Security Council resolution and has called on Tehran to cease activities involving ballistic missiles capable of delivering nuclear weapons. U.N. sanctions related to Iran's ballistic missile program expired in 2023.

Iran has characterized its satellite program as civilian and launched satellite carriers periodically as part of a long-standing effort to place its own satellites in space.
