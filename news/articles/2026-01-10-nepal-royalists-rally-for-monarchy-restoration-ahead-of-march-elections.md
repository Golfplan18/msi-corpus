Supporters of Nepal’s deposed royal family rallied in Kathmandu on Sunday, demanding the restoration of the monarchy ahead of March elections, the Associated Press reported.

The AP said the gathering was the first rally by supporters of ousted King Gyanendra since a wave of violent demonstrations by disgruntled youth in September installed an interim government that set fresh parliamentary elections for March.

Rally participants chanted “We love our king. Bring back the king” around a statue of Prithvi Narayan Shah, who started the Shah dynasty in the 18th century, according to the AP. The rally also called for bringing back King Gyanendra, the last Shah king, who was forced to step down and whose monarchy was abolished in 2008, when Nepal became a republic.

Protester Samrat Thapa told the crowd that “The last and only alternative for this country is king and monarchy only.” Thapa also said that “In the present context and the path country has taken after the Gen Z movement, there needs to be monarchy restored to manage the situation,” the AP reported.

Sunday marked the birth anniversary of Prithvi Narayan, and the AP said the annual rally had turned violent in past years, with clashes between demonstrators and police. The AP reported that two people were killed during a pro-king rally last March.

It said Sunday’s gathering was peaceful, with riot police keeping a close watch on the event. The AP also said Nepal’s royal family still enjoys significant support.

The interim government is headed by Sushila Karki, Nepal’s first female prime minister and a retired Supreme Court judge, the AP reported. The AP said the interim government took over following protests by Gen Z activists complaining of corruption, lack of opportunities, employment and poor governance, which were triggered by the previous government’s short-lived ban on social media.

The AP said Karki has been criticized for dragging her feet in filing corruption cases.
