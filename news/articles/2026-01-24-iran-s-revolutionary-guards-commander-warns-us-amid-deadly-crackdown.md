## Iran's Guards Commander Warns U.S. as Protest Crackdown Death Toll Soars

Mohammad Pakpour, head of Iran's paramilitary Revolutionary Guards, warned the United States and Israel on Jan. 24 against committing any "miscalculation" as military tensions escalated amid a nationwide crackdown on protests and the movement of U.S. warships to the region. The warning was issued through Nournews, a media outlet with ties to Iran's Supreme National Security Council.

Pakpour said the Revolutionary Guards were "more prepared than ever, with finger on the trigger" to execute orders. His statement came as the Trump administration positioned naval forces toward the Persian Gulf and issued repeated threats of military action.

The confrontation reflects the highest U.S.-Iran tensions in recent years, driven by the government's violent suppression of nationwide protests that erupted Dec. 28 over the collapse of the Iranian rial. According to activist reports, the death toll had reached 5,200 as of Jan. 24, with the number expected to continue rising due to internet blockages that have delayed casualty reports. The Iranian government reported an official death toll of 3,117 on Jan. 22, of which 2,427 were civilians and security force members.

More than 40,000 people have been arrested during the crackdowns. Many face threats of execution. Some detainees have been labeled "mohareb" — enemies of God — a capital charge that Iran used in mass executions in 1988 that killed at least 5,000 people. In recent years, Iran has become the world's leading executor: the United Nations reported that Iran executed at least 1,500 people in 2025, a 50 percent increase from 2024.

## Trump's Military Threat and Naval Deployment

U.S. President Donald Trump has repeatedly warned Iran against killing peaceful protesters or conducting mass executions. Trump claimed that Iran had halted executions of 800 detainees, though he did not disclose the source of the information. Iran's Attorney General Mohammad Movahedi flatly denied the claim on Jan. 21.

On Jan. 23, Trump told reporters aboard Air Force One that the United States had "an enormous fleet heading in that direction and maybe we won't have to use it." A U.S. Navy official, speaking on condition of anonymity to discuss military movements, said the USS Abraham Lincoln and other warships accompanying it were en route to the Indian Ocean.

Trump also referenced previous rounds of talks about Iran's nuclear program and threatened Teheran with military action that would make earlier White House offensives against uranium enrichment "seem insignificant." Israel conducted a 12-day campaign against Iran in June 2025, during which U.S. aircraft participated in bombing Iranian nuclear facilities.

## Airlines Suspend Regional Routes

The escalating tensions prompted European airlines to suspend flights to the region. Air France canceled two round-trip routes between Paris and Dubai over the weekend. Luxair, a Luxembourg-based carrier, delayed a Luxembourg-to-Dubai flight by 24 hours. Flight tracking data showed that KLM and Transavia canceled Saturday flights from Amsterdam to Dubai. Some KLM flights to Tel Aviv, Israel, were also canceled on Friday and Saturday.

Air France said it was "closely monitoring developments in the Middle East in real time" and continuing to evaluate the geopolitical situation in the territories it serves and overflies. Luxair said it was following developments and would make decisions about future flights based on "continuous evaluation."

## U.N. Concerns Over Execution Threats

The death toll from the crackdowns represents the highest casualty count from any round of protests or unrest in the Islamic Republic in decades, comparable to the chaos surrounding the 1979 Islamic Revolution.

Volker Türk, the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, expressed concern on Jan. 24 about what he described as "contradictory statements from Iranian authorities" on whether detainees arrested during the protests could face execution. He noted that Iran "remains one of the world's leading executors."

Human Rights News Agency, which tracks casualty reports through a network of on-the-ground activists, has been precise in its tallies during previous unrest. The agency's count has continued to rise even as Iran maintains what it says is the most comprehensive internet blockade in the country's history, now lasting more than two weeks.
