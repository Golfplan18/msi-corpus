A private helicopter crashed Friday morning in a remote mountainous area of Arizona, killing all four people aboard, the Pinal County Sheriff's Office said. The crash occurred around 11 a.m. near Telegraph Canyon, about 64 miles east of Phoenix, and may have been caused when the aircraft struck a recreational slackline strung across the mountains.

The sheriff's office said the slackline was more than half a mile long. "An eyewitness who called 911 reported seeing the helicopter strike a portion of the line before falling to the bottom of the canyon," the office said in a post on the social platform X.

Those killed were the 59-year-old pilot, two 21-year-old women and a 22-year-old woman, the sheriff's office said. Their names have not been released.

The helicopter had taken off from an airport in the town of Queen Creek, about 29 miles west of the canyon. The terrain was sufficiently remote that crews took several hours to travel by foot to reach the crash site. Flights were temporarily restricted over the area, the sheriff's office said.

The Federal Aviation Administration and the National Transportation Safety Board are investigating.

"Our prayers are with the victims and their families," the sheriff's office said.
