Afghanistan, already burdened by what the United Nations describes as one of the world's largest humanitarian crises, remains acutely vulnerable to flash floods because of decades of conflict, inadequate infrastructure, and widespread deforestation compounded by climate change. The UN and its partners launched a $1.7 billion appeal this week to assist nearly 18 million Afghans in urgent need.

Flash floods triggered by the season's first heavy rains and snowfall killed at least 17 people and injured 11 others across Afghanistan, Mohammad Yousaf Hammad, a spokesman for Afghanistan's National Disaster Management Authority, said Thursday.

The flooding, which began Monday, struck several areas of the country and disrupted daily life across central, northern, southern, and western regions, Hammad said. The floods also damaged infrastructure in affected districts, killed livestock, and affected 1,800 families, worsening conditions in already vulnerable urban and rural communities.

Among the dead were five members of a single family in Kabkan, a district in Herat province, where a roof collapsed Thursday, according to Mohammad Yousaf Saeedi, spokesman for the Herat governor. Two of the five victims were children.

Hammad said the authority has deployed assessment teams to the worst-affected areas, with surveys ongoing to determine further needs.

## Structural Vulnerability

Afghanistan, like neighboring Pakistan and India, is acutely vulnerable to extreme weather events, particularly flash floods following seasonal rains. Decades of conflict, poor infrastructure, deforestation, and the intensifying effects of climate change have amplified the impact of such disasters, especially in remote areas where many homes are constructed of mud and offer limited protection against sudden deluges.

## Humanitarian Context

The United Nations and other aid agencies warned this week that Afghanistan is expected to remain one of the world's largest humanitarian crises in 2026. The UN and its humanitarian partners launched a $1.7 billion appeal Tuesday to assist nearly 18 million people in urgent need in the country.
