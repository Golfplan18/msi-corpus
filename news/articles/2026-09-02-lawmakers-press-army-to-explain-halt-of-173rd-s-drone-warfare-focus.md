## Lawmakers press Army to explain halt of 173rd's drone warfare focus

**Subtype:** fact

- A bipartisan group of U.S. lawmakers is pressing the Army to explain why it told the 173rd Airborne Brigade to stop specializing in drone warfare.
- The 173rd, set up in November with about 600 soldiers to deploy wherever uncrewed systems were needed, was building its own drones and practicing tactics pioneered by Ukraine against Russia and employed by Iran against U.S. forces.
- "We have deep concerns that eliminating this specialized drone unit will limit our ability to learn from allies, particularly the Ukrainian Armed Forces, and hinder our efforts to modernize drone warfare at the speed necessary to compete on the modern battlefield," the lawmakers wrote to Army leadership.
- The order halting the unit's drone focus comes as modern battlefields increasingly rely on uncrewed systems and drone warfare has killed and wounded American troops.

## Brigade of about 600 soldiers was building its own drones

The 173rd Airborne Brigade, with about 600 soldiers, was set up in November to be deployed anywhere that drones were needed. The brigade was building its own uncrewed systems. A bipartisan group of lawmakers raised concerns about the order ending the brigade's drone specialization in a letter shared with The Associated Press.

"We have deep concerns that eliminating this specialized drone unit will limit our ability to learn from allies, particularly the Ukrainian Armed Forces, and hinder our efforts to modernize drone warfare at the speed necessary to compete on the modern battlefield," the lawmakers wrote. The letter pointed to the Ukrainian Armed Forces as a particular source of allied expertise in drone warfare.

The order halting the 173rd's drone focus comes as modern battlefields increasingly rely on uncrewed systems and as drone warfare has killed and wounded American troops.
