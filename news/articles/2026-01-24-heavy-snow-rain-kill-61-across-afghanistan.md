Heavy snow and rainfall killed at least 61 people across Afghanistan over the past three days, according to the country's National Disaster Management Authority. The storms injured more than 100 people and destroyed or partially destroyed 458 homes across 15 of Afghanistan's 34 provinces, the authority said Saturday. Officials cautioned that the death toll could rise as they gathered reports from remote areas.

The disaster underscores Afghanistan's deep vulnerability to extreme weather in a country already struggling with decades of conflict, poor infrastructure, economic collapse, and accelerating climate change. The United Nations has called Afghanistan one of the world's largest humanitarian crises heading into 2026.

According to the disaster management authority spokesman Yousaf Hammad, the storms also killed hundreds of animals across the affected provinces.

Afghanistan's vulnerability to extreme weather runs deep. In 2024, springtime flash floods killed more than 300 people across the country. The underlying causes are structural: decades of armed conflict have degraded infrastructure and left many communities unable to respond to emergencies. Widespread deforestation, a struggling economy, and intensifying climate change have amplified the impact, particularly in remote mountainous regions where homes are typically built of mud and offer limited protection against heavy snow or sudden flooding.

## A Chain of Recent Disasters

This vulnerability strikes hardest at a population already in crisis. Afghanistan's eastern provinces have been reeling since late August 2025, when an earthquake struck the region. Those quakes killed more than 2,200 people and destroyed villages. In November 2025, another earthquake struck the same region. The displaced from those quakes are especially at risk from extreme cold and severe weather.

In December, UNICEF warned that an estimated 270,000 children in areas affected by the earthquakes faced "severe risk of life-threatening diseases related to the cold." The winter storms that began three days ago have intensified that danger.

## Humanitarian Crisis at Historic Scale

The crisis spans far beyond any single disaster. In January, the United Nations said Afghanistan would "remain one of the world's largest humanitarian crises in 2026." The U.N. and humanitarian partners launched a $1.7 billion appeal to assist nearly 18 million people across the country in urgent need.
