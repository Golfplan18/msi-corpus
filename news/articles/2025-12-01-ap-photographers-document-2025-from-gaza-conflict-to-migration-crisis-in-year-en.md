The Associated Press released its 100 Photos of 2025 on Monday, a year-end gallery drawn from the work of photojournalists covering over 200 locations worldwide. The collection spans the Gaza war, the fall of Syria's Assad government, migration crises along Caribbean and Central American corridors, flooding that killed more than 130 people in Texas, and anti-corruption protests in Nepal. AP photo editors Benjamin Snyder, Enric Martí, and Jacqueline Larma curated the images; Ted Anthony wrote the accompanying text.

Among the 100 images is a photograph by Mariam Dagga showing Shaima Seiam, gravely injured, lying at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis beside the body of her infant daughter Ahlam, who was days from her first birthday, after their home was hit by an Israeli airstrike on April 16, 2025. According to the AP, Dagga was killed in another Israeli airstrike while covering the same hospital weeks after taking that image. ## Gaza

Other images from Gaza document the conflict's scope across the year. On Jan. 30, 2025, Israeli captive Arbel Yehoud — held hostage by Hamas since Oct. 7, 2023 — was photographed being escorted by fighters as she was handed over to the Red Cross in Khan Younis.

On March 1, 2025, the first day of Ramadan, Palestinians gathered at a table surrounded by rubble in Rafah, southern Gaza Strip, for iftar, the fast-breaking meal. A photograph from July 31, 2025 shows the bodies of Palestinians killed while attempting to reach aid trucks entering northern Gaza through the Zikim crossing, as they were prepared for burial at the morgue of Shifa Hospital in Gaza City.

## Migration and immigration

The gallery documents migration at multiple points along routes toward and away from the United States. On Feb. 23, 2025, Luis Sanchez and other Venezuelan migrants were photographed aboard a boat near Gardi Sugdub, along Panama's Caribbean coast, after abandoning hopes of reaching the United States amid U.S. immigration enforcement measures.

On July 22, 2025, Arturo Suárez — a Venezuelan migrant deported months earlier to El Salvador by the United States — was photographed being welcomed home by relatives in Caracas. A separate portrait dated Aug. 19, 2025 shows Luis, 30, a Venezuelan asylum seeker who fled after opposition political activity at university; working as an Amazon delivery driver in Orlando, Florida, while awaiting a decision on his asylum application, he said he received a work permit that allows him to support himself through the legal process.

The AP described some entries in the collection as contemplative. Petros Giannakouris photographed the Parthenon and the moon against a midnight-blue sky.
