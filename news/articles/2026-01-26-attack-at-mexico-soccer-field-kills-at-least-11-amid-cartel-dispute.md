Gunmen killed at least 11 people and injured 12 others during a gathering after an amateur soccer match in Salamanca, Guanajuato on January 25, 2026. The attack, marked by shell casings and blood-stained clothing left on the field, occurred in the Mexican state with the highest number of homicides in the country.

The violence underscores persistent cartel disputes in central Mexico as the country prepares to co-host the FIFA World Cup this summer, an event the government has promoted as a showcase for stability and security.

## The Attack

The massacre claimed the lives of at least 11 people gathered at a soccer field in Salamanca after an amateur match on January 25. At least 12 others were injured. Beer cans, candles, and blood-stained clothing covered the field the following day.

Guanajuato state Gov. Libia Dennise García said Monday that security forces had reinforced the region. "The state will act decisively to protect families, restore peace to the community and bring those responsible to justice," she said on social media.

## The Cartel Conflict

The violence reflects intensifying territorial conflict in Guanajuato, the Mexican state with the highest number of homicides in the country. The region has been the scene of fierce competition between the Santa Rosa de Lima cartel, which focuses primarily on fuel theft and trafficking, and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel, or CJNG. The Jalisco cartel is the fastest-growing criminal organization in Mexico, and both have been designated as terrorist organizations by the Trump administration.

According to a federal official familiar with the ongoing investigation who requested anonymity, initial evidence suggests that some of those killed were linked to a private security company associated with the CJNG. Messages attributed to the Santa Rosa de Lima cartel, found prior to the attack, referenced their dispute with the Jalisco organization.

## Attribution

Security analyst David Saucedo, who spent years based in Guanajuato, suggests the Santa Rosa de Lima Cartel was likely responsible for the attack. According to Saucedo, the group may have aimed to provoke a federal military surge into territory held by the Jalisco Cartel—a move that "undermines the image of security Mexico hopes to project on the eve of the World Cup."

## The Wider Crisis

The attack coincides with Mexico's preparations to co-host the FIFA World Cup this summer alongside Canada and the United States. The Mexican government has promoted the tournament as an opportunity to highlight progress in security and has positioned local soccer as a powerful tool for community development.

Nancy Angélica Canjura, a researcher for Causa Común, a nongovernmental organization that analyzes insecurity data, noted that attacks occurring in public spaces during social gatherings carry particular weight in the public consciousness. "You can't go out by choice," she said. "You shouldn't stay in public spaces."

Canjura pointed to patterns from 2025, when Guanajuato saw attacks at funerals, parties, and other public activities. "This dilutes the social fabric, limits life in the community and allows criminal groups to have more power," she said.
