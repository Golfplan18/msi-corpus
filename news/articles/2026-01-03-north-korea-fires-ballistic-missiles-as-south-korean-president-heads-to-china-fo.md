The launches are the latest in a series of weapons demonstrations that analysts say North Korea is staging ahead of its Workers' Party congress — the first in five years — expected in January or February, at which Kim Jong Un is likely to signal his posture toward renewed negotiations with the United States.

North Korea launched multiple ballistic missiles toward its eastern waters early Sunday, South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said, hours before South Korean President Lee Jae Myung departed for a summit in China expected to address Pyongyang's nuclear program.

South Korean military officials said they detected several launches from the Pyongyang region at approximately 7:50 a.m. and that the missiles traveled roughly 900 kilometers, or about 560 miles. South Korean and U.S. authorities were analyzing details of the launches, the Joint Chiefs said in a statement.

Japan's Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi said at least two launches had been confirmed. "They are a serious problem, threatening the peace and security of our nation, the region and the world," Koizumi told reporters.

South Korea's Defense Ministry said the launches violated United Nations Security Council resolutions banning North Korean ballistic activity and urged North Korea to "immediately cease provocative actions" and respond to South Korean efforts to restart talks and restore peace on the Korean Peninsula.

South Korea convened an emergency National Security Council meeting later Sunday. Officials briefed President Lee on details of the launches and on unspecified steps South Korea was taking, according to Lee's office.

## Launches coincide with Lee's departure for Beijing

The missiles were fired hours before Lee left Seoul to begin a four-day visit to China. Lee's office said he would ask Beijing — North Korea's principal ally and largest trading partner — to play "a constructive role" in promoting peace on the Korean Peninsula.

South Korea and the United States have long pressed China to use its influence to persuade North Korea to return to negotiations or abandon its nuclear program. Questions remain, however, over how much leverage Beijing holds over Pyongyang. China and Russia have repeatedly blocked U.S.-led efforts to tighten economic sanctions on North Korea in recent years.

## Weapons tests tied to party congress

The Sunday launches are the latest in a series of North Korean weapons demonstrations that analysts say are timed to an upcoming Workers' Party congress — the first in five years — expected in January or February. Analysts are watching the gathering to see whether North Korea will set a new policy toward the United States and respond to calls to resume negotiations.

North Korea has concentrated on expanding its nuclear arsenal since Kim Jong Un's 2019 summit with President Donald Trump collapsed without an agreement. Kim has also reinforced his diplomatic standing by aligning with Russia in its war in Ukraine and deepening ties with China.

Analysts say Kim may believe his leverage has grown considerably, positioning him to seek concessions from Trump if the two sides were to negotiate again.

North Korean state media reported Sunday that Kim visited a weapons factory the previous day to review multipurpose precision-guided weapons produced there and ordered officials to expand current production capacity by approximately 2.5 times.

## North Korea condemns U.S. operation in Venezuela

The launches followed a U.S. military operation Saturday that removed Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro from power and brought him to the United States to face narco-terrorism conspiracy charges — the most assertive U.S. effort to achieve regime change in a foreign country since the 2003 invasion of Iraq.

North Korea's Foreign Ministry condemned the U.S. action Sunday, saying it again demonstrated "the dishonest and brutal nature of the United States" and calling it "the most serious form of violation of sovereignty."

Leif-Eric Easley, a professor of international studies at Ewha Womans University in Seoul, said the operation sent a dual signal for Kim. "Kim Jong Un can feel vindicated in his efforts to build a nuclear deterrent, as he likely did after Trump's strikes on Iran," Easley said. "However, the leaders of hostile regimes probably live with increased paranoia after seeing how quickly Maduro was extracted from his country to face trial in the United States."

## Recent weapons activity

The Sunday launches follow intensified North Korean military activity in recent weeks. A few days before, North Korea announced it had fired long-range strategic cruise missiles into the sea. On December 25, the North Korean government published photographs showing what appeared to be progress in the construction of its first nuclear submarine.
