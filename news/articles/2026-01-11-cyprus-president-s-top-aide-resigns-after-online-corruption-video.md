Cyprus President Nikos Christodoulides accepted the resignation of his top aide, Charalambos Charalambous, after an online video posted recently alleged government corruption and portrayed close ties to the president as being used to secure benefits for prospective investors.

In a written statement on Monday, Christodoulides said he had accepted Charalambous’ resignation as an expression of “self-confidence and trust” that the allegations are untrue. He also said that Charalambous’ departure—he described him as “distinguished by his work ethic, character and integrity”—was “a loss of a valuable aide during our great effort to upgrade our country.”

Charalambous, the director of Christodoulides’ presidential office and the brother-in-law of the president, had earlier posted on Facebook that he would never allow his job in government to be “converted into a tool of manipulation to harm the Cyprus Republic and the President.” He said his appearance in the video was “deliberate distortions” and “selective editing,” aimed at manufacturing “false and misleading conclusions.”

The video, which was posted on social media last week, is described as an 8½-minute clip that features Charalambous boasting about his close relationship with the president and about securing foreign investments. The clip seeks to suggest influence peddling tied to those relationships, according to the Associated Press report.

The video also makes claims about Cyprus’ political and economic decisions, including an allegation that the Cypriot government would work to block EU sanctions against Russian oligarchs in exchange for corporate cash. It further claims that Christodoulides exceeded a 1 million-euro campaign funding cap by taking “off-the-books” cash donations for his 2023 presidential run.

Cypriot authorities said the video was a malicious disinformation attack bearing “all the hallmarks” of previous Russian campaigns against other countries. An initial analysis by the Cyprus Security Services said the video shows “the characteristics of organized Russian disinformation campaigns” known as Doppelganger, described as similar to a 2021 Russian online operation targeting other EU countries, the U.S. and Israel.

Christodoulides linked the situation to warnings his government received around Cyprus’ role in the European Union. He said the 27-nation bloc had warned Cyprus during its EU presidency that it could be the target of “hybrid” attacks, and he said EU members and other third countries are assisting Cyprus authorities in their investigation into the video.

The Associated Press report said the video surfaced a week after Cyprus assumed the EU’s rotating presidency and noted that Cyprus is described as a staunch supporter of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. The report said the timing raised suspicions among Cypriot officials about possible intentions tied to the presidency.

Despite the government’s disinformation characterization, the report said opposition parties criticized the president and his government, saying the video has “reenlivened” past allegations of widespread government corruption.
