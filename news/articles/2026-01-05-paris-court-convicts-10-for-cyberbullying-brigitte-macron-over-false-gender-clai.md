A Paris court on Monday found 10 people guilty of cyberbullying French first lady Brigitte Macron, convicting them for spreading false online allegations that she was born male, the Associated Press reported. Sentences ranged from suspended terms to eight months in prison, with all defendants ordered to attend cyberbullying awareness training and jointly pay 10,000 euros ($11,675) in compensation for moral damages.

The court described the posts as "particularly degrading, insulting, and malicious" and noted that "repeated publications have had cumulative harmful effects" on Macron, who said she launched legal proceedings to "set an example" in the fight against harassment.

## Convictions and sentences

The defendants — eight men and two women ranging in age from 41 to 65 — were accused of posting numerous comments falsely claiming that Brigitte Macron was born male and likening the 24-year age gap between her and President Emmanuel Macron to pedophilia, according to AP. Some posts were viewed tens of thousands of times.

Delphine Jegousse, 51, known online as Amandine Roy and described as a medium and author, received a six-month prison sentence. The court found she played a major role in spreading the false claims after she released a four-hour video on her YouTube channel in 2021, AP reported.

Aurélien Poirson-Atlan, 41, whose X account was suspended in 2024 after his name was cited in several judicial investigations, was sentenced to eight months in prison along with a gallery-owner defendant.

One defendant, a teacher who apologized during the trial, was not given a prison sentence but must complete the mandatory cyberbullying awareness training. Several defendants will have their social media access suspended for six months on the platforms where they made their posts. Under French law, prison sentences may be served at home, possibly while wearing an ankle monitor or under other conditions set by a judge.

The court said sentences were proportional to the seriousness of the comments. French judicial authorities did not publicly disclose the names of the defendants, though some identified themselves by speaking out.

## Macron and family testimony

Brigitte Macron, 72, did not attend the two-day trial held in October. Speaking on TF1 national television the day before the verdict, she said she launched legal proceedings to "set an example" in the fight against harassment.

Her lawyer, Jean Ennochi, said Monday that "what is important is that there are immediate cyberbullying awareness trainings, and for some of the defendants, a ban on using their social media accounts."

Her daughter, Tiphaine Auzière, testified during trial about what she described as the "deterioration" of her mother's life since the online harassment intensified. "She cannot ignore the horrible things said about her," Auzière told the court, adding that the impact had extended to the entire family, including Macron's grandchildren.

During the trial, several defendants told the court that their comments were intended as humor or satire and said they did not understand why they were being prosecuted.

## Background

The case stems from years of conspiracy theories falsely alleging that Brigitte Macron was born under the name Jean-Michel Trogneux — which is the name of her brother. The Macrons have also filed a defamation suit in the United States against conservative influencer Candace Owens over related false claims, AP reported.

Brigitte Macron, born Brigitte Auzière, first met Emmanuel Macron when he was a student and she was a teacher at his high school. The couple married in 2007. Emmanuel Macron, 48, has served as France's president since 2017.
