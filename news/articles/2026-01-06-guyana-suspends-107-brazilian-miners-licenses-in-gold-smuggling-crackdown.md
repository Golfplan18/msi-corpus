Guyana's Geology and Mines Commission suspended the gold-mining licenses of 107 Brazilian nationals on Monday and threatened them with deportation, accusing them of channeling their gold production out of the country rather than selling it to state-authorized buyers as required by Guyanese law, Commissioner Newell Dennison told the Associated Press on Tuesday.

The enforcement action, ordered by President Irfaan Ali, targets a cross-border trade that Guyana's own crime investigators have estimated drains as many as 15,000 troy ounces of gold from the country each week — a significant loss for a nation that counts gold as its second-largest foreign exchange earner after offshore oil.

## Summonses and suspended operations

Dennison said the commission issued summonses to all 107 affected permit holders, directing them to appear for records reviews. "Come in to us and let us examine their records. If they don't come, then consider operations suspended because when we try to reach some of them to discuss compliance, the information they have given us usually proves to be bogus," he said.

The commission acted on an edict that Ali issued late last week, giving miners 24 hours to comply with disclosure requirements or face license suspension and deportation.

## Scale of the problem

Guyanese authorities accuse the Brazilian miners — who operate powerful river and land dredges in the Amazon jungle of western and southwestern Guyana — of sending their production home rather than selling it through the state-run gold board and its licensed network of buyers.

Both the Geology and Mines Commission and a local miners association said they do not know how many thousands of Brazilians work in the sector. Most operate in remote areas near the Brazilian border, which they cross with little or no state oversight.

Previous studies by Guyana's special organized crime unit found that as many as 15,000 troy ounces of gold leave the country illegally each week, crossing the river border with Suriname before moving on to the United States, Europe, and the Middle East.

## High-profile smuggling case

The license suspensions follow a high-profile case that spans U.S. and Guyanese jurisdictions. In mid-2024, the U.S. Treasury Department sanctioned Guyanese businessmen Nazar Mohamed and his son Azruddin Mohamed, accusing them of smuggling more than 22,000 pounds (10,000 kilograms) of gold to the United States and evading more than $50 million in taxes. A South Florida grand jury later indicted the pair on charges linked to mail fraud and money laundering. Both are fighting extradition to the United States.

The case carries an unusual political dimension. Azruddin Mohamed, 38, founded a political party in May 2025 that won 16 of the 65 seats in Guyana's September general elections. He is awaiting a swearing-in as the country's opposition leader even as he contests extradition proceedings.

## Declining declarations, rising prices

Declared gold production in Guyana has fallen sharply over the period in which smuggling investigations have intensified. Gold declarations totaled 423,000 troy ounces in 2024, down from 641,828 ounces in 2019, according to the AP, even as global gold prices have climbed to around $4,500 per ounce.
