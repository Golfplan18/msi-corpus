Tuesday's disruption — caused by a power fault inside the 50-kilometer undersea link and a separate cable failure on the U.K. side — forced thousands of travelers to scramble for alternative flights and buses during the year-end holiday travel period.

PARIS — Trains resumed running through the Channel Tunnel in both directions Wednesday after an overnight repair resolved a power fault that left some passengers stranded for up to 11 hours, though Eurostar warned of continuing delays caused by the disruption.

Eurotunnel, the operator of the 50-kilometer (32-mile) undersea link between continental Europe and the United Kingdom, said the tunnel was back to "full capacity" after the fault was fixed overnight Tuesday. The company did not detail the cause of the failure.

Eurostar, which runs passenger trains through the tunnel, warned of continued possible delays and cancellations Wednesday because of "knock-on impacts" from the previous day's severe disruptions. Its website showed delays to London-Paris, London-Brussels, and London-Amsterdam trains in both directions, with early morning cancellations.

## A second failure on the U.K. side

A separate power malfunction on the U.K. side compounded Tuesday's chaos. An overhead power cable fell onto a Eurostar train from London to Paris near the tunnel entrance, Eurostar said, and an effort to move the train with its passengers inside proved "very complex." The UK-side failure also caused severe delays to two trains to Brussels, according to Eurostar.

Passenger Ghislain Planque described the experience to French broadcaster BFMTV. His Eurostar journey Tuesday evening from London was scheduled to take just under 90 minutes but instead took around 11 hours, with passengers stuck overnight on a train with only intermittent power.

"We were left without electricity, so with no heating, no air-conditioning, no possibility to charge phones," Planque said. "We were in total darkness for some of the time."

## Holiday travelers upended

Tuesday's hours-long interruption of cross-Channel train services triggered a cascade of cancellations that upended travelers' end-of-year getaway plans, prompting scrambles for alternative flights and buses.

The Channel Tunnel is a critical artery for passenger and freight traffic between Britain and the European continent, carrying Eurostar's high-speed rail service alongside vehicle-carrying shuttle trains operated by Eurotunnel.
