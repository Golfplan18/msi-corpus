A magnitude 5.8 earthquake struck northern Pakistan on Monday, killing at least one person and damaging dozens of mud-brick homes, according to authorities. The tremors caused significant damage across the Hunza Valley in the Gilgit-Baltistan region and triggered landslides that blocked several roads, the regional government said.

Pakistan lies along an active seismic zone and is frequently struck by earthquakes, with communities in the mountainous north particularly vulnerable to quake-triggered landslides and infrastructure damage.

The Gilgit-Baltistan government said in a statement that the tremors mainly caused damage in the Hunza Valley. The shaking was also felt in several districts in the northwest, according to the statement.

Landslides in Hunza and nearby towns blocked several roads, the government said. Rescue teams were still collecting information on casualties and damages at the time of the statement, it added.

The Pakistan Meteorological Department placed the quake's epicenter in Pakistan-administered Kashmir, a mountainous territory divided between Pakistan and India and claimed by both countries.

Pakistan's seismic risk is well documented. A magnitude 7.6 earthquake in 2005 killed thousands of people in Pakistan and Kashmir.
