A Gambian court on Thursday opened the murder trial of Sanna Manjang, one of the leaders of the "Junglers," a paramilitary death squad that operated under former dictator Yahya Jammeh. Manjang faces two counts of murder dating to 2006 and denies the charges. He was arrested in Casamance, southern Senegal, last November following a joint operation by the two countries.

The case represents one of the most direct accountability proceedings tied to Jammeh's 22-year rule, during which the Junglers were accused of arbitrary arrests, enforced disappearances, and summary executions.

## Charges

According to the bill of indictment, Manjang is accused of the murders of two men identified in the documents as Kajali Jammeh and Samba Wurry. State prosecutors said the alleged crimes were committed in 2006.

Manjang's counsel argued that the law under which he was charged has since been repealed. State prosecutors retorted that the crimes were committed in 2006, when the criminal code was still in force. The next hearing is set for Feb. 9.

## The Junglers

The Junglers paramilitary unit was accused during Jammeh's rule of arbitrary arrests, enforced disappearances, and summary executions. Some unit members who testified before the country's Truth, Reconciliation and Reparations Commission — created after Jammeh's 2017 ouster — said they carried out their actions at the direction of the former president.

## Jammeh's fall and Manjang's flight

Jammeh was forced to step down following an electoral defeat that ended his 22-year reign. He refused to acknowledge his opponent's victory, which prompted a threat of military action by the Economic Community of West African States. Manjang fled to Senegal in 2017. Gambian and Senegalese authorities conducted a joint operation that led to his arrest last November in Casamance, in southern Senegal.
