South Korean President Lee Jae Myung said Wednesday he asked Chinese President Xi Jinping to serve as a mediator to help resolve the North Korean nuclear crisis. Lee disclosed the request — made during their Monday summit in Beijing — to reporters traveling with him on the Shanghai leg of his China trip. Xi responded that patience was needed, Lee said.

North Korea has refused to engage in dialogue with Seoul and Washington since Kim Jong Un's nuclear diplomacy with President Donald Trump collapsed in 2019, leaving South Korea's channels to Pyongyang reportedly closed entirely. Lee's liberal government has been pushing to reopen talks since he took office in June 2025, but North Korea has largely ignored those overtures.

"We're making efforts but all our channels (with North Korea) are completely blocked so we can't communicate at all," Lee said in televised comments. "I told him it would be good for China to play the role of a mediator for peace. President Xi appraised our efforts and said we need to be patient."

China is North Korea's biggest trading partner and major diplomatic backer. South Korea and the United States have repeatedly asked Beijing to use that influence to persuade Pyongyang to resume long-dormant diplomacy or take steps toward denuclearization. China has historically urged restraint by all parties and in recent years blocked U.S.-led efforts to tighten sanctions on North Korea despite its weapons tests, which are banned under United Nations resolutions.

Lee also met separately with Chinese Premier Li Qiang on Tuesday, who likewise emphasized the need for patience. Lee said China committed to making efforts in a mediating role.

"Roles of neighbors are necessary. We requested China to play such a role, and China said it will anyway make such efforts," Lee said.

Lee told Chinese officials he was pursuing phased, gradual denuclearization steps by North Korea in exchange for corresponding benefits. He said a freeze on North Korea's nuclear and missile programs should come first, to halt further expansion of Pyongyang's arsenal and prevent potential proliferation. He said China shared his view on the approach.

"Leaving the current status would be a loss for Northeast Asia and the entire world, because (North Korea's) nuclear weapons are being produced continuously," Lee said.

North Korea has said it will not put denuclearization on the negotiating table again. In July 2025, Kim Yo Jong — Kim Jong Un's sister and a senior North Korean official — said Lee's government's "blind trust" in its alliance with the United States made it no different from his conservative predecessor. Many experts warn that rewarding North Korea for limited denuclearization steps could allow Pyongyang to exit diplomacy while retaining much of its nuclear program after winning some level of sanctions relief.
