The sinking — triggered by engine failure on the evening of Dec. 26 — has left a Spanish family's Christmas holiday in one of Indonesia's most celebrated national parks in tragedy, prompting Spain's ambassador to formally request continued search reinforcement and Indonesian authorities to extend operations beyond the standard seven-day limit.

Indonesian rescuers recovered a second unidentified body Sunday in the ongoing search for Fernando Martín, a Spanish soccer coach, and two of his children who have been missing since their tour boat sank in Komodo National Park waters on Dec. 26. The body was found floating near Padar island, about 2 kilometers (1.2 miles) from the site of the sinking, said Fathur Rahman, chief of the Maumere Search and Rescue Office. It was transported to a hospital in Labuan Bajo, the gateway town to the park in eastern Indonesia, for identification.

Authorities believe the body is that of a member of the family. It had not been formally identified as of Sunday.

The sinking — triggered by engine failure on the evening of Dec. 26 — turned a Christmas holiday in one of Indonesia's most celebrated natural parks into a tragedy for Martín, 44, coach of Valencia CF Women's B. The boat was carrying Martín, his wife, their four children, four crew members, and a local guide when it went down. Martín's wife and one child, along with the crew members and guide, were rescued in the hours following the incident. Martín, two sons, and another daughter, aged 9, 10, and 12, remained unaccounted for.

Three days after the sinking, rescuers found the first victim — a 12-year-old Spanish girl — floating near the northern waters of Serai island, about 1 kilometer (0.6 miles) from the sinking site. Indonesian authorities confirmed her identity through medical and forensic examination conducted in cooperation with Martín's wife.

The search operation, on its tenth day Sunday, continued across Komodo National Park waters. More than 160 personnel have been deployed, supported by police and the navy, combing four sectors in inflatable boats, navy ships, and rescue vessels equipped with sonar and underwater navigation gear. Divers were also deployed.

"We are determined to find all the victims," Rahman said, following a review by the joint search and rescue team that extended operations to Jan. 4. "We remain optimistic that the hard work of all SAR elements will yield results during this extended operation."

Under Indonesian law, search operations typically last seven days but can be extended when there are signs or chances of finding victims. Spain's ambassador formally requested continued reinforcement of the search in a Dec. 31 letter to Indonesian authorities.

Komodo National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage site in eastern Indonesia, is known for its rugged landscapes, pristine beaches, and the Komodo dragon, an endangered lizard found nowhere else on earth. The park draws thousands of international visitors annually for diving, trekking, and wildlife tours.

Indonesia is an archipelago of more than 17,000 islands where boats are a common form of transportation. According to the Associated Press, boat accidents occur frequently, attributed in part to lax safety standards and overcrowding.
