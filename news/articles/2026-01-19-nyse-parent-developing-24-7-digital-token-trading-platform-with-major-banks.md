Intercontinental Exchange, the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange, said Monday it is developing a new platform that would allow investors to trade digital tokens representing stocks, bonds and other assets around the clock — a significant structural departure from traditional exchange hours that remains contingent on regulatory approval.

The platform would operate separately from the NYSE itself, which runs only on weekdays, ICE said. It would support instant settlement of transactions, orders denominated in dollar amounts and stablecoin-based funding.

Assuming it clears regulatory scrutiny, the platform would power a new NYSE venue supporting the trading of tokenized versions of company shares, according to ICE.

## What tokenization involves

Tokenization applies the blockchain technology that powers cryptocurrencies to create digital tokens as stand-ins for conventional financial assets — stocks, bonds, real estate or even fractional ownership of a piece of art — that can be traded like crypto by virtually anyone, anywhere at any time, ICE said.

Stablecoins, a type of cryptocurrency typically bought and sold for $1, have helped fuel demand to tokenize other financial assets, according to the company.

## Bank partners and clearing ambitions

ICE said it is working with Citigroup, Bank of New York Mellon and other lenders to support tokenized deposits across its six clearinghouses around the globe.

The platform development is part of ICE's broader effort to extend its transaction clearing capabilities to handle 24/7 trading of tokenized securities and, potentially, the integration of tokenized collateral, the company said.
