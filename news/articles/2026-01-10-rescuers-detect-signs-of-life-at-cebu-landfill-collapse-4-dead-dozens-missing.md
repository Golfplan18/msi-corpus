CEBU CITY, Philippines — Rescuers in Cebu City, Philippines, detected signs of life Saturday in the rubble of a garbage avalanche that killed at least four workers and left more than 30 others missing at a waste management facility in the village of Binaliw. Twelve workers have been pulled from the collapsed mound with injuries since the disaster struck Thursday.

The collapse has renewed scrutiny of landfill safety across the Philippines, where open dumpsites and waste management facilities have long posed hazards to workers and nearby communities — a concern that drew national attention in 2000 when a garbage mound in suburban Manila collapsed and caught fire, killing more than 200 people.

Cebu City Mayor Nestor Archival said a 50-ton crane was traveling under police escort to the scene to assist excavation, describing conditions at the site as hazardous.

"Authorities confirmed the presence of detected signs of life in specific areas, requiring continued careful excavation and the deployment of a more advanced 50-ton crane, which is enroute with police escort," Archival said in a statement. "Safety of responders remains paramount due to hazards such as unstable debris and acetylene risks, prompting adjustments to the security perimeter and controlled access."

The four dead — including an engineer and a female office worker — were all employees of the facility, which has a staff of 110, according to Archival and police. The toll had stood at two dead as of Friday, when authorities also listed 36 workers as missing. Archival did not provide an updated count of the missing in his Saturday statement.

The cause of the collapse has not been determined. Jaylord Antigua, a 31-year-old office worker at the facility, told the Associated Press on Friday that the wall of garbage cascaded down and destroyed the administrative office he was in, striking without warning despite fairly good weather at the time. He said he extricated himself from the rubble — a tangle of twisted tin roofing, iron bars and heaps of debris — with bruises on his face and arms by crawling in darkness.

"I saw a light and crawled toward it in a hurry, because I feared there will be more landslides," Antigua said. "It was traumatic. I feared that it was my end, so this is my second life."

Cebu is a bustling port city of nearly a million people that serves as a regional hub for trade, commerce and tourism. Archival said preparations were underway to manage a "looming garbage collection issue" resulting from the collapse, without providing further details.

Landfills and open dumpsites have long raised safety and health concerns across the Philippines, particularly in areas near low-income communities where residents scavenge for reusable materials and food.

In July 2000, a garbage mound in a shantytown in suburban Quezon City, part of metropolitan Manila, collapsed and ignited a fire after days of stormy weather, killing more than 200 people. That disaster prompted a law requiring the closure of illegal dumpsites nationwide and improved waste management practices by authorities.
