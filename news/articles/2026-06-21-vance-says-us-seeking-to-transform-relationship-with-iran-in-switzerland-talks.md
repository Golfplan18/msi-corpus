U.S. Vice President JD Vance said Sunday the United States is looking "to transform our relationship" with Iran as renewed talks opened at the Bürgenstock lakeside resort in Switzerland, marking the latest round of negotiations aimed at ending a war that began in late February.

"Never before has the Iranian and American leadership met at such a high level," Vance told reporters ahead of the talks.

"What the president has asked us to do is turn over a new leaf to transform our relationship with the people of Iran, and to extend an outstretched hand that says to the people of Iran that if your leadership is willing to give up being a driver of regional instability, if they are willing to give up nuclear weapons ambitions for the long term, then the United States is willing to fundamentally transform our relationship with that country," he said.

Vance was joined by Trump's lead negotiators in the peace talks — Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, according to CNN — along with Pakistani and Qatari mediators, The New York Times reported.

"We've already made great progress over just the last few hours, and I expect that we will make additional progress in the hours to come," Vance told reporters.

Despite the Vice President's optimistic tone, he cautioned that Sunday's talks would not bring about an immediate resolution to the war.

The gathering is the latest in a series of negotiations between the two countries and mediators to attempt to end a conflict that began with U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran on Feb. 28. Trump signed a 14-point peace agreement with Iran on Wednesday while he was in France for the G7 Summit. The pact included the immediate cessation of fighting by all sides — including in Lebanon — plans to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and an easing of financial restrictions on Iran.

Iran, however, said it closed the strait again Saturday, accusing Israel of launching a fresh round of strikes on Hezbollah, the Iranian-backed militant group in Lebanon. The strikes killed at least 22 people.

Though fighting between Israel and Hezbollah has been central to the peace agreement between the United States and Iran, neither party was involved in Sunday's talks in Switzerland.
