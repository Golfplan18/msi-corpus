China sees Carney's visit as a moment to capitalize on fractures between Ottawa and Washington, though Chinese analysts cautioned that Canada's deep historical, economic, and security ties with the United States make a meaningful pivot toward Beijing unlikely in the near term.

Chinese state media pressed Canada on Wednesday to chart a foreign policy course independent of the United States as Prime Minister Mark Carney arrived in Beijing for a visit aimed at rebuilding a bilateral relationship left fractured for more than six years. Government-run publications framed Carney's arrival as an opportunity for Ottawa to assert what they called "strategic autonomy," citing President Donald Trump's tariffs and territorial rhetoric toward Canada as proof that alignment with Washington carries a mounting cost.

Carney, who took office last year, described the trip as part of a broader push to forge new trade partnerships and reduce Canada's dependence on the American market. Trump has imposed tariffs on Canadian exports and repeatedly suggested that Canada, a resource-rich country bordering the United States to the north, could become America's 51st state.

## Beijing's pitch

China's state-owned China Daily issued a pointed editorial this week making Beijing's case directly to Ottawa.

"If the Canadian side reflects on the root causes of the setbacks in bilateral relations over the past few years — the previous Justin Trudeau government's policies to contain China in lockstep with the United States — it will realize that it can avoid the same outcome by upholding its strategic autonomy in handling China-related issues," the paper wrote.

The editorial warned that continued deference to Washington would come at Canada's expense: "If Ottawa still chooses to subject its China policy to the will of Washington again in the future, it will only render its previous efforts to mend ties with Beijing in vain."

The state-run Global Times offered a parallel argument, saying: "Perhaps it was the heavy price paid for blindly following the U.S. in imposing high tariffs on China that awakened Ottawa's sense of strategic autonomy."

## Six years of acrimony

The relationship Carney is seeking to restore deteriorated beginning with the arrest of a Chinese tech executive in late 2018 at the United States' request. It worsened further in 2024, when the Trudeau government followed the Biden administration's lead and imposed a 100% tariff on Chinese-made electric vehicles, along with a 25% tariff on Chinese steel and aluminum. China retaliated with its own tariffs targeting Canadian canola, seafood, and pork.

Canadian officials said they expect Carney's trip to produce progress on trade but not a definitive elimination of any tariffs.

Beijing bristled under former President Joe Biden's efforts to deepen ties with European, Pacific, and North American allies as a counterweight to China. It now sees some prospect of loosening those bonds, though it remains cautious about how far any realignment would go, according to reporting by the Associated Press.

## Analysts urge caution

Chinese experts identified areas where the two countries might find common ground, pointing to shared unease over recent U.S. actions. Cui Shoujun, a foreign policy and Latin America expert at Renmin University of China, cited U.S. military intervention in Venezuela — which resulted in President Nicolás Maduro being brought to New York to face charges — and Trump's statements claiming Greenland, a Danish territory, should come under U.S. control.

"We can also see Canada's current state of considerable unease towards the U.S.," Cui said. "If the U.S. can claim Greenland, might it then lay claim to Canada?"

Other analysts were more measured. Zhu Feng, dean of the School of International Studies at Nanjing University, cautioned against overestimating the importance of Carney's visit, "because Canada is not only a neighbor of the United States but also an ally."

China acknowledges that countries like Canada face substantial constraints in pivoting toward Beijing, given their continuing reliance on U.S. economic and military strength, longstanding cultural ties with the United States, disputes with China over its export volumes and their effect on manufacturing employment, and disagreements over human rights and Taiwan, the AP reported.
