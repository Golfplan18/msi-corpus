The exchange of energy strikes unfolded as a Ukrainian delegation arrived in the United States for U.S.-led peace negotiations, with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warning that delays to diplomacy should prompt decisive international pressure on Moscow.

Ukrainian drone strikes damaged energy networks in Russia-occupied Zaporizhzhia on Sunday, leaving more than 200,000 households without power, according to the Kremlin-installed regional governor. Russia simultaneously carried out overnight attacks on Ukraine's own power grid, killing at least two people, Ukrainian officials said.

Yevgeny Balitsky, the Kremlin-appointed governor of the occupied portion of Zaporizhzhia, said in a Telegram post that nearly 400 settlements had their supply cut because of damage to power networks from Ukrainian drone strikes.

The dual-front energy attacks unfolded as a Ukrainian delegation arrived in the United States for U.S.-led peace negotiations, with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warning that delays to diplomacy should prompt decisive international pressure on Moscow.

## Russia's overnight strikes on Ukraine

Russia struck energy infrastructure in Odesa region overnight on Sunday, causing a fire that was promptly extinguished, according to Ukraine's Emergency Service. At least six people were wounded in Dnipropetrovsk region from Russian attacks, the emergency service said.

Zelenskyy said two people were killed in overnight Russian strikes that hit Sumy, Kharkiv, Dnipro, Zaporizhzhia, Khmelnytskyi and Odesa. During the week, Russia deployed more than 1,300 attack drones, 1,050 guided aerial bombs and 29 missiles of various types against Ukraine, Zelenskyy said.

Russia has targeted Ukraine's energy infrastructure throughout the nearly four-year war, concentrating strikes in winter months. Ukrainian officials describe the strategy as "weaponizing winter" — an effort to exhaust civilian will to resist.

"If Russia deliberately delays the diplomatic process, the world's response should be decisive: more help for Ukraine and more pressure on the aggressor," Zelenskyy said.

## US peace talks and Davos

A Ukrainian delegation arrived in the United States on Saturday for a U.S.-led diplomatic push to end the war. Zelenskyy said on Friday that the delegation would seek to finalize with U.S. officials documents for a proposed peace settlement covering postwar security guarantees and economic recovery.

If American officials approve the proposals, the United States and Ukraine could sign the documents at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, Zelenskyy said at a Kyiv news conference alongside Czech President Petr Pavel. Trump is expected to attend the Davos forum, according to organizers. Russia would still need to be consulted on any proposals.

## Nuclear plant repairs begin

Ukrainian crews began repair work Sunday on the backup power line connecting the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant to the electrical grid, under a ceasefire brokered by the International Atomic Energy Agency, the Vienna-based United Nations body said in an X post.

"Crucial repair works on the essential back up Ferosplavna-1 330 kV power line connecting Ukraine's ZNPP to the grid have begun under another IAEA-brokered ceasefire," the agency said.

The 330-kilovolt line was damaged and disconnected because of fighting. The plant — the largest in Europe, currently occupied by Russian forces — is a central issue in ongoing U.S.-brokered peace talks, according to the IAEA.

## Drone debris in Russia's North Ossetia

Two children and an adult were wounded when debris from a Ukrainian drone fell on a five-story residential building in the town of Beslan, in Russia's North Ossetia region, according to regional governor Sergei Menyaylo. Seventy people were evacuated from the building, which sustained damage to its roof and windows, Menyaylo said in a Telegram post.

Russia's Defense Ministry said its forces shot down or suppressed 63 Ukrainian drones overnight over Russia and the occupied Crimean Peninsula. One person was hospitalized in Russia's Krasnodar region east of Crimea following a separate drone strike, local authorities said.
