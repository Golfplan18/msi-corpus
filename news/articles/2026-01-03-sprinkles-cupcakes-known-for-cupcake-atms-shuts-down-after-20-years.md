Sprinkles Cupcakes, the bakery chain known for automated vending machines that dispensed fresh cupcakes in malls and airports across the United States, has shut down after 20 years of operation, according to its founder. The closure was announced Dec. 30.

The shutdown adds to a pattern of restaurant and retail brands acquired by private equity firms that have later closed or filed for bankruptcy, drawing comparisons on social media to Red Lobster and TGI Fridays.

Candace Nelson said she started the company in her own kitchen in 2005, after losing her job. The first location was a small Beverly Hills storefront that had previously been a sandwich shop. "Even though I sold the company over a decade ago, I still have such a personal connection to it, and this isn't how I thought the story would go," Nelson said.

Nelson sold the company to private equity firm KarpReilly LLC in 2012, when Sprinkles had expanded to 10 locations. KarpReilly owns dozens of other companies, including a health food home delivery service, a kombucha brand, and protein wellness shakes.

Neither KarpReilly nor Nelson provided a reason for the closure. KarpReilly did not respond to an emailed request for comment.

The company's website has removed all operational locations and lists no products for sale.

Sprinkles built a following in part through its cupcake-dispensing machines, which the Associated Press reported briefly went viral on TikTok for the "I love Sprinkles" jingle that played repeatedly while a mechanical arm delivered the dessert.

Private equity has dramatically expanded its influence in the restaurant industry over the past decade, investing $94.5 billion between 2014 and 2024, according to data from capital market company PitchBook. Some Sprinkles fans said on social media that the closure fit a larger trend in which private equity firms purchase restaurant and retail brands that later file for bankruptcy or shut down altogether.
