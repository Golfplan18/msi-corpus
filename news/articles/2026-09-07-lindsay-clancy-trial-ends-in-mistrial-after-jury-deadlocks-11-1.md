## Columnist Moira Donegan argues case reveals a 'warped understanding' of motherhood

The murder trial of Lindsay Clancy ended in a mistrial after a jury deadlocked 11-1, with the majority favoring a finding that Clancy could not be held legally responsible under a postpartum-psychosis defense, according to a Guardian opinion column by Moira Donegan published this week.

The six-week trial took place in Massachusetts, Donegan writes. According to the column, the jury forewoman informed the judge that the panel stood 11-1, with the majority favoring a finding that Clancy could not be held legally responsible for the killings.

The case stems from the January 2023 deaths of Clancy's three young children, who were strangled in the family's home in Massachusetts. After the killings, Clancy attempted to take her own life; she survived but was left paralyzed, according to the column.

Donegan writes that Clancy had been prescribed "a stunning array of psychiatric medications" and "increasingly powerful tranquilizers" by her doctors in the period before the killings. The defense argued that Clancy was suffering from postpartum psychosis, a severe mental health condition that can emerge in the weeks after childbirth.

Donegan argues that the case became a referendum on mothers, healthcare, and the status of women. She writes that this framing reflects a broader cultural failure to recognize and treat postpartum illness in new mothers.

The column frames the mistrial as a moment that should prompt reflection on how American society treats mothers, particularly those experiencing postpartum mental health crises. Donegan argues that Clancy was failed by a culture that does not adequately support mothers suffering from severe mental illness.

Donegan concludes that the case is less about Clancy's individual guilt or innocence than about what it reveals about American attitudes toward motherhood and mental illness. The mistrial, in her view, leaves unresolved not just the question of criminal responsibility but the broader question of how a society responds to mothers in crisis.
