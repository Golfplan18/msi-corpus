Michael Abatti, a 63-year-old farmer from California's Imperial Valley, pleaded not guilty Wednesday to a murder charge in the shooting death of his estranged wife at her home in an eastern Arizona mountain community.

Abatti made his first appearance in Navajo County Superior Court in Holbrook, Arizona, after being extradited from El Centro, California, where Arizona authorities arrested him on the murder charge. Kerri Ann Abatti was found dead at a home in Pinetop, Arizona, where she had moved after the couple separated.

The case involves two families with deep roots in their respective communities — one prominent in California agriculture for generations, the other among the founding settlers of the small Arizona town where the alleged killing occurred.

Investigators say Michael Abatti traveled from El Centro to Pinetop on Nov. 20, carried out the killing and returned to California early the next morning. Prosecutors said he is being held in the Navajo County jail on a $5.5 million bond. His attorney, Owen Roth, said his client is able to post bond.

Kerri Ann Abatti had filed for divorce, with proceedings pending in California at the time of her death. The couple, who married in 1992 and had three children, split in 2023 and were contesting financial matters in their divorce proceedings.

Kerri Ann Abatti was a descendant of early Latter-day Saints settlers who helped found Pinetop in the 1880s.

Michael Abatti comes from a long line of farmers in the Imperial Valley, the crop-rich region in southeastern California that is the largest user of Colorado River water and is known for growing leafy greens, melons and forage crops. His grandfather, an Italian immigrant, was among the region's early settlers, and his father helped start the Imperial Valley Vegetable Growers Association.
