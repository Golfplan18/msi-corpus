North Korea's military accused South Korea on Saturday of flying surveillance drones across their shared border, warning Seoul it would "be surely forced to pay a dear price" for what Pyongyang called "unpardonable hysteria." South Korea's Defense Ministry denied the accusation, saying it did not operate drones during the times North Korea specified and does not possess the types of aircraft Pyongyang described.

The standoff deals a further blow to South Korean President Lee Jae Myung's effort to restore ties with Pyongyang, which has rebuffed his overtures at every turn since he took office in June.

North Korea's military said Saturday that its forces used electronic warfare assets to bring down a South Korean surveillance drone flying over North Korean territory, accusing Seoul of a series of cross-border violations and warning of unspecified consequences. The drone, according to a statement from the General Staff of North Korea's Korean People's Army carried by state media, was equipped with two cameras that filmed unspecified locations inside the country.

Pyongyang said a second South Korean drone infiltrated its airspace on Sept. 27, 2025, before being forced down by electronic strikes. North Korean authorities said that aircraft also contained video data on "major objects" inside the country.

"The ROK military warmongers will be surely forced to pay a dear price for their unpardonable hysteria," the Korean People's Army General Staff statement said. The ROK — Republic of Korea — is South Korea's official name.

South Korea's Defense Ministry rejected the accusations. The ministry said it did not operate drones during the times North Korea specified and does not possess the types of aircraft Pyongyang described. Senior ministry official Kim Hong-Cheol said in a statement that authorities would investigate whether civilians flew the drones North Korea claims to have recovered. He said South Korea has no intention of provoking North Korea and would continue pursuing trust-building measures.

## Lee's outreach blocked at every turn

The incident dealt a further setback to South Korean President Lee Jae Myung, who has pushed to restore inter-Korean ties since taking office in June. North Korea has refused every overture. Lee said Wednesday he asked Chinese President Xi Jinping to serve as a mediator to ease tensions during a recent summit. Xi called for patience.

North Korea has shunned direct talks with South Korea and the United States since leader Kim Jong Un's nuclear diplomacy with U.S. President Donald Trump fell apart in 2019 over disputes about U.S.-led sanctions. Pyongyang has since concentrated on expanding its nuclear arsenal and declared a hostile "two-state" framework on the Korean Peninsula, formally abandoning the premise of eventual reunification.

## A recurring source of friction

Drone incidents have strained relations between the two Koreas repeatedly in recent years. In October 2024, North Korea accused South Korea of flying drones over its capital, Pyongyang, to drop propaganda leaflets on three occasions. South Korea's military said at the time it could not confirm the claim. Tensions rose sharply but subsided without major military action.

South Korea has also accused North Korea of flying drones across the border. In December 2022, Seoul fired warning shots, scrambled fighter jets, and dispatched its own surveillance drones over North Korean territory after what it described as North Korea's first cross-border drone flights in five years.
