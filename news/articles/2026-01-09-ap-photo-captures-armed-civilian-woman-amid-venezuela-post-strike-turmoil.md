In the hours after U.S. airstrikes that ended with the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, AP photojournalist Matias Delacroix photographed an armed woman on the back of a motorcycle at a gas station in La Guaira — a single frame that documented the blurring of civilian and armed life in the chaos that followed.

The photograph, published as part of AP's "One Extraordinary Photo" series, offers a visual record of how pro-government civilians mobilized in Venezuela's streets after the strikes, echoing a well-known image of armed resistance from decades earlier.

Delacroix, a Chilean-raised photojournalist based in Panama, said the image immediately called to mind a well-known 1990s photograph by a Chilean photographer showing a woman with a submachine gun.

"That visual echo made me pause," Delacroix said. "I was seeing a similar image resurface decades later, this time in Caracas and under very different circumstances."

## At the gas station

Delacroix said he took the photo at a gas station in La Guaira guarded by armed civilians and police. He had traveled there from Caracas by motorcycle after coordinating with an AP photo editor to expand coverage to the port area, where damage from U.S. strikes had been reported.

A small group of motorcyclists arrived at the gas station carrying weapons — some young, others older, he said. They questioned Delacroix about his presence. After he explained he was an international news photographer covering the aftermath of the strikes, they allowed him to continue working.

He had been working nonstop since the strikes began. Traveling by motorcycle, he said, allowed him to reach locations quickly and leave fast if conditions deteriorated.

## What the image shows

Delacroix said the photograph "condenses a tense moment into a single frame: armed civilians occupying public space, motorcycles symbolizing speed and control, and a woman carrying a weapon — a detail that immediately draws attention."

In the hours after the strikes, he said, "the line between civilian life and armed response blurred, conveying instability and fear without the need for explanation."
