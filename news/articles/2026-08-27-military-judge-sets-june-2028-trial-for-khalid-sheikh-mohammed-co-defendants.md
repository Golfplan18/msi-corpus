## Military judge sets June 2028 trial for Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, co-defendants

**Subtype:** fact

- A U.S. military judge on Wednesday set a June 5, 2028, trial date for Khalid Sheikh Mohammed and three other defendants accused of plotting the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks.
- The trial start is 18 months later than the January 2027 date prosecutors had requested, according to the judge's written ruling.
- Air Force Lt. Col. Michael A. Schrama wrote that the additional time is needed to resolve pretrial disputes, including disagreements about what evidence can be presented at trial.
- A trial previously scheduled for 2021 was canceled, and the 2028 date depends on deadlines and milestones that could push the start back further.
- The U.S. military and successive administrations have struggled for more than two decades to bring the case to trial for the man charged with planning one of the deadliest attacks ever on the United States.

## Trial would start nearly 27 years after attacks; 2021 date was canceled

Schrama's order was filed Wednesday. The three other defendants named alongside Mohammed are Walid bin Attash, Ali Abdul Aziz Ali, and Mustafa al-Hawsawi, all of whom face charges in connection with the Sept. 11 plot.

If the trial proceeds on the new schedule, it would begin nearly 27 years after the attacks, replacing a 2021 date that was previously set and later canceled. The June 2028 date assumes each of the deadlines and milestones Schrama outlined is met; if any are missed, the start could slip further.

The order extends a prosecution in which the U.S. military and successive administrations have struggled for more than two decades to bring to justice the man charged with planning one of the deadliest attacks ever on the United States.
