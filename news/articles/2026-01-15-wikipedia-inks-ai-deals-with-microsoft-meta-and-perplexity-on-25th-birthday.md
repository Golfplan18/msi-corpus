Wikipedia announced major business agreements with leading artificial intelligence companies on Thursday as the crowdsourced encyclopedia marked its 25th anniversary. The Wikimedia Foundation, which operates the site, signed deals with Amazon, Meta Platforms, Perplexity, Microsoft, and France's Mistral AI to access Wikipedia content at scale designed for AI training.

The agreements represent the foundation's effort to generate revenue from AI companies' reliance on Wikipedia's vast repository of human-curated knowledge, addressing questions about who bears the infrastructure costs of the artificial intelligence boom.

## Wikipedia Revenues from AI, 25 Years On

Wikipedia is monetizing artificial intelligence companies' reliance on its content, striking a series of licensing deals that address a growing tension: AI developers are training their models on the encyclopedia's knowledge at scale, but the site's infrastructure costs are borne by individual donors.

The new agreements are with Microsoft, Meta Platforms, Perplexity, Amazon, and Mistral AI. The companies are paying to access Wikipedia's 65 million articles in 300 languages "at a volume and speed designed specifically for their needs," according to the Wikimedia Foundation. Financial terms were not disclosed.

The deals come as Wikipedia marks 25 years of operation—a trajectory from scrappy experiment to the ninth most-visited website on the internet. They also underscore a shift in how AI development is financed: companies once scraped web content for free; now, a site with the scale and editorial quality of Wikipedia can demand compensation.

"You should probably chip in and pay for your fair share of the cost that you're putting on us," Wikipedia founder Jimmy Wales said in an interview. He noted the foundation relies on roughly 8 million individual donors, most of them modest contributors, who are "not donating in order to subsidize these huge AI companies."

## The Bot Problem

The foundation's push to monetize AI access reflects operational pressures. Human traffic to Wikipedia fell 8 percent, while visits from automated bots—sometimes disguised to evade detection—have surged, heavily taxing the site's servers as they scrape content to train AI language models.

Wikimedia Foundation CEO Maryana Iskander, who is stepping down on January 20 and will be replaced by Bernadette Meehan, said in a separate interview that the foundation must secure funding to maintain servers and infrastructure serving both individual readers and tech companies drawing data from Wikipedia.

The foundation had already established AI-licensing precedent: it signed Google as a customer in 2022 and reached agreements with smaller AI players like search engine Ecosia last year.

## What Wikipedia Gets in Return

The deals extend beyond revenue. The Wikimedia Foundation has outlined an AI strategy that could produce tools reducing tedious work for editors. While large language models are not yet good enough to write Wikipedia entries from scratch, they could update dead links by scanning surrounding text and searching online for replacement sources—the kind of task that currently requires manual labor.

AI could also reshape how readers search Wikipedia, Wales said. "You can imagine a world where you can ask the Wikipedia search box a question and it will quote to you from Wikipedia," he said. "It could respond by saying 'here's the answer to your question from this article and here's the actual paragraph. That sounds really useful to me.'"

## Criticism and Rivals

Wikipedia has faced criticism from figures on the political right, who have labeled it "Wokepedia" and accused the site of bias favoring progressive positions. Republican lawmakers in the U.S. Congress are investigating alleged "manipulation efforts" in Wikipedia's editing process that they said could inject bias and undermine neutral points of view.

A notable critic is Elon Musk, who last year launched Grokipedia, an AI-powered alternative encyclopedia. Musk has characterized Wikipedia as filled with "propaganda" and urged people to stop donating to the site.

Wales dismissed Grokipedia as a threat. "Large language models aren't good enough to write really quality reference material. So a lot of it is just regurgitated Wikipedia," Wales said. "It often is quite rambling and sort of talks nonsense. And I think the more obscure topic you look into, the worse it is."

Wales said he and Musk have not been in contact since Grokipedia launched. "I should probably ping him," Wales said. "How's your family? I'm a nice person. I don't really want to pick a fight with anybody."
