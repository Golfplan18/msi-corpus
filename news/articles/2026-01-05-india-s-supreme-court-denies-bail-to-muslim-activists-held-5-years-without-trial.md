India's Supreme Court on Monday denied bail to Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam, two Muslim student activists who have spent approximately five years in pretrial detention over charges connected to communal violence that killed 53 people in New Delhi in February 2020.

The court ruled that the two men had a "central role in the conspiracy" behind the riots, distinguishing their cases from five co-accused who were granted bail in the same proceeding. "Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam stand on a qualitatively different footing as compared to other accused," the Supreme Court said in its verdict, according to Bar and Bench, a legal news website. The court also ruled that the length of their pretrial detention was not a sufficient ground for release.

The ruling extends a detention that international rights organizations and U.S. lawmakers have called unlawfully prolonged and that has drawn sustained criticism over India's use of anti-terror statutes in cases involving activists and student leaders.

## The charges and the 2020 riots

Khalid and Imam were arrested under India's Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, known as UAPA, and accused of conspiring to incite the communal violence that swept parts of Delhi in February 2020. The riots, which left 53 dead — most of them Muslims — occurred during months-long protests against a 2019 citizenship law that critics said discriminated against Muslims.

The law permits suspects to be held in pretrial detention for extended periods, often resulting in years of imprisonment before a trial is completed. Dozens of other Muslims were charged in cases linked to the same riots and held under extended pretrial detention. Some of those cases later unraveled, according to the Associated Press, after police were unable to provide evidence linking detainees to the violence.

Prosecutors representing Delhi police told the court that the 2020 violence was not a spontaneous outbreak but a deliberate plot intended to tarnish India's global image, and that Khalid and Imam made provocative speeches and instigated the violence. Lawyers for the two activists denied the charges and argued that no evidence links their clients to the violence.

## International pressure

Khalid and Imam were prominent voices in nationwide protests against the Citizenship Amendment Act, which marked one of the most significant challenges to Prime Minister Narendra Modi's government.

Last week, eight U.S. lawmakers wrote to India's ambassador in Washington expressing concern over Khalid's prolonged pretrial detention and urging Indian authorities to grant him a fair and timely trial, the Associated Press reported.

Amnesty International said in a statement that Khalid's "imprisonment without trial exemplifies derailment of justice" and is "emblematic of a broader pattern of repression faced by those who dare to exercise their rights to freedom of expression."

International human rights organizations have also urged Imam's release, arguing that the extended detentions suppress dissent and breach fundamental legal protections.
