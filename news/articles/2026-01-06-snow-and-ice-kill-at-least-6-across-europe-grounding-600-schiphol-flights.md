THE HAGUE, Netherlands — Snow, ice, and freezing temperatures struck much of Europe on Tuesday, killing at least six people and forcing Amsterdam's Schiphol Airport to cancel some 600 flights as crews scrambled to clear runways and de-ice planes.

Authorities in France reported five deaths in weather-related traffic accidents — three in the Landes region of southwestern France and two more reportedly killed in the greater Paris area. Authorities in the Île-de-France region around Paris ordered trucks off the road as snowfall caused massive traffic jams on Monday; a blanket of snow covered the capital's rooftops by Tuesday. Heavy snowfall also forced the closure of six airports in northern and western France.

A sixth death was reported in Sarajevo, Bosnia, where a woman died Monday after a snow-covered tree branch fell on her head.

## Netherlands: Flights grounded, rails halted, scammers target stranded travelers

At Schiphol, one of Europe's busiest hubs, Dutch airline KLM warned travelers stranded by the cancellations that scammers posing as customer service staff were targeting passengers and that incorrect airline phone numbers were circulating online.

"Be alert to these phishing attempts. Do not respond to such messages, and do not share any personal or payment information," KLM said.

KLM also disclosed it was running short of the glycol fluid used with hot water to de-ice planes before takeoff, and said airports across Europe faced similar shortages after days of freezing temperatures. More snow was forecast for the Netherlands through the rest of the week.

Rail service suffered alongside air travel. Frozen points and an early morning software glitch threw the national network into turmoil; national railway company NS urged commuters to "only travel if it's absolutely necessary." Routes around Amsterdam remained largely closed through the morning because of icy conditions.

Some commuters resorted to bicycles. Thijs Rademakers, an 18-year-old student, chose to cycle in the eastern city of Arnhem rather than wait for public transport.

"It was tough, very slippery," he said. "Many people fall. Luckily, I didn't."

## Britain: Deep freeze, school closures, subway shut

A cold snap sent temperatures in northern parts of Britain down to minus 12.5 degrees Celsius (9.5 Fahrenheit) overnight, with snow disrupting rail, road, and air travel and closing hundreds of schools. Horse races and soccer matches were called off because of snow and frost.

A power failure caused by ice shut Glasgow's subway system.

Up to 15 centimeters (6 inches) of snow was forecast Tuesday for northern Scotland, where some residents had already been snowed in by earlier falls. Northeast Scotland lawmaker Andrew Bowie described the situation as "critical" and called for soldiers to be deployed to clear snow and deliver food and medical supplies to stranded communities.

## Balkans: Emergency measures, icy mountain roads

Heavy snow and rain swept through Balkan countries, swelling rivers and disrupting power and water supplies. Some western municipalities in Serbia introduced emergency measures. Authorities warned drivers to use extreme caution as many headed toward ski resorts for Orthodox Christmas — black ice stopped cars on the road to Mount Bjelasnica above Sarajevo on Tuesday morning.

Heavy wind and stormy seas battered the Adriatic coastline in Croatia and Montenegro. Video footage showed seawater sweeping through holiday cottages at Ada Bojana in southern Montenegro during the storm.

## Italy: Rome's Tiber swells, Epiphany crowd thinned

In Rome, weeks of steady rain swelled the Tiber River over its banks. Mayor Roberto Gualtieri issued an ordinance limiting public access to parks and other areas at risk for falling trees and flooding.

St. Peter's Square was only partially full Tuesday as a few thousand people gathered under umbrellas to hear Pope Leo XIV deliver his Epiphany blessing from the loggia of St. Peter's Basilica.

Snow dusted the northern city of Bologna and gave skiers in the Dolomites reason to cheer, though freezing temperatures are forecast for much of northern and central Italy through the coming days.
