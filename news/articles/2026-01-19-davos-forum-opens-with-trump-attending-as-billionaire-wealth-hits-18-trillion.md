DAVOS, Switzerland — Corporate leaders and government officials from across the globe gathered in Davos, Switzerland, on Tuesday as the World Economic Forum opened its four-day annual meeting, with U.S. President Donald Trump among the roughly 3,000 attendees. The event arrives against a backdrop of surging elite wealth and deepening public distrust of institutions — tensions that are likely to shape discussions throughout the week.

Oxfam, the international advocacy group, reported in a new study that global billionaire wealth rose by more than 16% last year, three times faster than the past five-year average, reaching more than $18 trillion. The group said the $2.5 trillion increase in billionaire wealth in a single year would be enough to eradicate extreme poverty 26 times over. Billionaire wealth has risen by more than four-fifths since 2020, Oxfam said, while nearly half the world's population remains in poverty. The report drew on Forbes magazine data tracking the world's richest people.

## A forum under changed leadership

The World Economic Forum is meeting this year without its founder, Klaus Schwab, who established the event 55 years ago and stepped down in April. New co-chairs Larry Fink, head of investment firm BlackRock, and Andre Hoffman, vice chair of pharmaceuticals firm Roche, have assumed leadership of an organization that has grown from a business-focused gathering into what its promoters describe as a global dialogue on humanity's most pressing challenges.

This year will mark the debut appearance at Davos of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, among some 850 CEOs and chairs of global companies. The future of artificial intelligence, its impact on work, and the prospects for artificial general intelligence are among the forum's key themes.

The presidents of Argentina, France, Indonesia, Syria, and Ukraine are also on hand, along with Trump, who is making his third visit to Davos as president.

## Trust in free fall

Public confidence in institutions has continued to erode, according to Edelman's annual Trust Barometer, released at the forum. The survey, which polled nearly 34,000 people in 28 countries, found that trade and recession fears have reached an all-time high since the barometer's launch 25 years ago, with optimism falling in developed countries in particular.

Nearly 70% of respondents said they believed institutional leaders — whether from business or government — deliberately mislead the public. About two-thirds said their trust was instead concentrated in the CEOs of their own employers or in fellow citizens and neighbors.

"People are retreating from dialogue and compromise, choosing the safety of the familiar over the perceived risk of change," said Richard Edelman, CEO of the firm. "We favor nationalism over global connection and individual gain over joint progress. Our mentality has shifted from 'we' to 'me'."

## Geopolitical tensions in the corridors

Trump's attendance comes as U.S. allies have expressed concern over his stated ambitions regarding Greenland and his administration's pressure tactics toward Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell. The forum's stated theme — "A spirit of dialogue" — carries particular weight given what Mirek Dušek, a forum managing director, described as a "more competitive, more contested" global landscape.

"It's really going to be a discussion at a very important moment," Dušek said. "Geopolitics is changing."

EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng were both scheduled to speak on Tuesday, the forum's opening day — representing the two international actors Davos organizers positioned as the primary counterweights to Washington on the global stage.

Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi had been scheduled to appear but was disinvited. WEF organizers said Monday that "the tragic loss of civilian lives" made it "not right" for Iran's government to be represented. Iran's leaders face U.S. sanctions related to their response to domestic protests.

Trump is expected to discuss housing and affordability in his Davos speech on Wednesday, according to the Associated Press.

## Protests outside the hall

Hundreds of demonstrators marched Saturday on an Alpine road into Davos behind a banner reading "No Profit from War," and a truck bearing a sign that read "World Economic Failure."

"It is worrying how Swiss politicians are courting warmongers and their profiteers in Davos," said Mirjam Hostetmann, president of Switzerland's Young Socialists, which helped organize the march. "The WEF will never bring peace, but will only fuel escalation."

Critics have long argued that the annual gathering generates more rhetoric than results, and some said Trump's return illustrated the disconnect between the forum's stated ideals and the interests of its participants.
