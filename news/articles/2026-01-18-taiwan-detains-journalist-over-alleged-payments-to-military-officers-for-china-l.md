The case is unusual in Taiwan, where espionage investigations typically target government and military personnel rather than journalists, and comes as Taipei intensifies scrutiny of potential infiltration by Beijing amid sustained Chinese military pressure on the island.

A television reporter and five current and former military officers were detained in Taiwan on Saturday after prosecutors alleged the journalist paid soldiers to pass military information to people in mainland China. Taiwan's Qiaotou District Prosecutors Office said a district court ordered the detentions.

CTi TV identified the detained reporter as Lin Chen-you, one of its political journalists and anchors who covers Taiwan's legislature. The network said it was unaware of the case details and urged that the judicial process be fair. The prosecutor's office referred to the reporter only by the surname Lin.

## Allegations

Prosecutors allege Lin paid amounts ranging from several thousand to tens of thousands of Taiwan dollars — the equivalent of tens to hundreds of U.S. dollars — to current military officers in exchange for their providing information to "Chinese individuals," according to the office's statement. Prosecutors did not specify who the Chinese individuals were or whether they were linked to the Chinese government.

Authorities raided the premises of the reporter and nine current and retired military personnel on Friday as part of an investigation into alleged violations of Taiwan's national security and corruption laws and disclosure of confidential information. CTi said its offices were not raided.

## Unusual case for Taiwan press

While Taiwan regularly pursues espionage cases within the government and military, allegations against journalists are unusual, according to the Associated Press.

CTi News has operated as an online news outlet since Taiwan's National Communications Commission refused to renew its cable television license in 2020, citing repeated violations and increasing complaints. A court later overturned that decision, but the legal dispute remains ongoing following appeals.

## Broader context

The detention comes as Beijing continues to increase military pressure on the island. China's military launched large-scale drills around Taiwan for two days last month after Washington announced large-scale arms sales to Taiwan. Beijing, which claims Taiwan as its own territory, has threatened to take control of the island by force if necessary.

China and Taiwan have been governed separately since 1949, when Communist Party forces prevailed in a civil war and Nationalist forces withdrew to Taiwan. Taiwan later transitioned from martial law to multiparty democracy.
