The disruptions struck one of Europe's busiest air corridors, forcing airlines to divert incoming Vienna flights as far as Munich, Frankfurt, Cologne and Venice, and stranding passengers at Prague's main railway station for hours — extending a pattern of winter weather disruptions that has battered European transport infrastructure since early January.

PRAGUE — Freezing rain and icy conditions halted flights at four major airports across central and eastern Europe on Tuesday, grounding and diverting air traffic at Budapest, Vienna, Prague and Bratislava as a cold snap spread disruption to rail, road and public transit networks.

Hungary's transport minister, János Lázár, wrote on social media that "freezing rain and extreme icing conditions" halted all departures and arrivals at Ferenc Liszt International Airport in Budapest.

At Vienna International Airport, one of central Europe's busiest hubs, incoming flights were diverted to airports including Munich, Frankfurt, Cologne and Venice. Spokesperson Peter Kleemann told the Austria Press Agency that departures were delayed but that operations were resuming in late morning.

Prague's Václav Havel Airport restricted arrivals, while Slovakia's international airport was closed for hours.

## Rail and road networks also disrupted

The disruptions extended well beyond the air network. Tram services in Budapest were disrupted due to accumulating ice after significant snowfall the previous week, the capital's public transit authority said. Numerous national and international rail services were canceled or delayed.

Austria's national railway operator, ÖBB, said delays were expected across the north and east, including Vienna, and urged travelers to postpone non-urgent journeys. Czech authorities reported train and bus cancellations and said passengers at the capital's main railway station faced hours-long delays. The D8 highway connecting the Czech Republic to Germany was closed after an accident on the German side; parts of eastern Germany saw icy roads.

## Romania facing lowest temperatures

Romania, which recorded heavy snowfall over the preceding week, faced temperatures forecast as low as -13 degrees Celsius in some areas, prompting some schools to move classes online.

Hungary's Meteorological Service said significant snowfall could be expected in the country's eastern regions, while rain and freezing rain was likely farther west.
