## US employers add 162,000 jobs in August, beating forecasts

**Subtype:** fact

- U.S. employers added 162,000 jobs in August, more than double the 65,000 economists had projected.
- President Donald Trump welcomed the hiring report in a social media post on Friday.
- Average hourly wages rose 3.1% year-over-year in August, the weakest annual pace since May 2021.
- Fuel prices have hit record levels since the United States and Israel attacked Iran in late February, frustrating voters facing higher costs.

## Voter frustration grows as fuel prices hit records since February

The August hiring rebound caught analysts off guard — economists had projected roughly 65,000 jobs, less than half the reported figure. Two months before midterm elections, President Donald Trump marked the report on social media. "Great jobs number just announced, breaking all estimates (except mine!) by double and triple - And you haven't seen anything yet!" he wrote.

Inflation has dominated conversations in business and households throughout the year, according to the Associated Press. Voters are increasingly frustrated by higher costs, particularly fuel prices that have hit record levels since the United States and Israel attacked Iran in late February. Trips to the grocery store and gas station are more painful than they were a year ago, and rising costs are impacting the decisions of both households and businesses.

Meager pay raises have made rising prices more painful for many. Average hourly wages rose 3.1% from a year earlier, the weakest year-over-year increase since May 2021, the AP reported.
