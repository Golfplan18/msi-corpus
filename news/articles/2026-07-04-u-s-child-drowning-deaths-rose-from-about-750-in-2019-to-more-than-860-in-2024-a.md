## Drowning is leading cause of death for children ages 1 to 4

More U.S. children have been drowning in recent years, according to data cited by the American Academy of Pediatrics, as doctors and others sound an alarm about the increase. Child drowning deaths rose from about 750 in 2019 to more than 860 in 2024, the most recent year with complete data.

“When drowning occurs, seconds matter,” said Dr. Rohit Shenoi, the lead author of a recent AAP warning on the issue. “Quick rescue and resuscitation can mean the difference between life, death and lifelong disability.”

About 4,000 to 5,000 Americans drown each year, the majority of them adults in natural bodies of water such as lakes, ponds or oceans. But drowning poses a much greater danger to children proportionally. It is the leading cause of death for children ages 1 to 4 and one of the top causes of death for those ages 5 to 14.

The drowning rate is higher for white children in the younger age group, but it is much higher for Black, American Indian and Alaska Native children in the older age group, according to the data.

Most drownings of very young children occur in swimming pools, though some happen in bathtubs. Some health leaders and advocates have been working to reverse the trend.
