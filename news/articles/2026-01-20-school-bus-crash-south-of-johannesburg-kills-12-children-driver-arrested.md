A school bus carrying children crashed head-on into a truck early Monday morning south of Johannesburg, killing 12 children, South African authorities said. The 22-year-old bus driver was arrested after being discharged from the hospital and faces 12 counts of culpable homicide, along with charges of reckless and negligent driving.

Many South African children depend on privately operated bus services for transportation to and from school, making vehicle and driver safety a critical concern for families.

## The Crash

The privately operated minibus was carrying children to various schools in Gauteng province when it collided with the truck. According to the provincial health department, nine of the victims were girls and three were boys. Five other children were hospitalized, along with the bus driver and a passenger in the truck.

## Driver Arrested and Charged

Witnesses said the bus had been overtaking other vehicles when it collided with the truck. The 22-year-old male driver was arrested after being discharged from the hospital. Gauteng province premier Panyaza Lesufi said the driver's permit to operate a minibus had expired at the time of the crash. He will be charged with 12 counts of culpable homicide and reckless and negligent driving.

## Safety Concerns

Schools reopened in South Africa last week after the end-of-year holidays. Safety advocates have previously warned about risks associated with privately operated bus services. Earlier this month, the Child Safe advocacy group urged parents to be careful about which bus services they use and to look out for poorly maintained vehicles and unlicensed operators.
