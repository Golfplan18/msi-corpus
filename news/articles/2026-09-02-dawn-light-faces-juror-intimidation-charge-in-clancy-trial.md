## Judge denies $50,000 bail, bars Light from courthouse grounds

Dawn Light, 56, has been charged with aggravated intimidation of a juror in connection with the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy, a Massachusetts court heard on Wednesday. Light was arrested a day earlier after authorities said they found her in a restricted area outside the Plymouth courthouse, where the livestreamed proceedings have drawn more than 200 journalists and a shifting group of women supporters to the coastal Massachusetts town.

According to prosecutors, Light was sitting in her parked car in a restricted section of the courthouse lot when officers approached on Tuesday. When asked whether she was taking videos, Light told officers she was not and explained that she was waiting for Clancy. A subsequent review of her phone's deleted folder, prosecutors alleged, turned up photographs of jurors exiting the courthouse, and she was taken into custody.

At Wednesday's arraignment, Massachusetts lawyers argued that the sanctity of the judicial system was at risk because of Light's conduct. They requested that the judge impose $50,000 (£37,000) cash bail; the request was denied. The judge ordered Light to stay away from the courthouse grounds and from anyone associated with the Clancy trial, and further ordered her not to produce or disseminate any photographs that could have been preserved on iCloud.

Light's defense attorney, Jennifer White, told the court the arrest was based on a misreading of her client's intentions. "This is a complete misunderstanding, Your Honour. My client was trying to get a picture of Ms Clancy," White said. Light, a registered nurse with no prior criminal record, pleaded not guilty to the charge.

After the proceeding, Light was surrounded by reporters as she left the courthouse. When one journalist asked why she had wanted to see Clancy, Light replied, "Why not?"

The juror-intimidation charge comes against the backdrop of a trial that has captivated national and international audiences. Clancy, 36, is charged with the murders of her three children at the family's Massachusetts home. The case has drawn intense interest on social media, where it has stoked broader public debate about mental health, postpartum psychosis, and the limits of criminal responsibility.

The jury has been deliberating since Thursday. Judge William Sullivan, who is overseeing the Clancy trial, has issued an order specifically prohibiting the photographing or filming of jurors. Jurors in the case are barred from watching or reading coverage of the proceedings and have been instructed to base their verdict solely on the evidence and testimony presented in court.

Prosecutors said the courthouse has taken extensive measures to protect the trial, which has created a spectacle in the small coastal town, and described Light's conduct as a serious matter. The group of women supporters who have gathered outside the courthouse over the past few weeks has varied in size, ebbing and flowing in numbers as the proceedings have unfolded.
