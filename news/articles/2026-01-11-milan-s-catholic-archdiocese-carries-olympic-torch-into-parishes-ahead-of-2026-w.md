MILAN — Milan's Catholic archdiocese has been carrying a symbolic Olympic torch through parishes, schools, and youth centers across the diocese since late 2022, building toward the 2026 Winter Games that will open in the city. The initiative, the Ora Sport on Fire Tour, aims to translate the values of the Olympic movement — human rights, peace, inclusion — into pastoral work with young people, according to the archdiocese.

Archbishop Mario Delpini described the program's purpose in a letter following its launch. "We believe that the Olympics represent a great educational opportunity in the meanings they will carry," Delpini wrote. "The city as an Olympic village is a metaphor to say that relationships are shaped by competing in mutual esteem."

The archdiocese's roughly 1,000 oratories — parish-based youth spaces central to Italian Catholic life — serve as the program's backbone. Practically all of them have a sports club, and many have offered youth athletics for generations. "Some even reach 100 years of history," said the Rev. Stefano Guidi, who heads the archdiocese's Service for Oratories and Sport. "For many kids, adolescents and families, this is their only possibility to practice sports."

## Rooted in the Olympic Charter

The Ora Sport on Fire Tour began with a study of the Olympic Charter, the founding document of the Olympic movement, and built its programming on the charter's values. Through sporting events, contests, and workshops, the archdiocese has promoted themes of human rights and peace in Christian-inspired schools and oratories across the diocese.

The program was developed by the archdiocese's sports and youth outreach offices in collaboration with its school pastoral services. Guidi said both the tour and the activities planned during the Games aim to highlight the inclusive and social dimensions of sport. "We hope that these values will help young people in our city grow," he said.

## A torch on the move

The symbolic torch travels throughout the diocese, visiting two pastoral zones per year. As each deanery receives it, gatherings, prayer, and talks on Olympic values are held. In Tradate, in northern Italy, children and adolescents transported the torch and reflected on its meaning. In Gallarate, sports activities focused on inclusion, teaching youths to recognize and respect differences and diverse abilities.

Paralympic athletes have featured prominently in the program's events. Swimmer Arianna Talamona spoke at an early gathering, which was streamed on the archdiocese's channels. "One thing I often feel like saying when I go into schools and meet students is to be patient and to have very clear ideas about their passions," Talamona said. "And if they have dreams and passions, it's important to cultivate them."

Swimmer Alberto Amodeo, who competed in both the Tokyo and Paris Paralympics, appeared as a guest at a diocesan sports gathering in Abbiategrasso to mark the launch of the tour's third year. He reflected on how the Games bring together athletes of different ethnicities and backgrounds. "These are beautiful results that will remain forever in my heart," Amodeo said.

## Varying formats, consistent principles

Programming has varied from one deanery to another. Some hosted sports-themed plays overseen by a professional theater company. Others organized film forums or large-scale sports activities. All remain tied to the upcoming Olympics.

Guidi said three principles run through all of the diocesan sport-related programs: conveying how sport develops each person's physical abilities, how it supports socialization, and how it develops respect for one's opponent.

"It therefore proposes a kind of growth that has the meaning of constant training of oneself and of one's relationships," Guidi said. "And the possibility of learning from one's mistakes."

Most oratory activities are offered at low cost, Guidi said, mainly through volunteer work. The archdiocese has also hosted encounters between religious leaders, athletes, and young people as part of the program's run-up to the Games, and plans additional activities during the Games themselves.
