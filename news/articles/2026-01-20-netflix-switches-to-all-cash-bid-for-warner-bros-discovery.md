The competing bids highlight deepening consolidation pressures in the entertainment industry, with antitrust authorities and trade groups warning that further mergers could trigger job losses and diminish content diversity. Political considerations also weigh on the outcome, with President Donald Trump suggesting unprecedented personal involvement in the deal's approval.

Netflix restructured its $72 billion acquisition proposal for Warner Bros. Discovery to an all-cash offer on Tuesday, seeking to overcome shareholder doubts and deflect a hostile bid from Paramount-Skydance. The revised deal maintains the $27.75 per share valuation agreed in December but simplifies the transaction structure, with Warner stockholders also receiving shares in Discovery Global, a newly separated entity. Both companies' boards approved the restructured offer, which Netflix and Warner expect to close within 12 to 18 months.

## Warner leadership backs Netflix deal

"The revised agreement brings us even closer to combining two of the greatest storytelling companies in the world," Warner CEO David Zaslav said in a statement. The companies said the all-cash structure would provide "more certainty of value for Warner stockholders and speed up the path to a shareholder vote," with the possibility of shareholder approval by April.

Netflix co-CEO Ted Sarandos said combining with Warner "will deliver broader choice and greater value to audiences worldwide both at home and in theaters — while driving job creation and long-term industry growth."

## Paramount's hostile campaign

Paramount-Skydance is waging an aggressive counter-campaign to block the Netflix deal. The company made an all-cash offer to acquire Warner's entire business — including networks like CNN and Discovery — for $77.9 billion, with an enterprise value of $108 billion including debt.

Warner stockholders had until 5 p.m. ET Wednesday to tender their shares in support of Paramount's bid, though that deadline could be extended further. Paramount has promised a proxy fight and announced plans to nominate its own slate of directors for Warner's next shareholder meeting. The company also filed a lawsuit in Delaware Chancery Court seeking to compel Warner to disclose how it values both offers, but a Delaware judge denied Paramount's request to expedite that proceeding.

Warner called the lawsuit "yet another unserious attempt to distract," while Paramount maintained that stockholders "should ask why their Board is working so hard to hide this information."

## Regulatory and political uncertainty

The competing bids face substantial antitrust scrutiny. Trade groups across the media and entertainment industry have sounded the alarm, warning that further consolidation could trigger job losses and diminish content diversity, with particularly negative consequences for filmmaking.

Political considerations also weigh heavily on the outcome. President Donald Trump has made unprecedented suggestions about his personal involvement in whether the deal will proceed, introducing another layer of uncertainty into the timeline.

Netflix's stock inched up just under 1 percent on Tuesday morning, while shares of Warner Bros. Discovery and Paramount-Skydance fell slightly.
