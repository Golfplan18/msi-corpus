## Family awaits answers a month after Mississippi teen found dead on Horn Island

**Subtype:** fact

- A month after 18-year-old Nolan Xavier Wells was found dead on Horn Island, Mississippi, his family and civil-rights advocates say investigators have not disclosed key findings.
- The Jackson County district attorney's office has said state autopsy results will not be released until a grand jury is convened.
- Attorney Ben Crump, who is representing the Wells family, said the state turned over the body for an independent autopsy without the throat intact, and that the cause and date of death were both undetermined.
- Community members and advocates have publicly questioned whether the investigation is receiving the same resources and attention it would if the racial circumstances were reversed.

## Community demands transparency as autopsy findings remain sealed

Wells joined friends for a boat trip to Horn Island, a barrier island off the Mississippi coast, in early July. He did not return with them, and his body was later discovered on the island. The Jackson County sheriff's office has said witnesses were interviewed, but authorities have disclosed virtually no investigative conclusions, and no one has been charged in connection with Wells's death.

The family's legal team, led by prominent civil-rights attorney Ben Crump, commissioned an independent autopsy after the state pathologist's exam. Crump said the state turned over the body without the throat intact, and the independent examination could not determine the cause or date of death. The Jackson County district attorney's office has declined to release the state autopsy results until a grand jury is impaneled.

The intervening weeks have been marked by growing public scrutiny. A widely circulated photograph shows Wells, who was Black, alongside three white companions on the boat. None of those companions have been publicly identified as suspects by law enforcement. Some community members and advocates have questioned whether the investigation would be moving more quickly if the racial composition of the group had been reversed.

"The uncertainty surrounding the investigation has led some community members and advocates to question whether the case is being handled with the same urgency it would receive if the racial circumstances were reversed," the Guardian reported, and added that Crump and others have directly compared the case to the 1955 lynching of Emmett Till — a Black teenager murdered in Mississippi after being accused of offending a white woman.

On 30 July, Wells's parents and Crump appeared at the National Urban League conference in New Orleans alongside the families of two other Black 18-year-olds who died in 2026. Crump tied the cases together, telling the audience that all three young men were in majority-white group settings before their deaths, and urging federal authorities to review the circumstances.

Two arrests have been reported in connection with the case, both for threats made against a local judge and the sheriff's office — not in relation to Wells's death itself.

Grand juries in Mississippi convene only a handful of times each year. Their proceedings are conducted in secret. A grand jury can issue an indictment only if it finds probable cause that a specific person committed a crime. In Jackson County, the district attorney's office has provided no timeline for when a grand jury might be called to consider the Wells case.
