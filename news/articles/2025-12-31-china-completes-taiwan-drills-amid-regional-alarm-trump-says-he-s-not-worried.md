The maneuvers marked a sharp escalation in tensions in the final days of 2025, underscoring Beijing's willingness to use large-scale military pressure to assert its claim over the self-governing island — and exposing a divide between regional neighbors alarmed by the drills and an incoming U.S. president signaling a different posture toward Beijing.

BEIJING — China's People's Liberation Army said Wednesday it had "successfully completed" two days of military exercises in waters off Taiwan, wrapping up an operation that drew condemnation from Japan and the Philippines while U.S. President Donald Trump said he was not concerned.

As MSI [previously reported](/articles/2025-12-31-china-s-pla-declares-justice-mission-2025-taiwan-drills-complete), the PLA declared its "Justice Mission 2025" exercises complete in a New Year's Eve announcement that offered no specifics about what constituted success or exactly when the drills had concluded.

The PLA said the operation had "fully tested the integrated joint operations capabilities of its troops." Senior Capt. Li Xi, spokesperson for the PLA's Eastern Theater Command, said troops would "keep strengthening combat readiness with arduous training" and would "resolutely thwart the attempts of 'Taiwan Independence' separatists and external intervention, and firmly safeguard state sovereignty and territorial integrity."

## Xi invokes Taiwan in New Year's address

The military announcement came as Chinese President Xi Jinping made a brief reference to Taiwan in his annual New Year's Eve address to the nation, saying Chinese people on both sides of the Taiwan Strait share "a bond of blood and kinship."

"The reunification of our motherland, a trend of the times, is unstoppable," Xi said.

Beijing has long insisted Taiwan is its sovereign territory and has promised to retake it by force if necessary. Taiwan has been self-governing since 1949, when Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalist forces retreated to the island after losing China's civil war to Mao Zedong's communists. Beijing sends warplanes and navy vessels toward Taiwan on a near-daily basis and has stepped up the scope and scale of exercises in recent years.

## Japan and Philippines condemn the drills

Japan's Foreign Ministry said the exercises were "an act that escalates tension in the Taiwan Strait" and said Tokyo had conveyed its concern to Beijing.

"Japan expects the issues surrounding Taiwan to be resolved peacefully through dialogue, which is a position that the Japanese government has consistently maintained all along," the ministry said in a statement. "The peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait are important for the entire international community."

Japan's prime minister had said in November she would not rule out military intervention if Taiwan faced a direct attack by the PLA.

In the Philippines — which has separate territorial disputes with China in the South China Sea — Defense Minister Gilberto C. Teodoro Jr. said he was "deeply concerned by China's military and coast guard actions around Taiwan," saying the maneuvers undermine stability "in an already fragile geopolitical environment."

"This heightened scale of coercion has implications that extend beyond cross-Strait relations and into the broader Indo-Pacific community," Teodoro said. "Basic principles of self-restraint must be observed."

## Trump signals a different posture

Trump said he was not concerned about the exercises, citing his relationship with Xi and noting that China had been "doing naval exercises for 20 years in that area."

The remarks contrasted with a mid-December announcement by the United States of a proposed arms sales package to Taiwan that, if approved by Congress, would represent the largest such aid package to the island ever — a move Beijing had criticized sharply.
