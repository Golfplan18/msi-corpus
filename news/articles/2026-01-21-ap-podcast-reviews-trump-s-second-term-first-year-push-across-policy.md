In the year since President Donald Trump’s second inauguration, the Associated Press said he has made sweeping policy changes, eliminated hundreds of government jobs, battled dozens of court cases, and deployed Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers to major American cities as part of an immigration push he campaigned on. The reporting came in a Jan. 22 episode of “The Story Behind the AP Story,” in which AP White House reporter Darlene Superville and AP Washington radio correspondent Sagar Meghani revisited major developments since Trump took the oath for a second time.

Host Haya Panjwani introduced the episode by saying the program would look back at the last year, including actions Trump took on policy, government operations, and renovations of the White House itself. Superville and Meghani framed several of the changes as carrying a consistent theme of “determination,” with Superville describing that as a defining element of Trump’s second first term.

The episode also focused on government operations and cost-cutting efforts. Panjwani said that during Trump’s campaign for office, he pushed for reform in government operations. Superville said the second term introduced the acronym DOGE for the Department of Government Efficiency, and she described Elon Musk as a major supporter of Trump who, in her account, brought an idea to slash government spending and the federal workforce. Superville said Musk joined the administration at the beginning and that an “army of young workers” reviewed federal departments and agencies to identify spending cuts and jobs that could be eliminated. She said the result was that thousands of federal workers no longer work for the federal government and that some spending has been slashed, while also saying the $2 billion figure Musk had described was not achieved.

On policing and crime in U.S. cities, the episode described Trump’s use of the National Guard. Superville said Trump sent the National Guard into a number of cities because he believed many areas were overrun by crime, naming murders, killings, and kidnappings among the offenses he associated with those places. She said the first area was Washington, D.C., and that Trump also attempted to do similar deployments in Chicago, Portland, and New Orleans. Meghani said uniformed National Guard presence remained in Washington, including inside train stations, and he described Trump as having wrongly stated that Washington was completely safe.

Immigration enforcement was another major focus, with the episode describing ICE operations as especially contentious in Minnesota. Meghani said the “flashpoint” of the Trump immigration raids involving ICE officers has become Minnesota. He described protests tied to the ICE raids and said there has been controversy involving a fatal killing by an ICE agent of Renee Good, adding that the Justice Department was investigating what happened. Meghani also said immigration remains a strength for Trump among his base, but that several Republicans have said the administration’s aggressive tactics in Minneapolis go too far, with Republicans pressing for the focus to move toward immigrants with criminal backgrounds. He said an AP-NORC poll found 38% of adults approve of Trump’s leadership on immigration and that approval fell by about a dozen percent among Republicans over roughly a year.

Foreign policy and conflict resolution were discussed as well. Meghani said Russia’s war in Ukraine remained unresolved, recalling that Trump had claimed he could solve it within 24 hours of entering the Oval Office. The episode said the war was still not over a year later, and Meghani described Trump as angry that a deal had not been reached. On the Middle East, Superville said Trump drew praise for a plan to solve tensions between Israel and the Gaza Strip, but the episode said the plan had not yet been fully adopted by the parties.

The discussion also extended to Venezuela and U.S. actions connected to it, as well as a dispute involving Greenland. Meghani said the episode pointed to expanding U.S. involvement across conflicts, including Russia, Ukraine, Gaza, and Venezuela. The episode said the administration carried out military strikes on suspected drug-running boats that killed people at sea, followed by an overnight raid into Caracas. It also said Trump indicated the United States would play a big role in controlling Venezuelan oil sales. Separately, Panjwani said the episode covered ongoing talks between the United States and Greenland and Denmark about Trump’s desire for the United States to own Greenland for national security interests, with Greenland and Denmark saying it would never happen.

The episode further addressed legal and political dynamics, including the Jeffrey Epstein case files. Panjwani said Trump resisted the full release of the Epstein files. Meghani said Congress had forced the Justice Department to release several thousand of the records and that there are more than two million of them overall. Meghani said DOJ described its work as redacting sensitive information to protect victims, and he said Democrats were still not satisfied. Superville added that Trump had initially been reluctant to release the files and described his relationship with Jeffrey Epstein in terms of friendship and images that had appeared in the media, saying Trump changed course only after momentum built in Congress toward legislation forcing release.

On domestic politics and the economy, Meghani said AP-NORC polling found 37% of adults approve of Trump’s handling of the economy. Superville and Meghani discussed disagreement over affordability and inflation, describing Trump as claiming Democrats made affordability issues by saying things are not affordable and as asserting that inflation had been defeated. The episode said Trump has focused on lower interest rates and has criticized Fed Chairman Jay Powell, and Superville said Powell alone does not determine interest rates because a committee of the Federal Reserve sets them. The episode also said Trump has used the renovation of Federal Reserve buildings as a way to press Powell, and it said Powell’s term ends in May.

The podcast also reviewed White House renovations described by Superville. She said Trump showed an unusual level of interest in redecorating and renovating the White House, and she said his responsibility for such changes generally belongs to the first lady. Superville described the biggest project as demolishing the East Wing to add a large ballroom, and she said Trump redecorated the Oval Office with gold touches and portraits of every president, added a copy of the Declaration of Independence, paved over and turned the Rose Garden into a patio, redid a bathroom in the Lincoln bedroom with marble, and installed two massive flag poles on the north and south lawns.

Finally, the episode looked ahead to 2026 midterm elections, saying the political landscape could affect the direction of Trump’s presidency. Panjwani said the midterms could play a major role in what happens next. Meghani said Trump has acknowledged an electoral pattern in which the party that wins the White House loses in midterms, and she said Democrats would have to flip four seats in the Senate to regain control. The episode also said Democratic leader Chuck Schumer told the AP that the path to regaining control was now much wider if Democrats could take one or both chambers of Congress. Superville said Trump was working to help Republicans retain control of the House and Senate, including encouraging states such as Texas, Missouri, and North Carolina to do redistricting early. The episode also reported that Trump told the House GOP conference that if Democrats win, they will impeach him.
