## Shein set for $1.7 billion Hong Kong listing as AI and robotics drive Chinese share-sales wave

**Subtype:** fact

- Shein, a Chinese-founded e-commerce and fast-fashion company, is due to begin trading in Hong Kong Tuesday with a $1.7 billion initial public offering, one of the city's biggest new share sales of the year.
- CXMT, China's largest memory chipmaker, raised more than $8.6 billion in Shanghai in July in the second-largest IPO on the mainland, with shares jumping 466% on the first day of trading.
- Unitree, a Chinese humanoid-robot maker, made its listing debut in Shanghai in August, with shares rising 460% on the first day of trading.
- S&P Global Market Intelligence analyst Ruiying Zhao said the current IPO boom is powered by investor appetite for AI and robotics, while trading on Shanghai's stock market is heavily driven by retail investors.

## Retail investors heavily drive Shanghai trading

CXMT, China's largest memory chipmaker, raised more than $8.6 billion on Shanghai's STAR Market in July — the second-largest IPO for the venue and the second-largest mainland China IPO on record. Shares in CXMT jumped 466% on the first day of trading.

Unitree, one of China's leading humanoid-robot makers, made its own listing debut in Shanghai in August. Shares in Unitree rose 460% on day one of trading.

"The current IPO boom is powered by investor appetite for AI and robotics," said Ruiying Zhao, a senior research analyst at S&P Global Market Intelligence. Trading on Shanghai's stock market is heavily driven by retail investors.

Shein's $1.7 billion Hong Kong listing on Tuesday caps the run of outsized Chinese share sales this summer. The fast-fashion company is one of the city's biggest new IPOs of the year.
