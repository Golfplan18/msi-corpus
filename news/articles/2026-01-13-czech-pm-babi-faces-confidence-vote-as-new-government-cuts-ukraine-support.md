PRAGUE — Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš's new coalition government faced a mandatory confidence vote in Parliament on Tuesday, with debate opening in the 200-seat lower house where the coalition holds a majority of 108 seats. Every new Czech administration must win such a vote to govern.

The Babiš government's return to power places the Czech Republic alongside Hungary and Slovakia among Central European EU and NATO members that have moved away from direct financial backing for Ukraine, reshaping the region's posture toward Kyiv as Russia's invasion continues.

"I'd like to make it clear that the Czech Republic and Czech citizens will be first for our government," Babiš said in a speech before the lower house on Tuesday.

Babiš, who served as prime minister in two governments from 2017 to 2021, returned to power after his ANO, or YES, movement won the country's October election. He formed a majority coalition with two smaller political groups: Freedom and Direct Democracy, an anti-migrant party, and Motorists for Themselves. The three parties, which share admiration for U.S. President Donald Trump, according to the Associated Press, created a 16-member Cabinet.

On Ukraine, Babiš has rejected both direct financial aid and guarantees for European Union loans to the country, joining Hungary's Prime Minister Viktor Orbán and Slovakia's Prime Minister Robert Fico in declining to back Kyiv financially.

The government said it would continue a Czech-administered initiative that acquired approximately 1.8 million artillery shells for Ukraine on markets outside the EU last year. The Czech Republic administered the effort on the condition that it would not contribute its own money.

Freedom and Direct Democracy, one of the junior coalition partners, has staked out a harder position: the party sees no future for the Czech Republic in the EU and NATO, and wants to expel most of the approximately 380,000 Ukrainian refugees currently in the country.

Motorists for Themselves, which controls the environment and foreign affairs ministries in the new Cabinet, has rejected the EU's Green Deal climate framework and proposed reviving the coal industry.
