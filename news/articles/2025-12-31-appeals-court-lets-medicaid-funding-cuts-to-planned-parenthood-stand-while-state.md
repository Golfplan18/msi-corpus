The ruling extends a string of adverse decisions for challengers to legislation President Donald Trump signed in July eliminating Medicaid reimbursements for abortion providers that received more than $800,000 in 2023 — a policy that has already forced a Maine clinic network serving the state's poorest and most rural communities to shut down primary care services.

A federal appeals court ruled Tuesday that the Trump administration can continue withholding Medicaid funding from Planned Parenthood and other health centers that provide abortions while a multistate lawsuit works through the courts.

The decision put on hold a preliminary injunction a federal district judge had issued in early December siding with a coalition of 21 states and the District of Columbia. The ruling follows a December 12 decision in which the same court overturned a separate injunction that had sided with Planned Parenthood — a development [MSI previously reported](/articles/2025-12-31-appeals-court-lets-medicaid-cuts-to-planned-parenthood-stand-during-suit).

The litigation centers on legislation President Donald Trump signed in July that eliminated Medicaid reimbursements for Planned Parenthood and other abortion providers that received more than $800,000 in 2023. Three lawsuits followed: two filed in Massachusetts, by Planned Parenthood and by the state coalition respectively, and a third filed in Maine by a network of clinics there.

"Although we are disappointed in the court's decision, we remain committed to holding the federal administration accountable and ensuring vulnerable Californians can access the health care they need," California Attorney General Rob Bonta's office said in a statement.

## Maine clinic network drops suit after shutting primary care

Maine Family Planning, which operates 18 clinics in poor and rural areas of the state, notified a federal court it was ending the Maine lawsuit, and the case was dismissed at the clinics' request. The network had already shut down its primary care operations in October after losing the federal funding.

"As the Trump administration has dismantled the nation's health care system, we have remained focused on delivering high quality care to our patients, particularly those across rural Maine," said George Hill, chief executive of Maine Family Planning. "Though our lawsuit will not continue, we are not wavering in our commitment to our patients and to advocating for the health care system that Mainers need and deserve."

The Center for Reproductive Rights, which represented Maine Family Planning, said clinic leaders chose to focus their resources on treating patients rather than continuing the legal challenge.

"The Trump administration on the other hand has no regard for patients or how the 'big beautiful bill' has made healthcare even harder to access in this country," the center said in a statement.

## States step in with direct funding

Planned Parenthood has said that nearly half its patients rely on Medicaid for health care aside from abortions — services already excluded from federal Medicaid coverage before the July legislation. At least eight states have directed state funds to compensate Planned Parenthood for the lost federal reimbursement.

Connecticut Gov. Ned Lamont recently announced $8.5 million for Planned Parenthood of Southern New England. Connecticut Attorney General William Wong called Tuesday's ruling a "disappointing setback" but said the legal process has a long way to go.

"This defunds essential, preventive healthcare, including cancer screenings, birth control, and testing and treatment of sexually transmitted infections," Wong said. "We will keep fighting to protect access to essential healthcare and to keep partisan politics out of doctors' offices."

The Department of Health and Human Services declined to comment on the litigation. Press secretary Emily Hilliard said the department "remains committed to protecting the integrity of Medicaid programs to ensure full compliance with the law."
