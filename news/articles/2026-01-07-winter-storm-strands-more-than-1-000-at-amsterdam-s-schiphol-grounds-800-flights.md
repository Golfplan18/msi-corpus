Snow and ice that pummeled large parts of Europe on Wednesday forced more than 1,000 stranded passengers to spend the night at Amsterdam's Schiphol Airport, as at least 800 flights were canceled at one of the continent's busiest aviation hubs and road and rail networks across the Netherlands and France buckled under fresh accumulation.

The cold snap disrupted travel from Scandinavia to western France, grounding more than 100 flights at Paris Charles de Gaulle airport and about 40 at Paris Orly, suspending bus service in the French capital, and prompting rail operators in multiple countries to warn of severe delays — while Berlin, separately, began restoring power to thousands of households that had gone without electricity for four days following a suspected far-left attack on high-voltage lines.

## Schiphol sets up field beds as hundreds of flights scrapped

Schiphol Airport, on the outskirts of Amsterdam, set up hundreds of field beds overnight and served breakfast to weary travelers as staff worked to clear snow from runways and deice airplanes, according to the Associated Press. At least 800 flights were canceled Wednesday at the hub.

National airline KLM said lines at the airport were decreasing, in part because passengers were warned on time that their flights were canceled and did not head to the airport.

Sonja Wurmlingel, trying to make her way home to Argentina, endured a cascade of cancellations. Her original flight via Düsseldorf was scrapped. She was rerouted through Paris — that flight was canceled, too. A train to Düsseldorf was the next option offered.

"I've come from the train and they told me there's no train," she said, adding after a long pause: "I don't know."

## Dutch roads and rails paralyzed

The Dutch road and rail networks were also hard hit. More than 700 kilometers (435 miles) of traffic jams clogged the country's roads as trucks slid across highways and slow-moving snow plows worked to clear lanes, the AP reported.

Rail operator NS urged travelers to "delay your journey if possible," warning that snow and icy temperatures had hit train travel harder than expected. Both domestic and international trains were affected, NS said.

## France bans trucks, halts Paris buses

France's national weather service, Météo France, placed large parts of northern and western France — including the Paris region — on alert for snow and black ice. French authorities advised people to work from home and avoid using cars. Trucks and school buses were banned from roads, and bus traffic in Paris was suspended Wednesday morning.

Transport Minister Philippe Tabarot said that more than 100 flights were canceled at Paris Charles de Gaulle airport and about 40 at Paris Orly.

French railway company SNCF warned passengers of disruptions and delays, saying that "snow on the tracks is forcing us to limit train speed and cancel trains or adjust traffic." Eurostar trains between Paris, London, and Brussels were also being delayed.

In Paris, a skier was seen sliding along the snowy bank of the Seine river, and roads and parks around the Eiffel Tower and the Louvre Museum were blanketed in snow.

## Berlin power restoration; Sweden and Finland also disrupted

There was some good news from Germany, where power was being restored to thousands of Berlin households that had been without electricity in freezing temperatures for four days. Authorities said the outage followed a suspected far-left attack on high-voltage lines.

Even countries accustomed to harsh winters reported problems. In the Swedish city of Göteborg, authorities took all of the city's trams out of service Wednesday morning, with broadcaster SVT reporting that snow banks churned up by passing trams posed a risk to other traffic.

In Finland, difficulties starting diesel buses that had been standing in the cold over the holidays and poor driving conditions led to cancellations and delays in the Helsinki area, Finnish broadcaster Yle reported.
