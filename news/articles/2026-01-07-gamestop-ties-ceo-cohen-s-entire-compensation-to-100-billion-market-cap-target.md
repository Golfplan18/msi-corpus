GameStop Corp. disclosed Wednesday in a regulatory filing that Chief Executive Ryan Cohen will receive no guaranteed pay, with his entire compensation package dependent on the company reaching a $100 billion market capitalization and $10 billion in cumulative earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.

The video game retailer said Cohen's award will fully vest only if both performance targets are met. "His compensation is entirely 'at-risk,' meaning he will only be paid if the company achieves significant market and operational goals," GameStop said in the filing. "This structure ensures that Mr. Cohen's incentives are directly aligned with creating long-term value for GameStop's stockholders."

The all-at-risk structure echoes a compensation arrangement Tesla shareholders approved for CEO Elon Musk, under which Musk would receive Tesla stock worth $1 trillion if he meets certain performance targets over the next decade, according to the Associated Press. GameStop — with a market capitalization of roughly $9.26 billion as of Wednesday — would need to multiply its value more than tenfold to trigger Cohen's full award.

## Package details

Cohen's package includes stock options allowing him to purchase more than 171.5 million common shares at $20.66 each. GameStop shareholders must approve the arrangement at a special meeting expected in March or April.

GameStop said the no-guaranteed-pay definition covers salary, cash bonuses, and stock that vests over time regardless of performance.

## Market reaction and context

GameStop shares rose 4% to $21.49 in midday trading Wednesday, according to the Associated Press.

The shares remain down substantially from May 2024, when Keith Gill — the influential investor known online as "Roaring Kitty" — appeared publicly for the first time in three years to declare his support for the retailer. Gill had helped ignite a meme stock craze in early 2021 that sent GameStop's share price above $120.
