European and Canadian leaders met Tuesday in Paris with U.S. envoys and senior European Union and NATO officials to agree on a framework of multilayered security guarantees for Ukraine after any ceasefire — the largest such gathering yet as the war approaches its fourth year. A joint statement pledged long-term military assistance and the possible deployment of a multinational force, but stopped short of binding commitments. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Wednesday he had not yet received a "clear, unambiguous answer" on whether allies would defend Ukraine if Russia attacked again after a peace deal.

The Paris summit marked a shift from planning contingencies toward specifying what post-ceasefire deterrence could look like, but key questions — including force sizes, financing, and command structures — remain unresolved, and any commitments would take effect only after a ceasefire and national approval processes in each participating country.

## The core commitment

The gathering drew 35 participants in person, including 27 heads of state or government, and marked the first time two U.S. envoys attended in person.

A joint statement said Ukraine's armed forces would remain "the first line of defense and deterrence," with allied partners committing to long-term military assistance and arms supplies even after fighting ends.

"We stand ready to commit to a system of politically and legally binding guarantees that will be activated once a ceasefire enters into force," the statement said.

French President Emmanuel Macron called it "a significant step" toward ending Russia's invasion. There was no immediate comment from Russian officials on the meeting.

## Zelenskyy's assessment

Zelenskyy said progress was made in the talks but stressed that commitments must still be ratified by individual countries before they can be implemented. He said countries had identified what forces were needed and how they would be operated, but that details of monitoring, force financing, and the size of Ukraine's army remained to be settled.

On Wednesday, asked whether Western European allies had agreed to come to Ukraine's defense if Russia attacked again after any peace deal, Zelenskyy said: "So far, I have not received a clear, unambiguous answer" to that question.

He also urged the United States to increase pressure on Moscow. "The main thing is for Ukraine to be a priority for (the U.S.), to find tools to pressure Russia," Zelenskyy said.

## Troop commitments and deployments

No immediate troop deployments were announced.

Macron said France was ready to send "several thousand" troops as part of a multinational force but said they would not be on the front line, positioning them instead "far away from the contact zone." He said Kyiv and Odesa were among the locations under consideration, and that at least 20 nations had pledged to deploy troops on land, at sea, or in the air.

British Prime Minister Keir Starmer said the United Kingdom and France "will establish military hubs across Ukraine and build protected facilities for weapons and military equipment to support Ukraine's defensive needs."

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Germany is "in principle not ruling anything out" regarding participation in security guarantees and suggested his country could station troops in countries bordering Ukraine.

Starmer said the talks had made "excellent progress" but cautioned that "the hardest yards are still ahead," noting that Russian attacks on Ukraine continue. "Putin is not showing that he is ready for peace," Starmer said.

## The U.S. role

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff said the United States "strongly stands behind" security guarantees, without detailing what Washington would provide militarily.

Jared Kushner, who also attended the meeting, said: "this does not mean we will make peace, but peace would not be possible without the progress that we have made today."

## Russia's position and remaining hurdles

Russia has insisted there can be no ceasefire without a comprehensive settlement and has ruled out NATO troop deployments on Ukrainian soil. Ukraine has warned that any ceasefire without firm guarantees could give Moscow time to regroup and attack again.

The size, structure, and financing of any supporting forces were not made public, and many elements remain to be negotiated. Any European troop deployments would face political hurdles and require approval from lawmakers in each participating country. Allies said they would participate in U.S.-led monitoring and verification of any ceasefire, though the details of how monitoring would work remain to be determined.
