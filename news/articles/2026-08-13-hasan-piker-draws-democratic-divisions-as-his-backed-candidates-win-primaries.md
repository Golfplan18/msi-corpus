## Hasan Piker draws Democratic divisions as his-backed candidates win primaries

**Subtype:** fact

- Hasan Piker, a 35-year-old left-wing livestreamer with 3.1 million Twitch followers, campaigned with progressive candidates in Michigan and Wisconsin in the run-up to recent primaries.
- Michigan Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, whom Piker supported, won a narrow Democratic primary victory over Rep. Haley Stevens; El-Sayed will face former Rep. Mike Rogers (R., Mich.) in November.
- Establishment Democrats including strategist James Carville and Sen. John Fetterman (D., Pa.) have criticized Piker's influence; Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D., N.Y.) and Sen. Bernie Sanders (I., Vt.) have aligned with him.
- Piker is barred from entering the United Kingdom; critics have accused him of antisemitism, citing remarks likening liberal Zionists to "liberal Nazis" and saying he would "vote for Hamas over Israel every time."
- After El-Sayed's primary win, the National Republican Senatorial Committee released an ad attacking El-Sayed for refusing to disavow Piker's remarks; El-Sayed called one of Piker's comments "dumb" but characterized related questions as "gotcha politics."

## Carville and Fetterman threatened to leave party over Piker's rise

Piker grew up in New Jersey and was raised in Turkey, in what he described as an affluent family. He graduated from Rutgers University in 2018, the same year he began livestreaming on Twitch. He previously worked for The Young Turks, the left-wing commentary site founded by his uncle Cenk Uygur, before going solo in 2020. In 2021, he purchased a West Hollywood home for $2.7 million. In a June interview with The Wall Street Journal, he was wearing a Denver Democratic Socialists of America hat and a T-shirt from the Palestinian Youth Movement while speaking from the home's recording studio, which he keeps stocked with soda and Zyn to power hours-long broadcasts.

In the weeks before the recent primaries, Piker campaigned with El-Sayed, taking selfies with students in Ann Arbor and attending a pool party for content creators in Detroit. He also traveled to Milwaukee to campaign for Wisconsin Democratic socialist Francesca Hong. El-Sayed's narrow victory over Stevens in the Michigan primary was a win for the populist left; Hong lost in Wisconsin.

Piker's role in those races has triggered disagreements within the Democratic Party. Carville and Fetterman have threatened to leave the party over his rise, though Carville later walked back his words. Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D., N.Y.) and Sen. Bernie Sanders (I., Vt.) have teamed up with him. When asked on a radio show about El-Sayed's decision to campaign with Piker, Stevens said, "I think that we should all be condemning some of the words of Hasan Piker."

Piker's commentary on Israel and its supporters has drawn accusations of antisemitism from critics. He has said that America "deserved 9/11," one of the remarks he said he could have phrased more carefully. He has likened liberal Zionists to "liberal Nazis" and said he would "vote for Hamas over Israel every time." He considers himself an anti-Zionist. He is currently barred from entering the United Kingdom because his presence "may not be conducive to the public good"; a spokesperson for the Home Office declined to comment on the status of his travel authorization.

In the Journal interview, Piker pushed back on the criticism. "There are people out there who speak about Jewish people from a very antisemitic perspective," Piker said. "Some of them are on the right, as a matter of fact. And those people do not get the same level of scrutiny that I do." He said he does not believe his comments could cause harm, even at a time when hostility toward Jews is rising globally.

After El-Sayed's primary win, the National Republican Senatorial Committee released an ad that seized on Piker's remarks and criticized El-Sayed for refusing to disavow them. Days later, El-Sayed called the 9/11 comment "dumb," though he also characterized questions about the quote as "gotcha politics." Sen. Rick Scott (R., Fla.) went further in an April letter to the editor of the Journal, calling Piker "an America-hating, terrorist supporting lunatic." "I stand by that," Scott said in an email to the paper. The two have a history: Piker once suggested that people who cared about Medicare or Medicaid fraud "would kill Rick Scott," referring to Scott's tenure as the onetime leader of a hospital chain that later settled with the government to resolve Medicare-fraud allegations. Twitch briefly suspended Piker's account after that remark.

Piker's policy positions include support for a wealth tax on billionaires, rent control, a free Palestinian state, and cuts to U.S. military spending in Israel. His appeal runs through a populist anti-establishment coalition that has grown louder in President Trump's second term. Among the candidates he has backed who have won, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani is the highest-profile. Piker rejects the label of kingmaker for socialist politicians but says he is on the front lines of a new vision for the left.

The November stakes in Michigan are high. El-Sayed will face Rogers, who has the backing of President Trump. Conventional wisdom among establishment Democrats has held that moderate candidates are the party's best shot at maintaining a blue stronghold, and many politicians and pundits have voiced doubts that El-Sayed can prevail. Piker disputes that logic. "I don't think that progressives have a harder time beating Republicans," he said. "I think that logic is flawed. It's just somewhat untested."

The broader question extends past 2026. "The Democrats never have and never will nominate a leftist candidate," Carville said in a phone interview. "So be very nice to people because you're going to have to be nice to them in the general." Piker framed the establishment's strategy as the problem. "I've told the Democrats over and over again, you can't podcast your way out of this problem," he said. "You have to change your policies. You have to change your outlook."
