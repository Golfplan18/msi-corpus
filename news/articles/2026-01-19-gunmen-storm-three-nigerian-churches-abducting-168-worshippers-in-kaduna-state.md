The mass abductions add to a sustained pattern of armed attacks in Nigeria's northern region, where gangs and armed groups have repeatedly struck remote communities with limited security coverage, drawing international attention and renewed dispute over how to characterize the country's escalating security crisis.

ABUJA, Nigeria — Gunmen attacked three churches simultaneously in the Kurmin Wali community of Kaduna state's Kajuru area in northwest Nigeria on Sunday, abducting at least 168 worshippers as services were underway, a state lawmaker told the Associated Press on Monday.

The attacks struck the Evangelical Church Winning All, a congregation belonging to the Cherubim and Seraphim denomination, and a Catholic church, said Usman Danlami Stingo, who represents the Kajuru area at the Kaduna state parliament.

"As of yesterday, 177 people were missing, and 11 came back. So we have 168 still missing," Stingo said.

Police in Kaduna state had not commented as of Monday. No group claimed responsibility for the attacks.

## Pattern of violence in Nigeria's north

Such attacks are common in Nigeria, Africa's most populous country, where multiple armed gangs and religious groups have struck remote communities in the northern region, where security forces and government services are limited. Kidnapping for ransom has grown into a widespread practice, with attacks on churches, schools, and villages occurring with frequency.

## International attention and disputed framing

Similar attacks on churches in recent months have prompted U.S. President Donald Trump and some American lawmakers to allege the persecution of Christians in Nigeria. On Dec. 25, the U.S. government launched military strikes in Sokoto, a state in the northwest, reportedly targeting an Islamic State affiliate operating in the region.

The Nigerian government has rejected descriptions of the country's security crises as a "Christian genocide," characterizing the violence as a broader security problem rather than religiously motivated persecution.
