The deaths at Maternity Hospital No. 1 in southwestern Siberia drew high-level condemnation from Russia's parliament and raised fresh questions about conditions in the country's regional medical facilities — at a time when Moscow has made reversing a declining birth rate a stated national priority.

Russian authorities opened a criminal investigation Tuesday into the deaths of nine newborns at a maternity hospital in the Siberian city of Novokuznetsk over the New Year's holiday period. Russia's Investigative Committee confirmed it had opened a case on charges of causing death by negligence. The hospital's chief physician has been suspended from his post while the probe is ongoing.

The deaths, concentrated at Maternity Hospital No. 1 in southwestern Siberia, drew high-level condemnation from Russia's parliament and raised fresh questions about conditions in the country's regional medical facilities — at a time when Moscow has made reversing a declining birth rate a stated national priority.

## What the health ministry said

The regional health ministry said that 234 children had been born at the hospital between Dec. 1 and Jan. 11, with 17 babies in serious condition in the intensive care unit as of Tuesday.

"Of the 17 infants in a critical condition, 16 of them were premature, including those with extremely low birth weight. All 17 had a severe intrauterine infection," the ministry said in a statement carried by Russian news agencies. "Unfortunately, nine children did not survive."

Most of the babies who died were premature, and all had been battling severe intrauterine infections — passed between mother and baby — according to the ministry.

## Suspension and investigation

Gov. Ilya Seredyuk announced Tuesday that Vitaly Kheraskov, the hospital's head doctor, had been suspended from his post while the investigation is ongoing.

Russia's Investigative Committee said it had opened a criminal case on charges of causing death by negligence. No further details about the investigation's targets or timeline were disclosed.

## Federal-level response

Valentina Matviyenko, speaker of Russia's upper house of parliament, called the deaths not only an irreparable loss for the families involved but a "tragedy for the state."

"All of the right lessons must be taken from this tragedy. Conclusions must be made at a federal level. It must never be repeated," she said in a statement.

Matviyenko's remarks noted that Russia has worked unsuccessfully in recent years to reverse its declining birth rate — a demographic trend the government has identified as a long-term national concern.
