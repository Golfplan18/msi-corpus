President Donald Trump's budget office this week ordered most federal agencies to compile data on federal money sent to 14 mostly Democratic-controlled states and the District of Columbia, according to a memo obtained by The Associated Press.

The Office of Management and Budget described the effort in the memo as a tool to "reduce the improper and fraudulent use of those funds." Agencies were instructed to submit the information by Jan. 28.

The 14 states are California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Illinois, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New Jersey, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont, Virginia and Washington. All but Minnesota are controlled by Democratic legislatures, and all but Vermont have Democratic governors. The District of Columbia is also included. The list includes all fully Democratic-controlled states except Hawaii, Maryland and New Mexico.

The memo did not explain why those states were selected. All but one — Virginia — were either included last year on the administration's list of sanctuary places or were home to at least one jurisdiction that was, according to AP. In Virginia, Democratic Gov. Abigail Spanberger's first act after taking office Saturday was to rescind a directive by former Republican Gov. Glenn Youngkin requiring law enforcement cooperation with immigration officials.

There is no strict definition for sanctuary policies or sanctuary cities, but the terms generally describe limited cooperation with U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

The order stops far short of suspending money. "This is a data-gathering exercise only," the memo said. "It does not involve withholding funds."

The request comes a week after Trump said he intended to cut off federal funding to states harboring sanctuary cities that resist his immigration policies, with the threatened action to begin Feb. 1.

Trump said at a White House news conference Tuesday — the same day the memo went to federal departments — that he still intended to cut off funding. "We're not going to pay them anymore. They are sanctuary for criminals," he said. "They can sue us and maybe they'll win, but we're not giving money to sanctuary cities anymore."

The memo asks for a swath of information about grants, loans and other federal funds provided to state and local governments in those states, as well as to institutions of higher education and nonprofits. Agencies are told not to report on money that goes directly to individuals, such as federal student aid.

The order applies to all federal departments and agencies except the Department of Defense, which the administration now refers to as the Department of War, and the Department of Veterans Affairs.

MSI previously reported that the same week the memo was issued, a federal judge ordered the Trump administration to keep child-care subsidies flowing in five states after the administration sought to freeze funding for child care and other social service programs citing fraud concerns. The targeted states in that action — California, Colorado, Illinois, Minnesota and New York — overlap with the list in the data-gathering memo.

The memo also does not include some states that are home to cities or counties on the administration's sanctuary list: Louisiana, Maryland, Michigan, New Mexico and Pennsylvania.
