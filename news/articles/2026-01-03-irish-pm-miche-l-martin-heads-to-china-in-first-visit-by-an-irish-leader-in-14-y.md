The visit is part of a pattern in which Beijing has been cultivating direct ties with individual European Union member states at a moment of broader friction between China and the EU over tariffs, electric vehicle duties, and human rights, with French President Emmanuel Macron and Spanish King Felipe VI both having met with Xi in separate trips in late 2025.

Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin will visit China beginning Sunday for a five-day trip that includes meetings with President Xi Jinping, Premier Li Qiang, and Zhao Leji, chairperson of the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress, China's Foreign Ministry announced Saturday. The trip marks the first visit by an Irish head of government to China in 14 years.

Martin will travel to both Beijing and Shanghai, the ministry said. Through the visit, China said it is ready to work with Ireland to expand cooperation and foster China-EU relations.

## China Courting EU Members Individually

Beijing has moved to strengthen ties with individual European Union member states even as the EU bloc and China remain at odds over tariffs and human rights. French President Emmanuel Macron and Spanish King Felipe VI both traveled to China separately in late 2025 and met with Xi.

The ministry announced Martin's visit, and Premier Li Qiang had extended the invitation to the Irish premier, the statement said.

## Trade Tensions in the Background

The diplomatic outreach comes against a backdrop of escalating trade friction between Beijing and Brussels. In December, China imposed provisional tariffs of up to 42.7% on EU dairy products including milk and cheese.

China has also initiated probes into European brandy and pork imports as countermeasures for the EU's tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles.

## South Korea Leader Also Arrives Sunday

China will separately host South Korean President Lee Jae-myung beginning Sunday for a four-day visit, during which he will meet with Xi — their second meeting in just two months, according to the Associated Press.

Lee said ahead of his trip that South Korea consistently respects the "One China" policy on Taiwan, in an interview with China's state broadcaster CCTV.

## Taiwan Tensions Shape Regional Context

Lee's visit comes at a moment of elevated regional tension over Taiwan. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said in November that Japan's military could get involved if China were to take action against Taiwan, which Beijing claims as its sovereign territory.

China conducted large-scale military drills around Taiwan for two days last week, which Beijing said were intended to warn against separatist and "external interference" forces.
