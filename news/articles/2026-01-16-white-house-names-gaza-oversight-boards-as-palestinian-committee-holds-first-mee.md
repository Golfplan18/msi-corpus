The White House on Friday named members of two international oversight boards for post-war Gaza governance as a Palestinian technocratic committee convened its first meeting in Cairo, advancing the U.S.-backed administrative framework for the territory following more than two years of fighting between Israel and Hamas.

The board appointments and committee launch represent the first concrete steps toward implementing the multilayered governance structure envisioned under Trump's plan, though major obstacles remain — including deploying an international security force and the difficult process of disarming Hamas.

## Palestinian committee convenes

The committee's leader, Ali Shaath, an engineer and former Palestinian Authority official from Gaza, pledged after the meeting to work quickly to improve conditions. He said reconstruction and recovery would take about three years and that he plans to focus first on immediate needs, including shelter.

"The Palestinian people were looking forward to this committee, its establishment and its work to rescue them," Shaath said in a television interview with Egypt's state-owned Al-Qahera News following the gathering.

Under Trump's plan, Shaath's technocratic committee will run day-to-day affairs in Gaza under the oversight of a Trump-led "Board of Peace," whose members have not yet been named, the White House said. The ceasefire that ended the conflict took effect Oct. 10. Israeli troops have since withdrawn from parts of Gaza, and thousands of displaced Palestinians have returned to what remains of their homes, the Associated Press reported.

## Two boards named

The White House said a separate executive board will work to carry out the vision of the Board of Peace. Its members include U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Trump envoy Steve Witkoff, Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, former British Prime Minister Tony Blair, Apollo Global Management CEO Marc Rowan, World Bank President Ajay Banga, and Trump's deputy national security adviser Robert Gabriel.

Nickolay Mladenov, a former Bulgarian politician and U.N. Mideast envoy, is to serve as the executive board's representative overseeing day-to-day matters, the White House said.

The White House also announced members of a second body, the "Gaza Executive Board," which will work with Mladenov, the technocratic committee, and an international stabilization force. In addition to Witkoff, Kushner, Blair, Rowan, and Mladenov, that board includes Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Qatari diplomat Ali Al-Thawadi, Hassan Rashad of Egypt's General Intelligence Agency, Emirati minister Reem Al-Hashimy, Israeli businessman Yakir Gabay, and Sigrid Kaag, the Netherlands' former deputy prime minister and a Mideast expert.

## West Bank killing

In the West Bank, friends and relatives gathered Friday to mourn Mohammad Na'san, a 14-year-old Palestinian boy killed by Israeli forces. The Palestinian Health Ministry confirmed his death and said he was the first child killed by the Israeli army in the occupied West Bank in 2026.

The village of al-Mughayyir, east of Ramallah, has become a recurring flashpoint. Residents said Israeli forces fired stun grenades and tear gas in an unprovoked attack. Israel's military said in a statement that the incursion came after Palestinians had hurled rocks at Israelis and set tires aflame.

"There was gunfire directed at citizens and farmers, the most dangerous of which occurred during the storming of the village as people were leaving the mosques. The streets were crowded with the elderly, children, women, and elders, and they began firing relentlessly," said Ameen Abu Aliya, head of the Al-Mughayyir village council.

Much of the community's agricultural land falls under Israeli military control. The AP reported that settlers and Israeli military bulldozers had destroyed olive groves in the area and that a children's park in the village was demolished.

According to the United Nations, 240 Palestinians — including 55 children — were killed by Israeli forces or settlers in the West Bank in 2025, while Palestinians killed 17 Israelis — including one child — in the region during the same period.

## Two children killed in Gaza

Also on Friday, a 7-year-old girl and a 16-year-old boy were killed in Beit Lahiya, Gaza, near the Yellow Line. Their bodies were taken to al-Shifa Hospital, the hospital said. No further details were immediately available.
