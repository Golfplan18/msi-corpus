Demonstrators gathered Saturday in Copenhagen in freezing temperatures wearing red baseball caps reading "Make America Go Away" — a spoof of Donald Trump's "Make America Great Again" slogan — to protest the U.S. president's stated ambitions to seize Greenland. Hundreds waved Danish and Greenlandic flags in front of Copenhagen City Hall and carried handmade signs mocking U.S. territorial claims over the autonomous Danish territory.

The caps have become a symbol of Danish and Greenlandic defiance as European governments rally behind Denmark against what demonstrators described as an unacceptable threat to Greenlandic sovereignty. Demand for the hats surged from a trickle to a weekend sellout after the Trump administration escalated its rhetoric over the island in recent weeks.

The mock hats were designed by Jesper Rabe Tonnesen, 58, owner of a Copenhagen vintage clothing store. Early batches sold poorly last year, but interest collapsed once the Trump administration moved beyond campaign-trail bluster.

"When a delegation from America went up to Greenland, we started to realize this probably wasn't a joke — it's not reality TV, it's actually reality," Tonnesen said. "So I said, OK, what can I do? Can I communicate in a funny way with a good message and unite the Danes to show that Danish people support the people of Greenland?"

Tonnesen said the caps sold out within a single weekend after demand spiked and that he has since ordered "several thousand" more. The original version featured a Danish wordplay: "Nu det NUUK!" — a twist on the phrase "Nu det nok," meaning "Now it's enough," with Nuuk, Greenland's capital, substituted in.

Several variants on the slogan have circulated on social media alongside the original. At Saturday's rally, protesters also carried signs reading "No Means No" and "Make America Smart Again."

Lars Hermansen, 76, a Copenhagen resident who wore one of the caps at the demonstration, said he attended to show solidarity with Greenland and to signal his opposition to the U.S. president. Kristian Boye, 49, said the gathering struck a lighthearted tone while conveying a serious message.

"I'm here to support the Greenlanders, who are going through a very hard time right now," Boye said. "They are being threatened with having their country invaded. I think it's totally unacceptable."

European governments have rallied behind Denmark, citing the need to defend Arctic regions and warning that U.S. threats against Greenland undermine Western security. Greenland, an autonomous Danish territory slightly larger than Saudi Arabia, has been the subject of repeated statements by Trump expressing a desire for the United States to acquire it.
