A U.S.-based activist agency said Sunday it has verified at least 3,919 deaths in Iran's crackdown on protests triggered by economic grievances, raising its count from a previous toll of 3,308. The Human Rights Activists News Agency also said 24,669 protesters have been arrested. The Associated Press said it was unable to independently confirm the figures.

The revised count came a day after Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei acknowledged for the first time that the protests had left "several thousand" dead, while attributing the casualties to the United States — marking the first admission from an Iranian leader of the scale of the casualties since unrest began Dec. 28.

The Human Rights Activists News Agency relies on a network of activists inside Iran to confirm all reported fatalities. It has tracked the death toll since the protests began Dec. 28 over the country's ailing economy. The death toll surpasses that of any other round of unrest in Iran in decades, according to the Associated Press. Iranian officials have not provided a clear figure.

## Khamenei and Trump trade accusations

On Saturday, Khamenei branded U.S. President Donald Trump a "criminal" for supporting the demonstrations and described protesters as "foot soldiers" of the United States. Trump, in an interview with Politico on Saturday, called for an end to Khamenei's nearly 40-year rule, calling him "a sick man who should run his country properly and stop killing people."

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, in a post Sunday on X, blamed "longstanding enmity and inhumane sanctions" imposed by the U.S. and its allies for hardships facing Iranians. "Any aggression against the Supreme Leader of our country is tantamount to all-out war against the Iranian nation," Pezeshkian wrote.

Trump had earlier told demonstrators that "help is on the way" and that his administration would "act accordingly" if the killing of protesters continued. He later struck a more measured tone, saying Iranian officials had "canceled the hanging of over 800 people" and that "I greatly respect the fact that they canceled." Iranian officials have repeatedly accused the United States and Israel of fomenting unrest in the country.

## Streets quiet; internet partially restored

No protests have been reported for days in Iran. On Saturday night, witnesses said some Iranians chanted anti-Khamenei slogans from the windows of their homes, with the chants reverberating through neighborhoods in Tehran, Shiraz, and Isfahan.

Authorities have blocked access to the internet since Jan. 8. Very limited services briefly functioned again Saturday. Access to some services, including Google, returned Sunday — though users said they could reach only domestic websites and email services remained blocked.

## One execution postponed

A close relative of detained protester Erfan Soltani said Sunday that the 26-year-old is in good physical health and was able to see family members after his planned execution was postponed. Somayeh, a 45-year-old relative living abroad who asked to be identified by first name only for fear of government reprisal, said the family had been told the execution would proceed on Wednesday but it was postponed when they arrived at the prison in Karaj, northwest of Tehran.

"I ask everyone to help in securing Erfan's freedom," Somayeh said in a video message.
