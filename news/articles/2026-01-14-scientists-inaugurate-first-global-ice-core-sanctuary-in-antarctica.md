Ice cores act as atmospheric time capsules, locking in gases, aerosols, pollutants and dust from past eras that scientists can analyze to reconstruct past climate conditions. Glaciers worldwide have lost between 2% and 39% of their ice regionally and about 5% globally since 2000, according to the Ice Memory Foundation, making the extraction and preservation of samples a race against time.

Scientists at the Concordia research station in the Antarctic Plateau inaugurated the world's first global repository of mountain ice cores on Wednesday, creating a frozen vault intended to preserve the Earth's atmospheric record as global warming eliminates glaciers around the world.

The Ice Memory Foundation, a European research consortium, opened the cave sanctuary at a constant temperature of around -52 degrees Celsius (-61 degrees Fahrenheit), deep inside a compacted snow drift five meters high. During the ceremony — livestreamed from the remote station — pairs of foundation team members carried box after box of ice cores into the cave for storage.

The first two sets of samples arrived from Mont Blanc in France and Grand Combin in Switzerland, completing a 50-day journey by refrigerated icebreaker and plane from Trieste, Italy.

"By safeguarding physical samples of atmospheric gases, aerosols, pollutants and dust trapped in ice layers, the Ice Memory Foundation ensures that future generations of researchers will be able to study past climate conditions using technologies that may not yet exist," said Carlo Barbante, vice chair of the Ice Memory Foundation and a professor at Ca' Foscari University in Venice.

## Why scientists race to preserve ice cores

Each layer of compressed glacier ice locks in a snapshot of the atmosphere from the period when snow fell: trapped gases, aerosols, volcanic ash, industrial pollutants and microscopic dust tell researchers what the Earth's climate looked like hundreds or thousands of years before modern instrumentation existed. As glaciers disappear, those layers vanish permanently.

Since 2000, glaciers worldwide have lost between 2% and 39% of their ice regionally and about 5% globally, according to the Ice Memory Foundation. Scientists have already identified and drilled ice cores at 10 glacier sites worldwide and plan to transport those samples to the Concordia cave sanctuary in coming years.

Celeste Saulo, secretary-general of the U.N. World Meteorological Organisation, said the samples are irreplaceable scientific tools.

"These ice cores are not relics … they are reference points," Saulo said. "They allow scientists now and in the future to understand what changed, how fast and why."

## About the Ice Memory Foundation

The Ice Memory project was launched in 2015 by a consortium of research institutes from three countries. From France: the National Centre for Scientific Research, the French National Research Institute for Sustainable Development, and the University of Grenoble-Alpes. From Italy: the National Council of Research and Ca' Foscari University in Venice. From Switzerland: the Paul Scherrer Institute.

Over the coming decade, the foundation said, it aims to craft an international convention to preserve and safeguard the samples for future generations to study.
