Trump's tariff announcement sets up a direct test of U.S. partnerships with eight core NATO allies over Arctic sovereignty, drawing coordinated condemnation from Copenhagen to Helsinki and a joint statement from all eight governments reaffirming their commitment to Danish and Greenlandic sovereignty.

COPENHAGEN, Denmark — Eight European governments on Sunday condemned President Donald Trump's announcement that they face 10% tariffs beginning next month in retaliation for opposing American control of Greenland, warning the measures threaten NATO unity and risk a "dangerous downward spiral" in transatlantic relations.

Denmark, Norway, Sweden, France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, and Finland are on Trump's list of targeted countries, according to the Associated Press. It was not immediately clear whether the tariffs would affect the European Union as a bloc.

Trump indicated the tariffs were retaliation for the deployment of troops from the eight European nations to Greenland. The European governments said those troops were sent in response to Trump's own call for strengthened Arctic security.

## Allied reactions

"Threats have no place among allies," Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre wrote on social media. "Norway's position is firm: Greenland is part of the Kingdom of Denmark. Norway fully supports the sovereignty of the Kingdom of Denmark. There is broad agreement in NATO on the need to strengthen security in the Arctic, including in Greenland."

Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson said the eight-country list understated the breadth of opposition. "We will not allow ourselves to be blackmailed," he wrote on social media. "I will always stand up for my country, and for our allied neighbors. This is an EU issue that concerns many more countries than those now being singled out."

French President Emmanuel Macron wrote that "No intimidation or threats will influence us, whether in Ukraine, Greenland or anywhere else in the world when we are faced with such situations." He added: "Tariff threats are unacceptable and have no place in this context."

British Prime Minister Keir Starmer said in a statement that Greenland's future "is a matter for the Greenlanders and the Danes" and that NATO allies should collectively address Arctic security. "Applying tariffs on allies for pursuing the collective security of NATO allies is completely wrong," Starmer said.

Dutch Foreign Minister David van Weel called the tariffs "inappropriate" in an interview Sunday. "As allies, I don't think this is how you should treat each other; not seek dialogue with each other, but try to put pressure on each other. So no, I'm very unhappy about this," van Weel said.

Finnish President Alexander Stubb, who has a noted personal rapport with Trump built around a shared interest in golf, wrote that "Among allies, issues are best resolved through discussion, not through pressure." He added: "Tariffs would undermine the transatlantic relationship and risk a dangerous downward spiral."

Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said Denmark has received broad international support. "At the same time, it has become even clearer that this is an issue that extends far beyond our own borders," Frederiksen said in a statement, adding it is "all the more important" that allies "stand firm on the fundamental values that created the European community. We want to cooperate, and we are not the ones seeking conflict."

German government spokesperson Stefan Kornelius said Berlin is coordinating its response with European partners. "The Federal Government has taken note of the statements made by the U.S. President," Kornelius wrote on social media. "It is in closest coordination with its European partners. Together, we will decide on appropriate responses at the appropriate time."

## Joint statement

The eight governments also issued a joint statement Sunday defending the troop deployment that triggered Trump's tariff threat.

"As members of NATO, we are committed to strengthening Arctic security as a shared transatlantic interest. The pre-coordinated Danish exercise 'Arctic Endurance,' conducted with Allies, responds to this necessity. It poses no threat to anyone," the statement read.

The governments said they "stand in full solidarity with the Kingdom of Denmark and the people of Greenland" and pledged continued coordination. "Tariff threats undermine transatlantic relations and risk a dangerous downward spiral. We will continue to stand united and coordinated in our response. We are committed to upholding our sovereignty," the joint statement said.
