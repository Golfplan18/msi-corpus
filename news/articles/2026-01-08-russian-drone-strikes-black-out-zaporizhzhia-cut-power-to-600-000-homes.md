KYIV — Russian drone strikes temporarily knocked out power to Ukraine's entire Zaporizhzhia region and left more than 600,000 households in the central Dnipropetrovsk region without electricity overnight Wednesday, Ukrainian officials said Thursday.

Emergency crews restored power to Zaporizhzhia within four hours — the first complete regional blackout since Russia's invasion nearly four years ago — but hundreds of thousands of Dnipropetrovsk residents remained without electricity Thursday afternoon, private energy company DTEK said.

The strikes were the latest in Russia's sustained campaign to disable Ukraine's power grid during winter, a strategy Ukrainian officials call "weaponizing winter."

"There is no military rationale for such strikes on energy facilities and infrastructure that leave people without electricity and heating in winter conditions," Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on social media.

Zelenskyy also called on allies to act. "It is important that our partners around the world respond to Russia's abuse of people," he said.

## Grid attack and regional impact

The Zaporizhzhia region, which before the war had a population of around 1.5 million, lost electricity entirely for the first time since Russia's invasion. DTEK said repairs in Zaporizhzhia were completed, while restoration work in Dnipropetrovsk continued into the afternoon.

Russia has repeatedly targeted Ukraine's electricity grid since the start of the war, seeking to deny civilians heat and running water during winter months.

Later Thursday, Zelenskyy posted that there was information suggesting "a new massive Russian attack may occur tonight" and urged residents to heed air alerts and take shelter in the coming days.

## Additional strikes

During the day Thursday, Russia launched two ballistic missiles on Kryvyi Rih, hitting apartment buildings and injuring 10 people, with one man in critical condition, according to Oleksandr Vikul, head of the regional military administration.

The Russian Defense Ministry said its air defenses downed 66 Ukrainian drones overnight over Russian regions, the illegally annexed Crimea, the Black Sea and the Azov Sea.

## Diplomatic backdrop

The bombardment came as U.S.-led diplomatic efforts to end the nearly four-year war continued. Ukraine and its Western allies have been working to establish a framework for a peace settlement, but Moscow has given no public sign it is willing to compromise.

Zelenskyy said Ukraine had presented U.S. officials with possible options for a peace settlement and that Washington would discuss them with Russia. "We're waiting for feedback on whether the enemy is truly ready to end the war," he said.

A postwar security guarantee agreement between the United States and Ukraine "is ready to be finalized," Zelenskyy said, indicating he could sign it alongside U.S. President Donald Trump. The agreement is designed to deter any future Russian attack, but would take effect only after a peace deal is reached. Ukraine, the United States and European countries are still discussing postwar reconstruction and development, Zelenskyy said.
