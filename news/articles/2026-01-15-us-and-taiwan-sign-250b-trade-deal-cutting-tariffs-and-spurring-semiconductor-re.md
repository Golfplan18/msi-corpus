The United States and Taiwan signed a trade agreement Thursday that secures $250 billion in Taiwanese tech investments in the United States in exchange for a reduction in tariffs on Taiwanese goods to 15%, the U.S. Department of Commerce announced. The deal cuts the rate the Trump administration had levied on Taiwanese imports — initially set at 32%, later lowered to 20% — to match the level applied to U.S. Asia-Pacific trading partners including Japan and South Korea.

The Commerce Department called the agreement "a historic trade deal that will drive a massive reshoring of America's semiconductor sector," a goal it said would be advanced through new U.S.-based industrial parks. The deal's announcement coincided with record quarterly earnings from Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Corp. and the company's disclosure of sharply higher capital spending plans centered on its Arizona fabrication plants.

## Deal terms

Under the agreement, the Commerce Department said it would establish several "world-class" U.S.-based industrial parks to expand domestic production capacity. Certain Taiwanese imports will be exempt from tariffs entirely, including generic pharmaceuticals and aircraft components. Taiwanese semiconductor producers that invest in the U.S. will receive additional favorable tariff treatment, including exemptions, the department said.

Taiwan's executive branch confirmed the deal's key provisions, saying its companies would invest $250 billion across semiconductors, artificial intelligence applications, and energy. The Taiwanese government said the "Taiwan model" would help expand the global competitiveness of the island's technology industry while deepening strategic cooperation between the two nations.

Beijing, which claims Taiwan as part of China, rejected the agreement. One day before the signing, Chinese officials called it "an economic plunder" by the United States on Taiwan.

## TSMC posts record profit, raises spending plans

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Corp., the world's largest computer chipmaker and a major supplier to Nvidia and Apple, reported net profit of 506 billion new Taiwan dollars ($16 billion) for the October-December quarter, a 35% surge from a year earlier that exceeded analysts' estimates. Revenue for the quarter rose 21% from a year earlier to more than 1.046 trillion new Taiwan dollars ($33 billion).

The company said it plans to raise its capital expenditure budget to $52 billion to $56 billion for 2026, up from approximately $40 billion last year — an increase of nearly 40%.

"We expect our business to be supported by continuous strong demand for our leading edge process technologies," Wendell Huang, TSMC's chief financial officer, said on a conference call. He added that spending would be "significantly higher" in the next three years.

TSMC has pledged approximately $165 billion in U.S. investments and said Thursday it was accelerating construction of new fabrication plants in Arizona, aiming to build a fabrication plant cluster to meet demand from clients.

TSMC chairman and CEO C.C. Wei pushed back on concerns about an AI investment bubble, saying he was confident demand from customers was real.

"I'm also very nervous about it, you bet," Wei said. "AI is real. Not only real, it's starting to grow into our daily life."

## Market position and AI backdrop

TSMC's Taiwan-listed shares have risen 59% over the past 12 months. With a market capitalization of approximately $1.4 trillion, TSMC is Asia's most valuable listed company.

Analysts from Morningstar said TSMC "is immune from market share shifts as almost every AI company relies on TSMC to make chips ranging from application-specific integrated circuits to GPUs," adding that the reliance "translates into strong pricing power." The analysts said TSMC also carries a "robust backlog from deep-pocketed customers" that buffers against any short-term dips in demand.

Microsoft, Meta, and Alphabet have all announced significant capital commitments to AI infrastructure. Alphabet passed the $4 trillion market capitalization mark this month, the fourth major technology company to reach that threshold after Nvidia, Apple, and Microsoft.

The US-Taiwan deal is one of several trade agreements the Trump administration has reached since announcing a broad tariff plan in April 2025 to address trade imbalances. The administration also maintains a one-year trade truce with China.
