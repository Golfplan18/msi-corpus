## New data shows ACA enrollment fell broadly after subsidies expired

- States across the country saw steep drops in the number of people covered by the Affordable Care Act over the past year, with around 2.6 million fewer Americans holding ACA plans in February 2026 compared with a year earlier, according to new federal data.
- Ohio and Oklahoma each lost nearly one-third of their ACA enrollees, according to the new data.
- The new figures provide the first complete 50-state breakdown of enrollment declines following the expiration of enhanced premium subsidies.
- The data was posted in late June by the Trump administration and first reported by The Associated Press.

## Ohio, Oklahoma lost nearly one-third of enrollees, data shows

The data, posted in late June by the Trump administration and first reported by The Associated Press, provides the first complete 50-state breakdown of enrollment declines since the enhanced subsidies expired in January.

The figures show that around 2.6 million fewer Americans had ACA plans in February 2026 compared with the same time last year. Ohio and Oklahoma each lost nearly one-third of their ACA enrollees.
