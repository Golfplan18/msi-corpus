The inauguration completes Doumbouya's formal transition from junta chief to elected head of state in one of West Africa's poorest nations, where critics say he suppressed opposition before a vote designed to entrench his rule, while supporters point to a Chinese-led iron ore mega-project as the engine of an economic turnaround.

Mamadi Doumbouya, who seized power in Guinea in a 2021 military coup, was sworn in Saturday as the country's president before an audience of African leaders and continental organization representatives at a new 55,000-seat stadium in the suburbs of Conakry, the capital.

Doumbouya won Guinea's December presidential election under a newly adopted constitution that removed a ban on military officers seeking the presidency and extended presidential terms from five years to seven. Critics accused him of suppressing opposition and dissent in the lead-up to the vote.

"I fully appreciate the immense responsibility that the people of Guinea have entrusted to me following the presidential election," Doumbouya said at the ceremony. "This mandate that has just been given to me is not a personal honor; it is a commitment to the Guinean people. A commitment to address the various governance challenges facing our country."

Representatives of the African Union Commission and the Economic Community of West African States Commission attended alongside African heads of state.

## An economy awaiting transformation

Guinea is rich in mineral resources, but half of its roughly 15 million people live in poverty and face record levels of food insecurity, according to the World Food Program.

The Simandou iron ore project — a 75-percent Chinese-owned development at what is considered the world's largest iron ore deposit — has been the junta's central economic priority. Production at the site began late last year after decades of delay. Authorities have said the project is expected to create thousands of jobs and spur investment in education and health.

## Supporters and skeptics

Among those at the ceremony, Rokiatou Kaba, a 28-year-old law student from Kankan Prefecture — Doumbouya's hometown — said the inauguration signaled a new chapter for the country.

"Guinea is fully back on the international stage. Economic takeoff is imminent, prosperity is guaranteed," Kaba told the Associated Press.

Not everyone shared that view. Hassmiou Baldé, a 26-year-old economics student who attended the ceremony, described what he saw as a hollow exercise.

"This is all just theater," Baldé said. "After driving out all the real opposition, he surrounded himself with minor, unknown rivals." He added: "It's a charade. A power grab."

## A regional pattern

Guinea is among several West African countries to have experienced a coup or coup attempt since 2020, as military officers have moved to capitalize on popular frustration over deteriorating security, struggling economies, and disputed elections.
