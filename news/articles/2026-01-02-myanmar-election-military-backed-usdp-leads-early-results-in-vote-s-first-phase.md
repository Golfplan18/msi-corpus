## Early vote results show USDP leading, election body begins publishing winners

Myanmar’s military-appointed election body has begun announcing winners from the first phase of the country’s three-part general election, AP reported Saturday, saying the military-backed Union Solidarity and Development Party, or USDP, had won the majority of seats as widely expected.

The announcement followed balloting on Dec. 28 in 102 townships, the first round of the election conducted in multiple phases. In a statement described by AP, the military government said more than 6 million people—about 52% of more than 11 million eligible voters in the first phase—cast ballots.

## UEC seat totals in Pyithu Hluttaw and other parties’ results

AP reported that the Union Election Commission, or UEC, announced in the state-run Myanma Alinn newspaper on Saturday that the USDP won 38 seats in the 330-seat Pyithu Hluttaw lower house. The report said that many seats from the Dec. 28 election were still awaiting declaration, indicating the early figures were partial.

A separate announcement named USDP leader Khin Yi as the winning representative from his constituency in the capital, Naypyitaw. AP said the announcement reported he won 49,006 of the 68,681 votes cast.

The UEC statement also listed the Shan Nationalities Democratic Party and the Mon Unity Party as winning one seat each, according to AP.

## USDP officials say the party won most contested seats in the first phase

While the published results were described as partial, AP reported that USDP leaders expressed confidence in the first phase. A senior USDP official told AP on Saturday that the party had won 88 seats out of 102 contested in the first phase.

AP reported the official said the party ran unopposed in 29 constituencies, facing no challengers or rivals. The official also told AP that the USDP won 85% of contested seats in regional legislatures, with AP saying complete results would only be known after later phases.

AP reported that the official spoke on condition of anonymity because he was not authorized to release information.

## Election schedule shaped by fighting

Myanmar’s election is being held in three phases due to ongoing armed conflict. AP reported the first round took place on Dec. 28 in 102 townships, nearly a third of the country’s 330 townships.

AP reported that the remaining phases were scheduled for Jan. 11 and Jan. 25, and that 65 townships would not participate because of fighting.

## Political context: military role in legislature and president selection

Myanmar has a two-house national legislature totaling 664 seats, AP reported. Under the constitution, AP said the military automatically receives 25% of seats in each house.

AP also reported that the party with a combined parliamentary majority can select the new president, name a Cabinet, and form a new government.

AP said more than 4,800 candidates from 57 parties are competing for seats in national and regional legislatures, but that only six parties were competing nationwide with the possibility of gaining political clout in parliament, with the USDP described as the strongest contender.

## Critics say the system lacks fairness; opposition groups urged a boycott

AP reported that critics of Myanmar’s current system said the election was designed to add a facade of legitimacy to the status quo. They also charged that the polls were neither free nor fair, citing the exclusion of major parties and government repression of dissenters, and they said opposition groups had called for voters to boycott the election.

AP described how military rule began in February 2021 when soldiers ousted the elected government of Aung San Suu Kyi. It said the military blocked her National League for Democracy party, or NLD, from serving a second term despite winning the 2020 election.

AP further reported that the NLD was dissolved in 2023 along with 39 other parties after refusing to register under new military rules, and it said the takeover triggered widespread popular opposition that has grown into a civil war.
