Thousands of Yemenis gathered Saturday in the southern city of Aden to reject the dissolution of the Southern Transitional Council, a UAE-backed separatist organization that announced its own disbandment a day earlier following clashes with Yemen's internationally recognized government. Supporters of the STC assembled in the Khor Maksar district — home to Aden's international airport and a stronghold of the separatist movement — where armed groups loyal to the council secured the protest area, according to an Associated Press journalist at the scene.

Thousands of demonstrators gathered Saturday in the southern Yemeni city of Aden to reject the dissolution of the Southern Transitional Council, a UAE-backed separatist organization that announced its own disbandment a day earlier following months of clashes with Yemen's internationally recognized government.

Supporters of the STC assembled in the Khor Maksar district — home to Aden's international airport and a longstanding stronghold of the separatist movement — where armed groups loyal to the council secured the protest area, according to an Associated Press journalist present at the scene. Demonstrators waved flags of southern Yemen and held posters of STC leader Aidarous al-Zubaidi, who fled Aden to the United Arab Emirates earlier this month.

## A fractured movement

The protest exposed a sharp internal dispute over the STC's fate. On Friday, STC Secretary-General Abdulrahman Jalal al-Sebaihi announced the council would shut down all of its bodies and offices inside and outside Yemen, citing internal disagreements and mounting regional pressure from neighboring Gulf states. But STC spokesman Anwar al-Tamimi quickly contested that decision, writing on X that only the full council, under its president, could authorize such steps — an assertion that highlighted the depth of divisions within the separatist movement.

Protesters chanted slogans against Saudi Arabia and Yemen's internationally recognized government and demanded the reestablishment of an independent state in the south. Fadl Mahomed, one of the demonstrators who spoke to AP, said he attended to show solidarity with the STC and its exiled leader.

"We will remain in the squares until the restoration of the state of South Arabia," Mahomed said, referring to the People's Democratic Republic of Yemen, which existed as an independent state from 1967 until its 1990 unification with northern Yemen.

The protest's organizers issued a statement rejecting the dissolution and calling for an independent southern Yemeni state.

## Government calls for dialogue

Former Yemeni Foreign Minister Abdel-Malek al-Mekhlafi, now an adviser to the chairman of the presidential council, offered a sharply different assessment Saturday, calling for dialogue to reintegrate those not implicated in crimes into Yemen's military and security agencies.

"The page of the transitional council has been turned," al-Mekhlafi wrote on X, "because of its mistakes, corruption, arrogance and its use of force against the state and people of the south."

## Saudi-UAE partnership collapses

Saturday's unrest is the latest rupture in Yemen's decade-long civil war. Houthi rebels backed by Iran seized the capital, Sanaa, in 2014, forcing the internationally recognized government into exile — first southward, then to Saudi Arabia. A Saudi-led coalition that included the UAE entered the conflict the following year in an effort to restore the government.

Over the years, the coalition's internal cohesion frayed. Tensions between Riyadh and Abu Dhabi erupted last month when UAE-backed forces seized control of Hadramout and Mahra provinces — including oil-rich areas on Saudi Arabia's border — and took over the presidential palace in Aden. Saudi-backed government forces subsequently pushed the separatists out of those positions.

Established in April 2017, the STC served as an umbrella organization for groups seeking restoration of an independent southern Yemen and drew financial and military backing from the UAE. Al-Zubaidi, who also held a seat on Yemen's ruling Presidential Transitional Council, reportedly declined to attend de-escalation talks in Riyadh before being transported to Abu Dhabi through Somalia, according to Saudi Arabia. An STC delegation did attend those talks last week before announcing the council's dissolution.
