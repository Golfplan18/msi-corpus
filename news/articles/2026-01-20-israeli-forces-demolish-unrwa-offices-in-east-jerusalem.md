Israeli forces on Tuesday bulldozed the headquarters of the United Nations Relief and Works Agency in east Jerusalem's Sheikh Jarrah neighborhood and fired tear gas at a vocational school serving Palestinian refugee youth, marking Israel's most dramatic escalation against the agency that provides humanitarian services to millions across the region.

The demolitions represent the culmination of a yearlong Israeli campaign against UNRWA following a law banning the agency, and precede a broader effort to deregister aid organizations operating in Gaza and the occupied West Bank.

Israeli forces confiscated equipment and ordered out private security guards from the compound. The facility in east Jerusalem had not operated for nearly a year due to safety concerns. Roland Friedrich, UNRWA's West Bank director, said the escalation had been building for years. "What we saw today is the culmination of two years of incitement and measures against UNRWA in east Jerusalem," Friedrich said.

The vocational school, which sits on the outskirts of Jerusalem in Qalandia, remained intact but was subjected to tear gas firing on Tuesday afternoon. The facility provides job training in technology and welding to more than 300 young Palestinian refugees. Some children heading home were overcome by the gas. A 15-year-old was struck in the eye with a rubber bullet, according to the Palestinian Authority's Jerusalem governorate.

## Israeli law banning UNRWA takes physical form

Israel's Foreign Ministry said the demolition enforced a new law that bans UNRWA and severs ties with the agency. The ministry noted that Israel owns the property and rejected claims that the action violated international law.

Israeli political leaders gathered at the demolished site to celebrate. National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir called it "a historic day." An Israeli flag was hoisted above the ruins.

The law, passed by Israel's Knesset in 2025, had restricted UNRWA operations, closing facilities and leaving the agency's headquarters undefended. The demolition on Tuesday marks the most dramatic enforcement of the ban to date.

## Israel's accusations and UNRWA's response

Israel has long accused UNRWA of infiltration by Hamas and asserted that some of the agency's employees participated in the October 2023 attack that triggered Israel's two-year war in Gaza. Israel has provided limited public evidence for the claims, which the United Nations has denied.

UNRWA leaders have said they acted swiftly to remove employees accused of involvement in the 2023 attack. The agency has also denied allegations that it tolerates or collaborates with Hamas.

## International condemnation and broader warnings

U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres condemned the destruction on Tuesday. Deputy U.N. Spokesperson Farhan Haq said the Secretary-General views "the continued escalatory actions against UNRWA" as "wholly unacceptable" and "inconsistent with Israel's clear obligations under international law."

Philippe Lazzarini, UNRWA's commissioner-general, issued a broader warning about the precedent set by the demolition. "This must be a wake-up call," Lazzarini said. "What happens today to UNRWA will happen tomorrow to any other international organisation or diplomatic mission, whether in the Occupied Palestinian Territory or anywhere around the world."

## UNRWA's reach and the widening aid crackdown

UNRWA is mandated to provide aid and services to some 2.5 million Palestinian refugees in Gaza, the occupied West Bank, and east Jerusalem. The agency also serves approximately 3 million additional refugees in Syria, Jordan, and Lebanon. For decades, UNRWA has operated schools, provided health care, and maintained essential infrastructure across refugee camps in the region.

The agency's operations have been curtailed progressively since Israel's Knesset passed legislation banning it in 2025. The International Court of Justice ruled in October that Israel must allow UNRWA to deliver humanitarian assistance in Gaza—a decision that the latest demolition appears to disregard.

The targeting of UNRWA is part of a wider Israeli effort to restrict international aid groups. Israel has enacted laws requiring nongovernmental organizations to refrain from activities that "delegitimize Israel" or support boycotts, and demanding they register staff lists as a condition of continued operation.

Israel has informed dozens of humanitarian organizations—including Doctors Without Borders and CARE—that their operating licenses would expire at the end of 2025. The organizations have said the restrictions are arbitrary and have warned that the bans would harm people in desperate need of humanitarian assistance across Gaza and the occupied West Bank.

The Israeli military reported that attacks by Jewish settlers against Palestinians and Israeli security forces in the West Bank increased 27 percent in 2025 compared with 2024. Severe incidents rose by more than 50 percent, according to internal statistics from the Israeli military and the country's Shin Bet domestic security service.

The rising violence has emptied Palestinian villages since the outbreak of the Israel-Hamas war, according to B'Tselem, an Israeli rights organization assisting affected residents. Israeli authorities maintain a mixed relationship with settlers—at times dismantling unauthorized outposts while also deploying forces to protect them from Palestinians.
