## Ex-Bucknell coach charged in football player's collapse death

**Subtype:** fact

- Former Bucknell University strength and conditioning coach Mark Kulbis was charged Monday with felony aggravated hazing, as well as misdemeanor involuntary manslaughter, reckless endangerment, and hazing, in the 2024 death of freshman football player Calvin "CJ" Dickey Jr., according to the Pennsylvania Attorney General's office.
- Dickey collapsed during a team workout after Kulbis ordered players to perform conditioning drills including "up-downs" and plank holds, prosecutors said.
- Dickey had sickle-cell trait, a medical condition that can increase the risk of serious injury or death following extreme exertion, according to the attorney general's office.
- Kulbis was the only coach in the training room when Dickey passed out, officials said; the freshman died two days later.

## Freshman had sickle-cell trait, prosecutors say

The Pennsylvania Attorney General's office announced the charges Monday, alleging that Kulbis directed the drills during a 2024 training session. According to prosecutors, Kulbis told Dickey and other players to complete "up-downs" — a conditioning exercise that involves dropping to the ground and springing back up — along with plank holds.

Dickey, who had been recruited to play lineman positions at the university in Pennsylvania, began struggling during the exercises and lost consciousness, the attorney general's office said. Kulbis was the only coach present in the training room at the time, according to investigators.

The freshman was taken to a hospital, where he died two days later.
