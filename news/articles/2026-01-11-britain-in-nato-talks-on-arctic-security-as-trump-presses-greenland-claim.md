The consultations come as tensions mount between Washington and Copenhagen over Trump's repeated assertions that the U.S. must control Greenland, a semiautonomous Danish territory of about 57,000 people where the U.S. already operates a military base and which NATO ally Denmark has defended.

LONDON — Britain said Sunday it is in discussions with NATO allies about strengthening Arctic security to counter Russia and China, as U.S. President Donald Trump renewed his vow to acquire Greenland.

"One way or the other, we're going to have Greenland," Trump told reporters aboard Air Force One on Sunday as he flew back to Washington. He said making a deal for the island would be "easier" and insisted the U.S. needed to acquire it.

UK Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander said the NATO consultations were "business as usual" rather than a direct response to Trump's statements. "Whilst we haven't seen the appalling consequences in that part of the world that we've seen in Ukraine, it is really important that we do everything that we can with all of our NATO allies to ensure that we have an effective deterrent in that part of the globe against Putin," Alexander told the BBC.

Greenland is a semiautonomous region of NATO ally Denmark with a population of about 57,000. The U.S. already operates a military base on the island. Denmark's prime minister has warned that a U.S. takeover would threaten the alliance.

Trump dismissed that concern Sunday. "I like NATO," he said, before questioning "whether or not, if we needed NATO, would they be there for us? I'm not sure they would." NATO's Article 5 mutual defense guarantee, which treats an attack on one member as an attack on all, has been invoked once — following the September 11, 2001 attacks on the United States, which led to NATO's operation in Afghanistan.

Tensions have grown between Washington and Copenhagen as the Trump administration has renewed its Greenland warnings. Denmark's ambassador to the U.S., Jesper Møller Sørensen, pushed back after U.S. Greenland envoy Jeff Landry posted on social media that the "U.S. defended Greenland's sovereignty during [World War II] when Denmark couldn't." Sørensen said Denmark has always stood alongside the U.S. and that only Greenlanders should decide their future. "Let's continue to address security challenges in the Arctic as partners & allies," Sørensen wrote. Danish officials are scheduled to meet with Secretary of State Marco Rubio this week.

Peter Mandelson, Britain's former ambassador to the United States, said he did not expect Trump to take Greenland by force. "He's not a fool," Mandelson said. "We are all going to have to wake up to the reality that the Arctic needs securing against China and Russia. And if you ask me who is going to lead in that effort to secure, we all know, don't we, that it's going to be the United States."

Ed Davey, leader of the Liberal Democrat Party, proposed that Britain offer to deploy troops to Greenland in a joint command with Denmark. "If Trump is serious about security, he'd agree to participate and drop his outrageous threats," Davey said. "Tearing the NATO alliance apart would only play into the hands of Putin."

It remained unclear how other NATO members would respond if the U.S. moved to forcibly take control of Greenland, or whether they would come to Denmark's defense under the alliance's collective commitments.
