Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán accused Ukraine on Monday of seeking to interfere in elections scheduled for April 12 and ordered Kyiv's ambassador to be summoned to the foreign ministry. The move marks the latest escalation in Orbán's campaign warning voters that Ukraine poses an existential threat to Hungary's security and sovereignty. With his right-wing Fidesz party trailing in most polls ahead of the elections, Orbán has promoted the unsubstantiated claim that Hungarians would be forcibly conscripted to fight in Ukraine if his party loses power.

In a video posted to social media on January 26, Prime Minister Viktor Orbán said Ukraine's political leaders, including President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, "made grossly offensive and threatening statements against Hungary and the Hungarian government." Orbán did not specify which statements he was referring to.

"Our national security services have evaluated this latest Ukrainian attack and determined that what happened is part of a coordinated series of Ukrainian measures to interfere in the Hungarian elections," Orbán said, adding he had instructed the foreign minister to summon Ukraine's ambassador.

## The Anti-Ukraine Campaign

Orbán, who has held power for 16 years, has intensified his anti-Ukraine campaign as the elections approach. He has accused his leading rival, opposition leader Péter Magyar, of entering into a pact with Kyiv to overthrow his government and establish a pro-Western administration—claims made without providing evidence.

## Zelenskyy's Response

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy responded at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland in January, criticizing Orbán. "He lives off European money while trying to sell out European interests," Zelenskyy said. "If he feels comfortable in Moscow, it doesn't mean we should let European capitals become little Moscows."

## Hungary's Opposition to EU Aid

Hungary's government has strongly opposed European Union financial and military aid for Ukraine and has vowed to veto any EU steps toward Ukrainian accession to the bloc. In January, Orbán's government launched a "national petition" urging voters to sign in opposition to continued EU financial support for Kyiv.

The diplomatic confrontation underscores the sharp divide between Hungary and most of the European Union over support for Ukraine, and reflects Orbán's strategy of positioning Ukraine as a political threat to Hungarian voters amid his reelection bid.
