Zia's death closes a political chapter spanning more than 41 years. Her Bangladesh Nationalist Party, whose acting head is her exiled son Tarique Rahman, is the front-runner in national elections scheduled for February.

Hundreds of thousands of mourners filled Dhaka's Manik Mia Avenue outside the national parliament building Wednesday for funeral prayers for former Prime Minister Khaleda Zia, who died at 80 after a prolonged illness. Representatives from 32 countries attended the ceremony, according to local media reports cited by the Associated Press. Zia was buried in the late afternoon with state honors beside the grave of her husband, a former president assassinated in a military coup in 1981.

As MSI previously reported on the scale of the public outpouring that gathered in Dhaka as the prayers began, <a href="/articles/2025-12-31-hundreds-of-thousands-mourn-khaleda-zia-at-dhaka-funeral-prayers">mourners traveled overnight from rural areas across Bangladesh</a>, with crowds spilling into major streets kilometers from the venue to join prayers.

Bangladesh's interim government, headed by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Muhammad Yunus, declared a three-day national mourning period and designated Wednesday a public holiday. Flags flew at half-staff nationwide.

## Dignitaries from across South Asia

India's External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar traveled to Dhaka, met Zia's elder son Tarique Rahman, and delivered a personal letter from Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Pakistan's National Assembly Speaker Sardar Ayaz Sadiq, Nepal's Foreign Minister Bala Nanda Sharma, and Bhutan's Minister for Foreign Affairs and External Trade Lyonpo D. N. Dhungyel also attended the ceremony.

Soldiers formed an honor guard and saluted as Zia's family members stood nearby during the burial. Her coffin, draped in Bangladesh's national flag, was transported by van from the hospital to her residence and then to the funeral venue. Authorities said about 10,000 security officials, including soldiers, were deployed around the venue to maintain order.

## Four decades in politics

Zia came to politics after her husband's assassination and rose as an opposition leader during a nine-year movement against a former military dictator who was ousted in a mass uprising in 1990. She became prime minister for the first time in 1991 after a landslide victory in the country's first parliamentary election under the restored democratic system — the first woman to hold the office — and went on to serve two full terms and a brief third term. She led the Bangladesh Nationalist Party for more than 41 years, until her death.

Her political career was defined by a long rivalry with former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, who headed the Bangladesh Awami League and ruled Bangladesh for 15 years before being ousted in a mass uprising in August 2024. Hasina has been in exile in India since Aug. 5, 2024. She was sentenced to death in November on charges of crimes against humanity related to last year's uprising, according to the Associated Press.

Zia's son Tarique Rahman serves as acting head of the Bangladesh Nationalist Party. The party is the front-runner in national elections scheduled for February, according to the Associated Press.
