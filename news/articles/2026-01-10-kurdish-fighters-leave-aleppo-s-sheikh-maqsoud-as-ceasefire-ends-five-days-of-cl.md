The evacuation resolves the immediate standoff in Aleppo but leaves unresolved the core dispute over integrating Kurdish forces into Syria's national army — the disagreement that triggered the bloodshed and that U.S. Special Envoy Tom Barrack warned risks "undermining the progress achieved since the fall of the Assad regime."

ALEPPO, Syria — Kurdish fighters completed their evacuation from a contested neighborhood in Syria's northern city of Aleppo early Sunday under an internationally mediated ceasefire agreement, officials said, ending five days of violent clashes that killed at least 22 people and displaced more than 140,000.

State-run news agency SANA reported buses transported the last of the fighters from the Sheikh Maqsoud neighborhood to northeastern Syria, under the control of the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces. An Associated Press journalist at the scene confirmed buses leaving Sunday; officials said the transports carried 360 fighters. Civilians and detained fighters had departed in earlier transports on Saturday.

SDF commander Mazloum Abdi announced the agreement Sunday. "Through international mediation to halt the attacks and violations against our people in Aleppo, we have reached an understanding leading to a ceasefire and the safe evacuation of martyrs, the wounded, trapped civilians, and fighters from the Achrafieh and Sheikh Maqsoud neighborhoods to northern and eastern Syria," Abdi said in a post on X. He called on "mediators to uphold their promises to stop the violations and work towards the safe return of the displaced to their homes."

## Origins of the clashes

Fighting broke out Tuesday in the predominantly Kurdish neighborhoods of Sheikh Maqsoud, Achrafieh, and Bani Zaid after the Syrian government and the SDF failed to advance talks on how to merge Kurdish forces into the national army. Syrian security forces subsequently captured Achrafieh and Bani Zaid and declared Sheikh Maqsoud a "closed military zone" on Friday night as they launched what they described as a "clearing operation."

Kurdish forces said at least 12 civilians were killed in the Kurdish-majority neighborhoods during the five days of clashes. Government officials reported at least 10 civilians killed in surrounding government-controlled areas. The fighting was described as the most intense between the two sides since the fall of then-President Bashar Assad in December 2024.

## Drone strikes and airport closure

Several drone strikes were reported across Aleppo on Saturday. An explosive drone struck the Aleppo Governorate building shortly after two Cabinet ministers and a local official held a news conference there on developments in the city; there was no immediate word on casualties. Syria's state TV aired footage of the impact and attributed the strike to Kurdish fighters. The SDF denied the report, saying its fighters did not attack a civilian target.

Aleppo International Airport halted civilian flights until further notice following the drone activity, state TV said.

## U.S. envoy calls for restraint

U.S. Special Envoy to Syria Tom Barrack held talks in Damascus on Saturday with top officials, including President Ahmad al-Sharaa, urging all parties to end the fighting.

"Violence risks undermining the progress achieved since the fall of the Assad regime and invites external interference that serves no party's interests," Barrack said in comments posted on X. "We urge all parties to exercise maximum restraint, immediately cease hostilities, and return to dialogue," he said, adding that the clashes undermine a deal reached in March between the government and the Kurdish leadership. Barrack said Washington's objective "remains a sovereign, unified Syria — at peace with itself and its neighbors — where equality, justice, and opportunity are extended to all its people."

Barrack had also discussed developments on Friday in Amman with Jordan's Foreign Minister Ayman Safadi; Safadi indicated Jordan was prepared to support efforts to consolidate the ceasefire, Barrack said.

## Disputed conduct allegations

Each side accused the other of starting the violence and of deliberately targeting civilian neighborhoods and infrastructure, including ambulance crews and hospitals. Syria's Information Minister Hamza al-Mustafa said Kurdish fighters used civilian buildings including hospitals and clinics during the fighting. The Kurdish-led Democratic Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria said security forces targeted Khaled Fajr Hospital in Sheikh Maqsoud, endangering patients and paramedics, and called on the international community to force government forces to halt the shelling. Neither claim was independently verified.
