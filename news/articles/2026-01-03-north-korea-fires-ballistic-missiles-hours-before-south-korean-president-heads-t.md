SEOUL — North Korea launched multiple ballistic missiles toward its eastern waters early Sunday, South Korea's military said, hours before South Korean President Lee Jae Myung departed for Beijing to seek China's help restarting long-stalled diplomatic talks with Pyongyang.

South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff said it detected several launches from North Korea's capital region at approximately 7:50 a.m. local time. The missiles flew about 900 kilometers, or 560 miles, before South Korean and U.S. authorities began analyzing the details, the military said.

The launches are the latest in a series of weapons demonstrations by North Korea ahead of its ruling Workers' Party congress, expected in January or February, while Seoul's new president attempts to enlist Beijing as a mediator in the Korean Peninsula's nuclear standoff.

## Regional governments condemn the launches

South Korea's Defense Ministry said the launches violated United Nations Security Council resolutions that ban any ballistic missile activities by North Korea, and urged Pyongyang to cease what it called provocative actions and respond to Seoul's push to restart peace talks.

Japanese Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi confirmed at least two launches. "They are a serious problem, threatening the peace and security of our nation, the region and the world," Koizumi told reporters.

The U.S. military said in a statement that the launches did not "pose an immediate threat to U.S. personnel or territory, or to our allies." The statement added that the United States "remains committed to the defense of the U.S. homeland and our allies in the region."

South Korea convened an emergency national security council meeting later Sunday. The council briefed President Lee, then in transit to China, with details of the launches and unspecified South Korean steps taken in response, according to the presidential office.

## Lee seeks China's role ahead of Pyongyang congress

The launches came hours before Lee departed for a four-day visit to China, where he planned to meet with President Xi Jinping. Lee's office said he would request that China, North Korea's major ally and largest trading partner, take "a constructive role" in promoting peace on the Korean Peninsula.

South Korea and the United States have long pressed China to exercise its influence over Pyongyang to persuade Kim to return to negotiations or abandon his nuclear program. China, along with Russia, has repeatedly blocked U.S.-led efforts to tighten economic sanctions on North Korea in recent years.

## Weapons push ahead of Workers' Party congress

Experts said North Korea is seeking to show off or review its defense achievements ahead of the Workers' Party congress, the first of its kind in five years. South Korea's spy service said the congress will likely convene in January or February. Analysts are watching the gathering to see whether North Korea will set new policy toward the United States and whether Kim will signal any willingness to resume long-stalled negotiations.

The Sunday launches extended a weeks-long streak of weapons activity. North Korea test-fired what it called long-range strategic cruise missiles the previous Sunday. On Dec. 25, North Korea released photos showing what appeared to be progress in the construction of its first nuclear-powered submarine.

On Saturday, Kim Jong Un visited a weapons factory to review multipurpose precision-guided weapons, according to the official Korean Central News Agency. KCNA reported Kim ordered officials to expand the factory's production capacity by about 2.5 times.

North Korea has focused on expanding its nuclear arsenal since Kim's diplomatic talks with U.S. President Donald Trump collapsed in 2019. Kim has since aligned North Korea with Russia over its war in Ukraine and deepened ties with China — moves analysts say are designed to increase his leverage in any future negotiations with Washington.

## North Korea invokes Maduro as it defends nuclear program

North Korea's Foreign Ministry on Sunday condemned a U.S. military operation that removed Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro from power and brought him to the United States to face narco-terrorism charges. The ministry called the operation "the most serious form of encroachment of sovereignty" and said it revealed "the rogue and brutal nature of the U.S."

The U.S. action drew a pointed assessment from a Seoul-based analyst. "Kim Jong Un may feel vindicated about his efforts to build a nuclear deterrent, as he likely did after Trump's strikes on Iran," said Leif-Eric Easley, professor of international studies at Ewha Womans University in Seoul. "However, leaders of hostile regimes will probably live with greater paranoia after seeing how quickly Maduro was extracted from his country to stand trial in the United States."
