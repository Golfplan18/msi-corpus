The Associated Press said it has been relying on the Human Rights Activists News Agency, a U.S.-based nonprofit, to report the death toll from Iran's nationwide protests, as a government-imposed internet shutdown has blocked most channels for independent verification. Iran's government has not provided overall casualty figures, and state media has offered limited information about the demonstrations.

The disclosure explains how wire services and human-rights organizations are assembling a casualty record at a moment when the Iranian government has severed most domestic communications — a strategy that has drawn concern from press-freedom groups tracking the protests.

The Human Rights Activists News Agency, known as HRANA, is a tax-exempt nonprofit registered with the U.S. Internal Revenue Service. The agency was founded about 20 years ago; one of its founders is Keyvan Rafiee, an Iranian political activist who faced repeated imprisonment before leaving Iran for the United States.

AP said HRANA has maintained an accurate record through multiple years of demonstrations in Iran. The agency relies on a network of activists inside the country to confirm all reported fatalities, a process that includes speaking with medical officials, family members, and community leaders, as well as checking against funeral notices and other official documents.

With communications greatly limited inside Iran, AP said it has been unable to independently confirm HRANA's figures. The wire service has also drawn on videos that have appeared online and appear to have left the country via Starlink satellite dishes. AP said it authenticates such footage by checking it against known locations and events, consulting regional experts, and verifying that the content is consistent with its own reporting.

Iranian state media has provided little coverage of the demonstrations, making it difficult to gauge their scale. Brief video clips showing people in the streets or the sound of gunfire have circulated online, offering fragmented glimpses of conditions inside the country.
