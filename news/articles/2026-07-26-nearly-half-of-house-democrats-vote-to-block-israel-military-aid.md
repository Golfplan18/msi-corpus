## Nearly half of House Democrats vote to block Israel military aid

**Subtype:** fact

- More than 100 House Democrats voted this month for an amendment to cut off U.S. military financing for Israel, with the share of Democratic House members opposing aid at 48% in the 2026 vote, up from 17% in 2024, according to a Wall Street Journal analysis.
- Former Speaker Nancy Pelosi voted for the amendment, saying she did so "for the message that it sends."
- Two House Democratic leaders — Minority Whip Katherine Clark and Rep. Joe Neguse — voted to cut off aid after all five Democratic leaders supported Israel in 2024.
- Millennial Democrats' share backing Israel aid was 34% in the 2026 vote, compared with 84% in 2024.
- Ten freshman Democrats voted against aid in districts where their predecessors had supported it.

## Millennial Democrats' Israel aid support at 34% in 2026 vote

The amendment, introduced by Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.), would have cut off all U.S. military financing for Israel. It failed, with most Republicans united in opposition and only Massie supporting his own measure, according to the Journal.

The vote represented a shift from 2024, when the House passed a $26.4 billion emergency spending package that included support for Israel's defenses. In that year, about three dozen Democrats voted against the package. In the July 2026 vote, more than 100 Democrats voted to cut off the financing.

Former Speaker Nancy Pelosi, a longtime supporter of Israel, voted for the amendment. "While this amendment is ill-conceived, I vote yes for the message that it sends," Pelosi said, according to the Journal.

The shift extended into Democratic leadership. In 2024, all five House Democratic leaders voted in favor of Israel aid. In the July vote, two leaders — Minority Whip Katherine Clark of Massachusetts and Rep. Joe Neguse of Colorado — voted in opposition. Clark called the holding of the amendment vote a "stunt by Republicans" but said she voted for the measure because the "status quo is not tenable," the Journal reported.

The generational divide within the caucus was pronounced. Millennial Democrats shifted the most: 84% voted to maintain Israel aid in 2024, compared with 34% in the July vote. Generation X was the only age cohort in which a majority of House Democrats voted to preserve the aid, according to the Journal.

Ten freshman Democrats voted to block aid in districts where their predecessors had voted to maintain it in 2024. The reverse pattern was less common. Three freshmen whose predecessors had voted against Israel aid — including Reps. Wesley Bell of Missouri and George Latimer of New York, who defeated progressive incumbents in heated 2024 primaries — voted to maintain the aid, the Journal reported.

Among Democrats running in House races rated competitive by nonpartisan analysts, only two voted against Israel aid and one did not vote, reflecting the political sensitivity of the issue in swing districts, according to the Journal.

Members of the Congressional Progressive Caucus made up the majority of Democratic votes against Israel aid, with their numbers more than quadrupling since 2024. Thirty-four other Democrats who are not caucus members also voted to block the financing, the Journal's analysis found.

No Republican except Massie supported the amendment, a shift from 2024 when 21 Republicans voted against Israel aid. With President Donald Trump in the White House, the anti-foreign-aid wing of the Republican Party held its fire, according to the Journal.
