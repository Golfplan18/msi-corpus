Elizabeth Hurley testified Thursday in London that the publisher of the Daily Mail tapped her phones, placed microphones outside her windows, and stole her medical records — accusations she described as involving "monstrous, staggering things." The actor is among seven celebrities, including Prince Harry and Elton John, suing Associated Newspapers Ltd. over allegations that the publisher hired private investigators to conduct unlawful surveillance over two decades.

The case, unfolding in London's High Court over nine weeks, centers on whether one of Britain's largest newspaper publishers systematically violated the privacy of public figures by directing secret surveillance against them.

Elizabeth Hurley described her experience with nearly physical revulsion. "The best way I can describe it is like there is someone peeping into your life and into your home," she testified Thursday in London. "It makes me feel as if my private life had been violated by violent intruders — that there had been sinister thieves in my home all along and that I had been living with them completely unaware."

The actor was testifying in a lawsuit against the publisher of the Daily Mail, accusing the organization of systematic privacy invasion through phone taps, surreptitious microphones, and stolen medical records. She is one of seven co-claimants including Prince Harry and Elton John suing Associated Newspapers Ltd. The case, in its second week of a nine-week trial in London's High Court, centers on allegations that the publisher hired private investigators to conduct unlawful surveillance on celebrities and public figures over two decades.

## The Pattern of Alleged Violations

Hurley said the newspaper's violations included specific acts: "The Mail's unlawful acts against me involve landline tapping my phones and recording my live telephone conversations, placing surreptitious mics on my home windows, stealing my medical information when I was pregnant with Damian, and other monstrous, staggering things."

She claims that fifteen articles about her, published between 2002 and 2011, relied on information obtained through unlawful means. Several articles concerned the 2002 birth of her son, Damian, and an accompanying paternity dispute with film producer Steve Bing.

Hurley has brought similar phone hacking lawsuits against the publishers of the Daily Mirror and The Sun, adding this case to a broader pattern of media-accountability litigation in Britain.

## The Publisher's Denial and Defense

Associated Newspapers Ltd. denies the claims and has called them preposterous. The company said that the articles at issue were reported using legitimate sources and that many sources will be named by Daily Mail and Mail on Sunday employees during the company's defense phase.

The court allowed Prince Harry to attend Hurley's testimony, where he sat in the courtroom to show his support. Harry testified earlier in the trial, describing the emotional toll his years of battles against the British media have taken on him and his family.

## A Complicating Factor

The case has been complicated by the involvement of Gavin Burrows, a former private investigator. In 2020, Burrows claimed under oath that he had stolen Hurley's information at the behest of the newspapers. Burrows has since disavowed that sworn statement entirely, saying he never worked for the Mail.

## A Mother's Concern

Hurley expressed deep concern about the implications of the case becoming public knowledge for her son, now a model and actor himself, who was present in the courtroom.

"I felt really mortified that my son would be able to read all this stuff one day," she said, becoming emotional as lawyers showed her copies of the disputed articles. "I feel really bad that that day is today when all this stuff is being regurgitated."

She added, "Yet again, everyone's privacy is being invaded in this terrible way, and I feel very helpless about that."
