The Feast of the Baptism of the Lord, which closes the Roman Catholic liturgical Christmas cycle, has served as an annual occasion for popes to administer baptism personally since St. John Paul II established the tradition in 1981.

VATICAN CITY — Pope Leo XIV baptized 20 infants beneath Michelangelo's frescoed ceiling in the Sistine Chapel on Sunday, performing in his first baptismal ceremony as pontiff an annual Vatican tradition that closes the Christmas liturgical season.

The infants, all children of Vatican employees at the Holy See, were brought to the font by their parents, godparents, and siblings. The ceremony, which proceeded without tears and minimal fussing, observes the Feast of the Baptism of the Lord — the day the Catholic Church commemorates Jesus' baptism in the River Jordan. The event is depicted on the Sistine Chapel's north wall in a fresco by Pietro Perugino.

Speaking to the families gathered in the chapel, Leo drew an analogy between material necessity and spiritual need.

"When we know something is essential, we immediately seek it for those we love. Who among us, in fact, would leave a newborn without clothing or nourishment, waiting for them to choose when they grow up how to dress and what to eat?" the pontiff said. "Dearest ones, if food and clothing are necessary to live, faith is more than necessary, because with God, life finds salvation."

The baptismal font was placed within a bronze base representing the Tree of Life. During the ceremony, each father received a candle representing the Christian light that "illuminates our path."

After the chapel ceremony, Leo addressed the crowd assembled in St. Peter's Square, extending his blessing to all infants receiving baptism in these days, "in particular those born in difficult conditions of health or external danger."

The pope closed with a wish to the gathered families: "I wish you to continue with joy during the year that has just begun and for all of your life, certain that the Lord will always accompany your steps."

The annual tradition was established in 1981 by St. John Paul II.
