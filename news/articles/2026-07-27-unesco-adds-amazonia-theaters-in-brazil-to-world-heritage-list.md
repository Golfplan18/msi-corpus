## UNESCO adds Amazonia theaters in Brazil to World Heritage List
**Subtype:** fact
- The U.N. cultural agency UNESCO on Monday added two theaters in the Brazilian Amazon to its World Heritage List.
- The Amazonia Theaters comprise Theatro da Paz in Belem, Para state, and Teatro Amazonas in Manaus, Amazonas state.
- Both were built in the late 19th century at the height of the rubber boom.
- Brazil said it nominated the two theaters jointly because they represented the same historical period and embodied a shared project to modernize and integrate the Amazon region into global commercial and cultural circuits.

UNESCO Recognizes Theatro da Paz and Teatro Amazonas as World Heritage Sites

## Theatro da Paz and Teatro Amazonas, two historic theaters in the Brazilian Amazon, have been added to UNESCO's World Heritage List. The theaters, located in Belem and Manaus, were built during the late 19th century rubber boom and showcase a unique blend of European architectural influences and local materials and symbols.

The Amazonia Theaters, as they are collectively known, were designed as "symbols of political and urban ambition." Theatro da Paz, inaugurated in 1878, underscored Belem's role as a key port city, while Teatro Amazonas, inaugurated in 1896, reflected the rapid urban development of Manaus during the rubber boom.

According to UNESCO, the theaters were built to modernize and integrate the Amazon region into global commercial and cultural circuits. Brazil nominated the two theaters jointly because they represented the same historical period and embodied a shared project.

The addition of these theaters to the World Heritage List highlights the cultural significance of the Amazon region and its rich history. Theatro da Paz and Teatro Amazonas are not only important cultural landmarks but also symbols of the region's history and development.
