## Body camera video showed Massey apologizing before fatal shot

Sean Grayson, the former Illinois sheriff's deputy convicted of killing Sonya Massey, died Sunday while serving a 20-year prison sentence. Grayson's attorney, Daniel Fultz, confirmed that his client died earlier Sunday but declined to provide further details.

Grayson, 32, was convicted of second-degree murder in October in the fatal shooting of Massey, a 36-year-old Black woman who had reported a possible prowler outside her Springfield-area home. Body camera video of the July 2024 encounter showed Massey crouching in her kitchen and apologizing moments before Grayson shot her in the face. He was sentenced in January to the maximum 20 years in prison.

Grayson had been diagnosed with colon cancer before Massey's killing, and his attorneys said at his sentencing that the disease had spread to his liver and lungs. In May, only months into his sentence, he sought medical release under an Illinois law for prisoners with terminal illnesses or serious medical conditions. The Illinois Prisoner Review Board denied his request last month, about a month before his death.
