## Republicans tie Piker to Abdul El-Sayed as Democrats reject the focus

Hasan Piker has become a political flashpoint for Democrats during the 2026 election cycle. The online streamer has made controversial remarks about Sept. 11 and American Jews that have been documented, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Piker has also maintained a visible connection to Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic candidate for Senate in Michigan. He campaigned with El-Sayed, attended El-Sayed’s primary election night party and spoke to cameras after El-Sayed won.

That connection has given Republicans an opening to question whether Piker represents a broader direction for the Democratic Party. Rep. Lisa McClain, a Republican from Michigan, said Piker was “attached to your hip” during El-Sayed’s primary. She added, “Hasan Piker is who the Democrats are.”

Democrats have rejected that emphasis. Rep. Debbie Dingell, a Democrat from Michigan, told voters that the election should center on what would happen if they elected El-Sayed to the United States Senate. “I am done so don’t any reporter ask me again about Hasan Piker,” Dingell said. “It’s not what November is about.”

Dingell’s comments leave a separate question about the issues Democrats intend to emphasize in November. The Journal described a new and highly progressive group of Democratic candidates on ballots across the country. The candidates have energized some Democratic primary voters while unsettling others.

Republicans hope figures such as Piker will intensify those concerns among Democrats and motivate Republican voters. The Journal reported that the effect could matter in one or two races, enough to influence the balance.

The dispute is unfolding as El-Sayed campaigns for a Senate seat in Michigan. Piker’s participation in the primary campaign has made him part of the Republican argument about El-Sayed, while Democratic officials are seeking to keep the focus on the Senate race and the issues they say will matter to voters.
