Hill's account has taken on new weight now that the Trump administration has ousted Maduro and declared it will direct Venezuelan policy while the war in Ukraine continues. She said U.S. actions in Venezuela now give Moscow rhetorical cover to defend its own territorial ambitions as consistent with how great powers behave.

Russia's government signaled in 2019 that it would step back from its support for Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro if the United States granted Moscow a free hand in Ukraine, according to Fiona Hill, who served as President Donald Trump's senior adviser on Russia and Europe at the time.

Hill said the approach was never elevated to a formal offer but was conveyed through repeated diplomatic hints. Her 2019 congressional testimony describing it resurfaced this week after the U.S. military operation that captured Maduro.

## The 2019 approach

The Russians repeatedly floated the idea of a "very strange swap arrangement between Venezuela and Ukraine," Hill said during the 2019 congressional hearing. She told the Associated Press this week that Russia also pushed the concept through articles in Russian state media invoking the Monroe Doctrine — a 19th-century principle under which the United States opposed European intervention in the Western Hemisphere and agreed in turn to stay out of European affairs. Trump cited the Monroe Doctrine to justify the Venezuela operation.

Anatoly Antonov, Russia's then-ambassador to the United States, hinted many times to Hill that Russia was willing to allow the United States to act as it wished in Venezuela if the U.S. did the same for Russia in Europe, Hill told AP.

"Before there was a 'hint hint, nudge nudge, wink wink, how about doing a deal?' But nobody (in the U.S.) was interested then," Hill said.

## Hill's Moscow trip

Trump dispatched Hill — then his senior adviser on Russia and Europe — to Moscow in April 2019 to deliver a direct response. She told Russian officials "Ukraine and Venezuela are not related to each other."

At that point, she said, the White House was aligned with allies in recognizing Venezuelan opposition leader Juan Guaido as the country's interim president.

## The changed landscape

Seven years later, the situation is different. After the U.S. operation removed Maduro, the Trump administration said it would "run" Venezuela policy. Trump has also threatened to take over Greenland — a self-governing territory of Denmark and a NATO member — and threatened military action against Colombia for facilitating global cocaine sales.

Hill said the Kremlin will be "thrilled" with the idea that large countries — such as Russia, the United States, and China — get spheres of influence because it proves "might makes right."

Trump's actions in Venezuela make it harder for Kyiv's allies to condemn Russia's designs on Ukraine as "illegitimate," Hill told AP. "We've just had a situation where the U.S. has taken over — or at least decapitated the government of another country — using fiction," she said.

The Trump administration has described its Venezuela raid as a law enforcement operation and insisted that capturing Maduro was legal.

## Russia's response

Russian President Vladimir Putin had not commented on the military operation to oust Maduro as of January 6. Russia's Foreign Ministry issued statements condemning U.S. "aggression" but did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Hill's account.
