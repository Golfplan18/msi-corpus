The informal overture — never formalized as a diplomatic offer — has taken on new weight following the U.S. operation to capture Maduro, which Hill said validates the sphere-of-influence logic Moscow had long been promoting and complicates the position of Ukraine's allies in condemning Russia.

Fiona Hill, who served as President Donald Trump's senior adviser on Russia and Europe, said Russian officials in 2019 repeatedly raised the idea of a sphere-of-influence swap — Russia would withdraw support for Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in exchange for U.S. acquiescence to Russian ambitions in Ukraine. Her 2019 congressional testimony describing the overture resurfaced this week, days after a U.S. military operation captured Maduro in Caracas.

Hill told The Associated Press that the proposal never took the form of a formal diplomatic offer. Then-Russian Ambassador to the United States Anatoly Antonov repeatedly hinted to her that Russia was prepared to allow the United States to act freely in Venezuela if the United States extended the same latitude to Russia in Europe, she said.

"Before there were 'hints, nudges, winks, proposals,'" Hill said. "But nobody [in the United States] was interested then."

Russia promoted the idea in part through articles in Russian media invoking the Monroe Doctrine — the 19th-century principle by which the United States declared opposition to European interference in the Western Hemisphere in exchange for American non-intervention in European affairs. Trump later cited the Monroe Doctrine to justify the U.S. incursion in Venezuela.

## The 2019 rejection

Trump dispatched Hill to Moscow in April 2019 to deliver a message rejecting any linkage between the two countries. She said she told Russian officials directly: "Ukraine and Venezuela are not related to each other."

At the time, the White House was aligned with its allies in recognizing opposition leader Juan Guaidó as Venezuela's legitimate interim president.

## Why the old offer now matters

Seven years later, Hill said, the landscape has shifted in ways that validate the logic Russia was promoting. Following the U.S. capture of Maduro, the Trump administration said the United States would now "direct" Venezuela's policy. Trump has also renewed his campaign to acquire Greenland — an autonomous Danish territory and member of the NATO military alliance — and threatened Colombia with military consequences over its role in cocaine trafficking.

The Kremlin, Hill said, would be "delighted" by a world in which major powers — Russia, the United States, and China — hold recognized spheres of influence, because it demonstrates that "might makes right."

Hill told the AP the Venezuela operation also damages the diplomatic position of Ukraine's allies. "We just had a situation where the United States has taken control, or at least decapitated the government of another country, using a fictitious story," she said.

The Trump administration has characterized the Venezuela operation as a judicial action, asserting that capturing Maduro was lawful under U.S. drug trafficking indictments.

Russia's Foreign Ministry did not respond to a request for comment on Hill's account. President Vladimir Putin has not publicly addressed the operation, though Russia's Foreign Ministry issued statements condemning what it called U.S. "aggression."
