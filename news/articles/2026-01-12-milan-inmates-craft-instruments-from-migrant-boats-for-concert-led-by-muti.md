Inmates at Italy's largest prison built musical instruments from the wood of seized migrant-smuggling boats, and conductor Riccardo Muti led those instruments in a special performance at the Opera prison in Milan on Saturday. The Cherubini Youth Orchestra played on violins, violas, and cellos fashioned from faded boat planks, in front of the inmates who crafted them.

The concert was the public face of a rehabilitation initiative called Metamorphosis, which aims to transform both discarded material and incarcerated people into contributors to society — an undertaking that draws its raw material from the central Mediterranean migration crisis, along which the United Nations says tens of thousands of people have died or gone missing since 2014.

## The instruments and their origin

The violins, violas, and cellos are recognizable by the faded blue, green, and yellow paint of the salvaged wood from which they were made. The boats arrived at Opera after being seized by Italian authorities, some still containing remnants of the migrants' belongings.

"These instruments are made from the tragic wood of these boats that were trying to bring people to safety and democracy," Muti said to the audience of inmates and guests.

The Metamorphosis project ties two transformations together: discarded, sea-worn wood becomes fine instruments, and incarcerated people become craftsmen. The Opera prison on Milan's southern edge houses over 1,400 inmates, including 101 mafiosi held under a strict near-total-isolation regime, according to the Associated Press.

## The performance

Saturday's program included works by Italian composers Antonio Vivaldi and Giuseppe Verdi. A chorus of singers from San Vittore, another Milan prison, joined the orchestra for a rendition of "Va' Pensiero" — also known as "The Chorus of the Hebrew Slaves" — from Verdi's opera "Nabucco."

After the performance, Muti said the audience had moved him. "Hearing these people, who are here serving their sentences, but who seem so serene and so clearly and openly eager to find a sense of harmony in their lives through music … has been an enrichment of my experience as a musician and as a man," he said.
