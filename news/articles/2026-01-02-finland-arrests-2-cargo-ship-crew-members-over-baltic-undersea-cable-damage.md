Finnish authorities arrested two crew members of the cargo ship Fitburg on Thursday in connection with damage to an undersea telecommunications cable in the Gulf of Finland, Helsinki police said. The cable, owned by Finnish telecommunications provider Elisa and classified as critical underwater infrastructure, was found damaged early Wednesday in Estonia's exclusive economic zone. Police also placed two other crew members under travel bans as part of the ongoing investigation.

The arrest adds to a pattern of Baltic undersea infrastructure damage that Western officials have attributed to deliberate sabotage linked to Russia following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, though Finnish investigators said Thursday they are not yet speculating on whether a state-level actor was behind the latest incident.

Finnish authorities arrested two members of the cargo ship Fitburg's crew on Thursday in connection with damage to an undersea telecommunications cable in the Gulf of Finland, Helsinki police said. The cable, owned by Finnish telecommunications provider Elisa and considered critical underwater infrastructure, was found damaged early Wednesday in Estonia's exclusive economic zone. Police placed two additional crew members under travel bans as part of the continuing investigation.

Helsinki police have opened an investigation into aggravated criminal damage, attempted aggravated criminal damage, and aggravated interference with telecommunications, the department said in a statement Thursday.

Police declined to release the ranks or nationalities of the two arrested crew members or further details about the suspects.

## The ship and its crew

The Fitburg was flagged in St. Vincent and the Grenadines and had been traveling from Russia to Israel at the time of the incident. All 14 crew members — nationals of Russia, Georgia, Azerbaijan, and Kazakhstan — were detained by Finnish authorities, police said.

Finnish National Police Commissioner Ilkka Koskimäki said earlier in the week that the vessel was dragging its anchor for hours when it was discovered in Finland's exclusive economic zone. Investigators are not speculating on whether a state-level actor directed the damage, he said.

Finnish Customs separately reported finding structural steel in the Fitburg's cargo that originated in Russia and falls under European Union sanctions. "Import of such sanctioned goods into the EU is prohibited under EU sanctions regulations," the agency said in a statement, adding that it continues to investigate the sequence of events and the applicability of EU sanctions law.

## Context: a recurring pattern in the Baltic

The undersea cables and pipelines of the Gulf of Finland crisscross one of Europe's busiest shipping lanes, linking Nordic, Baltic, and central European countries. The infrastructure supports trade, energy security, and communications for thousands of people, in some cases reducing dependence on Russian energy resources.

The Fitburg incident follows similar damage on Christmas Day 2024, when the Eagle S — a Russia-linked oil tanker flagged in the Cook Islands — cut undersea cables between Finland and Estonia. Finnish customs officials and the European Commission described the Eagle S as part of Russia's shadow fleet of fuel tankers: aging vessels with obscure ownership, acquired to evade Western sanctions during the war in Ukraine and operating outside Western-regulated insurance systems.

Finnish authorities charged the Eagle S's captain and two senior officers with aggravated criminal mischief and aggravated interference with communications. A Finnish court dismissed those charges in October 2025 because the damaged cables were located outside Finland's territorial waters. The Kremlin denied involvement in the infrastructure damage.

Western officials have described repeated incidents of Baltic cable and pipeline damage as part of a broader pattern of alleged sabotage linked to Moscow following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
