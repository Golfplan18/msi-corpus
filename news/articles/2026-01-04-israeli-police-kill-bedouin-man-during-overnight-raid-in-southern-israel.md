TEL AVIV — Israeli police shot and killed a 36-year-old Bedouin Arab man during an overnight raid on his village in southern Israel, the mayor of a nearby town confirmed Sunday, raising new tensions between the Israeli government and the country's Bedouin minority.

The victim, Muhammed Hussein Tarabin, died in the village of Tarabin, which shares his family name, during what Israeli police described as an arrest operation in which a man had "endangered" forces. Talal Alkernawi, the mayor of the nearby town of Rahat, confirmed Tarabin's death.

The killing came as Israeli police have conducted approximately two dozen arrests over the past week in the village — part of what authorities describe as a crackdown on crime in the Negev desert region. Bedouin residents and advocates say the operations reflect chronic government neglect, accelerating house demolitions, and political pressure ahead of elections expected later this year.

## What the family says

Relatives told the Israeli news site Haaretz that Tarabin was inside his home when he was shot. His 11-year-old son, Hussein, described the events in a video statement. Men in uniform arrived at the family's house during the night, the boy said. He heard shots and found his father's body lying on the ground.

## Government response

National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir, who oversees the police force, expressed support for the officers. "Anyone who endangers our police officers and fighters must be neutralized," he posted on X.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Sunday that Israel would do everything to prevent the Negev desert from becoming the "wild south." He congratulated Ben-Gvir on leading the operation and said he planned to visit the region in the coming days.

## The week-long operation

Israeli police have conducted the large-scale operation in the village of Tarabin for roughly a week. Nati Yefet, a spokesman for the regional council of unrecognized villages in the area, said most of those arrested had been quickly released.

"They're looking for people, crime-related things, but they didn't find anything," Yefet said. He accused Ben-Gvir of intensifying the raids in the run-up to anticipated elections.

Marwan Abu Frieh, of the Arab rights group Adalah, said Israel has stepped up house demolitions in recent years, leaving thousands of residents without shelter and worsening the conditions of communities often denied basic services.

## Bedouin communities and longstanding tensions

Israel has more than 200,000 Bedouin citizens — the poorest members of the country's Arab minority, which also includes Christian and Muslim urban communities. The Arab population makes up roughly 20 percent of Israel's approximately 10 million people. While Arab citizens hold voting rights, they frequently face discrimination and tend to identify with Palestinians in the occupied West Bank and the Gaza Strip.

About one-third of Bedouin citizens live in villages that the Israeli government considers illegal. Israel describes its operations as efforts to bring order to a lawless area; Bedouin leaders accuse the government of neglect, attempting to destroy their way of life, or seeking to relocate them to less desirable areas.
