Two high-speed trains collided near the village of Adamuz in southern Spain on Sunday evening after one derailed and veered into the path of an oncoming train, killing at least 21 people and hospitalizing 75 others, Spanish Transport Minister Óscar Puente said. The crash occurred at 7:45 p.m. local time on a flat stretch of track outside Córdoba. Puente said rescuers had removed all survivors by midnight but warned the death toll could rise.

The collision struck one of Europe's most extensive high-speed rail networks — Spain operates more than 3,100 kilometers of track for trains running above 250 kph — and drew expressions of condolence from Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, King Felipe VI, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen.

ADAMUZ, Spain — A high-speed train traveling from Málaga to Madrid derailed Sunday evening in southern Spain and slammed into an oncoming passenger train, killing at least 21 people and sending 75 others to hospitals, Spanish officials said.

The rear section of the Iryo-operated train, carrying approximately 300 passengers, left the rails near the village of Adamuz, outside Córdoba, at 7:45 p.m. and struck the front of a Renfe train carrying approximately 200 passengers from Madrid to Huelva, according to rail infrastructure operator Adif. The collision knocked the Renfe train's first two carriages down a four-meter (13-foot) embankment.

"Tonight is one of deep sadness for our country," Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said on X. "I want to express my sincerest condolences to the family and loved ones of the victims."

## Cause unknown; track had been renovated months earlier

Transport Minister Óscar Puente said the cause of the crash was unknown. He described it as "a truly strange" incident, noting that the accident occurred on a flat stretch of track that had been renovated in May and that the derailed train was less than four years old. An inquiry could take as long as a month, he said.

Puente said the worst damage was to the front section of the Renfe train.

Iryo issued a statement saying it "deeply lamented what has happened" and that it was working with authorities.

## Rescue operation extended through the night

Andalusia regional President Juanma Moreno said most of the 75 hospitalized passengers were taken to Córdoba, including 15 with serious injuries. He said emergency workers would work through the night to remove bodies from the wreckage.

"We have a very difficult night ahead," Andalusia regional health chief Antonio Sanz said.

Civil Protection chief María Belén Moya Rojas said the crash site was in a hard-to-reach area. Local residents brought blankets and water to the scene. Spain's military emergency relief units, the Civil Guard, and Civil Defense personnel all responded.

The Spanish Red Cross set up an assistance center in Adamuz for emergency services and people seeking information about passengers.

## Passengers broke windows to escape

Salvador Jiménez, a journalist for Spanish broadcaster RTVE who was aboard one of the trains, said by telephone that "there was a moment when it felt like an earthquake and the train had indeed derailed." He said passengers used emergency hammers to break windows and that some walked away without serious injuries. Videos from the scene showed people crawling through windows as carriages leaned at steep angles.

Francisco Carmona, the firefighter chief of Córdoba, said one of the trains was badly mangled with at least four wagons off the rails.

## Spain operates Europe's largest high-speed rail network

Spain has more than 3,100 kilometers (1,900 miles) of high-speed rail track — the largest such network in Europe for trains running above 250 kph (155 mph), according to the European Union. Renfe carried more than 25 million passengers on its high-speed trains in 2024.

Adif said high-speed train service between Madrid and cities in Andalusia would be suspended Monday.

Spain's worst train accident this century occurred in 2013, when 80 people died after a passenger train derailed in the country's northwest. An investigation found the train was traveling at 179 kph (111 mph) on a stretch with an 80 kph (50 mph) speed limit when it left the tracks.

King Felipe VI and Queen Letizia expressed condolences on social media. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said she was following "the terrible news" from Córdoba. "Tonight you are in my thoughts," she wrote in Spanish.
