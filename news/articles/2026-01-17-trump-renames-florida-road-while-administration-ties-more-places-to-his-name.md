President Donald Trump marked the renaming of a 4-mile (6-kilometer) stretch of road in Florida with his name on Friday, as critics and supporters continued to debate how often the president’s administration has tied federal assets and other institutions to his branding.

The ceremony took place at Trump’s Florida home to mark the change for a road stretching from the airport to his Mar-a-Lago estate in Palm Beach. The route was renamed the “President Donald J. Trump Boulevard,” with Trump thanking local officials for the dedication.

Trump said that when people see the sign at night—lit up with the new name—they’ll feel pride in the country rather than in him. “When people see that the beautiful sign is all lit up nice at night and it says ‘Donald J. Trump Boulevard,’ they’ll be filled with pride. Just pride. Not in me. Pride in our country,” he said.

The AP reported that the road dedication was part of a broader pattern as the first year of Trump’s second term wrapped up. According to the report, Trump’s allies and administration have put his name on the U.S. Institute of Peace, the Kennedy Center performing arts venue, and a new class of battleships that has not yet been built.

The report also described additional branded initiatives, including “Trump Accounts” for tax-deferred investments; a TrumpRx government website soon to offer direct sales of prescription drugs; a “Trump Gold Card” visa that costs at least $1 million; and what it called the “Trump Route for International Peace and Prosperity,” described as a transit corridor included in a deal his administration brokered between Armenia and Azerbaijan.

Jeffrey Engel, a presidential historian at Southern Methodist University, said the timing and scope stood out historically. “At no previous time in history have we consistently named things after a president who was still in office,” Engel said, adding that the practice could be extended to a president who is still alive. He said those tributes are meant to be memorials for those who have passed.

White House spokeswoman Liz Huston defended the approach, saying initiatives linked to the TrumpRx website include “overdue upgrades of national landmarks, lasting peace deals, and wealth-creation accounts for children” and that they were historic efforts enabled by Trump’s leadership. Huston also said the administration’s focus is not branding but delivering on Trump’s “Making America Great Again” goal.

The White House pointed to examples used to argue the practice is not entirely new, citing that the nation’s capital was named for President George Washington and that the Hoover Dam was named for Herbert Hoover while each was serving as president.

In contrast, independent Sen. Bernie Sanders introduced legislation that would ban naming or renaming federal buildings or land after a sitting president. Sanders said Trump is a narcissist who likes to see his name up there, and he argued that using federal buildings to enhance Trump’s position resembles the “Great Leader” mentality associated with North Korea. Sanders said that if Americans want to name buildings after deceased presidents, that is acceptable, but he said naming while in office is not what people want.

Supporters of the naming efforts said the honors are deserved. The AP reported that Rep. Claudia Tenney introduced legislation to designate June 14 as “Trump’s Birthday and Flag Day,” and that Rep. Greg Steube introduced legislation to rename the Washington-area Metro as the “Trump Train.” Rep. Addison McDowell introduced legislation to rename Washington Dulles International Airport as “Donald J. Trump International Airport,” arguing that Trump should be honored for his work, including combating fentanyl, and describing Trump as “one of the most consequential presidents ever.”

Beyond proposals targeting federal sites, the report said more efforts are underway in Florida, including work by Republican state lawmaker Meg Weinberger to rename Palm Beach International Airport as “Donald J. Trump International Airport.” The AP also noted that Florida has already seen street name changes during Trump’s presidency, including a December 2024 renaming in Hialeah to “President Donald J. Trump Avenue.”

Trump, speaking after that street renaming, said “Anybody that names a boulevard after me, I like,” and later described how people tell him when roads are renamed for him. “It’s a beginning, right? It’s a start,” he said.
