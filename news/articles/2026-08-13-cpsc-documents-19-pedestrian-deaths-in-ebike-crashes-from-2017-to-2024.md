## Seven-year toll roughly equals a single day of car-pedestrian deaths

The US Consumer Product Safety Commission documented 19 pedestrian deaths in ebike crashes across the country between 2017 and 2024, according to figures cited by The Guardian. The seven-year total is roughly equal to the average number of pedestrians killed by motorists in a single day of 2024.

The Guardian published the CPSC totals alongside a 49-second video showing an ebike rider being arrested after reportedly attempting to evade police on a reckless-driving complaint. The clip ran on the Guardian's US news section in August 2026.

The 19 fatalities translate to an annual average of fewer than three ebike-pedestrian deaths per year over the seven-year period. The Guardian described the figures as recently compiled by the agency.

According to the Guardian's caption, the rider was detained after reports of reckless driving and an attempt to evade police. The Guardian did not identify the rider, the jurisdiction, or the date of the incident.

Motor vehicles, by contrast, kill pedestrians at a far higher rate. The Guardian's comparison points to motor-vehicle-pedestrian deaths, which the paper says average roughly 19 per day in 2024.
