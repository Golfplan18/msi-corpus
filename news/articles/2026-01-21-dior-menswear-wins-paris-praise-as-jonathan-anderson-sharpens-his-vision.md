The show carries commercial weight well beyond any single collection: Anderson is the first designer in Dior's modern history to hold creative responsibility for women's ready-to-wear, haute couture and menswear under a single hand — an unusually concentrated wager by parent conglomerate LVMH at a moment of broad pressure across the luxury sector.

PARIS — Jonathan Anderson's latest menswear show for Dior drew a strong response Wednesday at Paris Fashion Week, where spare staging at an annex of the Rodin Museum put the clothes at the center and prompted one front-row attendee to say "Dior is back. It's a good day for fashion."

The 41-year-old Northern Irish designer, credited with transforming Loewe into one of luxury's most admired labels before joining Dior as creative director, stripped the décor to near-nothing — curtained walls, minimal set — and let tightly constructed silhouettes carry the argument. By the accounts of those in the room, they did.

Robert Pattinson, Lewis Hamilton and SZA were among the VIPs in attendance, according to the Associated Press.

## Coats as the collection's backbone

Outerwear supplied the collection's strongest statement, the AP reported, with coats described as the backbone in cut and stance. Anderson riffed subtly on the house's most guarded codes — the Bar jacket and the postwar New Look silhouette — through a faint curve at the hip and a hint of structure, without reproducing the original ceremony.

Tailoring ran slender throughout: elongated jackets, shrunken blazers, tailcoats, cropped Bar jackets and lean trousers. Outerwear extended from bombers into brocade capes, balloon-back field jackets and cocooning coats. Lace-up shoes with small heels and loafers grounded the gender-fluid silhouettes without anchoring them to a single tradition.

The palette held to somber tones across much of the show. Two elements broke from it: neon-yellow wigs that opened the presentation and glittering glam-rock epaulettes near its close.

Dior's house notes framed the collection's characters as modern-day flâneurs moving through Paris and cited Paul Poiret — the early-20th-century designer known for fluid forms and wide-ranging references — as a departure point. The notes cast the clothes as a coherent contradiction: Dior tailoring alongside denim and parkas, couture history beside technical outerwear.

## LVMH's broad wager on Anderson

The show carries stakes that extend beyond any single season.

Dior is a flagship house of luxury conglomerate LVMH and a central pillar of the group's fashion-and-leather-goods earnings at a time when luxury demand has come under pressure across the sector, the AP reported. Anderson arrived at Dior after the long tenure of Maria Grazia Chiuri — the house's first female creative director — ended last year with increasingly mixed critical notices in some quarters, according to the AP.

LVMH placed an unusually concentrated bet on Anderson: he is the first designer in Dior's modern history to hold creative responsibility for women's ready-to-wear, haute couture and menswear under a single hand.

The competitive backdrop sharpened the spotlight on the show. Rival luxury group Kering has been managing a prolonged slump at its flagship brand Gucci, with steep sales declines weighing on the group, the AP reported. With Kering's biggest runway names absent from the official menswear and couture schedules this week in Paris, scrutiny fell more squarely on LVMH's marquee houses.
