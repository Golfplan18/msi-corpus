## Jackson moves ahead in Maine Senate delegate race after rivals quit

**Subtype:** fact

- Troy Jackson, a former state legislator, has moved into a strong position to replace Graham Platner as the Democratic U.S. Senate candidate in Maine after several opponents dropped out of the race and endorsed him, the Associated Press reported.
- Maine Secretary of State Shenna Bellows withdrew Sunday morning, saying Democrats did not have "a day to waste in unifying" around the goal of defeating Republican incumbent Susan Collins.
- Former state CDC director Nirav Shah, Maine Beer Company co-founder Dan Kleban, and candidate Jordan Wood also ended their campaigns and endorsed Jackson.
- Jackson's campaign said it secured hundreds of delegate commitments, including all of its preferred delegates for Cumberland County, the state's most populous, ahead of the July 25 nominating convention.

## Bellows, Shah endorse former state senator for Platner seat

The quick change in the landscape of the race came on a weekend when party committees across the state began choosing delegates for a nominating convention that will meet July 25 to select a new candidate to face Republican U.S. Sen. Susan Collins in November, according to the Associated Press.

Between Saturday and Sunday, Jackson's campaign said it had secured hundreds of delegates who had promised to back him at the convention, including all of their preferred delegates for Maine's most populous county, Cumberland. His strength caused an exodus, the AP reported.

Bellows withdrew Sunday morning, saying Democrats did not have "a day to waste in unifying" around the goal of defeating Collins. Shah dropped out and endorsed Jackson in the early evening, and was quickly followed by Kleban and Wood, both of whom also endorsed Jackson, according to the AP.
