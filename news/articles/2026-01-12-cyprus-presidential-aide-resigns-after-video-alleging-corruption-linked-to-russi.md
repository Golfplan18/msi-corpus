NICOSIA, Cyprus — The director of Cyprus's presidential office resigned Monday after a social media video alleged that he and other government-connected figures exploited close ties to President Nikos Christodoulides to steer foreign investment in exchange for cash.

Charalambos Charalambous, who is also the president's brother-in-law, stepped down as Christodoulides issued a written statement accepting the resignation, calling it an act of "self-confidence and trust" that the allegations are untrue. "The departure of Mr. Charalambous, who is distinguished by his work ethic, character and integrity, constitutes a loss of a valuable aide during our great effort to upgrade our country," Christodoulides said.

Charalambous posted on Facebook before the resignation that he would never allow his government role to be "converted into a tool of manipulation to harm the Cyprus Republic and the President," calling his appearance in the video a product of "deliberate distortions" and "selective editing" designed to manufacture false conclusions.

## What the video alleges

The 8½-minute video, posted on social media last week, features Charalambous alongside a former energy minister and the chief executive of a major construction company. In the footage, the figures appear to boast about their closeness to the president and their capacity to arrange foreign investments — language that strongly suggested influence peddling, according to the AP report.

The video further claims Christodoulides exceeded a 1 million-euro ($1.16 million) campaign funding cap by accepting off-the-books cash donations during his 2023 presidential run.

The most serious allegation holds that the Cypriot government agreed to work against EU sanctions targeting Russian oligarchs in return for corporate cash payments.

## Russian disinformation assessment

Cypriot authorities called the video a malicious disinformation attack bearing "all the hallmarks" of previous Russian campaigns. An initial Cyprus Security Services analysis said the footage exhibits "the characteristics of organized Russian disinformation campaigns" known as Doppelganger — similar to a 2021 Russian online operation that targeted EU member states, the United States and Israel.

The video's timing heightened official suspicions. It surfaced approximately one week after Cyprus — a staunch supporter of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy — assumed the EU's six-month rotating presidency. Christodoulides said Monday that the 27-nation bloc had already warned Cypriot officials they could be targeted by "hybrid" attacks during the presidency, and that EU members and other countries are now assisting Cypriot authorities in the investigation.

## Opposition reaction

Despite the disinformation assessment, opposition parties were scathing in their criticism of Christodoulides and his government, saying the video had reignited past allegations of widespread government corruption.
