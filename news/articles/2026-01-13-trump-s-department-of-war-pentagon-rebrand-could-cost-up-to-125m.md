The Congressional Budget Office analysis released Wednesday found that renaming the Pentagon’s U.S. Department of Defense as the “Department of War” could cost as much as $125 million, depending on how broadly and quickly the change is implemented.

The executive order that authorized “Department of War” as a secondary title was signed in September by President Donald Trump. The analysis comes as the name change remains subject to Congress’s approval for a permanent, statutory change, and lawmakers have not taken up such legislation with urgency, the report said.

Trump said the switch was meant to signal to the world that the United States is “a force to be reckoned with,” and he complained that the Department of Defense’s name was “woke,” according to the analysis described by The Associated Press.

The Pentagon has also used the rebrand in day-to-day signage and online branding since the order, with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth directing changes soon after. Hegseth had employees remove large gold letters spelling “Secretary of Defense” outside his office and replaced the sign on his door to read “Secretary of War,” AP reported. The Pentagon’s website also shifted from defense.gov to war.gov the same day the executive order was signed.

Pentagon officials told lawmakers at the time that they could not offer a cost estimate because they expected expenses to fluctuate, and they promised a clearer estimate later. In the new CBO assessment, the agency projected costs starting from “at least a few million dollars” if the name change were phased in with minimal implementation, but rising as high as $125 million if the change were rolled out broadly and rapidly throughout the department.

The assessment said a “modest implementation” within the agency—if the change happened internally—would cost roughly $10 million, a figure AP said the report characterized as something that would most likely be absorbed within the Pentagon’s existing budgets. The report also warned that a statutory renaming could cost “hundreds of millions of dollars,” depending on how Congress and the Defense Department choose to proceed.

The CBO analysis took into account the scale of the Pentagon’s physical footprint, noting that the department has more than 6.5 million square feet of office space and that many signs, logos and seals have remained unchanged. AP reported that it is not clear whether alterations to the name have been carried out across the department’s numerous military facilities around the world.

Republican lawmakers introduced legislation to make the name change official shortly after Trump signed the executive order, including Sens. Mike Lee, Rick Scott and Marsha Blackburn, but the measure has not progressed, AP reported. The executive order also tasked Hegseth with recommending actions needed to change the name permanently, and AP said Pentagon officials did not answer questions about what, if any, recommendations have been made.

AP said the CBO analysis was requested by Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer and Sen. Jeff Merkley. The agency was created more than 50 years ago to provide impartial analysis supporting the budget process.
