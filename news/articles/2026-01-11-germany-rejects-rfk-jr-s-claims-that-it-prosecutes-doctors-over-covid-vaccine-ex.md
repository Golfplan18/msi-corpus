The exchange marks an early friction point between the new U.S. health secretary and a major European ally. German officials offered point-by-point rebuttals, saying pandemic-era prosecutions were limited to fraud — the issuance of fake vaccination certificates and false mask exemption documents — and that patient choice was never restricted.

Germany's health minister on Saturday rejected allegations by U.S. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. that the German government prosecutes physicians for issuing COVID-19 vaccine and mask exemptions, calling his statements "completely unfounded, factually incorrect, and must be rejected."

Kennedy posted a video on Saturday claiming that "more than a thousand German physicians and thousands of their patients now face prosecution and punishment for issuing exemptions from wearing masks or getting COVID-19 vaccines during the pandemic." He said he had sent German Health Minister Nina Warken a letter based on reports coming out of Germany that the government was "limiting people's abilities to act on their own convictions when they face medical decisions." Kennedy did not provide specific examples or identify which reports he was referring to.

Warken disputed each of Kennedy's core claims. "During the coronavirus pandemic, there was never any obligation on the medical profession to administer COVID-19 vaccinations," she said. "Anyone who did not want to offer vaccinations for medical, ethical, or personal reasons was not liable to prosecution, nor did they have to fear sanctions."

She added that criminal prosecution was only pursued for fraud and document forgery — "such as the issuance of false vaccination certificates or fake mask certificates" — and that in Germany patients "are also free to decide which therapy they wish to undergo."

Kennedy, in his video, said his letter argued that "Germany has the opportunity and the responsibility to correct this trajectory, to restore medical autonomy, to end politically motivated prosecutions." He characterized the German government as replacing the doctor-patient relationship with "a dangerous system that makes physicians enforcers of state policies."

Former German Health Minister Karl Lauterbach, who led the country's pandemic response, also responded directly to Kennedy on X. "In Germany, doctors are not punished by the government for issuing false medical certificates. In our country, the courts are independent," Lauterbach wrote. He said Kennedy "should take care of health problems in his own country," citing "short life expectancy, extreme costs, tens of thousands of drug deaths and murder victims."

During the pandemic, a majority of Germans supported COVID-19 vaccination. A smaller minority of vaccine skeptics staged protests, which were at times supported by far-right movements, according to the Associated Press.
