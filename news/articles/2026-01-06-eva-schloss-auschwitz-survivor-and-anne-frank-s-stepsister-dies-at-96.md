Eva Schloss, the Auschwitz survivor who devoted her post-war life to Holocaust education and was the stepsister of teenage diarist Anne Frank, died Saturday in London at the age of 96, the Anne Frank Trust UK announced. Schloss, born Eva Geiringer in Vienna in 1929, served as honorary president of the trust, which she co-founded to help young people challenge prejudice and hatred.

Her death removes one of the last eyewitnesses of the Nazi genocide who spent decades translating personal survival into sustained public education — speaking in schools, prisons, and at international conferences well into her 90s, and insisting that combating hatred requires starting with the youngest generations.

Britain's King Charles III said he was "privileged and proud" to have known Schloss. "The horrors that she endured as a young woman are impossible to comprehend and yet she devoted the rest of her life to overcoming hatred and prejudice, promoting kindness, courage, understanding and resilience through her tireless work for the Anne Frank Trust UK and for Holocaust education across the world," the king said.

## From Vienna to Auschwitz

Schloss's family fled Vienna for Amsterdam after Nazi Germany annexed Austria, where she became friends with Anne Frank, another Jewish girl of the same age. Frank's diary would become one of the most famous chronicles of the Nazi genocide.

Like the Franks, Eva's family spent two years in hiding to avoid capture after the Nazis occupied the Netherlands. They were eventually betrayed, arrested, and sent to Auschwitz. Schloss and her mother Fritzi survived until Soviet troops liberated the camp in 1945. Her father Erich and brother Heinz died at Auschwitz.

Anne Frank died of typhus in the Bergen-Belsen concentration camp at the age of 15, months before the end of the war.

After the war, Schloss moved to Britain and married German Jewish refugee Zvi Schloss, settling in London. In 1953, her mother married Otto Frank — the only member of his immediate family to survive — forging the formal family bond that made Eva Frank's stepsister.

## Decades of Silence, Then a Mission

Schloss did not speak publicly about her experiences for decades, later describing the wartime trauma as having made her withdrawn and unable to connect with others.

"I was silent for years, first because I wasn't allowed to speak. Then I repressed it. I was angry with the world," she told the Associated Press in 2004.

After she addressed the opening of an Anne Frank exhibition in London in 1986, Schloss made Holocaust education her mission for the remaining four decades of her life. She spoke in schools and prisons, appeared at international conferences, and published books including *Eva's Story: A Survivor's Tale by the Stepsister of Anne Frank.*

She kept campaigning into her 90s. In 2019, she traveled to Newport Beach, California to meet teenagers photographed making Nazi salutes at a high school party. The following year she joined a campaign urging Facebook to remove Holocaust-denying material from the social networking site.

"We must never forget the terrible consequences of treating people as 'other,'" Schloss said in 2024. "We need to respect everybody's races and religions. We need to live together with our differences. The only way to achieve this is through education, and the younger we start the better."

## Survivors

Zvi Schloss died in 2016. Eva Schloss is survived by their three daughters, as well as grandchildren and great-grandchildren.

Her family remembered her as "a remarkable woman: an Auschwitz survivor, a devoted Holocaust educator, tireless in her work for remembrance, understanding and peace." The family said in a statement: "We hope her legacy will continue to inspire through the books, films and resources she leaves behind."
