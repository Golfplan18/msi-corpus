## Summary

**Subtype:** fact

- The U.S. Justice Department has indicted former Cuban President Raúl Castro on murder and conspiracy charges for ordering the 1996 shootdown of two civilian exile planes.  
- The indictment alleges Castro authorized deadly force against the exile group Brothers to the Rescue, killing four U.S. nationals and destroying the aircraft.  
- Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche said Castro is expected to appear in a U.S. court, either voluntarily or by other means.  
- Cuban President Miguel Díaz‑Canel denounced the indictment as politically motivated and defended the shootdown as lawful self‑defense after alleged airspace violations.  
- Families of the victims, including Marlene Alejandre‑Triana, called the charges “long overdue,” and Miami residents such as Peter Hernandez welcomed the move as a step toward accountability.  

### Prosecution and charges

U.S. prosecutors filed the indictment on Wednesday in Miami, naming Raúl Castro, his brother Fidel Castro, five other individuals—including Cuban MiG pilots—and charging them with conspiracy to kill U.S. nationals, multiple counts of murder and destruction of aircraft. The murder and conspiracy charges carry a maximum penalty of death or life imprisonment, though it remains uncertain whether Castro will ever be taken into U.S. custody.

### U.S. pressure on Cuba

The indictment arrives amid heightened U.S. pressure on Cuba under the Trump administration, which has intensified economic sanctions, blockades on fuel and goods, and diplomatic efforts to open the Cuban economy to American investment. The move mirrors earlier U.S. actions against foreign leaders, such as the indictment of former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro on drug‑related charges.

### Cuban government response

Cuban President Miguel Díaz‑Canel condemned the indictment, labeling it “a political action without any legal basis” and asserting that the 1996 shootdown was “legitimate self‑defense” after repeated airspace violations by “notorious terrorists.” He posted on X that the Cuban people fully support Raúl Castro, writing “Homeland or Death, We Will Prevail.”

### Victims’ families and exile community

Marlene Alejandre‑Triana, whose father Armando Alejandre Jr. was among those killed, said the charges are “long overdue.” Peter Hernandez, a Miami resident, called Castro “a criminal” and expressed support for the U.S. sending its military to arrest him.

### Legal context

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche noted that the United States regularly indicts individuals outside its borders and uses a variety of methods to bring them to justice. He said the indictment reflects a broader effort to hold foreign officials accountable for actions that resulted in the deaths of U.S. nationals. 

--- 

The indictment marks a rare instance of the United States seeking criminal accountability for a former foreign leader’s Cold‑War‑era actions, highlighting both the enduring legacy of the 1996 shootdown and the intensifying geopolitical contest between Washington and Havana.
