Brazil's foreign ministry said Sunday it has taken over Mexico's diplomatic interests in Peru with the Peruvian government's consent, stepping into a two-month standoff rooted in Mexico's grant of asylum to a former Peruvian prime minister convicted on rebellion charges.

The takeover marks an escalation in the diplomatic dispute between Peru and Mexico triggered by Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum's decision to grant asylum to Betssy Chávez, who had been sentenced to more than 11 years in prison.

Brazil's foreign ministry said in a statement that it is "granting a request from the Mexican government" with the "consent of the Peruvian government." The ministry added it will guard the Mexican embassy building in Lima, the residence of its head of mission, assets, and files.

Brazilian officials confirmed to The Associated Press that Brazil's flag was flying over the building starting January 24. They spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss the matter publicly.

## The Diplomatic Standoff

Peru severed diplomatic relations with Mexico in November to protest Mexico's decision to grant asylum to Betssy Chávez, a former Peruvian prime minister who had been sentenced to more than 11 years in prison on charges of rebellion. Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum granted Chávez asylum on November 3.

The two countries maintained consular relations until the Brazilian takeover, though Mexican diplomats had withdrawn from Peru in November following the severing of relations.

## Chávez's Situation

The 36-year-old Chávez has lived in the Mexican embassy since being granted asylum. Police remain stationed outside the building, seeking to arrest her if she leaves.

Chávez was convicted of participating in a December 7, 2022 initiative by former President Pedro Castillo to dissolve Congress, an act that ultimately led to Castillo's removal from office. Peru has faced ongoing political instability, with multiple leaders ensnared in recent crises.

## Peru's Escalating Response

Peru's Congress declared Sheinbaum persona non grata and labeled the asylum granted to Chávez as an act of interference in Peru's internal affairs. With Mexico requesting Brazil's assistance in protecting its diplomatic interests, the standoff entered a new phase.

Mexican authorities did not comment on the Brazilian takeover.
