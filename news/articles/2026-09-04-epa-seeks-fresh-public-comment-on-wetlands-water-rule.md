## EPA seeks fresh public comment on wetlands water rule

- The Environmental Protection Agency is requesting additional public comments as it works to redefine the scope of the Clean Water Act and significantly limit which wetlands the law covers.
- The EPA said its new "supplemental" rule will help the agency and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers evaluate a wider range of options for a final rule that fully implements the Supreme Court's decision in Sackett v. EPA.
- The request, announced Friday, comes nearly a year after the EPA proposed a rule defining "waters of the United States," or WOTUS, based on a 2023 Supreme Court ruling that removed federal protections for vast areas of wetlands.
- The Associated Press reported that the plea for new information is highly unusual and reflects the complexity of a water rule that has been argued in the courts for decades and reached the Supreme Court twice since 2006.

## Supplemental rule aims to implement Sackett decision

The EPA's request for additional public comments reflects the legal complexity of a water rule that has been litigated for decades and reached the Supreme Court twice since 2006. The supplemental rulemaking will help the agency and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers evaluate a wider range of options as they work toward a final rule that fully implements the Supreme Court's 2023 Sackett decision.
