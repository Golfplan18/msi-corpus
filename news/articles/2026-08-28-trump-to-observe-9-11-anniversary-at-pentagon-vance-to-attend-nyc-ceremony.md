## NYC memorial banned political speeches since 2012 to stay nonpartisan

President Donald Trump will mark the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001, attacks at the Pentagon in Washington, D.C., rather than at the World Trade Center site in New York City, according to people briefed on the plans who spoke to media outlets.

Those familiar with the plans said Trump chose the Pentagon because he wants to deliver remarks on the anniversary, and politicians are not permitted to give remarks at the New York City site. The National September 11 Memorial and Museum has barred politicians from speaking at the location since 2012, citing its effort to remain nonpartisan.

Vice President JD Vance plans to attend the New York City anniversary event, officials said.

White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly said in a statement that "as a proud New Yorker, President Trump has spoken about his own experiences watching the horrific events of September 11, 2001." On the 25th anniversary, she said, "the president will remember those who were killed at the hands of evil terrorists, honor their loved ones, and pay tribute to the brave first responders who put their lives on the line to rescue their fellow Americans."

Trump marked the anniversary of the attacks at the Pentagon last year. He visited Ground Zero in 2024 and previously gave remarks at the Flight 93 National Memorial in Shanksville, Pennsylvania.
