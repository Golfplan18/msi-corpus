CAPE TOWN — South Africa declared a national disaster Sunday over torrential rains and floods that have killed at least 30 people in its northern provinces, damaged thousands of homes, and washed away roads and bridges across the region.

The declaration was made by the head of the National Disaster Management Center and announced by the government. It allows national authorities to coordinate the response across affected areas. The Ministry of Cooperative Governance and Traditional Affairs said the worst flooding struck the provinces of Limpopo and Mpumalanga, where all fatalities occurred, and that at least three other provinces had also been hit by the severe weather.

The disaster comes as parts of South Africa and neighboring Mozambique and Zimbabwe have experienced heavy rains for weeks. More than 100 people have died across the three countries since the rains began late last year, the Associated Press reported.

## Damage and missing persons

The premier of Limpopo province said the flooding had caused approximately $240 million in damage in her province alone, with many houses and buildings washed away completely.

Rescue teams continued Sunday to search for four missing people, including five-year-old Siyanda Baloyi, believed to have been swept away when his home in Limpopo province flooded last Thursday. Authorities also reported that Andile Mngwevu, a government official from South Africa's Ekurhuleni municipality east of Johannesburg, was missing after the vehicle he was traveling in was swept away by floodwaters in Mozambique; other occupants of the vehicle were also unaccounted for.

## Kruger National Park closure and reopening

Flooding forced the closure of Kruger National Park and the evacuation of more than 300 tourists and staff from flooded camps to other parts of the reserve. The park, one of the world's largest game reserves and a major international tourist destination bordering Mozambique and Zimbabwe, sustained extensive damage as floodwaters struck Mpumalanga and Limpopo provinces. Authorities said the park was set to reopen for visitors following the flooding-forced suspension.

## Pattern of flooding in southern Africa

The floods follow a series of severe weather events across southern Africa in recent years. More than 100 people died in South Africa's Eastern Cape province during flooding last year. More than 400 people died in flooding in the eastern KwaZulu-Natal province in 2022.
