## Rubio, Bessent flew on decoy plane as Trump was secretly moved, official says

**Subtype:** fact

- A senior U.S. official told The Associated Press on Wednesday that Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent flew on what functioned as a decoy plane when they departed last month's NATO summit in Turkey while President Donald Trump was relocated to a smaller military jet in response to an Iranian threat.
- The official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said Rubio was briefed on the aircraft switch and the Iranian threat behind it but could or would not say why he stayed on the larger plane.
- The official said Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was likely aware of the arrangement but the official did not have direct confirmation of his knowledge of the operation.
- The official said security precautions for the president — who has been the target of several assassination attempts — are more robust than those for top members of the Cabinet.

## Presidential security protocols are more robust than for Cabinet, official says

Rubio and Bessent, the two most senior members of Trump's Cabinet, remained on the larger aircraft that served as the decoy while Trump was relocated separately to the smaller military jet, according to the official, who was briefed on the situation. The disclosure, reported by AP writer Matthew Lee, adds another layer of detail to the public account of how Trump returned from the Ankara summit.

The official said Rubio was aware of the aircraft switch and of the Iranian threat behind it but "could or would not say" why the secretary of state had stayed on the larger plane. The official added that Bessent was "likely" aware of the arrangement but said the official did not have direct confirmation of the treasury secretary's knowledge of the operation.

The official said security precautions for the president — who has been the target of several assassination attempts — are more robust than those for top members of the Cabinet.
