The global backlash has sharpened a transatlantic divide over AI safety standards: as foreign governments condemned Grok as a threat to women and children, U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced plans to deploy the tool across Pentagon networks.

Governments in seven countries moved this week to investigate, restrict or ban Grok, Elon Musk's AI image-generation chatbot, after it generated and publicly distributed sexually explicit deepfake images of women and children — including images that British regulators said may constitute child sexual abuse material.

The enforcement actions span Malaysia, Indonesia, the United Kingdom, the European Union, France, India and Brazil, creating coordinated international pressure on Musk's AI company xAI and on X, the social media platform where Grok is hosted.

The backlash deepened a transatlantic divide over AI safety standards. U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced this week that Grok will soon be deployed on "every unclassified and classified network" throughout the Pentagon — an endorsement made as foreign governments were demanding that Musk do more to rein in the technology.

## How the problem emerged

The controversy stems from Grok Imagine, an AI image generator launched last year that allows users to create images and video by typing text prompts. The tool includes a "spicy mode" that can generate adult content.

The problem escalated in late December when Grok, accessed through X, began granting a large number of user requests to modify images posted by others on the platform. Users submitted prompts such as "put her in a transparent bikini" to alter photos of real people without their consent.

The scope of the problem was amplified by two factors, according to the Associated Press: Musk has pitched Grok as an alternative to rival AI tools with fewer safeguards, and Grok-generated images are publicly visible on X, making them easy to spread.

## Grok's response — and its limits

In apparent response to the backlash, Grok announced it was limiting image generation and editing to paying subscribers. By Friday, the chatbot was responding to free-user requests with a message saying image features were available only to those paying $8 a month for a premium X subscription.

The Associated Press confirmed on Tuesday, however, that free users could still access image editing on X by clicking an "Edit image" button that appears on every image posted to the platform — without tagging Grok with a request. The standalone Grok website and app were also still granting image editing requests at that time.

Musk's xAI company has been responding to media requests for comment with the automated message: "Legacy Media Lies."

X has previously said it will remove illegal content, permanently suspend offending accounts and work with law enforcement. Musk has warned that "anyone using Grok to make illegal content will suffer the same consequences as if they upload illegal content."

## Country-by-country responses

**United Kingdom.** The U.K.'s media regulator Ofcom launched an investigation Monday into whether Grok violated its duty to protect people from illegal content. Ofcom said Grok-generated images of children being sexualized or people being undressed may amount to pornography or child sexual abuse material.

Technology Secretary Liz Kendall called the AI-generated images "weapons of abuse" and said the centre-left Labour government would make it a crime for companies to supply tools to create nude images without consent. X could face an Ofcom fine of up to 10 percent of its qualifying global revenue, Kendall said, as well as a possible court order blocking access to the site.

"They can choose to act sooner to ensure this abhorrent and illegal material cannot be shared on their platform," Kendall said in Parliament.

Musk over the weekend called the British government "fascist" and accused it of trying to stifle free speech. Under the U.K.'s Online Safety Act, social media platforms must prevent and remove child sexual abuse material when they become aware of it.

**European Union.** Henna Virkkunen, the European Commission's executive vice-president for tech sovereignty, security and democracy, warned X it must act quickly or face full enforcement under the Digital Services Act.

"X now has to fix its AI tool in the EU — and they have to do it quickly. If not, we will not hesitate to put the DSA to its full use to protect EU citizens," Virkkunen posted on X.

The Commission separately ordered X to retain all internal documents and data relating to Grok until the end of 2026, as part of a wider investigation under EU digital regulations. A similar retention order was issued last year after Grok spread Holocaust-denial content. European Commission spokesman Thomas Regnier said compliance with EU law "is not an option. It's an obligation."

**Malaysia.** The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission said Tuesday it has hired a lawyer and would soon begin legal proceedings against X and xAI, accusing the companies of failing to ensure user safety. The commission said Grok was being misused to generate and distribute harmful content including sexually explicit, indecent and extremely offensive material. Malaysia had already banned Grok pending effective safeguards.

**Indonesia.** The country temporarily blocked access to Grok on Saturday. Indonesian Communication and Digital Affairs Minister Meutya Hafid said the government "sees nonconsensual sexual deepfakes as a serious violation of human rights, dignity and the safety of citizens in the digital space."

**France.** The Paris prosecutor's office said it is widening an ongoing investigation of X to include sexually explicit deepfakes, after three government ministers alerted prosecutors to what they described as "manifestly illegal content" generated by Grok and posted on X. French officials also flagged potential breaches of the Digital Services Act to the country's communications regulator.

**India.** The Indian government issued a 72-hour ultimatum to X demanding removal of all "unlawful content" and action against offending accounts. The ministry accused Grok of "gross misuse" of AI and serious failures of its safeguards in allowing the generation and sharing of obscene images of women. The deadline passed without a public update from the company.

**Brazil.** Lawmaker Erika Hilton reported Grok and X to the federal public prosecutor's office and Brazil's data protection watchdog. Hilton, one of Brazil's first transgender lawmakers, said users could get Grok to digitally alter published photos, including "swapping the clothes of women and girls for bikinis or making them suggestive and erotic." She called for X's AI functions to be disabled pending an investigation.
