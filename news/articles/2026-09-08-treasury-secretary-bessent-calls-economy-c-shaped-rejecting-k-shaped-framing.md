## Planet Money examines limits of letter-based economy shorthand

The NPR Planet Money podcast released an episode on September 8, 2026 titled "Has the economy gone C-shaped?", examining the alphabet-soup shorthand Americans use to describe economic conditions. The segment spotlighted Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's recent argument that the letter "C" describes today's economy better than the widely used "K."

Host Paddy Hirsch reported that Bessent said he was "sick of hearing about this K-shaped economy" and proposed the letter C as an alternative. The C, Hirsch wrote, is "a more optimistic letter for how lower- and middle-class Americans are faring in today's economy."

The K-shaped framing generally describes widening inequality, with the fortunes of rich and poor diverging — visualized as the upper and lower arms of the letter K pointing in opposite directions.

Hirsch wrote that the desire to reduce the economy to a letter is not new. "This desire to Sesame Street the economy isn't a surprise: we're always looking for ways to shorthand or telegraph the essence of what's going on in the economy at any given time," Hirsch wrote. "But assigning something as basic as a letter to something as complicated as the economy seems way too reductive. Even for Planet Money!"

The episode aired on NPR's Planet Money podcast on September 8, 2026, and was written and hosted by Paddy Hirsch.
