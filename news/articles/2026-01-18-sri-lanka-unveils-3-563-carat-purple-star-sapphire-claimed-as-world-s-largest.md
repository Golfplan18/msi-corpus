Owners of a 3,563-carat purple star sapphire unveiled the polished gem in Colombo, Sri Lanka, on Saturday, claiming it is the world's largest documented natural purple star sapphire and offering it for sale at a price estimated between $300 million and $400 million.

Sri Lanka has long been recognized as a leading source of fine sapphires, with the southern town of Rathnapura — where the stone was reportedly unearthed — serving as the country's gem-mining center. If the record claim withstands broader international scrutiny, the stone would set a new benchmark for the island nation's gem industry.

A group of anonymous owners unveiled a 3,563-carat purple star sapphire in Colombo on Saturday, saying the round, polished gem is the world's largest documented natural stone of its kind and that they are ready to sell it for a price estimated at $300 million to $400 million.

The gem, named "Star of Pure Land," was presented to the media by Ashan Amarasinghe, a consultant gemologist who assessed the stone.

"This is the largest purple star sapphire of its kind," Amarasinghe said, adding that the gem "shows a well-defined asterism. It has six rays asterism. That's something special out of all the other stones."

The stone was found in a gem pit near Rathnapura — a town in southern Sri Lanka known as the "city of gems" — in 2023, according to one of the owners. The owners purchased it along with other gems that year, and recognized it as an exceptional specimen approximately two years later. Two independent laboratories have since certified the stone.

The owners are identified publicly only as the "Star of Pure Land Team" and have not revealed their identities for security reasons.

International valuers have estimated the stone's worth at $300 million to $400 million, Amarasinghe said. Sri Lankan sapphires are internationally recognized for their color, clarity, and optical quality.
