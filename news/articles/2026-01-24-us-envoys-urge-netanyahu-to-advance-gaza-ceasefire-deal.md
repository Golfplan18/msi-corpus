The Trump-brokered ceasefire has reached a critical juncture. While the U.S. works to advance the agreement, Netanyahu faces domestic pressure from hostage families demanding the return of remains before Israel fully withdraws. The reopening of the Rafah border crossing—the principal indicator of progress into the second phase—hinges on navigating these competing demands.

Top U.S. envoys met with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Saturday to press for advancement of the Gaza ceasefire into its second phase, the prime minister's office said. The meeting with Jared Kushner, a Middle East adviser to President Donald Trump, and Steve Witkoff, Trump's special envoy, centered on recovering the remains of the last remaining hostage in Gaza and planning next steps for demilitarizing the territory.

## Border Crossing and Second Phase Prospects

The reopening of the Rafah border crossing between Gaza and Egypt is the principal indicator of progress into the second phase. Ali Shaath, head of a future technocratic government in Gaza, said the crossing will open in both directions this coming week. Israel has not confirmed this timeline, saying only that it would consider opening the crossing this week.

Border reopening would enable humanitarian access and accelerate Gaza's reconstruction. Egypt's top diplomat pressed for immediate opening on Saturday, speaking by phone with Nickolay Mladenov, a Bulgarian diplomat serving as Trump's high representative for Gaza. The two discussed deploying an international monitoring force at the crossing, enabling Palestinians to enter and exit the territory, and planning Israeli force withdrawals.

"Implementing the second phase is a key entry point to launch Gaza's reconstruction," Egypt's Foreign Ministry said in a statement. The ministry did not specify timing for when the crossing would open for civilian travelers or for evacuation of sick and wounded persons. Israel is expected to address the crossing's status during its Cabinet meeting on Sunday.

## Hostage Families Demand Return of Remains

The family of Ran Gvili, whose body remains in Gaza, called Saturday for increased pressure on Hamas to force a resolution. "President Trump himself stated this week in Davos that Hamas knows exactly where our son is being held," the family said. "Hamas is deceiving the international community and refusing to return our son, the last remaining hostage, in what constitutes a clear violation of the agreement it signed."

Hamas said Wednesday it has provided all available information on Gvili's remains to ceasefire mediators. The organization alleged that Israel is obstructing search efforts in areas under Israeli control in Gaza. The ceasefire took effect on October 10.

## Civilian Deaths Continue Despite Truce

On Saturday, an Israeli military strike killed two Palestinian teenagers in Gaza. According to Shifa Hospital in Gaza City, the boys—cousins aged 13 and 15—were searching for firewood when struck. They died in an area Israel's military designated as safe for Palestinians, approximately 500 meters from the Yellow Line separating Israeli-controlled eastern Gaza from the rest of the territory.

Israel's military said it had targeted militants who crossed the Yellow Line and planted explosives threatening Israeli troops. The military denied that those killed were children.

Since the ceasefire began, more than 480 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli fire, according to Gaza's Health Ministry. The ministry, part of the Hamas-led government, maintains detailed casualty records that U.N. agencies and independent experts consider generally reliable. Israel disputes these figures but has not provided its own casualty count.
