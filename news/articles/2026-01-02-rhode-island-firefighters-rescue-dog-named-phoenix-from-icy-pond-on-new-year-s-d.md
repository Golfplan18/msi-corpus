A yellow Labrador named Phoenix fell through the ice on a pond in Westerly, Rhode Island, on New Year's Day and had to be pulled to safety by firefighters wearing ice rescue suits, the Misquamicut Fire Department said Thursday. The dog was out for a walk with his owner when he wandered onto a thin layer of ice and broke through to the center of the pond. Phoenix was declared free of injuries, and both fire departments involved called the rescue "a successful first call of 2026."

The rescue took place in sub-freezing conditions — 26 degrees Fahrenheit with a wind chill of 14 degrees, according to the National Weather Service — and prompted the Misquamicut Fire Department to remind residents that no ice over a body of water should be treated as safe.

WESTERLY, R.I. — A yellow Labrador named Phoenix was rescued from an icy pond on New Year's Day in Rhode Island after he wandered onto thin ice during a morning walk and broke through to the center, the Misquamicut Fire Department said.

Volunteer firefighters and emergency officials from the Misquamicut and Watch Hill fire departments responded early Thursday morning. When they arrived, they found Phoenix struggling in slushy, icy water and unable to reach shore on his own, according to the department.

Rescuers from both departments pulled on ice rescue suits — designed to protect the body from frigid temperatures — and entered the pond. They brought Phoenix to safety without further incident.

The National Weather Service reported an air temperature of 26 degrees Fahrenheit around 9 a.m. Thursday, with wind chill pushing the effective temperature to 14 degrees.

"It was the chillest dog I've ever seen in my life," Steve Howard, deputy chief of the Misquamicut Fire Department, said in a phone interview Friday. "The dog never made a sound. He was pretty chill."

Firefighters were evaluated for possible hypothermia following the rescue but did not require treatment. Phoenix was declared free of injuries.

Howard said he followed up with the dog's owner later that day and received a characteristically low-key update on the patient's recovery.

"He got a little bit of extra food last night," Howard said. "And he took a little nap."

The two fire departments described the incident as "a successful first call of 2026" in a statement posted to Facebook.

The Misquamicut Fire Department used the occasion to issue a broader safety reminder about frozen bodies of water.

"No ice is ever safe. Our firefighters train extensively for cold water and ice rescues, but these situations are extremely dangerous," the department said.
