The strikes, conducted Tuesday and Wednesday, brought the total number of known U.S. boat attacks to 35 and the death toll to at least 115 since the campaign began in early September, according to figures announced by the Trump administration. The latest strikes and the CIA operation represent a significant escalation in the Trump administration's dual-front pressure campaign against alleged narco-traffickers at sea and, now, inside Venezuela — whose president, Nicolás Maduro, faces narco-terrorism charges in the United States. The U.S. military struck five more alleged drug-smuggling boats over two days on Tuesday and Wednesday, killing eight people while others who jumped overboard may have survived, U.S. Southern Command said Wednesday.

Southern Command, which oversees military operations in South America, did not reveal where the attacks occurred. Previous strikes in the campaign have taken place in the Caribbean Sea and in the eastern Pacific Ocean.

## Tuesday's strike: convoy of three boats

A video of Tuesday's attack, posted by Southern Command on social media, showed three boats traveling in close formation — an unusual arrangement, the military noted. Southern Command said the vessels were in a convoy along known narco-trafficking routes and "had transferred narcotics between the three vessels prior to the strikes." The military did not provide evidence to support the claim.

Three people were killed when the first boat was struck. People in the other two boats jumped overboard and moved away from the vessels before those boats were attacked, Southern Command said. It notified the U.S. Coast Guard to activate search and rescue efforts, though its statement did not confirm whether those who jumped were recovered.

The Coast Guard notification was significant. In early September, U.S. forces struck a disabled boat a second time, killing survivors of the initial attack. Some Democratic lawmakers and legal experts said the follow-up strike constituted a crime; the Trump administration and some Republican lawmakers maintain it was legal.

## Wednesday's strikes: two more boats, five killed

U.S. forces struck two more boats on Wednesday, killing five people who were allegedly smuggling drugs along known trafficking routes, Southern Command said in a separate statement. It did not reveal the body of water in which the attacks occurred and provided no evidence of the alleged trafficking. Videos posted alongside the statement showed a boat in the water and explosions.

## CIA strikes inside Venezuela for the first time

As MSI previously reported, the five latest strikes extend a campaign that has now produced at least 35 known boat attacks and at least 115 deaths since early September, according to figures announced by the Trump administration — <a href="/articles/2025-12-31-us-military-strikes-five-more-alleged-drug-boats-killing-8">a toll that has grown steadily as the operations continued through December</a>.

Separately, the CIA carried out a drone strike last week against a docking area in Venezuela believed to have been used by drug cartels, according to two people familiar with details of the classified operation who requested anonymity to discuss it. The strike was the first known direct U.S. operation on Venezuelan soil since the maritime campaign began in September.

Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, who faces narco-terrorism charges in the United States, has been the target of a broader administration pressure campaign. Along with the boat strikes, the Trump administration has built up military forces in the region as part of that effort.

## Trump's justification

President Donald Trump has defended the campaign as a necessary escalation to stem the flow of drugs into the United States, asserting that the country is engaged in an "armed conflict" with drug cartels.
