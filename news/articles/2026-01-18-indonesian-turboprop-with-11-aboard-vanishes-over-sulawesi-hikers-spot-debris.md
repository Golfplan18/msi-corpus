A turboprop aircraft carrying 11 people lost contact with air traffic control Saturday afternoon over the mountains of South Sulawesi province, Indonesia, while on approach to a regional airport, officials said. Hikers on a nearby peak subsequently reported finding scattered debris, markings consistent with the airline's logo, and small fires, prompting rescue teams to redirect their efforts to steep terrain in the area.

Indonesian authorities deployed air force helicopters, drones, and ground units after the ATR 42-500 operated by Indonesia Air Transport vanished from radar over Maros district. Three of the 11 aboard were government fisheries officials conducting an airborne maritime surveillance mission.

A turboprop aircraft carrying 11 people vanished from radar Saturday afternoon over the mountains of South Sulawesi province, Indonesia, while on approach to a regional airport, officials said. Hikers on a nearby peak subsequently reported finding scattered debris and small fires, prompting rescue teams to redirect their efforts to steep terrain in the area.

The ATR 42-500, operated by Indonesia Air Transport, was flying from Yogyakarta, on the island of Java, to Makassar, the capital of South Sulawesi province, when it disappeared at 1:17 p.m. local time (0517 GMT), said Endah Purnama Sari, a spokesperson for Indonesia's Transportation Ministry. The plane was last tracked in the Leang-Leang area of Maros district, a mountainous part of South Sulawesi, she said.

Radio contact was lost after air traffic controllers instructed the crew to correct the aircraft's approach alignment. "After the last ATC instructions, radio contact was lost and controllers declared the emergency distress phase," Sari said.

Hikers on Mount Bulusaraung reported finding scattered debris, a logo consistent with Indonesia Air Transport markings, and small fires still burning at the scene. Maj. Gen. Bangun Nawoko, the Hasanuddin military commander for South Sulawesi, said those reports were passed to authorities and were being verified by rescue teams working to reach the area.

"The sightings were reported to authorities and are being verified by rescue teams attempting to reach the area," Nawoko said.

Steep terrain at Bulusaraung National Park, which links Maros and Pangkep districts, complicated ground access for rescue teams, Nawoko said. Multiple search teams supported by air force helicopters, drones, and ground units were deployed to the mountains, Sari said.

The plane carried eight crew members and three passengers. Minister of Marine Affairs and Fisheries Sakti Wahyu Trenggono told reporters at a late Saturday news conference that the three passengers were employees of his ministry, aboard as part of an airborne maritime surveillance mission supporting Indonesia's fisheries management operations.

Weather conditions at the time of the disappearance showed clouds and approximately 8 kilometers (nearly 5 miles) of visibility, Sari said. Rescue teams were believed to have focused their ground search on mountains where the aircraft was thought to have deviated from its approach to Sultan Hasanuddin International Airport, she said.

Indonesia, the world's largest archipelago nation, with more than 280 million people spread across more than 17,000 islands, relies heavily on air transport and ferry service to connect its territory. The country has experienced repeated transportation accidents on land, sea, and air, which the Associated Press attributed to poorly enforced safety standards.
