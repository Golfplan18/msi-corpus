## Background on Johnson’s Jan. 6 case

Adam Johnson has filed to run as a Republican for an at-large seat on the Manatee County Commission in Florida, landing in the news on the fifth anniversary of the Jan. 6, 2021, U.S. Capitol riot. Johnson was convicted after he grabbed then-House Speaker Nancy Pelosi’s podium during the riot and posed for photographs, according to the Associated Press.

The AP reported that Johnson was photographed smiling and waving as he carried the podium. The outlet also said prosecutors described him as placing the podium in the center of the Capitol Rotunda, posing for pictures and pretending to make a speech.

## Johnson links his candidacy to the date and the “buzz”

Johnson filed Tuesday, the AP said, and told WWSB-TV that it was “not a coincidence” that he filed for office on Jan. 6. He said, “it’s definitely good for getting the buzz out there.”

The AP also reported that Johnson’s campaign logo is an outline of the viral photograph of him carrying Pelosi’s podium.

## Conviction, sentence, and his shifting framing

Johnson pleaded guilty in 2021 to entering and remaining in a restricted building or ground, a misdemeanor, the AP reported. The outlet said Johnson equated the offense to “jaywalking” in an interview.

In the AP report, Johnson said, “I think I exercised my First Amendment right to speak and protest.” The outlet also said that after driving home from the Capitol riot, Johnson bragged that he “broke the internet” and was “finally famous,” as described by prosecutors.

Johnson served 75 days in prison followed by one year of supervised release, the AP reported. The judge also ordered Johnson to pay a $5,000 fine and perform 200 hours of community service, according to the Associated Press.

At sentencing, the AP reported that Johnson told U.S. District Judge Reggie Walton that posing with Pelosi’s podium was a “very stupid idea.” The outlet said Johnson now regrets his action only because of the prison sentence, and it cited his statement: “I walked into a building, I took a picture with a piece of furniture, and I left.”

## Filing in a deeply Republican county

Four other Republicans have filed to run so far in the Aug. 18 primary, the AP reported, and the incumbent is not seeking reelection. Johnson is the latest Jan. 6-related figure to seek office, the AP said, and it noted that at least three people implicated in the riot ran unsuccessfully for Congress in 2024 as Republicans.

The AP also reported that there are signs the Republican Party is welcoming back more people convicted of Jan. 6 offenses after Donald Trump pardoned some of them.

## Wider political context in Florida, including Jake Lang

The Associated Press also pointed to Jake Lang, saying he announced his candidacy for Florida’s vacant U.S. Senate seat after Marco Rubio. The AP reported that Lang had been charged with assaulting an officer, civil disorder and other crimes before he was pardoned.

## Johnson’s local priorities and legal dispute with Manatee County

In addition to his candidacy, Johnson has been involved in litigation involving Manatee County, the AP reported. The outlet said that in March 2025, Johnson sued Manatee County and six commissioners after the county decided not to seek attorney’s fees from someone who sued the county and dropped the lawsuit.

The AP said the county called Johnson’s claims “completely meritless and unsupported by law.”

In statements associated with his campaign, the AP reported that Johnson objects to high property taxes and overdevelopment in the county south of Tampa and said current leaders are wasteful.

Johnson said he expects more scrutiny than other candidates. The AP cited him saying, “I will be more heavily scrutinized than any other candidate who is running in this race,” and added that he described it as “a positive and a good takeaway for every single citizen,” arguing that voters “for once in our life, we will know our local politicians who are doing things.”
