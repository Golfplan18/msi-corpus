## Primary season upends both parties' original campaign roadmaps

When midterm campaigning began, each major political party had a clear game plan, the Associated Press reported. Republicans would emphasize border security and "an economy that was finally shaking off years of high inflation." Democrats would focus attention on "a deeply unpopular President Donald Trump" to energize their voters.

It has not worked out that way, according to the AP. With a little more than three months to go, both parties are "grappling with self-inflicted wounds and messy missteps" that have complicated their paths to victory.

The AP reported that the messy primaries have made the midterm outcome unusually difficult to predict. Both parties entered the cycle with straightforward strategic assumptions — Republicans would leverage border security and economic messaging against vulnerable Democrats, while Democrats would make the election a referendum on Trump's second term.

Instead, the AP reported, the primary season has produced a landscape in which neither party's original game plan has unfolded as expected. The structural dynamics of the current political environment have produced primary contests that neither party's leadership fully controls.

As the primary calendar continues through the summer, both parties must reconcile the candidates that emerge from intraparty conflict with the general-election strategies they originally planned. The AP reported that with November approaching, both parties face paths to victory that are considerably more complicated than they appeared when campaigning began.
