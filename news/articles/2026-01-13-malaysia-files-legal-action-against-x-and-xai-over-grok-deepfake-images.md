KUALA LUMPUR, Malaysia — Malaysian authorities said Tuesday they will take legal action against Elon Musk's social media platform X and its artificial intelligence subsidiary xAI, accusing both companies of failing to remove sexually explicit and nonconsensual images generated by the Grok chatbot.

The Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission said it served formal notices to X and xAI this month ordering removal of the harmful content but received no response. The commission said it has appointed a lawyer and that legal proceedings would begin soon.

The announcement makes Malaysia one of the first governments to pursue litigation over Grok's image-generation capabilities, as regulators in the United Kingdom, the European Union, and India separately escalate pressure on a tool critics say fails to prevent its use to produce explicit material involving women and children.

## Scope of the complaint

The commission said it identified misuse of Grok to generate and distribute harmful content "including sexually explicit, indecent, extremely offensive as well as non-consensual manipulated images," according to its statement.

"Content allegedly involving women and children is a matter of great concern. Such conduct is against Malaysian law and undermines the security commitments" stated by the companies, the commission said.

Malaysia and Indonesia were the first countries to block access to Grok, the commission said, a step taken days before Tuesday's announcement of impending legal action.

## Grok's image-generation features

Grok was launched in 2023 and is free to use on X. An image-generation feature called Grok Imagine was added last year and included what the company described as a "spicy mode" capable of producing adult content.

The tool has drawn criticism for generating manipulated images, including depictions of women in sexually explicit poses as well as images involving children, according to the Associated Press.

Last week, Grok limited image generation and editing to paying users following a global backlash over sexualized deepfakes, but critics said the change did not fully address the problem.

## International regulatory pressure

The United Kingdom said Monday it was moving to criminalize nudification apps. Britain's media regulator also launched an investigation into whether Grok broke the law by allowing users to share sexualized images of children.

Grok has also faced scrutiny in the European Union and India, according to the AP.

Musk and his companies have not publicly commented on the Southeast Asian restrictions. When contacted by media, xAI responded with an automated reply: "Legacy Media Lies."
