HAVANA — Cuba's two main groups of Santería priests released their annual Letra del Año on Friday, warning of heightened violence, armed conflict, and disease in 2026. Both the Comisión de la Letra del Año de 10 de Octubre and the Asociación Yoruba named Oggún — the deity governing metals, weapons, roads, and conflict — as the ruling spiritual force for the year ahead.

The dual forecasts arrive as Caribbean tensions have mounted, with U.S. naval deployments and threats from President Donald Trump's administration against Venezuela polarizing regional alliances. Santería priests cited those tensions explicitly in their warnings. Santería is Cuba's most widely practiced religion and has spread to communities across Latin America, Spain, and the United States.

"The danger of war is there," said Victor Betancourt, a babalawo, or Santería priest, who helps organize the Comisión's ceremony. "Cuba should use diplomacy. We would be the biggest losers," he said of any military confrontation.

## The ruling signs

The Comisión identified Ogunda Masa as the ruling sign accompanying Oggún for 2026. Its prophetic prayer reads: "loss installed by inattention to what was indicated," according to a document the priests provided to the Associated Press.

Priests interpreted the sign as a warning that the consequences of accumulated, unresolved problems — including sanitation conditions in cities — would become apparent. Oya serves as the accompanying deity to Oggún in the year's forecast, the priests said.

The Comisión's document predicted a rise in infectious, stomach, and liver diseases, along with a proliferation of fires and accidents, an increase in violent and criminal acts, and "significant changes within government."

The Asociación Yoruba released a separate Letra del Año also naming Oggún as the ruling deity, though under a different sign — Ogunda Otrupom — which similarly warned of conflict, violence, and disease.

## Warnings, not remedies

Babalawo Lázaro Cuesta told reporters that "the Letra del Año does not have the capacity [by itself] to resolve problems, only to warn of them," and called on governments and individuals to take actions to improve living conditions and avoid conflict or war.

## About the ceremony

Thousands of Cubans await the Letra del Año each year. Religious households across the island look to the proclamations for guidance on rituals and recommended actions for the months ahead.

Groups of babalawos — the patriarchs or heads of religious households — traditionally gather on December 31 to perform drum ceremonies and make offerings before drawing the Letra. They then interpret its prophecies and announce them in the first days of January.

Santería developed through the syncretism of Spanish colonial religious traditions and those of enslaved Africans brought to the island. The practice has spread to Mexico, Venezuela, Brazil, Spain, and the United States, where significant Cuban communities maintain the tradition.
