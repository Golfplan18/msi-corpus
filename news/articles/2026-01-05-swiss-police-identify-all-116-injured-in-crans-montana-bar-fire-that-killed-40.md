CRANS-MONTANA, Switzerland — Swiss police said Monday they had identified all 116 people injured in the fire that tore through a crowded New Year's celebration at Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana, revising a previous count of 119 after three people admitted to hospitals on the night of the disaster were linked in error to the blaze. The fire, which broke out at about 1:30 a.m. on New Year's Day, killed 40 people — the youngest of them 14 years old — and left 83 of the 116 injured still hospitalized as of Monday, according to a police statement.

Swiss authorities have opened a criminal investigation into the bar's two managers, who are suspected of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm, and involuntarily causing a fire, according to the Valais region's chief prosecutor. Investigators believe festive sparkling candles placed atop Champagne bottles ignited the blaze when they came too close to the ceiling.

## Victims from more than a dozen countries

The injured include 68 Swiss citizens and 21 French nationals, along with 10 Italians, four Serbs, two Poles, and one person each from Australia, Belgium, Bosnia-Herzegovina, the Czech Republic, Luxembourg, Portugal, and the Republic of Congo, police said. Four dual nationals were also among the injured: one holding French and Finnish citizenship, one French and Italian, one Swiss and Belgian, and one Italian and Filipino.

Police said 83 of the 116 injured remained in hospitals but did not provide further details or specify their ages.

## Identification complicated by burn severity

The severity of burns made it difficult to identify some victims of the disaster, police said, requiring families to supply authorities with DNA samples. Authorities announced Sunday evening that they had completed identification of all 40 people who died.

On Monday, Italian authorities flew home the bodies of five victims from the airport in Sion, the regional capital. Officials stood quietly as Swiss police pallbearers carried coffins through a line of firefighters and soldiers to an Italian Air Force C-130 cargo plane. Mourners embraced before relatives boarded the aircraft.

## Criminal investigation

The Valais region's chief prosecutor said the two bar managers are suspected of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm, and involuntarily causing a fire. Investigators have said they believe sparkling decorative candles placed on Champagne bottles to mark the New Year ignited the fire when they came too close to the ceiling of the crowded venue.
