Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. announced Monday the discovery of a new natural gas deposit northwest of Palawan province, near the disputed South China Sea, in a find that could help shield the country's most populous region from a looming power crisis. The deposit lies within Philippine waters, about 5 kilometers east of the aging Malampaya gas field, which supplies more than 20 percent of Luzon's electricity and is projected to decline sharply within years.

The discovery, named Malampaya East 1, is estimated to contain about 98 billion cubic feet (2.7 billion cubic meters) of gas and was completed without accidents or environmental incidents, Marcos said. It extends the operational life of the Malampaya complex at a moment when Philippine energy planners had been preparing for supply shortfalls — while sidestepping, at least for now, the Chinese opposition that has blocked deeper South China Sea exploration elsewhere.

## The deposit and what it could supply

Malampaya East 1 was found by a consortium working about 5 kilometers (3.1 miles) east of the main Malampaya field, where commercial gas production began more than two decades ago. Initial tests showed 60 million cubic feet (1.6 million cubic meters) of gas could be extracted daily from the well, Marcos said, though he did not say when commercial production could start.

That output, if sustained, could eventually supply power to more than 5.7 million households or nearly 200,000 schools for a year, Marcos said.

"This helps Malampaya's contribution and strengthens our domestic gas supply for many years to come," Marcos said. "Aside from the natural gas, the discovery also includes condensate, which is a high-value liquid fuel."

Marcos said further tests and an additional drilling in the area would be done "to pursue more potential gas resources."

He also said Filipino engineers led the drilling effort. "We're proud that Filipinos led this drilling and, more importantly, they completed it without any accident or environmental incidents," Marcos said.

## Malampaya's role and the power-crisis concern

The Malampaya gas-to-power facility has been a cornerstone of Philippine electricity generation, supplying more than 20 percent of Luzon's electricity. Luzon is the most populous island in the Philippine archipelago and home to Manila, the capital, and the country's main financial and business district.

Experts had predicted the existing Malampaya field could run out of gas within a few years, raising concerns about a potential power crisis in the region. Marcos extended Malampaya's exploration contract by 15 years in 2023.

The new deposit falls within the Philippines' Exclusive Economic Zone — a 200-nautical-mile (370-kilometer) stretch from the Philippine coastline where the country holds exclusive rights to explore and harness resources under the 1982 United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea.

## South China Sea context

The announcement comes against the backdrop of unresolved territorial disputes across the South China Sea. China claims sovereignty over virtually the entire waterway and has reinforced its presence there through coast guard and naval patrols in recent years.

Philippine efforts to explore for oil and gas in another offshore area, the Reed Bank, have been stalled for years because of opposition from Beijing, which claims sovereignty over that zone as well. The Reed Bank lies west of Palawan, in the fringe of the South China Sea. China has similarly opposed Vietnam's oil and gas exploration in the disputed region.

Malaysia, Brunei and Taiwan have also been involved in long-running territorial standoffs in the area.

The Malampaya field, by contrast, lies within waters the Philippines considers part of its sovereign EEZ, providing a legal basis for the exploration now yielding results even as broader South China Sea disputes remain unresolved.
