PARIS — The French government on Wednesday defended its decision to grant citizenship to actor George Clooney, his wife Amal Clooney, and their twin children, saying the couple "contribute, through their distinguished actions, to France's international influence and cultural outreach."

The naturalization of the Kentucky-born star of the "Ocean's" series of heist films, human rights lawyer Amal Clooney, and their 8-year-old twins, Ella and Alexander, was announced over the weekend in the Journal Officiel, where French government decrees are published.

The defense came after Marie-Pierre Vedrenne, a junior minister at the Interior Ministry, publicly raised concerns that some of the Clooneys' new compatriots might view the couple as having received special treatment. "The message being sent is not good," Vedrenne said in an interview with broadcaster France Info. "There is an issue of fairness that, in my eyes, is absolutely essential."

Vedrenne's superior at the Interior Ministry, Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez, said he signed the naturalization decree and offered a different verdict. "It's a big chance for our country," Nuñez said.

## The legal basis

The Foreign Ministry said the Clooneys were eligible under a French law that allows for the naturalization of foreign nationals who contribute to France's international influence and economic well-being. It argued that France's cinema industry will benefit from George Clooney's profile as a global film star and said that Amal Clooney regularly works with academic institutions and international organizations in France.

"They maintain strong personal, professional and family ties with our country," the ministry said. "Like many French citizens, we are delighted to welcome Georges and Amal Clooney into the national community" — giving the actor's first name the French spelling with an added "s."

## Life in France

The Clooneys purchased an estate in France in 2021, which the actor has described as the family's primary residence. French media have reported the family lives part-time at a luxury 18th-century villa outside the town of Brignoles in southern France, where French privacy laws shield their children from unauthorized photographs.

Whether George Clooney, 64, retained his American citizenship following the naturalization was not clear. Amal Clooney, 47, was born in Lebanon and raised in the United Kingdom. She was naturalized under her maiden name, Amal Alamuddin. The twins were born in London.

George Clooney, who has been promoting his film "Jay Kelly," has described his own French as "horrible, horrible" and said he is learning the language through a language-learning app. He said his wife and children speak it well.

"They speak French in front of me so that they can say terrible things about me to my face and I don't know," Clooney joked in an interview with French broadcaster Canal+.

In an interview with Esquire in October, Clooney said concerns about celebrity culture and privacy drove his decision to raise his children in France. "I was worried about raising our kids in L.A., in the culture of Hollywood," he said. "I don't want them to be walking around worried about paparazzi. I don't want them being compared to somebody else's famous kids."

Growing up in France, he added, "they have dinner with grown-ups and have to take their dishes in. They have a much better life."
