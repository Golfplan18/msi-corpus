Dermatologists are pushing back against multi-step shower routines promoted on social media and TikTok, saying the elaborate regimens can strip skin of its protective natural oils and are largely unnecessary. Three skin experts told the Associated Press that a simple daily shower — not an extended, product-heavy ritual — is what the skin actually needs.

"I'm kind of appalled by the shower routines," said Dr. Olga Bunimovich, a practicing dermatologist and assistant professor at the University of Pittsburgh.

Experts say a short lukewarm shower with a fragrance-free cleanser and a moisturizer applied afterward is sufficient for most people, and that daily exfoliation, double cleansing, and antibacterial soaps can do more harm than good.

## What dermatologists actually recommend

A daily shower with lukewarm water and a fragrance-free hypoallergenic cleanser, followed by a hydrating lotion or oil applied to damp skin, will do the trick, dermatologists said. Hot water and extended time in the shower strip the skin of the natural oils it needs to maintain its barrier, leaving it vulnerable to dryness and irritation.

Dr. Nicole Negbenebor, a dermatologic surgeon at University of Iowa Health Care, said the skin's function as the body's primary external barrier means it can be harmed by aggressive cleansing routines. "It's one of the biggest barriers you have," she said. "It's you in your natural elements. So you want to treat it right, and then sometimes there can be too much of a good thing."

On oils: Negbenebor said they function as a sealant rather than a moisturizer. Water hydrates the skin; oil applied to damp skin locks in that moisture. The two work together but serve different roles.

## Double cleansing and antibacterial soap

Some influencers promote double cleansing — an oil-based cleanser to break down residue, followed by a water-based cleanser — as a necessary step for full-body skin care. Dermatologists said the practice, when applied to the whole body, is unnecessary.

"Not only do I not think that we need to double cleanse our skin … people overuse soap all the time," Bunimovich said. "You should not be soaping up all of your skin, period." Soap, she said, should be limited to the privates and skin folds.

Antibacterial soaps — popular in some social media routines — dry out skin too much for daily use, according to dermatologists. An exception applies to people with hidradenitis suppurativa, an autoimmune skin condition that causes painful boils and abscesses, for whom antibacterial soap can be beneficial.

## Exfoliation

Exfoliation removes dead skin cells and is beneficial, dermatologists said — but daily manual exfoliation using body scrubs or rough loofahs can cause irritation, particularly for people with dry skin, eczema, or acne. Rashes after exfoliation may signal the practice is too frequent.

A gentler alternative involves products containing lactic or glycolic acid, used sparingly rather than daily.

Dr. Lisa Akintilo, a dermatologist at NYU Langone Health, said routines that combine both cleansing and hydration represent a meaningful improvement for many people. "It's nice to hear about skincare routines that do involve both cleansing and also providing hydration," she said, "because that's missing in a lot of people's skin care routine."

## Water use

Showers account for nearly 17% of Americans' indoor water use, the Environmental Protection Agency says. Multiple states facing moderate to severe drought conditions called for water conservation measures in 2025, including shorter showers.

Dermatologists said the routine they recommend — enough time to clean the body with lukewarm water — supports both skin health and conservation.
