Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei branded U.S. President Donald Trump a "criminal" on Saturday for supporting protesters who have challenged the Islamic Republic since late December, and said the crackdown had left "several thousand" people dead — the first acknowledgment by an Iranian leader of the scope of the casualties.

Khamenei, in a speech broadcast by state television, blamed demonstrators rather than security forces for those deaths, claiming protesters arrived armed with live ammunition imported from abroad and had destroyed mosques and educational centers. "Through hurting people, they killed several thousand of them," he said.

The statement marked the first public acknowledgment by an Iranian leader of the scale of casualties from the protests that began Dec. 28. The U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency has reported at least 3,308 people killed — a figure the AP has been unable to independently confirm — making the crackdown the deadliest wave of unrest in Iran in decades, according to the agency. Iran has been under a near-total internet blackout since Jan. 8; limited connectivity briefly returned Saturday.

## Khamenei's accusations

In his speech, Khamenei cited Trump's statement that "We do support you, we do support you militarily" as the basis for calling the U.S. president a criminal.

"We do consider the U.S. president a criminal, because of casualties and damages, because of accusations against the Iranian nation," Khamenei said, who as Supreme Leader has final say on all state matters. He described protesters as "foot soldiers" of the United States and reiterated accusations that the U.S. seeks domination over Iran's economic and political resources.

Khamenei said rioters were armed with live ammunition imported from abroad but did not name any source countries. "We do not plan, we do not take the country toward war," he said. "But we do not release domestic offenders, worse than domestic offenders; there are international offenders. We do not let them alone either." He urged officials to pursue the cases.

## Trump responds

Trump, in a Saturday interview with Politico, called Khamenei "a sick man who should run his country properly and stop killing people" and described Iran as "the worst place to live anywhere in the world because of poor leadership."

"It's time to look for new leadership in Iran," Trump added.

The exchange followed a shift in Trump's tone from the previous day, when he said Iran had "canceled the hanging of over 800 people" and that he "greatly respect[ed] the fact that they canceled." He did not clarify the source of that information. His remarks were seen as a possible signal that he was pulling back from the prospect of military action.

Tehran Prosecutor General Ali Salehi dismissed those comments. "Trump always makes futile and irrelevant statements," Salehi said, via the official IRNA news agency. "Our attitude is severe, preventive and fast." He did not elaborate.

In recent days, Trump had told protesting Iranians that "help is on the way" and said his administration would "act accordingly" if killings continued or if detained protesters were executed.

## Uneasy calm; internet briefly restored

Iran has returned to an uneasy calm after the crackdown. No protests had been reported for days in Tehran, where shopping and street life returned to normal, and Iranian state media reported no new unrest.

Authorities blocked all internet access on Jan. 8. Text messaging and very limited internet services briefly resumed Saturday in parts of Iran, according to witnesses, and some users reported limited access to international services via virtual private networks. Internet monitoring service Cloudflare and access advocacy group NetBlocks reported slight increases in connectivity Saturday morning. Iran's semiofficial Mehr news agency also reported limited access but offered no explanation.

## Pahlavi call goes unanswered

A call by Iran's exiled Crown Prince Reza Pahlavi for protesters to take to the streets from Saturday to Monday had not been heeded as of Saturday afternoon, according to the AP. Pahlavi, whose father was overthrown in Iran's 1979 Islamic Revolution, has support among die-hard monarchists in the diaspora but has struggled to gain wider appeal within Iran.

## Diplomatic context

Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian, in a phone conversation with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Friday, accused the U.S. and Israel of meddling in the unrest — a charge Iranian officials have repeated throughout the weeks-long crisis. Iranian officials have also repeatedly accused the U.S. and Israel of fomenting unrest in the country.

The death toll reported by HRANA — which relies on a network of activists inside Iran to confirm fatalities and has been active throughout years of prior demonstrations — exceeds that of any other round of protests or unrest in Iran in decades, the agency said.
