ABOARD AIR FORCE ONE — President Donald Trump said Sunday he is inclined to bar ExxonMobil from Venezuela's oil sector after the company's chief executive voiced deep skepticism at a meeting with oil industry executives about investing in the country, creating a public rift over a centerpiece of the administration's Venezuela strategy.

Trump made the remarks to reporters as he departed West Palm Beach, Florida.

"I didn't like Exxon's response," Trump said, according to the Associated Press. "They're playing too smart."

The friction highlights the gap between the Trump administration's push to use American oil companies to rebuild Venezuela's petroleum infrastructure and the private sector's wariness about the country's history of asset seizures, ongoing U.S. sanctions, and chronic political instability.

On Friday, Trump gathered oil industry executives and sought to reassure them about doing business in Venezuela. He told those assembled that their companies would deal directly with the United States rather than with Venezuela's government, the AP reported. Some in attendance remained unconvinced.

Darren Woods, chief executive of ExxonMobil, the largest American oil company, said at the Friday meeting that Venezuela's current conditions ruled out investment. "If we look at the commercial structures and frameworks in place today in Venezuela, today it is not investable," Woods said, according to the AP.

Also on Friday, Trump signed an executive order intended to shield Venezuelan oil revenues from use in judicial proceedings. The order was made public Saturday. It stated that seizure of such funds could "undermine critical U.S. efforts to secure economic and political stability in Venezuela," the AP reported.

Venezuela has a history of state asset seizures, faces ongoing U.S. sanctions, and has endured decades of political uncertainty — conditions Woods said made current investment untenable.

Recruiting American oil companies to invest in Venezuela and help rebuild its petroleum infrastructure has been a top priority for the Trump administration following the capture of former President Nicolás Maduro. The White House has framed its effort to manage Venezuela in economic terms: Trump has seized vessels transporting Venezuelan crude, announced that the U.S. is taking control of sales of 30 to 50 million barrels of previously sanctioned Venezuelan oil, and stated plans to control such sales globally on an indefinite basis, the AP reported.
