Haiti's Transitional Presidential Council voted to remove Prime Minister Alix Didier Fils-Aimé on Thursday, council members Edgard Leblanc Fils and Leslie Voltaire announced Friday, deepening political turmoil in a nation where gang violence controls most of the capital and large swaths of the interior. The council said it would find a replacement within 30 days, defying calls from the U.S. State Department to maintain stability in the leadership of the Caribbean nation.

The removal of Fils-Aimé, whom the council appointed just two months ago, signals deepening divisions within the transitional government as it approaches its scheduled February 7 dissolution date. The move threatens to complicate Haiti's path to elections currently set for August and December—the first opportunity to restore a fully elected government since President Jovenel Moïse's assassination in July 2021.

## Council Defies U.S. Calls

The U.S. State Department had emphasized Thursday that maintaining Fils-Aimé in office was "fundamental" to Haiti's efforts to end gang violence. Secretary of State Marco Rubio spoke with Fils-Aimé to reaffirm White House support, stating that gang violence "can only be stopped with firm, consistent leadership with the full support of the Haitian people." The department warned that politicians backing "violent bands" would pay a high price.

The council provided no public explanation for the removal. Leblanc said the decision was "in the interest of the country" and that international partners would have to respect the council's autonomous choice. Voltaire stressed that although the council recognizes Fils-Aimé's "capacity and enthusiasm," "the population is not getting what it needs."

Voltaire also pushed back against international pressure, saying the council had initially selected Fils-Aimé without outside interference. "Everyone is looking for a Haitian solution to the crisis," he said, "but when we start to find a Haitian solution to the crisis, the international community intervenes with all its might."

## Violence Spirals Out of Control

Gang violence has escalated dramatically since Moïse's death, with armed groups estimated to control approximately 90 percent of Port-au-Prince and large areas of central Haiti. The United Nations reported more than 8,100 murders between January and November 2025, though the organization cautioned the figure likely understates the actual death toll.

## Transition Timeline Threatened

Michael Deibert, author of two books on Haiti, warned that failure to dissolve the council by February 7 "could add another element of volatility and uncertainty to the political situation in a country that is already struggling with a breakdown of constitutional order and incredibly severe crises of violence and insecurity." He noted that political alliances in Haiti are "very fluid and transactional," suggesting further upheaval may unfold in coming weeks.

Of the council's nine members, only Leblanc and Voltaire appeared at Friday's announcement. Seven members have voting rights, with five votes needed for removal. It remained unclear whether Laurent Saint-Cyr, the council's current leader, supported the action. Saint-Cyr had stated earlier in the week that he opposed any attempt to undermine government stability before February 7.
