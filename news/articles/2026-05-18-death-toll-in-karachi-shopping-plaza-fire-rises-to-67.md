The death toll from a shopping plaza fire in Karachi, Pakistan, has risen to 67, according to Dr. Summaiya Syed, the city's police surgeon. The fire, which erupted Saturday at Gul Plaza in Pakistan's southern port city, claimed dozens more victims than initially confirmed, with rescue teams still searching the severely damaged building on Thursday as family members of the missing waited nearby for news.

The disaster reflects the latest in a pattern of deadly fires across Karachi linked to poor safety standards and weak regulatory enforcement. The city has experienced repeated catastrophic blazes in recent years, highlighting inadequate fire codes and inspection practices.

## Rescue and Identification Efforts Continue

Police surgeon Dr. Summaiya Syed said rescue teams were still searching through the severely damaged Gul Plaza on Thursday, more than three days after the fire began. Most of the remains discovered were found in fragments, making identification extremely difficult. The previous death toll stood at 34 before authorities discovered additional remains.

The Sindh provincial government reported that around 70 people were missing after the fire spread rapidly through the four-story structure. Rescue operations continued with police deployed around the plaza to prevent relatives from entering the unstable building while search teams worked methodically through the debris.

## Family Members Struggle for Answers

Family members of the missing gathered outside the destroyed plaza and nearby hospitals, waiting for news and providing DNA samples to aid in identification efforts. Some relatives grew frustrated with the pace of rescue operations.

"They are not conducting the search properly," said Khair-un-Nisa, who stood outside the building in tears. She explained that a relative who had gone shopping had been missing since Saturday.

Saadia Saeed expressed similar frustration about her brother, who she said had been trapped inside since Saturday night. "I am ready to go inside the plaza to look for him, but police are not allowing me," she said.

The families accused authorities of moving too slowly and claimed that faster action could have saved additional lives. Police maintained the cordon to protect both the relatives and rescue workers from the structural dangers posed by the damaged building.

## Investigation and Cause

Investigators said the blaze erupted at a time when most shop owners were either closing for the day or had already left. The fire spread rapidly through merchandise stocked in the shops. Police indicated that a short circuit may have triggered the blaze, though the investigation remained ongoing.

The goods that fueled the rapid spread included cosmetics, clothing, and plastic items—highly flammable materials that allowed the fire to intensify quickly through the building.

## Pattern of Deadly Fires in Karachi

Karachi has a long history of deadly fires often linked to poor safety standards, weak regulatory enforcement, and illegal construction. In November 2023, a shopping mall fire in the city killed 10 people and injured 22. One of Pakistan's deadliest industrial disasters occurred in 2012, when a garment factory fire killed at least 260 people.

The repeated fires have raised questions about building codes, fire-safety inspections, and enforcement mechanisms across Pakistan's largest port city.
