A Ukrainian delegation arrived in the United States on Saturday for talks with Trump administration officials on a proposed peace settlement, as Russian forces struck energy infrastructure in Ukraine's Kyiv and Odesa regions overnight, cutting electricity to more than 20 settlements in freezing temperatures, Ukraine's Ministry of Energy said.

Kyrylo Budanov, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's chief of staff, said on Telegram that he had arrived in the U.S. to discuss "the details of the peace agreement." Budanov said he, together with Ukrainian negotiators Rustem Umerov and Davyd Arakhamia, would meet with U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff, President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, and Army Secretary Dan Driscoll.

The simultaneous developments — diplomatic engagement in Washington and a fresh wave of Russian strikes on Ukrainian civilian infrastructure — highlight the fragility of U.S.-led peace efforts nearly four years into the war, with Zelenskyy arguing that the ongoing bombardment is eroding even the limited openings for dialogue.

## Zelenskyy warns strikes undermine diplomacy

Zelenskyy said the principal task for the Ukrainian delegation was to convey how ongoing Russian strikes are undermining diplomacy. Writing on Telegram, he said the strikes are "constantly worsening even the small opportunities for dialogue that existed. The American side must understand this."

Zelenskyy said Friday that the delegation would try to finalize with U.S. officials documents for a proposed peace settlement covering postwar security guarantees and economic recovery. If American officials approve the proposals, the U.S. and Ukraine could sign the documents at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, the following week, Zelenskyy said at a Kyiv news conference with Czech President Petr Pavel. Trump is expected to attend the Davos gathering, according to organizers.

Russia would still need to be consulted on any such proposals, according to the Associated Press.

## Strikes cut power and heat as temperatures plunge

Russia struck energy infrastructure in Ukraine's Kyiv and Odesa regions overnight, Ukraine's Ministry of Energy said. More than 20 settlements in the Kyiv region lost power following the attacks.

In Kharkiv, Ukraine's second-largest city, Mayor Ihor Terekhov said a Russian attack on a critical infrastructure facility in the city's industrial district Saturday could seriously affect power and heating. Three people were wounded in the attack.

"We're talking about serious strikes on the system that keeps the city warm and lit," Terekhov wrote on Telegram, adding that the system is "constantly operating at its limits." Each new strike, he said, means "maintaining a stable supply will become even more difficult, and recovery will be longer and harder."

Zelenskyy said the most difficult energy situations were in the cities of Kyiv, Kharkiv, and Zaporizhzhia and surrounding regions. He said Ukraine needed to increase electricity imports and acquire additional equipment from partners.

## A sustained winter bombardment

Russia has repeatedly targeted Ukraine's power grid throughout the war, a strategy Ukrainian officials describe as "weaponizing winter." Nighttime temperatures have dropped to minus 18 degrees Celsius (zero Fahrenheit).

Ukraine's energy minister, Denys Shmyhal, said Friday that Russia had conducted 612 attacks on energy targets over the previous year. The strikes have intensified in recent months as winter temperatures fell.

Ukrainian authorities have introduced emergency measures in response, including temporarily easing curfew restrictions to allow people to access public heating centers set up by the government. Shmyhal said hospitals, schools, and other critical infrastructure remain the top priority for electricity and heat.

Officials instructed state energy companies Ukrzaliznytsia, Naftogaz, and Ukroboronprom to urgently purchase imported electricity covering at least 50% of their own consumption, Shmyhal said.
