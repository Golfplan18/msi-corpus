The disaster renewed longstanding concerns about the safety of open landfills and dumpsites across the Philippines, where collapses have repeatedly endangered workers and residents of nearby poor communities. A similar collapse at a Quezon City dumpsite in July 2000 killed more than 200 people and prompted legislation requiring the closure of illegal dumpsites nationwide.

CEBU CITY, Philippines — A mound of garbage and debris collapsed onto workers at a landfill in the village of Binaliw in Cebu city on Thursday afternoon, killing two people, injuring about a dozen and leaving 36 others missing, officials said Friday.

Thirteen workers were rescued alive overnight, but one — a female landfill worker — died while being transported to a hospital, said regional police director Brig. Gen. Roderick Maranan. The body of a 25-year-old engineer who worked at the facility was recovered Friday afternoon, Cebu City Mayor Nestor Archival said.

The avalanche buried or trapped workers in low-slung buildings at the facility, which employs 110 people. All of the dead and missing were landfill and waste management workers, officials said.

Jaylord Antigua, a 31-year-old office worker at the landfill, said the collapse came rapidly and without warning, on a clear day. It destroyed his office.

"I saw a light and crawled toward it in a hurry, because I feared there will be more landslides," Antigua told the Associated Press. "It was traumatic. I feared that it was my end, so this is my second life."

Among the structures struck was a warehouse where workers sorted recyclable waste and other rubbish, Maranan said.

## Search and rescue

Rescuers with earthmoving equipment were scouring the debris field Friday as relatives waited at the site. A woman was seen weeping openly and urging rescue teams to move faster, according to the Associated Press.

"All response teams remain fully engaged in search and retrieval efforts to locate the remaining missing persons with strict adherence to safety protocols," Archival said in a statement posted on Facebook.

"The city government assures the public and the families of those affected that all necessary measures are being taken to ensure safety, transparency, accountability and compassionate assistance as operations continue," Archival said.

Search and rescue efforts would proceed indefinitely, officials said.

## History of dumpsite dangers

Open landfills and dumpsites have long posed safety and health risks in many Philippine cities and towns, particularly near poor communities where residents scavenge the heaps for recyclables and food scraps.

The Binaliw collapse recalled a larger disaster in July 2000, when a massive mound of garbage at a dumpsite in a shantytown in suburban Quezon City collapsed after days of stormy weather. That avalanche also ignited a fire, killing more than 200 people, damaging scores of shanties and leaving many others missing. The disaster prompted the Philippine government to enact legislation requiring the closure of illegal dumpsites and mandating improved waste management by authorities.
