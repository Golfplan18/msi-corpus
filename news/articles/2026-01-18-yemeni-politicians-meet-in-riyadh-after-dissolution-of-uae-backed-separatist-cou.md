Yemeni politicians convened Sunday in Riyadh for their first public gathering since the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council was dissolved, discussing the future of southern Yemen ahead of a Saudi-sponsored conference whose dates have not yet been announced. The meeting came weeks after Saudi Arabia bombed STC positions following the separatist group's December offensive, exposing fractures within the coalition fighting Iran-backed Houthi rebels.

The talks marked the first formal political step for southern Yemen's separatist movement since the STC dissolved earlier this month, with former council leaders presenting a public show of unity while the group's deposed leader remained absent — reportedly in Abu Dhabi after Saudi Arabia accused the UAE of spiriting him there.

RIYADH, Saudi Arabia — Yemeni politicians convened Sunday in Saudi Arabia's capital for their first public gathering since the UAE-backed Southern Transitional Council was dissolved, discussing the future of southern Yemen as a Saudi-sponsored conference on the region's political future awaits a date.

The meeting came weeks after Saudi Arabia bombed STC positions following the separatist group's December offensive, which exposed fractures within the coalition fighting Iran-backed Houthi rebels. Saudi-backed government forces have since regained control of Hadramout and Mahra provinces, according to the Associated Press.

Participants said they had received "direct and practical assurances" from Saudi officials that members of forces allied with the now-dissolved STC would receive their salaries and "support to enhance their capabilities."

"This meeting expresses a unified southern will, represented by southern leaders from all segments of society and governorates, seeking a just, secure and guaranteed solution to our cause," the politicians said in a joint statement, the AP reported.

"There is no doubt that today's moment is historic, and we are at a juncture that requires everyone's cooperation," said Abdel-Rauf al-Saqaf, a senior STC official, at the gathering. "The southern demand is clear: the restoration of the southern state."

## Leader absent amid Saudi-UAE rift

STC leader Aidarous al-Zubaidi reportedly declined to attend the Riyadh talks and instead sent a delegation. Saudi Arabia has accused the UAE of smuggling al-Zubaidi to Abu Dhabi through Somalia after the Saudi-backed Presidential Leadership Council expelled him and charged him with treason, the AP said.

Sunday's meeting included STC leadership members who have been in Riyadh for approximately two weeks. Their participation was intended to counter claims by al-Zubaidi's supporters that the delegation had been held against their will in Saudi Arabia, the AP reported.

The escalation between the two Gulf states, once co-leaders of the coalition against the Houthis, reflected broader fragmentation of the anti-Houthi alliance. Saudi Arabia bombed STC positions after the group refused to withdraw from areas it had seized in its December offensive, including Hadramout on the border with Saudi Arabia, according to the AP.

## Background

The STC was established in April 2017 as an umbrella organization for groups seeking to restore South Yemen as an independent state, as it existed between 1967 and 1990. It received financial and military support from the UAE. The STC leadership dissolved the council earlier this month, the AP said.

Yemen's civil war began in 2014, when Houthi rebels backed by Iran descended from their northern stronghold and seized the capital Sanaa, forcing the internationally recognized government to flee first southward, then into exile in Saudi Arabia. The conflict involves sectarian and tribal grievances alongside the involvement of multiple regional powers, the AP reported.
