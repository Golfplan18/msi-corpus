Argentina's government repaid in December a $20 billion currency swap credit line from the United States, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced Friday. Bessent said in a post on X that President Javier Milei's government had "quickly and completely repaid" its limited credit line under the agreement signed in October. Argentina's Central Bank confirmed the December payment.

"Stabilizing a strong American ally, and generating tens of millions in profit for Americans, is a great achievement," Bessent said. He added that the U.S. Exchange Stabilization Fund, which was tapped to fund the credit line, no longer holds Argentine pesos following the repayment.

The repayment marks a significant step in Argentina's effort to restore financial credibility after decades of economic instability, though the country's foreign exchange reserves remain at low levels and it faces additional debt obligations in 2026.

The credit line had provided dollar liquidity to Argentina's government ahead of the country's October legislative elections. Milei's Libertarian Party won those elections by a wide margin, bolstering support for his strict austerity program and helping restore some foreign investor confidence.

Bessent said it is in the United States' special interest to "set the course for Latin America," describing "a strong and stable Argentina" as essential to consolidating "a prosperous Western Hemisphere." He said Milei "continues to firmly fulfill the renewed mandate of the Argentine people in favor of economic freedom" and that the country "has regained access to financial markets" and has "implemented encouraging changes in its monetary and exchange rate policy framework."

Argentina's Economy Minister Luis Caputo thanked the Trump administration for "the trust in our economic policy," calling the news "excellent." In a post on X, Caputo said: "It is an excellent reality for our country to have been able to build this geopolitical alliance and to know that we have the explicit support of the most important country in the world."

Despite the December repayment, Argentina's foreign exchange reserves remain at low levels. The country faces renewed debt pressure in 2026 from outstanding loans with the International Monetary Fund and other multilateral lenders.
