## Defense and prosecution rest competing theories in Clancy murder trial

The deadlocked jury in the Lindsay Clancy murder trial was expected to keep trying to reach a verdict Thursday in Plymouth, Massachusetts, after telling the judge twice that it could not agree on whether the mother is criminally responsible for killing her three children, the Associated Press reported. If jurors remain unable to reach a unanimous verdict, the case could end in a mistrial, the AP reported.

Clancy, a 36-year-old former labor and delivery nurse, does not deny strangling her children in the basement of the family home in 2023, according to AP correspondent Leah Willingham, who reported from Plymouth. Prosecutors have identified the children as Cora, Dawson and Callan Clancy, ages 5, 3 and 8 months.

The central legal question before the jury is whether Clancy is criminally responsible for the killings. Her attorney has argued that postpartum psychosis led to her actions. Prosecutors say she knew what she was doing when she killed the three children.

Willingham reported that the trial has generated intense public interest, with reporters and spectators packing the courtroom throughout the livestreamed proceedings. The livestreamed format has allowed viewers beyond the Plymouth courthouse to follow the case.

"The case has also sparked deep public divisions over her culpability and the issues around maternal mental health after childbirth," the AP report said.

Jury deliberations were scheduled to continue Thursday, the AP reported.
