Syrian security forces began deploying Saturday into the Sheikh Maqsoud neighborhood of Aleppo after five days of intense fighting with Kurdish fighters that killed at least 22 people and displaced more than 140,000. The Syrian army declared the district a closed military zone Friday night, launching what it described as a "clearing operation." Civilian flights at Aleppo International Airport were suspended after drone attacks struck the city during the day, state television reported.

The clashes are the most intense in Syria since the fall of former President Bashar Assad in December 2024, and erupted Tuesday after the Syrian government and the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) failed to reach an agreement to merge their military units into the national army. U.S. Special Envoy Tom Barrack traveled to Damascus on Saturday and called on all parties to halt hostilities immediately.

## Five days of fighting

The clashes began Tuesday in the predominantly Kurdish neighborhoods of Sheikh Maqsoud, Achrafieh, and Bani Zaid in northern Aleppo. Government troops captured Achrafieh and Bani Zaid in the days that followed. By Saturday, security forces were combing Sheikh Maqsoud after asking residents to remain in their homes for their safety. Hundreds of people who had fled the area days earlier waited at the neighborhood's entrances, hoping to return once operations concluded.

Each side accused the other of initiating the violence and of deliberately targeting civilian areas and infrastructure, including ambulances and hospitals.

The Kurdish-led Democratic Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (DAANES), which governs much of Syria's northeast, said security forces attacked the Khaled Fajr hospital in Sheikh Maqsoud, endangering patients and paramedics, and called on the international community to intervene and force Damascus to end the bombardments.

Syrian Information Minister Hamza al-Mustafa told state television that Kurdish fighters used civilian structures — including hospitals and clinics — during the clashes.

According to the Kurdish side, at least 12 civilians were killed in Kurdish-controlled neighborhoods. Syrian government officials reported at least 10 civilian deaths in adjacent areas under Damascus's control.

## Drone strike on government building

An explosive drone struck the Aleppo Governorate building Saturday afternoon shortly after two cabinet ministers and a local official held a press conference there, state television reported. No casualties were reported from that strike. State television blamed Kurdish fighters; the main Kurdish-led force denied the reports, saying its fighters had not attacked a civilian target.

State news agency SANA reported that two Kurdish fighters blew themselves up when surrounded by security forces without causing casualties. Associated Press journalists in the area said they could hear bursts of gunfire when government-deployed drones flew over Sheikh Maqsoud.

## U.S. calls for ceasefire

Barrack met in Damascus on Saturday with senior Syrian officials including President Ahmad al-Sharaa. On Friday, he had discussed the situation with Jordanian Foreign Minister Ayman Safadi in Amman. Jordan offered support for efforts to consolidate a ceasefire and secure the peaceful withdrawal of Kurdish fighters from Aleppo, Barrack said.

"The violence risks undermining the progress made since the fall of the Assad regime and invites external interference that does not serve the interests of any party," Barrack said in comments posted to X. "We urge all parties to exercise maximum restraint, immediately cease hostilities and return to dialogue."

Barrack said the fighting undercuts an agreement reached in March between the Syrian government and Kurdish leadership. He described recent events in Aleppo as "deeply concerning" and said Washington's goal "remains a sovereign and unified Syria, at peace with itself and its neighbors, where equality, justice and opportunity extend to all its people."
