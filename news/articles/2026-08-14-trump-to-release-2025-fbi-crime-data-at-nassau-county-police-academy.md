## Nassau County stop targets House, governor races

President Donald Trump is visiting the Nassau County Police Academy in suburban Long Island on Friday, where the White House said he will release new FBI crime statistics showing a decrease in violent crime for 2025. The visit is aimed at boosting Republican candidates in closely contested races across his home state, pivoting to one of his strongest political issues ahead of the November midterms.

White House spokesperson Lauren Bis framed the forthcoming data as confirmation of Trump's central campaign promise on public safety. "President Trump promised to make America safe again and the data proves he has done just that," Bis said. "Unlike Defund-the-Police Democrats, President Trump will always stand with our law enforcement officers and deliver safer American neighborhoods."

The Long Island venue carries direct political weight. Trump narrowly won Nassau County in 2024, flipping it for Republicans for the first time in more than two decades. The suburban county is now home to two Democratic-held House seats that Republicans see as winnable this fall, giving the president's appearance stakes in several congressional contests.

The stop also coincides with the political base of Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman, a Trump-endorsed Republican who is challenging Democratic Gov. Kathy Hochul in this year's gubernatorial race. Trump's endorsement ties the president directly to Blakeman's challenge.

Trump will be joined at the academy by Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel, according to the White House. Nassau County operates one of the largest police forces in the nation, a feature that gives the venue symbolic weight for a public-safety message.

The political backdrop for the visit is more complicated. Trump faces lagging approval ratings as he works to lift candidates in closely contested races, and the administration is contending with what the Associated Press characterized as a "deeply unpopular war in Iran" with no end in sight. Those headwinds have raised questions about how much Trump's presence will help Republican candidates in November.
