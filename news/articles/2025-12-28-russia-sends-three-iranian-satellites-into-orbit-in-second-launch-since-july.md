Russia launched three Iranian communications satellites into orbit Sunday from the Vostochny launchpad in eastern Russia, Iranian state television reported — the second such launch in five months and a visible marker of the deepening strategic alignment between Moscow and Tehran.

A Russian rocket placed the satellites — named Paya, Kowsar and Zafar-2 — on a 500-kilometer (310-mile) orbit. Paya, at 150 kilograms (330 pounds), is the heaviest satellite Iran has ever deployed into orbit, according to the report. Kowsar weighs 35 kilograms (77 pounds); the report did not specify Zafar-2's weight.

The satellites are equipped with cameras capable of up to 3-meter resolution, designed for use in water-resources management, agriculture and environmental monitoring. Their operational life span is up to five years.

The launch is the latest step in a pattern of space-technology cooperation that has advanced alongside a formal Russian-Iranian strategic partnership, drawing continued U.S. criticism that Tehran's satellite launches defy United Nations Security Council resolutions.

Russia has been regularly sending Iran's satellites into orbit as a long-standing project between the two countries. As [MSI previously reported](https://mainstreetindependent.com/articles/2025-12-28-russia-launches-three-iranian-communications-satellites-into-orbit), a Russian rocket sent the Iranian communications satellite Nahid-2 into orbit in July — a launch that established the accelerating cadence now evident in Sunday's mission.

Russia signed a "strategic partnership" treaty with Iran in January and strongly condemned the Israeli and U.S. strikes on Iran during a 12-day air war in June that killed nearly 1,100 Iranians, including military commanders and nuclear scientists, according to the Associated Press. Iran's retaliatory missile barrages killed 28 people in Israel.

The United States has said Iran's satellite launches defy a U.N. Security Council resolution and has called on Tehran to undertake no activity involving ballistic missiles capable of delivering nuclear weapons. U.N. sanctions related to Iran's ballistic missile program expired in 2023.
