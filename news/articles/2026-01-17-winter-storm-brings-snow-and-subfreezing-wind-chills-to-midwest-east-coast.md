A winter storm swept across the Midwest and East Coast on Saturday, producing whiteout conditions in northeastern Ohio, sub-zero wind chills from the Plains to the Northeast, and near-freezing temperatures as far south as Florida, the National Weather Service said. The Weather Prediction Center forecast "impactful snowfall" from the western Florida Panhandle to Maine beginning late Saturday, with below-average temperatures expected to persist into early next week.

The storm marks the second consecutive year that unusual winter weather has reached normally temperate Southern states, with a National Weather Service meteorologist describing back-to-back Florida snow events as "pretty anomalous for the area."

## Whiteout conditions strike the Great Lakes region

In northeastern Ohio, sudden bursts of heavy snow and gusty winds created whiteout conditions Saturday, according to the National Weather Service. Snow squall conditions moved into the Cleveland metro area and were expected to continue east into Pennsylvania and parts of eastern New York.

"Expect visibilities of less than a quarter of a mile and rapid snow accumulation on roadways. Travel will be difficult and possibly dangerous in the heavy snow," the National Weather Service warned.

## East Coast braces for widespread snowfall

The Weather Prediction Center forecast "impactful snowfall" to begin late Saturday up and down the East Coast, stretching from the western Florida Panhandle to Maine. Connecticut, Massachusetts and Rhode Island were expected to receive up to 4 inches (10 centimeters) of snow by Sunday night.

Sub-zero wind chills were forecast from the Plains to the Midwest and Northeast, with the coldest conditions expected in the Upper Midwest on Sunday night, the Weather Prediction Center said. Below-average temperatures were predicted to persist across much of the Central and Eastern United States into early next week.

## Southern states face rare winter conditions

Oklahoma, Tennessee, Georgia and Florida were forecast to see near-freezing temperatures at least through the weekend. In central Georgia south of Atlanta, up to 3 inches (7.6 centimeters) of snow was forecast, with the heaviest accumulation expected between 6 a.m. and 11 a.m. The National Weather Service's Atlanta office warned of slippery roads during the snowfall and again Sunday night into Monday morning as remaining moisture refreezes.

In Tallahassee, Florida, some snowfall was possible Sunday morning, though the city's National Weather Service office said accumulation was unlikely. "So here in Tallahassee, the likelihood of any snow accumulation is not zero, but it's very low. I mean the ground will be just too warm for anything to stick and accumulate," said Kristian Oliver, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service's Tallahassee office.

If Tallahassee sees snow, it would mark the second consecutive year that Florida experienced winter precipitation. In January 2025, up to 10 inches (25 centimeters) fell in parts of the Panhandle as part of a record-breaking storm that also brought snow to normally warm cities including Houston and New Orleans.

"On average we have an event like this maybe every few years. But having two back to back I'd say is pretty anomalous for the area," Oliver said.
