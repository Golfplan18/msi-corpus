PARIS — French Olympic swimming champion Yannick Agnel will stand trial on charges of rape and sexual assault involving the 13-year-old daughter of his former coach, the prosecutor general's office in Colmar announced Thursday.

The investigative chamber of the Colmar appeals court issued the order after finding that Agnel, now 33, was an adult at the time of the alleged acts. He has 10 days from the ruling to appeal to France's Court of Cassation, the country's highest court.

The alleged acts took place in 2016, when Agnel was 24 and the alleged victim was 13, according to French judicial officials. The incidents reportedly occurred across multiple locations: the eastern French city of Mulhouse, where Agnel was training at the time; Rio de Janeiro during that year's Summer Olympics; Spain; and Thailand.

Agnel was first arrested in December 2021. He acknowledged at the time having had a relationship with an underage girl but denied that any coercion was involved. His legal representative did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Thursday's ruling.

The charges proceed under a 2021 French law that classifies all sexual contact with a child under 15 as rape, carrying a maximum sentence of 20 years in prison — a reform that brought France's penal code closer in line with many other Western nations, which had long categorized such conduct as rape regardless of whether force or threat was documented.

Agnel won two gold medals at the 2012 London Olympics, in the 200-meter freestyle and the 4x100-meter freestyle relay, and retired from competitive swimming in 2016.
