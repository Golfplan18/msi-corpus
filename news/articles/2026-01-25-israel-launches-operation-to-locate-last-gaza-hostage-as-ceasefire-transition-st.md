Israel said Sunday its military was conducting a large-scale operation to locate Ran Gvili, the last remaining hostage in Gaza, as the recovery of his remains has become a condition for moving forward with the second phase of a U.S.-brokered ceasefire.

The recovery of all remaining hostages, alive or dead, has been central to the first phase of the ceasefire that took effect in October. With the second phase negotiations now underway, Gvili's remains have become a focal point between Israel, Hamas, and international mediators.

## Searching for Gaza's final hostage

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office said that upon completion of the operation and in accordance with agreements made with the United States, Israel would open the Rafah border crossing with Egypt. Israeli military officials told local media the operation could take days to complete.

Israel's military said it was searching a cemetery in northern Gaza near the Yellow Line, which marks Israeli-controlled portions of the territory. An Israeli military official said Gvili may have been buried in the Shijaiya-Tuffah area of Gaza City. The official spoke on condition of anonymity because the operation was still underway.

The search operation includes rabbis and dental experts working with specialized military teams, according to the military.

## Hostage recovery stalls ceasefire progress

The return of all remaining hostages, alive or dead, has been central to the first phase of the ceasefire that took effect on October 10. The previous hostage was recovered in early December.

The Trump administration declared in recent days that the second phase of the ceasefire is underway. But Gvili's family has urged Netanyahu's government not to enter that phase until his remains are returned.

Israel has repeatedly accused Hamas of dragging its feet in recovering the final hostage. In a statement Sunday, Hamas said it had provided all the information it had about Gvili's remains and accused Israel of obstructing search efforts in areas of Gaza under Israeli military control.

## Attack on UN refugee agency in Jerusalem

The shuttered headquarters of the U.N. agency for Palestinian refugees in east Jerusalem was set ablaze overnight, days after Israeli bulldozers demolished parts of the compound.

It was not known who started the fire. Roland Friedrich, the agency's West Bank director, said Israeli settlers were observed at night looting the main building for furniture, and that multiple holes were cut in the fence.

Israel's fire department sent teams to prevent the blaze from spreading. In May 2024, the U.N. agency, known as UNRWA, said it was closing its compound after settlers set fires to its fence.

"This is the latest attack on the U.N. in the ongoing attempt to dismantle the status of Palestine refugees," UNRWA Commissioner-General Philippe Lazzarini told the Associated Press.

UNRWA's mandate is to provide aid and services to some 2.5 million Palestinian refugees in Gaza, the Israeli-occupied West Bank and east Jerusalem, as well as 3 million more refugees in Syria, Jordan and Lebanon. But the agency's operations were curtailed when Israel's Knesset passed legislation last year severing ties with UNRWA and banning it from functioning in what Israel defines as its territory, including east Jerusalem.

Israel has long accused UNRWA of being infiltrated by Hamas and alleges that some of its employees were involved in the 2023 attack that triggered the two-year war in Gaza. UNRWA leaders have said they took swift action against employees accused of taking part in the attack and have denied allegations that the agency tolerates or collaborates with Hamas.
