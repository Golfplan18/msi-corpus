The cold snap illustrates how a sustained extreme-weather event can cascade across multiple countries' transportation and public-services infrastructure simultaneously, forcing governments to shutter schools, halt rail networks and restrict road travel within days of one another.

Thousands of tourists were stranded at Kittilä airport in Finnish Lapland on Sunday after flights were canceled because temperatures dropped to minus 37 degrees Celsius (minus 34.6 degrees Fahrenheit), making aircraft de-icing and other ground operations too difficult to carry out, Finland's national public broadcaster Yle reported.

The deep freeze has gripped wide regions of northern, central and eastern Europe over several days and is more severe than in typical winters, according to the Associated Press. The Finnish Meteorological Institute predicted temperatures in the Kittilä area would approach minus 40 C (minus 40 F) on Monday, with the extreme cold expected to persist.

## Germany: rail shutdown and school closures

In Germany, rail operator Deutsche Bahn halted all service in the north of the country on Friday because of heavy snowfall, the AP reported. Passengers were still experiencing widespread delays and cancellations on Sunday.

Authorities in North Rhine-Westphalia — Germany's most populous western state — announced that all schools would remain closed and move to online instruction on Monday after forecasts called for icy roads across the region.

## Baltic states issue travel warnings

In Estonia and Lithuania, authorities urged drivers to postpone all nonessential travel ahead of expected blizzards. Latvia issued a snow alert for the western part of the country.

Heavy snowfall, high winds and icy roads have made road travel difficult across parts of Europe as the cold wave continues.
