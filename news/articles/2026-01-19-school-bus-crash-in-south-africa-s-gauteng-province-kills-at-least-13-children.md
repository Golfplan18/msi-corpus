School transport safety drew renewed attention from South Africa's highest officials following the crash, with the basic education minister saying driver error is a frequent cause of accidents involving vehicles carrying children.

A truck and a school minibus collided head-on in South Africa's Gauteng province early Monday, killing at least 13 children, authorities said. At least 11 of the students died at the scene southwest of Johannesburg; two others succumbed to their injuries at area hospitals. Seven additional passengers were transported to hospital for further medical care.

The private minibus was carrying students to primary and high schools in the southwestern part of Johannesburg when the crash occurred around 7 a.m., police said. Witnesses told authorities the driver was overtaking stationary vehicles when the minibus struck the truck head-on.

Gauteng Emergency Services transported five patients to Sebokeng Hospital and two others to Kopanong Hospital. The bus driver also suffered injuries and was among those taken to hospital.

Police said the incident is under investigation and the truck driver will be questioned. Matome Chiloane, the Gauteng provincial education department minister, told reporters that the school bus driver would also be investigated for possibly driving recklessly.

President Cyril Ramaphosa expressed sadness at the loss of life, saying national and provincial authorities would provide affected families and schools with psychosocial support.

"Our children are the nation's most precious assets and we must do all we can — from observing the rules of the road to the quality of service providers," Ramaphosa said.

Basic Education Minister Siviwe Gwarube said many accidents involving school transport are caused by driver error. She called on the Department of Transport to ensure that vehicles entrusted with transporting schoolchildren are roadworthy.

Parents gathered at the crash scene, witnesses said, with some weeping as emergency services recovered books and stationery from the wreckage.
