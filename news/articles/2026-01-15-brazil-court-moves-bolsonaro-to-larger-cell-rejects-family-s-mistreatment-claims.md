Brazilian Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes on Thursday ordered former President Jair Bolsonaro transferred from federal police headquarters in Brasília to a substantially larger cell at the Papuda Penitentiary Complex, also in the capital. The move upgrades Bolsonaro from a 12-square-meter room to a 54-square-meter unit with a 10-square-meter outdoor area he can access at will.

Moraes simultaneously rejected accusations by Bolsonaro's family that the former president has been mistreated or denied adequate medical care in custody. "Regrettably and falsely, there has been a systematic attempt to delegitimize the regular and lawful execution of the custodial sentence of Jair Messias Bolsonaro, which has been carried out with full respect for human dignity," Moraes said in the court order.

## New Accommodations

The new space at Papuda resembles an apartment, with a double bed, kitchen, laundry, and living room. Bolsonaro will also receive increased family visit time, and physiotherapy equipment including a treadmill and bicycle will be installed.

Moraes denied Bolsonaro access to a smart TV, citing the risk that internet connectivity could enable improper communication with the outside world or facilitate illegal acts.

The Supreme Court's press office said the transfer had already taken place.

## Medical Assessment Ordered

Moraes ordered a medical examination to assess Bolsonaro's health and determine whether transfer to a penitentiary hospital is warranted. The justice noted that Papuda has a health post staffed exclusively for prisoners in custody, including doctors, nurses, dentists, psychologists, a psychiatrist, and a pharmacist.

Bolsonaro's lawyers have sought a transfer to house arrest on medical grounds. His wife, Michelle Bolsonaro, and his sons have repeatedly asserted that he is not receiving adequate medical care. Moraes rejected those claims in Thursday's order.

Bolsonaro has made several trips to a nearby hospital since beginning his sentence, most recently after falling out of bed and hitting his head. He has also been hospitalized multiple times since being stabbed at a campaign event before the 2018 presidential election.

## Background

Bolsonaro began serving a 27-year sentence in November for attempting to overthrow Brazil's democracy after his 2022 electoral defeat. He and several allies were convicted by a panel of Supreme Court justices. The plot included plans to kill President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, Vice President Geraldo Alckmin, and Justice Moraes, as well as efforts to encourage an insurrection in early 2023.

Bolsonaro was also convicted of leading an armed criminal organization and attempting the violent abolition of the democratic rule of law. He has denied wrongdoing.

In Thursday's order, Moraes said Bolsonaro had been convicted of "extremely serious crimes" and that the custodial sentence was not a "hotel stay or a vacation colony," a characterization the justice said statements from Bolsonaro's sons had "mistakenly seem to demand."
