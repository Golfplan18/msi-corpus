Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov warned Tuesday that President Trump's pursuit of Greenland has created a 'deep crisis' within NATO and could trigger conflict between alliance members. Speaking at a Moscow press conference, Lavrov said the move undermines the Western concept of a rules-based international order and threatens the alliance's unity.

The comments underscore mounting tensions within the NATO alliance as Trump pursues territorial ambitions. They also signal Russia's effort to exploit Western divisions during negotiations over ending the war in Ukraine, where disagreements persist on settlement terms.

## NATO and Greenland Dispute

Lavrov warned specifically that Trump's approach could create a situation where "a NATO member attacks another NATO member," signaling the divisions he sees within the alliance. He characterized the prospect as unprecedented, saying "it was difficult to imagine before that something like this could happen."

The Russian foreign minister said Trump's actions contradict the Western concept of a rules-based international order. "Now it is not the Collective West that writes the rules, but only one of its representatives," he said, describing the shift as "a great upheaval for Europe."

Lavrov also rejected the characterization of Greenland as integral to Denmark, saying the territory "is not a natural part of Denmark"—an assertion that echoes Russian arguments about territorial legitimacy elsewhere. He denied that Russia and China harbor intentions to threaten Greenland, though he said Russia would "certainly" observe the situation and draw conclusions once it is resolved.

## Measured Support Amid Disagreement on Ukraine

Despite his criticism of Trump's Greenland push, Lavrov expressed support for the president's broader diplomatic initiatives. He offered cautious approval of Trump's proposed Peace Board, describing it as evidence that "even with the philosophy of policy that [the U.S.] has, [it] considers it necessary to try to convince a group of nations to cooperate."

Lavrov specifically praised Trump's efforts to negotiate an end to the war in Ukraine, calling the United States the only Western nation to have "expressed understanding of the necessity of taking into account Russian interests" and offered "solutions considering the fundamental causes of the crisis."

Yet Lavrov criticized European allies of Kyiv for attempting to amend Trump's proposals while Ukrainian forces face implacable Russian attack. He claimed the original Trump proposal—accepted by Putin during a summit in Alaska—included protections for Russian speakers in Ukraine and the Ukrainian Orthodox Church affiliated with Moscow. These provisions were removed in the latest version proposed by Kyiv and its European partners, he said.

"We will not allow ourselves the luxury of letting the Kyiv regime take a break and rearm itself once again," Lavrov stated. He assessed that Russia is unlikely to reach agreement with European leaders, saying they have "sunk too deeply in hatred toward Russia" to find common ground.

## U.S. Conduct and Conditional Engagement

Lavrov criticized the United States for the capture and extradition of Venezuelan leader Nicolás Maduro on drug trafficking charges, calling the action "crude military intervention." He noted Russia is awaiting the U.S. to fulfill its promise to release two Russian crew members from a seized Russian-flagged tanker and said the U.S. has threatened Cuba and other Latin American nations.

The Russian foreign minister also highlighted Washington's refusal to accept Putin's proposal to extend the New START nuclear-arms treaty beyond its expiration next month.

Yet Lavrov signaled Russia remains willing to continue dialogue with the United States on the basis of mutual respect for national interests. He cited remarks by U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio during talks in Riyadh in February about the importance of finding areas where the two powers can cooperate while preventing disagreements from escalating into confrontation. "I responded that I fully share this philosophy and logic," Lavrov said.
