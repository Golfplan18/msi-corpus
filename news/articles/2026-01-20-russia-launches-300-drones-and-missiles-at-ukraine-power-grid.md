The assault demonstrates Russia's strategy to deny civilians essential services and erode their ability to resist through winter, even as diplomatic efforts proceed alongside military operations. Ukraine's air defenses have become stretched by the sustained campaign, with the ammunition expended overnight costing approximately 80 million euros.

Russia bombarded Ukraine with more than 300 drones and missiles in a nighttime assault on the power grid Tuesday, according to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. The attack knocked out heating to more than 5,600 apartment buildings in Kyiv as temperatures fell to minus 20 degrees Celsius (minus 4 Fahrenheit).

The bombardment occurred as Ukrainian officials pursue U.S.-led peace negotiations. A Ukrainian negotiating team arrived in the United States on Saturday, tasked with conveying how relentless Russian strikes undermine diplomatic progress, Zelenskyy said. Nearly 80 percent of the affected buildings in Kyiv had recently had their heating restored following a major Russian barrage on January 9, said Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko.

## Sustained Campaign Against Energy Infrastructure

Russia has escalated aerial attacks on electricity supply, aiming to deny Ukrainians heat and running water and wear down their resistance almost four years after Moscow launched its full-scale invasion on February 24, 2022. Ukraine is enduring one of its coldest winters in recent years.

Ukraine's air force shot down or jammed 27 missiles and 315 drones during the overnight attack, while five missiles and 24 drones hit 11 locations across the country. The Russian Defense Ministry claimed its forces targeted Ukrainian military and industrial installations and energy and transport infrastructure used by the Ukrainian armed forces.

The air defense ammunition Ukraine used against the Russian missiles overnight cost approximately 80 million euros ($93 million), Zelenskyy said. He announced the appointment of a new deputy air force commander, Pavlo Yelizarov, and said Ukraine's air defense system will be transformed, though he provided no additional details. Ukraine depends on sophisticated air defense systems produced by Western countries, especially the United States, to counter these ongoing attacks.

## International Alarm Over Infrastructure Attacks

U.N. Human Rights Chief Volker Türk said he was outraged by the repeated large-scale attacks on Ukraine's energy infrastructure, which especially affect children, older people and those with disabilities. The strikes "can only be described as cruel," he said in Geneva, adding that "targeting civilians and civilian infrastructure is a clear breach of the rules of warfare."

Rafael Mariano Grossi, director-general of the International Atomic Energy Agency, said several electrical substations providing power vital for nuclear safety in Ukraine were affected by the attacks. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha described Russia's strike as a wake-up call to world leaders and called for urgent assistance and additional sanctions on Russia.

## Peace Negotiations Continue Despite Military Pressure

Zelenskyy said the Ukrainian negotiating team would also attempt to finalize documents for a proposed peace settlement relating to postwar security guarantees and economic recovery. If American officials approve the proposals, the U.S. and Ukraine could sign the documents at the World Economic Forum in Davos this week, he said.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Tuesday that presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev plans to meet with American representatives at Davos. Media reports indicated the officials would include U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, President Donald Trump's son-in-law.
