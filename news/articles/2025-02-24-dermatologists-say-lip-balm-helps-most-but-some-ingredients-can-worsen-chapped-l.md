Lip balm can seal moisture against the cold, dry conditions that cause chapped lips, but dermatologists caution that fragrances, flavors and certain preservatives found in popular products can trigger allergic reactions that worsen symptoms rather than relieve them.

For people whose lips sting after applying balm, a fragrance-free, petroleum-based product may be the safest alternative, two dermatologists told the Associated Press.

Dry lips peak in winter, when cold outdoor air and low-humidity heating indoors combine to strip moisture from skin. Most users benefit from lip balm, but a subset finds that common product additives cause persistent or worsening chapping — a pattern dermatologists say they encounter regularly in their practices.

## How lip balm works

Lip balm works primarily as a barrier against conditions that dry skin, said Dr. Jenna Lester, a dermatologist at UCSF Health. "That is a good barrier for the external forces," like cold wind, Lester said.

## When it makes things worse

Some users cannot tolerate certain common additives. Problem ingredients include flavoring additives and lanolin — a waxy substance derived from sheep found in many moisturizers — as well as a preservative called formaldehyde and oxybenzone, an ingredient in chemical-based sunscreens.

These reactions are driven by the immune system and can occur at any time, after any number of product uses, and may persist for months after someone stops applying the product, said Dr. Caroline Mann, a dermatologist at Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis.

"Those are the people who will say, 'I tried these things. I thought my lips would get better, but they just keep getting worse and worse,'" Mann said.

For sensitive users, dermatologists recommend a fragrance-free balm whose label lists petrolatum as the active ingredient.

## Other approaches

Covering lips with a scarf when outdoors and running a humidifier indoors can reduce moisture loss without any product at all. Drinking water also helps, since dehydration can contribute to dry lips.

One reflex to avoid is lip-licking. While licking the lips provides short-term comfort, saliva evaporates quickly and pulls additional moisture along with it, leaving lips drier than before.

People whose lips continue to sting after reducing balm use should consider seeing a dermatologist, Mann said.

"If you do these things, you'll get through the winter with soft, supple lips," Mann said.
