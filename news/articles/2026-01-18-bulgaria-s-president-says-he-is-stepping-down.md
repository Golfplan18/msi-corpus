Bulgaria’s left-leaning President Rumen Radev announced Monday that he plans to step down from the mostly ceremonial post, saying he will formally submit his resignation to the Constitutional Court on Tuesday, according to a televised address.

Radev said his televised resignation submission will be made to the Constitutional Court on Tuesday. He made the announcement while speaking during the continuing political turmoil that has left Bulgaria struggling to form a stable government.

The constitution provides that the vice president, Iliana Yotova, must be sworn in by parliament to take the post until the end of the presidential mandate, after Radev’s resignation, the AP reported.

In his address, Radev said: “The battle for the future of our homeland lies ahead, and I believe we will face it together with all of you — the worthy, the inspired, and the unyielding! We are ready. We can, and we will succeed!”

AP reported that Radev has indicated he may contest an upcoming election widely expected after protests forced a center-right government to step down. The left-leaning president, the AP said, had thrown his support to the protests in early December and had been an opponent of the government.

Radev did not say Monday what his plans are, the AP reported. The announcement also comes amid public expectation that he will form a new political party, and it is taking place as Bulgaria moves toward another round of elections amid continuing failures to form a government.

The AP reported that large anti-corruption protests last month forced the resignation of the governing coalition led by the center-right GERB party. Attempts to form a new government within the current parliament have since failed, and Bulgaria is headed toward its eighth parliamentary election since 2021.

Radev’s resignation will also end a second mandate that, according to the AP report, runs through 2026. The AP described his resignation as the first by a head of state in Bulgaria’s post-communist history.

Asked recently about forming a new party, Radev said there was a need for a party that “unites all democrats — left and right — regardless of where they belong or whether they are politically active at all, because we all need fair elections and democratic, free development,” the AP reported.
