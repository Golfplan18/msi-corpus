The readings offer a tentative sign of recovery for an economy grappling with a prolonged property downturn, industrial overcapacity, and the looming prospect of renewed trade tensions with the United States — though analysts cautioned the upturn may prove short-lived.

BANGKOK — China's official manufacturing purchasing managers index crossed into expansion territory in December for the first time in eight months, the National Bureau of Statistics reported Wednesday, as orders picked up ahead of New Year holidays and builders rushed to complete projects.

The official PMI rose to 50.1 — just above the 50 threshold separating expansion from contraction on a scale to 100. A separate private-sector survey conducted by RatingDog, a Chinese credit research and analysis firm based in Shenzhen, also registered 50.1 for December.

The better-than-expected readings partly reflect easing pressure from an extended truce in trade tensions with the United States. They also suggest manufacturers ramped up production ahead of New Year holidays, when many companies close for several days. China's Lunar New Year falls in mid-February.

## Uneven gains across sectors

High-tech manufacturing led the advance, with an official PMI of 52.5 in December — up 2.4 percentage points from November. Equipment manufacturing and the consumer goods industry both registered 50.4. PMI measures for food, textiles, clothing, and electronics were above 53.

The gains were not shared across all segments. Factory activity for the small and mid-sized enterprises that account for the largest share of employment in China remained in contractionary territory, the National Bureau of Statistics said. Conditions for retailers and restaurants also deteriorated as consumers cut back on spending.

China's economy is forecast to grow at a pace just below the official target of about 5% for 2025, supported by activity in high-tech industries and exports, according to the National Bureau of Statistics report.

## Export sales and hiring weakened

RatingDog's December report told a more cautious story beneath the headline number. Despite the increase in overall orders, new export sales fell slightly and hiring weakened, the firm said.

"Overall, the manufacturing sector regained growth at the end of 2025," RatingDog founder Yao Yu said in a statement. "However, the improvement was marginal, with the impact of promotions and new products appearing impulse-driven and their sustainability requiring observation."

The report also noted that higher costs for raw materials, especially metals, have squeezed company profit margins. Exporters raised prices for the first time in three months to help offset those higher costs, RatingDog said.

## Xi pledges stronger policy support

At a New Year's gathering covered by Chinese state media Wednesday, President Xi Jinping vowed to promote "high-quality development" and to carry out "more positive macroeconomic policies" while ensuring social harmony and stability.

China's leaders are grappling with long-term challenges that include a yearslong slump in the property sector and excess capacity in industries including automaking, where damaging price wars have intensified.

Julian Evans-Pritchard of Capital Economics cautioned that the December upturn may be partly a product of a slight increase in government spending rather than a durable shift in momentum.

"The big picture is that the structural headwinds from the property downturn and industrial overcapacity are set to persist in 2026 and there appears to be limited appetite among policymakers for a big increase in demand-side stimulus," Evans-Pritchard said in a report.
