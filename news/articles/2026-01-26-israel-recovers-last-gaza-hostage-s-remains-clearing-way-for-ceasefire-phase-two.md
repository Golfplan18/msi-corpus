Israel on Monday brought home the remains of Ran Gvili, a 24-year-old police officer killed during the October 7, 2023 attack that sparked the war with Hamas. The recovery closes the first phase of the ceasefire and clears the path to phase two, which will involve disarming Hamas, deploying international security forces, and establishing new governance in Gaza. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called the recovery "an incredible achievement" for Israel and its soldiers.

Gvili's remains were the final hostage recovery required in the ceasefire's first phase. Hamas said it has met that obligation, but the next phase will confront more intractable challenges: disarming an entrenched militant organization and negotiating Gaza's political future.

## Recovery and the reopened Rafah crossing

Gvili's remains were found in a cemetery in northern Gaza, along the dividing line between Gaza and Israel, according to a military official. Dozens of people including his relatives, military officers, and members of his police unit received the coffin at an Israeli army post on the border. Convoys carried it north to Tel Aviv, where military and police personnel lined nearby roads to pay respects.

"You should see the honor you're receiving here," Gvili's father, Itzik, said as he kissed the coffin, draped in an Israeli flag. "The entire police is here with you, the entire army is with you, the entire people. I'm proud of you."

Netanyahu's office announced Sunday that Israel would open the Rafah crossing between Gaza and Egypt once the search for Gvili concluded. The crossing has been largely shut since May 2024 except for a brief period early last year. Palestinians view the crossing as a vital link to the outside world.

An official with the United Nations' children's agency said Monday that supplies have been backed up in Egypt and are ready to move into Gaza once the crossing opens to aid traffic. "The next phase needs to include bringing not only more humanitarian and commercial supplies but also permanent shelter materials and items to repair infrastructure," said Ted Chaiban, UNICEF's deputy executive director.

## Phase two: Disarming Hamas and negotiating Gaza's future

The ceasefire's second phase will require more difficult negotiations. Netanyahu told the Israeli parliament Monday: "The next phase is disarming Hamas and demilitarizing the Gaza Strip. The next phase is not reconstruction." The phase also calls for deploying an international security force, withdrawing Israeli soldiers, and establishing new governance structures in Gaza.

These objectives have already proven contentious. Netanyahu's government and U.S. President Donald Trump had called repeatedly for Gvili's recovery before other negotiations could move forward. Trump posted on social media that the recovery was "an impossible thing to do."

Palestinians in Gaza expressed hope that opening the Rafah crossing will ease their humanitarian crisis. "We hope this will close off Israel's pretexts and open the crossing," said Abdel-Rahman Radwan, a Gaza City resident whose mother has cancer and requires treatment outside Gaza.

Ahmed Ruqab, a father living with his family of six in a tent in the Nuseirat refugee camp, called for mediators and the United States to pressure Israel to allow more aid. "We need to turn this page and restart," he said.

## The conflict's toll in lives and prisoners

The October 7, 2023 attack on Israel killed about 1,200 people and saw 251 taken hostage. Before Gvili's recovery, 20 living hostages and the remains of 27 others had been returned to Israel since the ceasefire began, most recently in early December.

Israel's military operations have killed at least 71,660 Palestinians since 2023, according to Gaza's Health Ministry, which is part of the Hamas-led government. The ministry maintains detailed casualty records that are viewed as generally reliable by U.N. agencies and independent experts. More than 480 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli fire since the ceasefire began.

Israel has released roughly 2,000 Palestinian prisoners under the ceasefire deal and returned the bodies of more than 300 Palestinians to Gaza, though officials have struggled to identify them.

## Journalists press for independent access

The Foreign Press Association on Monday petitioned Israel's Supreme Court to allow journalists to enter Gaza freely and independently. The FPA represents dozens of global news organizations and has pushed for independent media access to Gaza for months. Israel has barred reporters from entering independently since the 2023 attacks, citing risks to journalists and soldiers.

FPA lawyers told the court that the restrictions are unjustified, noting that aid workers are moving in and out of Gaza. They said tightly controlled military-supervised visits are no substitute for independent access. The court is expected to rule soon.
