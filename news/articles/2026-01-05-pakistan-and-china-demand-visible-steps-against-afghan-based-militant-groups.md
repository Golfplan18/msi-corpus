Pakistan and China called Monday for "visible and verifiable" steps to eliminate terrorist organizations based in Afghanistan, according to a joint statement issued after talks between the two countries' foreign ministers. The statement said groups "operating from Afghanistan continue to pose serious threats to regional and global security" and pressed Kabul to prevent its territory from being used as a staging ground for attacks against other countries. Afghanistan's government did not immediately respond.

The joint communiqué reflects deepening concern in Beijing and Islamabad over Afghan-based militancy at a moment when Pakistan-Afghanistan relations remain strained following cross-border airstrikes in October, a Qatar-brokered ceasefire, and diplomatic talks in Istanbul that produced no results.

## Talks and the joint statement

The statement followed meetings between Pakistan's Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi in Beijing on December 4, according to the Associated Press.

China praised Pakistan for what it described as "comprehensive counterterrorism measures" and for protecting Chinese citizens and projects linked to the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor, a flagship component of Beijing's Belt and Road Initiative. Thousands of Chinese workers and engineers are engaged in CPEC projects connecting China's western Xinjiang region to Pakistan's Gwadar port on the Arabian Sea.

The security of Chinese personnel in Pakistan has become more urgent in recent years. In 2024, five Chinese nationals were killed when a suicide car bomber struck a bus in northwest Pakistan, the AP reported.

## Strained Pakistan-Afghanistan ties

Pakistan has repeatedly accused Afghanistan's Taliban government of sheltering the Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan, a militant group blamed for attacks inside Pakistan that have increased since 2021. The TTP is a distinct organization from Afghanistan's Taliban, which has governed the country since 2021 and says it does not allow its territory to be used for attacks against other countries.

In early October, Pakistan carried out airstrikes on what it described as TTP hideouts inside Afghanistan, killing dozens of alleged insurgents. Afghan forces retaliated, targeting Pakistani military posts. Afghanistan's government claimed to have killed 58 Pakistani soldiers; Pakistan acknowledged losing 23 troops.

Qatar brokered a ceasefire in Doha after the October fighting. Follow-up talks in Istanbul failed to produce additional results, according to the AP.

## CPEC extension stalled

In August, top diplomats from Pakistan, China, and Afghanistan met in Kabul and pledged to work toward extending the CPEC into Afghanistan, but the effort has not visibly advanced.
