Millions of Buddhists across Asia will observe the birthday of the historical Buddha this month on one of two dates set by different lunisolar calendar traditions, according to the Associated Press. The holiday, known as Vesak in several countries, marks not only the birth but also the enlightenment and death of Siddhartha Gautama — the prince born near the present-day India-Nepal border who became known as the Buddha, or "the awakened one."

The split in dates reflects Buddhism's spread across cultures with distinct calendar systems: several East Asian countries observe the holiday on the eighth day of the fourth month of the lunisolar calendar — May 15 this year — while South and Southeast Asian nations in the Theravada tradition observe the first full moon of May, which falls on May 23, the AP reported.

## Birth and life of the historical Buddha

Siddhartha was born in Lumbini, at what is today the border between India and Nepal, to Maya, the wife of Suddhodana, king of the Shakya clan, according to Buddhist tradition as recounted by the AP. His father sheltered him from pain and suffering, but the prince began to reflect on existence after witnessing sickness, old age and death.

Siddhartha then engaged in six years of ascetic practice and attained enlightenment at the age of 35 in Bodh Gaya in northeast India, the AP reported. He became known thereafter as the Buddha — a title meaning "the awakened one."

## South Korea and the Koreas

Buddha's birthday is a national holiday in South Korea. The highlight of the celebration in Seoul is the Yeondeunghoe, a lotus lantern festival featuring thousands of colorful lighted paper lanterns shaped like lotus flowers and displayed in temples and streets, the AP reported. Many temples provide free meals and tea to all visitors on the day.

In North Korea, the holiday is not an official observance but has been marked in Buddhist temples there since 1988, according to the AP. In 2018, Buddhist monks from North and South Korea held joint services when animosities between their governments eased. Such exchange programs have been stalled in recent years due to tensions over North Korea's nuclear program, the AP reported.

## China and Japan

In China, worshippers perform a bathing ceremony involving the pouring of scented blessed water over a statue of the infant Buddha, the AP reported. In Japan, April 8 is observed as Buddha's birthday and celebrated in Buddhist temples as Hana Matsuri — the flower festival — when a small flower hall decorated with colorful blooms is set up on temple grounds and devotees pour sweet tea over a statue of the baby Buddha.

## South and Southeast Asia

In South and Southeast Asia, the Mahabodhi Temple in Bodh Gaya is decorated and devotees perform special prayers under the bodhi tree associated with the Buddha's enlightenment, according to the AP. In India and Nepal, sweet rice porridge is served to recall the story of Sujata, a maiden said in tradition to have offered the Buddha a bowl of milk porridge.

In Malaysia and China, caged animals and birds are released on the holiday because people believe it generates good karma, the AP reported. In Sri Lanka, homes and streets are decorated with candles and paper and bamboo lanterns, with festivities featuring devotional songs, decorative structures called "pandals," burning of incense, and electric light displays depicting stories from Buddha's life.

In Vietnam, the birthday remains a popular festival but is not a public holiday — a status it held from 1958 to 1975 in what was formerly South Vietnam, according to the AP.
