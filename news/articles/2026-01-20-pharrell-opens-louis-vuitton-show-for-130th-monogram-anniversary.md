Pharrell Williams marked the start of Louis Vuitton's 130th anniversary celebration of its monogram with a Fall-Winter 2026 menswear show on Tuesday at the Louis Vuitton Foundation in Paris. The show combined fashion with pageantry, featuring live performances by a gospel choir and orchestra alongside a glass-walled apartment set through which models moved between scenes.

The presentation launched a yearlong brand celebration centered on the house's most recognizable signature, the monogram, while showcasing Williams' continuing direction of the Vuitton menswear line through silhouettes and details rooted in 1970s ease and functional design.

## Design Language: Utility Meets Heritage

Inside the Louis Vuitton Foundation, guests circled a grassy runway. At its center sat a glass-walled, minimalist apartment — part bedroom, part display box — where models kept entering and exiting like characters crossing scenes in a film.

Williams approached the collection through what has become his consistent vocabulary at Vuitton: readable from a distance, layered in detail when examined closely, and always tethered to the house's heritage as a maker of travel goods. This season, his lens was 1970s ease spiked with functional utility.

## The Palette and Silhouette

The color story grounded itself in autumn tones: grays, browns, black, denim, and cream. Then it broke into contrasts — bubblegum pink, baby blue, and emerald green — that kept the presentation from settling into something too restrained.

Silhouettes ran long and loose. Baggier trousers swept into A-line forms; suits were often topped with parka coats, a high-low collision that has become one of Williams' signatures. Details carried the design's argument. Shirts flashed with glimmering surfaces. Bows and jabot-style collars delivered the 1970s reference without veering into costume. Utility came through in the hardware language — ties, toggles, belts, zippers — and in faux-fur collars that read as both functional and decorative. Patent Oxford shoes added hard, glossy punctuation beneath the softer shapes.

Williams also pushed what could be described as a slightly undone finish: wrinkled tops that looked intentionally lived-in rather than carelessly made. At the same time, he widened the fit menu, including skin-tight knits and cleanly fitted suits alongside the season's broader movement toward bagginess.

## The Anniversary Moment

A monogrammed puffer emerged as the season's obvious anniversary hero item, its oversized silhouette combining the utilitarian and the emblematic. An Art Nouveau travel case rendered in stained glass rolled through on a trolley — absurd, beautiful, and precisely on-message for a house that has built its identity around the idea of departure as a form of luxury.

The event itself operated as full brand spectacle. A gospel choir and full orchestra performed live from the balconies, transforming what might have been a straightforward runway presentation into something closer to a staged musical sequence. The guest list mixed music, film, and digital celebrity: SZA, Usher, Future, and Jackson Wang, among others. BamBam of the Korean boy band GOT7 made a runway debut, marking a crossover moment within the show's broader embrace of celebrity and music culture.

The Fall-Winter 2026 collection marks the opening of a yearlong celebration centered on the monogram — the house's most recognizable signature and the design element that has anchored Vuitton's identity since its founding in 1896.
