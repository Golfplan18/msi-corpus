The inconclusive session highlights an unresolved central question in Syria's post-Assad political transition: whether the SDF — a U.S.-backed force of tens of thousands that controls much of northeastern Syria — will maintain its structure within the new national military or be disbanded with its fighters individually absorbed, a distinction complicated by Turkey's firm opposition to the SDF joining as a cohesive unit.

Syrian government officials met Sunday in Damascus with the top commander of the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces to discuss the SDF's planned integration into Syria's national army, state media reported, but the talks produced no "tangible results" that would accelerate implementation of the agreement.

The SDF said a delegation led by its top commander, Mazloum Abdi, held talks with government officials in Damascus related to the military integration process. The SDF later said the talks had concluded, with details to be released later. State television said the sides agreed to hold further meetings at a later date.

## A deadline already missed

Syria's interim President Ahmad al-Sharaa signed a deal with the SDF in March calling for the Kurdish-led force to merge with the national army by the end of 2025. That deadline passed without resolution, with disagreements persisting over how the integration would proceed.

The central sticking point is whether the SDF would remain a cohesive unit within the new military structure or be dissolved, with its fighters individually absorbed into the army. The SDF has tens of thousands of fighters and is the main force to be incorporated.

The March agreement also called for all border crossings with Iraq and Turkey, along with airports and oil fields in northeastern Syria, to come under the central government's control. Prisons holding approximately 9,000 suspected members of the Islamic State group are also expected to transfer to government authority under the deal.

## Turkey's opposition

Turkey has been an influential actor in Syria and has opposed the SDF joining the national army as a single unit. Ankara considers the SDF a terrorist organization because of its association with the Kurdistan Workers' Party, known as the PKK, which has waged a long-running insurgency in Turkey, although a peace process between the Turkish government and the PKK is now underway.

Clashes broke out between Syrian security forces and SDF fighters in the northern city of Aleppo in late December during a visit to Syria by Turkey's foreign minister.

## Rockets strike Damascus

Sunday's talks in Damascus came hours after three rockets struck a western neighborhood of the capital, causing some damage to a mosque and a telecommunications center. State media reported no casualties. The state news agency described the rockets as "random" and provided no details on their origin or who was responsible.
