WASHINGTON — The Justice Department has expanded its review of documents related to convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein to 5.2 million and assigned more than 400 attorneys to the effort, according to a person briefed on a letter sent to U.S. Attorneys. The department does not expect to release additional documents until Jan. 20 or 21 — more than a month after the Dec. 19 deadline Congress set by law.

The expanding scope of the review, which also covers documents related to Epstein's longtime associate Ghislaine Maxwell, has drawn pressure from lawmakers in both parties who demand compliance with the disclosure statute. The files are expected to name additional individuals involved in or aware of Epstein's abuse of women and girls, a prospect that has roiled both parties heading into midterm election season.

As MSI [previously reported](/articles/2025-12-31-justice-department-reviews-5-2-million-epstein-files-misses-deadline), the Justice Department missed the congressionally mandated Dec. 19 deadline for releasing the Epstein files. The 5.2 million document figure and the number of attorneys assigned to the review were first reported by The New York Times. The person briefed on the letter spoke on the condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss it.

## DOJ describes 'all-hands-on-deck' effort

Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche said Wednesday that lawyers from the Justice Department in Washington, the FBI, the Southern District of Florida, and the Southern District of New York are working "around the clock" to review the files.

"We're asking as many lawyers as possible to commit their time to review the documents that remain," Blanche said. "Required redactions to protect victims take time, but they will not stop these materials from being released."

The White House did not dispute the figures and pointed to a statement from Blanche describing the administration's review as an "all-hands-on-deck approach."

## Congressional pressure from both parties

Attorney General Pam Bondi is facing pressure from lawmakers after the Justice Department's rollout of information lagged behind the statutory deadline. Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican who helped lead the effort to pass the law mandating the release, posted on social media this week: "Should Attorney General Pam Bondi be impeached?"

Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer said on social media that the document figures "shows Bondi, Blanche, and others at the DOJ have been lying to the American people about the Epstein files since day one," noting that documents released so far represented a fraction of the total under review.

Rep. Ro Khanna, a California Democrat who also led the effort to pass the disclosure law, told the Associated Press that the expanding review showed the law is working.

"We are willing to give DOJ a few extra weeks to comply, provided they release the survivors' statements to the FBI naming the other rich and powerful men who abused them or covered up and the prosecution memos about charges that were dropped against Epstein and co-conspirators," he said. "When all the information comes out, this will shock the conscience of the nation."

A late-January release would put the Justice Department roughly five weeks past the statutory deadline. Some key lawmakers said they are willing to let the process play out before taking direct action against the administration.

## What released documents have shown

A tranche of documents released before Christmas showed that President Donald Trump flew on Epstein's private jet in the 1990s, when the two had a friendship that later ended. The initial release revealed little new detail about the extent of their relationship. The same documents included photos of former President Bill Clinton with women whose faces were obscured.

Republicans on the House Oversight Committee have focused on connections to the Clintons and are seeking to compel both Bill Clinton and former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton to appear for a deposition in January.

Democrats have argued that the Trump administration's handling of the files demonstrates a concern for the wealthy and well-connected over accountability.

"Unlike the President, we don't care who's in the files," said Rep. Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the oversight panel. "Anyone that's involved in the abuse of women and girls should be held accountable."
