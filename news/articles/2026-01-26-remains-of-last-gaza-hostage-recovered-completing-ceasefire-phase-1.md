The remains of Ran Gvili, the last hostage held in Gaza, were recovered Monday, ending an agonizing search that had stalled Israel's ceasefire agreement with Hamas. Gvili, a 24-year-old police officer, was killed on Oct. 7, 2023, while fighting militants who attacked Kibbutz Alumim during the Hamas-led assault that triggered the war. His body was identified by dental records during an exhumation operation in northern Gaza.

The recovery marks the completion of Phase 1 of the Trump administration's ceasefire plan and removes a major obstacle to advancing toward longer-term arrangements—including international force deployment in Gaza and formation of a temporary Palestinian government—that have remained stalled as negotiations faltered.

On the morning of Oct. 7, 2023, Gvili was at home recovering from a broken shoulder and on medical leave from his elite police unit. After hearing that militants were attacking a music festival nearby, he rushed to assist fellow officers.

Gvili and his team were heading toward the Nova Music Festival, where militants killed at least 364 people and took more than 40 hostage. But they encountered militants first at Kibbutz Alumim, a border village.

According to his mother Talik Gvili, in an interview with Ynet, "He radioed his team to warn that more vehicles carrying terrorists were approaching. He opened fire, and they came at him. He fought them alone, injured in both his leg and arm, and he took down those monsters."

At the entrance to Kibbutz Alumim stands a sign displaying Gvili's photo in uniform with the inscription "Rani, the Shield of Alumim," crediting his defense with saving kibbutz residents' lives. Unlike other Israeli communities attacked that day, Alumim survived, with residents crediting men like Gvili and emergency response teams for the defense.

Migrant workers on the kibbutz met a different fate. Left exposed in agricultural areas outside the kibbutz's defensive perimeter, 22 foreign nationals were killed, according to the Israeli newspaper Haaretz.

The military confirmed Gvili's death four months after the Oct. 7 attack. His body remained missing until Monday.

On Sunday, Hamas said it had exhausted all efforts to locate Gvili and provided Israel with information about the body's potential location in northern Gaza. After a major operation to exhume dozens of bodies from a cemetery, Israel's military identified Gvili's remains by his teeth.

Footage on Israeli media showed dozens of soldiers, arms wrapped around each other, singing religious songs as his body was identified and exhumed.

## The broader toll

Gvili was one of 251 people, mostly civilians, who were abducted during the Oct. 7 attack, when Hamas-led militants killed more than 1,200 people. In the ensuing war in Gaza, approximately 71,660 Palestinians were killed, according to Gaza's Health Ministry, which maintains casualty records seen as generally reliable by U.N. agencies and independent experts.

As part of the ceasefire agreement that took effect Oct. 10, Hamas released 20 living hostages and, over two months, the bodies of 27 of 28 deceased hostages. Hamas had previously said it could not locate Gvili's body.

## Ceasefire implications

His recovery marks the completion of Phase 1 of the Trump administration's 20-point ceasefire plan.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has pledged to reopen the Rafah crossing, which connects Gaza and Egypt and has been largely closed since May 2024. Palestinians consider the crossing their lifeline to the outside world.

The next steps of the ceasefire, which American envoys have been pushing to advance, will be more complex. They include deploying an international force to secure Gaza, disarming Hamas, and forming a temporary Palestinian government to manage daily affairs under supervision of an international board led by Trump.

"The entire people of Israel are moved to tears," President Isaac Herzog posted on X. "This was an operation of immeasurable importance in fulfilling the sacred obligation to redeem captives."

Herzog noted it was the first time since 2014 that Israel did not have any hostages held in Gaza.

## Family response

Gvili's sister Shira Gvili told the Associated Press in early December, when the last remaining hostage aside from her brother was released, "We see all the other families whose sons came back and we see in their eyes that they have relief. This is why it's so important. Because we want to move on with our with our life and just remember Rani."

His mother wrote on Facebook after receiving the news Monday: "The first to go, the last to leave. Our hero."

Gvili is survived by his parents, a sister, and a brother.
