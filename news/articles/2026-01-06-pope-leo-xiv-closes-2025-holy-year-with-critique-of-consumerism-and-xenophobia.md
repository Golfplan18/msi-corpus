The closing ends a year that dominated Leo's early papacy and, by his own framing, delayed his broader agenda. With the Jubilee complete, Leo has summoned the world's cardinals to the Vatican for two days of meetings beginning Wednesday — a signal, Vatican observers said, that his pontificate is now beginning in earnest.

VATICAN CITY — Pope Leo XIV kneeled in prayer at the threshold of the Holy Door of St. Peter's Basilica on Tuesday, then rose and pulled the two doors shut, formally closing the Catholic Church's 2025 Holy Year with a homily that condemned consumerism and hostility toward migrants.

The ceremony, held on the Feast of Epiphany, concluded a Jubilee marked by historic circumstances: opened by a frail Pope Francis in December 2024, the Holy Year continued through Francis' death and the subsequent conclave that elected his successor. Only once before in recorded history, in 1700, has a Holy Year been opened by one pope and closed by another.

The Vatican claimed 33,475,369 pilgrims had participated in the Jubilee, though organizer Archbishop Rino Fisichella acknowledged at a press conference that the figure was a rough estimate and could include double counting. Neither he nor Italian officials provided a breakdown distinguishing Holy Year pilgrims from Rome's overall tourist figures for the period.

## A homily against 'distorted economy' and closed borders

In his homily, Leo said the Jubilee had invited all Christians to reflect on Biblical teachings to welcome the stranger and resist "the flattery and seduction of those in power."

"Around us, a distorted economy tries to profit from everything," Leo said. "Let us ask ourselves: has the Jubilee taught us to flee from this type of efficiency that reduces everything to a product and human beings to consumers? After this year, will we be better able to recognize a pilgrim in the visitor, a seeker in the stranger, a neighbor in the foreigner, and fellow travelers in those who are different?"

He carried the theme to the basilica's loggia, where he delivered a special Epiphany prayer to thousands gathered below in the rain-soaked piazza, many sheltering under colorful umbrellas and ponchos. Leo recalled that Jubilees have historically included appeals for peace and "a redistribution of the land and its resources" to those in need.

"In the place of inequality, may there be fairness, and may the industry of war be replaced by the craft of peace," he said.

## Leo's agenda resumes

The special audiences, Masses and public programming required by the Jubilee dominated Leo's first months as pontiff and in many ways delayed his own priorities. With the Holy Year now sealed, Leo has summoned the world's cardinals to the Vatican for two days of meetings beginning Wednesday to discuss governing the 1.4-billion-strong Catholic Church.

The agenda includes the issue of the liturgy — an indication that Leo intends to address divisions within the church over the celebration of the old Latin Mass.

Rome spent some 4 billion euros ($4.3 billion) in public funds on infrastructure projects tied to the Jubilee. The main undertaking was a pedestrian piazza along the Tiber River linking Via della Conciliazione to the nearby Castel Sant'Angelo, with a major road that had separated the two landmarks diverted to an underground tunnel.

## Seven centuries of Holy Years

Rome's Holy Year tradition dates to 1300, when Pope Boniface VIII inaugurated the first Jubilee — an occasion so significant that Dante referenced the crowds of pilgrims in his *Inferno*. Massive public works have long accompanied the observances, including the Sistine Chapel, commissioned by Pope Sixtus IV for the Jubilee of 1475.

Leo has announced the next Jubilee will be held in 2033, to mark what Christians believe was the death and resurrection of Christ in A.D. 33.
