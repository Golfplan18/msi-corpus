Opposition parties lost all parliamentary seats in Benin’s election, according to provisional results released by the electoral commission on Saturday evening.

The legislative vote took place weeks after a deadly military takeover attempting to overthrow President Patrice Talon was foiled, authorities said, after the episode lasted a few hours.

The provisional count showed that out of five parties running, only the Republican Bloc and the Progressive Union for Renewal—both aligned with Talon—won seats in the assembly. The Republican Bloc is set to have 49 lawmakers, and the Progressive Union for Renewal is set to have 60, based on the provisional results.

A new electoral code requires parties to clear thresholds in both national and district-level voting to be eligible for seat allocation. Under the code, a party must obtain 20% of the national vote and 20% in each of the 24 electoral districts.

The main opposition party, The Democrats, won around 16% of the vote but failed to reach the 20% threshold, meaning it would receive no lawmakers, according to the provisional results.

Guy Mitokpe, the spokesperson for The Democrats, said the results confirm the struggle the party has been waging for about two years. “These results confirm the struggle that (The Democrats) party has been waging for about two years,” Mitokpe said. He added that the party had denounced what it views as an electoral code that “heavily favored parties aligned with the president,” calling it “an exclusionary electoral code.”

Mitokpe cited the party’s exclusion from other races as an example. “As proof, we won’t have a candidate in the presidential election, and we were excluded from the municipal elections,” he said.

The electoral commission reported voter turnout of 36.73%. The commission said the results must now be confirmed by Benin’s Constitutional Court.

Benin, which has seen coups in the years since it gained independence from France in 1960, has experienced relative calm in the past two decades, the Associated Press reported. The country is set to elect a new president in April, and Talon, 67, is barred from running after a decade in office.

The AP report said Talon’s close ally, Finance Minister Romuald Wadagni, is seen as the front-runner to replace him, as the main opposition candidate was barred from running for failing to meet required endorsements. It also said that while Talon’s tenure saw a period of economic growth, critics accuse him of clamping down on political opposition and human rights.
