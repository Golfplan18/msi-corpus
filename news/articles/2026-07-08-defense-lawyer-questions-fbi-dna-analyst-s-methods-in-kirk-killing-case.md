## Defense lawyer questions FBI DNA analyst's methods in Kirk killing case

**Subtype:** fact

- Defense lawyer Michael Burt questioned an FBI DNA analyst's methods during a preliminary hearing in the Charlie Kirk killing case.
- Burt argued the analyst could not match Tyler Robinson to DNA samples from a rifle found wrapped in a towel at Utah Valley University.
- Deputy Utah County Attorney Ryan McBride said the reliability of the DNA testing could be examined if the case goes to trial.
- The five-day preliminary hearing in Provo, Utah, began Tuesday and is expected to continue Wednesday.

## Prosecutor says DNA reliability a trial matter

A member of Tyler Robinson's defense team interrogated a DNA analyst from the FBI on Tuesday about the techniques she used to connect Robinson to a rifle found wrapped inside a towel at Utah Valley University, where Kirk was shot in September while speaking to a crowd.

Defense lawyer Michael Burt cast doubt on the analyst's conclusions — a theme that is likely to come up again during the five-day preliminary hearing.

"She can't match Mr. Robinson to the questioned samples," Burt concluded.

Deputy Utah County Attorney Ryan McBride countered that the reliability of the DNA testing could be examined if the case goes to trial. He suggested the preliminary hearing was not the time to take up the matter.
