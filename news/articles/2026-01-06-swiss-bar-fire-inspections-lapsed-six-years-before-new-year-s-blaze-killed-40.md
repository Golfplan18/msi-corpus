The disclosure adds an institutional accountability dimension to a criminal investigation already under way against the bar's managers, and prompted the municipality to announce new measures — including a ban on indoor fireworks — just five days after one of the deadliest bar fires in European history.

Fire safety inspections had not been conducted at Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana for six consecutive years before a New Year's Day blaze killed 40 people and injured more than 100, the head of the Swiss resort town's municipal government said Tuesday.

Nicolas Féraud told a news conference that the local council discovered after reviewing documents that "periodic checks were not carried out between 2020 and 2025." Valais canton regulations require annual fire safety inspections of buildings accessible to the public, and regional authorities say municipalities are responsible for conducting them.

"We regret this bitterly," Féraud said. He added that judicial authorities would determine what influence the lapsed inspections may have had on events leading to the fire, and said he could not immediately explain why checks had gone unconducted for so long.

Inspections that included fire safety checks had been carried out at Le Constellation in 2016, 2018, and 2019, the municipality said. Modifications were requested after each round but no concerns about soundproofing were raised. In September 2025, an external expert commissioned to review soundproofing concluded the bar complied with anti-noise rules without making further remarks.

## Criminal investigation under way

Swiss authorities have opened a criminal investigation into the bar's two managers. The Valais region's chief prosecutor said the managers are suspected of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm, and involuntarily causing a fire.

Investigators believe sparkling candles placed atop Champagne bottles ignited the blaze when they came too close to the ceiling, according to the Associated Press. Authorities are examining whether soundproofing material on the ceiling conformed with fire regulations and whether such decorative candles were permitted inside the venue. The fire broke out at approximately 1:30 a.m. on New Year's Day.

## Victims identified; French probe opened

The severity of burns sustained by some victims complicated identification and required families to supply DNA samples. Investigators finished identifying all 40 dead by Sunday and confirmed identification of all 116 injured on Monday. The victims came from several countries.

On Monday, the Paris prosecutor's office announced it was opening a parallel probe to assist the Swiss investigation and make it easier for families of French victims to communicate with Swiss investigators. Nine French citizens were killed — the youngest 14 years old — and 23 French nationals were injured.

## Capacity and permits

Records from prior inspections listed a maximum capacity of 100 people on the bar's ground floor and 100 in its basement. It remains unclear how many people were inside Le Constellation when the fire broke out; investigators have said that number may never be known.

The bar's owner obtained a permit to build a veranda in 2015 and also carried out interior modifications that did not require a permit, the municipality said.

## Municipal response

The municipality announced it has decided to ban indoor fireworks and will commission an external agency to conduct inspections of such establishments going forward.
