## Five states agree to host nuclear waste storage campuses

**Subtype:** fact

- Five states — Tennessee, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Utah, and Idaho — have agreed to non-binding agreements with the federal government to host Nuclear Lifecycle Innovation Campuses that would store more than 95,000 metric tons of spent nuclear fuel and radioactive waste from across the country.
- The campuses would also include uranium enrichment, spent-fuel reprocessing, fuel fabrication, advanced reactor deployment, power generation, and data centers, according to the U.S. Energy Department.
- The states' Republican governors said the deals would help the United States reclaim its leadership in civilian nuclear energy and create thousands of jobs.
- Spent nuclear fuel is currently stored at reactor sites and former nuclear facilities nationwide and has been piling up without a permanent disposal site, as the planned Yucca Mountain repository in Nevada has been stalled for years by local opposition.

## Non-binding deals aim to address spent fuel buildup at reactor sites

The U.S. Energy Department announced the non-binding agreements with Tennessee, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Utah and Idaho. The states would host Nuclear Lifecycle Innovation Campuses that would include waste sites as well as uranium enrichment, spent-fuel reprocessing, fuel fabrication, and potentially advanced reactors and data centers, the department said.

If developed, the sites would represent significant progress toward addressing more than 95,000 metric tons of spent nuclear fuel and radioactive waste that has accumulated at nuclear plants and former nuclear sites across the country.

The agreements come after decades of failed efforts to establish a permanent underground storage site. Plans for the Yucca Mountain repository in Nevada were stalled amid staunch opposition from most residents and officials, with spent fuel remaining at reactor sites and former nuclear facilities nationwide, according to the Energy Department.

The states' Republican governors described the initiative as a chance to help America reclaim its leadership in civilian nuclear energy and to create thousands of jobs through new nuclear projects.
