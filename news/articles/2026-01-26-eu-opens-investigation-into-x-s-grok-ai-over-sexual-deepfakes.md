The European Union opened a formal investigation Monday into Elon Musk's social media platform X after his artificial intelligence chatbot Grok generated nonconsensual sexualized deepfake images. The investigation centers on whether X has met its legal obligations under the Digital Services Act, the European Union's comprehensive regulation for keeping internet users safe from harmful online content.

The scrutiny marks an intensification of international regulatory pressure on Grok and X over artificial intelligence-generated sexual content, following similar governmental action in Southeast Asia and coordinated pressure from 35 U.S. state attorneys general.

The European Commission said the investigation will examine whether Grok, launched last summer by Elon Musk's AI company xAI, complies with the Digital Services Act. The law applies only to the largest online platforms and requires them to contain the spread of illegal content including manipulated sexually explicit images and content that "may amount to child sexual abuse material." Regulators are also widening a separate ongoing investigation into X's content recommendation systems.

The severity of the problems became apparent late last month when Grok began granting large numbers of user requests to modify images posted by others. The tool allows users to edit photos to undress people and depict them in revealing clothing. Researchers said some images appeared to include children.

"Non-consensual sexual deepfakes of women and children are a violent, unacceptable form of degradation," said Henna Virkkunen, an executive vice president at the European Commission overseeing technology, security and democracy.

Virkkunen said in a statement that the investigation will determine "whether X has met its legal obligations under the DSA, or whether it treated rights of European citizens — including those of women and children — as collateral damage of its service."

## Regional and regulatory actions

The backlash extends beyond Europe. Malaysia and Indonesia blocked access to Grok earlier this month. Malaysian authorities lifted their temporary restriction on Friday after the company implemented additional security measures. Malaysian regulators said they met with X's representatives and would continue monitoring the situation.

In the United States, attorneys general in 35 states sent a letter to X requesting disclosure of its plans to prevent Grok from creating nonconsensual sexual deepfakes and asking how it will eliminate such content from the platform.

"We strongly urge you to be a leader in this space by further addressing the harms resulting from this technology," the state attorneys general wrote.

## Company response and strategic shifts

An X spokeswoman said the company remains "committed to making X a safe platform for everyone" and has "zero tolerance" for child sexual exploitation, nonconsensual nudity and unwanted sexual content. X announced plans to switch Grok's AI system to choose which posts users see on the platform.

The company said on January 14 that it would stop allowing users to depict people in "bikinis, underwear or other revealing attire," but only in jurisdictions where such depictions are illegal.

The EU investigation covers Grok only as it operates on X, not the chatbot's standalone website or app. The Digital Services Act applies only to the bloc's largest platforms, so X's implementation falls under the regulation while Grok's other channels do not.

There is no deadline for the bloc to resolve the investigation. It could result in X pledging to change its behavior, or in a substantial fine. In December, Brussels issued X a €120 million fine for other Digital Services Act violations, including deceptive design practices related to blue checkmarks.

The bloc has also been scrutinizing X over allegations that Grok generated antisemitic material and has requested further information from the company.
