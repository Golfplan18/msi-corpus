## Canada ranks in top three for 45 of 50 US states

The United States remains the larger trading partner in aggregate terms, but Canada is the top customer for 26 US states and ranks among the top three for 45 of the 50 states, according to a BBC News analysis published September 5.

The analysis, presented in a video by BBC reporter Jessica Murphy, names three states as illustrative: Maine, Michigan, and Wisconsin. In each, Canada holds the top position.

Murphy attributes Prime Minister Mark Carney's negotiating leverage in the tariff dispute to the geographic and economic integration that runs across multiple US state-level markets. The BBC frames the exchange as an "escalating trade war" and a "spiralling dispute" between Canada and the United States.

The analysis reports that Carney "has room to manoeuvre" in the dispute and asserts that "Canada has more leverage than it may seem" relative to the aggregate bilateral figures. The piece identifies Carney as the figure positioned to act on that leverage in the tariff exchange with Washington.

The state-level picture sits alongside the broader fact that the United States remains the larger of the two economies. The BBC analysis uses this contrast to argue that Canada's leverage reaches further than the overall trade balance would imply.

The video, produced by Eloise Alanna, was published on the BBC News video page and directs viewers to the outlet's separate written coverage for ongoing developments in the tariff exchange.
