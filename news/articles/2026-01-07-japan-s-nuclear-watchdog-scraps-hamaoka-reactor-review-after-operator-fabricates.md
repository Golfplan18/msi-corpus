Japan's nuclear watchdog said Wednesday it is scrapping safety screening for two reactors at the Hamaoka nuclear power plant after the plant's operator acknowledged that workers fabricated seismic data to underestimate earthquake risks at the coastal facility, which sits above one of the country's most hazardous fault zones. The Nuclear Regulation Authority suspended the review for Hamaoka's No. 3 and 4 reactors and said it is considering inspecting the headquarters of Chubu Electric Power Co.

The disclosure is a setback to Japan's push to accelerate nuclear reactor restarts, a government priority driven by rising energy costs and carbon-reduction targets, and renews safety concerns in a country where public opinion remains divided more than a decade after the 2011 Fukushima Daiichi meltdowns.

NRA Chair Shinsuke Yamanaka said the regulator will decide the case next week without waiting for the utility's own investigation to conclude.

"Ensuring safety is the first and foremost responsibility for nuclear plant operators," Yamanaka said. "It is outrageous and it's a serious challenge to safety regulation."

## Whistleblower and Admission

The NRA said it began an internal investigation in February after receiving a whistleblower tip that Chubu Electric had for years provided fabricated data that underestimated potential seismic risks. The regulator confirmed the falsification and the utility acknowledged the fabrication in mid-December, Yamanaka said.

Chubu Electric President Kingo Hayashi acknowledged Monday that workers at the utility used inappropriate seismic data with an alleged intention to underestimate seismic risks. Hayashi apologized and pledged to establish an independent panel to investigate.

The utility had applied for safety screening to resume operations at the No. 3 and 4 reactors in 2014 and 2015. Two other reactors at the plant are being decommissioned, and a fifth is idle.

The screening, including data that had already been approved, would have to start from scratch or possibly be rejected entirely, Yamanaka said.

## Seismic Exposure

The Hamaoka plant is located about 200 kilometers (125 miles) west of Tokyo in a coastal area known for potential risks from so-called Nankai Trough megaquakes, making the accuracy of seismic data a load-bearing element of any restart application.

## Nuclear Restart Context

Less than a quarter of Japan's commercial nuclear reactors are operational in the aftermath of the 2011 Fukushima Daiichi meltdowns. Of Japan's 57 commercial reactors, 13 are currently in operation, 20 are offline, and 24 others are being decommissioned, according to the NRA.

Public opinion in Japan remains divided over nuclear power, with lingering safety concerns stemming from the Fukushima disaster continuing to shape the political environment around any restart efforts.
