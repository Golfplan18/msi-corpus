Another ethics complaint has been filed against Nevada Lt. Gov. Stavros Anthony, alleging he used his office’s social media presence on X to promote his book for personal financial benefit, according to the complaint shared with The Nevada Independent.

The complaint was filed by political consultant and former Executive Director of Planned Parenthood Votes Lindsey Harmon, The Nevada Independent reported through Associated Press distribution. The filing contends Anthony promoted his newly published book on X on a page designed to look like his official lieutenant governor account, including a link to the state’s website for his office.

Nevada law prohibits public officials from using their positions—including governmental time, property and equipment—to gain personal advantage, according to the complaint’s description. The filing alleges Anthony’s use of social media is a category E felony under a separate Nevada law.

The book at the center of the complaint is titled “Nights of Fire: An Account of Civil Unrest in Las Vegas During the Aftermath of the Rodney King Trial.” The Nevada Independent reported that it was published Nov. 27.

After the complaint was filed, Anthony tweaked his X account so it no longer links to his government website and instead links to his official campaign account, The Nevada Independent reported, citing screenshots shared with the outlet. Anthony’s office did not immediately return a request for comment.

The Nevada Independent also reported that earlier this January screenshots captured Anthony posting campaign endorsements from the same X account, including an endorsement for Clark County Sheriff Kevin McMahill. When asked about those endorsements earlier this month, the lieutenant governor’s office told The Nevada Independent that the account had been Anthony’s personal page since 2012.

The ethics complaint is the latest against Anthony as he faces mounting scrutiny over the use of state resources to advance personal agendas. This summer, Nevada’s ethics commission took issue with Anthony for using state resources for a task force aiming to keep transgender athletes out of women’s sports, and it proposed dismissing the matter instead of continuing proceedings if Anthony completed corrective actions.

Those proposed actions included coming into compliance with ethics law, training his entire staff, and adopting a social media policy for his office that prohibits using government accounts to promote political campaigns, The Nevada Independent reported. Anthony declined to enter into a deferral agreement, the ethics commission confirmed to The Nevada Independent, and he requested that the commission further consider the matter.

The complaint process described by The Nevada Independent has multiple stages, with initial stages confidential until an adjudicatory hearing, when a panel determines the outcome. If the panel finds a violation of Nevada ethics law, penalties can include mandatory training, civil penalties upwards of $5,000, or a petition for the removal of the public officer.

The Nevada Independent reported that filing a complaint does not mean the commission will take action. The commission must first review the complaint and determine whether there is an ethics issue and then make a report detailing the situation. It also reported that the ethics commission complaint process carries no criminal liability for ethics violations.

The lieutenant governor’s office has also been the subject of other recent reporting, including The Nevada Independent’s earlier report that Anthony ousted a former staffer and replaced her with a prominent conservative activist as he heads into his re-election bid. The Nevada Independent also reported that in November, an ethics complaint against the office’s chief of staff, Rudy Pamintuan, was forwarded to the commission for review.
