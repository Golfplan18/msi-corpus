Pakistani counterterrorism police raided multiple militant hideouts in Karachi on Monday, seizing approximately 2 tons of explosives and arresting three suspects, officials said. The bomb-making materials and detonators were intended for attacks in Pakistan's largest port city by members of the Baloch Liberation Army, a separatist group that authorities have banned, senior police officials said at a news conference.

The operation reflects the continued reach of Baloch insurgents into Karachi, where the outlawed BLA has carried out a series of attacks — including a 2022 suicide bombing that killed three Chinese teachers — as the group intensifies operations more than two decades into an insurgency rooted in demands for autonomy and a larger share of Balochistan's natural resources.

## Operation details

Zulfiqar Ali Larik, a senior counterterrorism official, said one vehicle loaded with explosives was ready to be used and that the materials had been transported from Balochistan, the southwestern province where the separatist insurgency is based. Larik alleged the BLA receives backing from India, a claim New Delhi denies.

Ghulam Azfar Mahesar, a deputy inspector general of police, said the raids were conducted jointly by police and intelligence agencies. One suspect was arrested initially, and information obtained during interrogation led to the arrest of two others, he said. Additional raids were underway to apprehend remaining members of the network who had fled, Mahesar said.

## BLA insurgency and recent attacks

Baloch separatists have waged an insurgency since the early 2000s, seeking greater autonomy and, in some cases, independence from Pakistan, while demanding a larger share of Balochistan's natural resources.

In March 2025, BLA insurgents killed 33 people, mostly soldiers, in an assault on a train carrying hundreds of passengers in Balochistan. Security forces killed 50 attackers and rescued the remaining passengers.

Authorities say the BLA has increasingly sought to use female attackers, apparently to avoid detection. Last week, police detained a teenage girl whom authorities said had been radicalized and recruited online by the BLA to carry out a suicide attack. Officials determined she was a victim rather than a suspect and filed no criminal charges against her.

In 2022, a female suicide bomber linked to the BLA killed three Chinese teachers near a university campus in Karachi. Over the years, the group has expanded its operations, regularly targeting security forces, civilians, and Chinese nationals working on projects tied to the China-Pakistan Economic Corridor, a multibillion-dollar infrastructure initiative.

Pakistan has also accused neighboring Afghanistan of supporting the BLA and the Pakistani Taliban, two groups that have intensified attacks in the country in recent years.
