The early return, unprecedented in NASA's ISS history, also required the cancellation of the agency's first planned spacewalk of 2026 — an excursion that would have prepared the station for future solar panel installations. Three other astronauts remain aboard and are unaffected by the shortened mission.

NASA cut short a four-person International Space Station mission Thursday after one crew member developed an undisclosed medical condition, marking the agency's first medical evacuation from the orbiting laboratory. The affected crew member is stable, and NASA said the situation does not constitute an emergency. The agency declined to identify which astronaut is affected or describe the nature of the condition, citing patient privacy.

The early return also required NASA to cancel its first planned spacewalk of 2026 — an excursion intended to prepare the station for future solar panel installations to boost its power supply.

## 'Erring on the side of caution'

Dr. James Polk, NASA's medical and health director, said the agency is erring on the side of caution for the crew member and that the situation does not rise to the level of a medical emergency aboard the station. Polk said this is NASA's first medical evacuation from the International Space Station, though astronauts have previously received treatment aboard for conditions including toothaches and ear pain.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said he is proud of the swift, agency-wide effort to ensure the safety of the crew.

## The crew

The four astronauts returning ahead of schedule are NASA's Zena Cardman and Mike Fincke, Kimiya Yui of Japan, and Oleg Platonov of Russia. They arrived at the station in August 2025 aboard a SpaceX vehicle for a planned stay of at least six months.

Fincke and Cardman had been assigned to conduct the canceled spacewalk, during which they would have made preparations for a future deployment of solar panels to provide additional electrical power to the station.

This was Fincke's fourth trip to the space station and Yui's second. For both Cardman and Platonov, it was their first spaceflight, according to NASA.

## Operations continue aboard the station

Three other astronauts remain on the station and are unaffected by the shortened mission: NASA's Chris Williams and Russian cosmonauts Sergei Mikaev and Sergei Kud-Sverchkov. They arrived in November 2025 aboard a Soyuz rocket for an eight-month stay and are expected to return home in the summer.

NASA has contracted SpaceX to deorbit the International Space Station in late 2030 or 2031, with plans for a controlled reentry over the ocean.
