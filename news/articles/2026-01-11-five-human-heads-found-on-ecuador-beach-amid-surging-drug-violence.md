Police in Ecuador found five human heads hanging from ropes affixed to wooden poles on a beach in the small fishing port of Puerto López on Sunday, authorities said, attributing the scene to a conflict between criminal groups. A warning sign placed beside the heads was directed at alleged extortionists of local fishermen, according to a police report. The incident struck Manabí province, where two separate attacks killed six people apiece in the preceding two weeks and where a state of emergency remains in force.

The killing underscores Ecuador's descent into cartel-driven bloodshed: 2025 was the country's deadliest year on record, with more than 9,000 homicides logged by official figures, surpassing the previous record of 8,248 set in 2023.

Five human heads were found hanging from ropes affixed to wooden poles on a beach in Puerto López, a small fishing port on Ecuador's southwestern coast, police said Sunday. A warning sign was placed beside the heads, directed at alleged extortionists of local fishermen, according to authorities.

A police report attributed the scene to a conflict between criminal groups. Drug-trafficking networks with links to transnational cartels operate in the area and have used fishermen and their boats for illicit activities, authorities said.

## Record violence in Manabí province

The discovery is the latest in a sequence of deadly incidents in Manabí province. Police had increased controls in Puerto López after a massacre left six people dead approximately two weeks earlier. A second armed attack three days after that killing left six more dead in Manta, also in Manabí.

A dispute over territory and control of drug-trafficking routes has driven violent episodes across the province, authorities said.

Ecuador has enforced a state of emergency in nine of its 24 provinces, including Manabí. The measure restricts certain civil rights and seeks to contain the spiral of violence, particularly in coastal areas. Police said Saturday that control and surveillance operations had been carried out in Puerto López under the emergency framework.

## Ecuador's deadliest year on record

2025 was Ecuador's most violent year on record, with more than 9,000 homicides according to official figures — surpassing the previous record of 8,248 deaths set in 2023.

The country has been in a wave of violence for more than four years after becoming a logistical hub for the storage and distribution of drugs that enter mainly through the northern border with Colombia and the southern border with Peru.
