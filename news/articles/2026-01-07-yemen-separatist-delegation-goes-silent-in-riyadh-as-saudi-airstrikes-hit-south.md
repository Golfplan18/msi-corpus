ADEN, Yemen — A 50-member delegation from Yemen's Southern Transitional Council arrived in the Saudi capital of Riyadh on Wednesday for talks aimed at reducing tensions among rival anti-Houthi factions, then went silent — phones switched off, whereabouts unknown — as Saudi Arabia launched more than 15 airstrikes overnight on separatist positions in the country's south, the group said.

Two civilians died and 14 were wounded in the strikes on al-Dhale governorate, according to STC representative Amr al-Bidh.

"We went to Riyadh to talk. What we received was bombing," al-Bidh told reporters. "This is unjustified and unfortunate."

The breakdown marks the sharpest rupture yet in the fragile coalition of forces that has spent a decade fighting Iranian-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen's civil war, raising the prospect of open armed conflict among factions that were, until recently, nominal allies in the Arab world's poorest country.

## Coalition goes silent

The STC, the most powerful group in southern Yemen, said one delegation member posted a message on X after arriving in Riyadh, but all contact then ceased. The silence came hours after Yemen's Saudi-backed Presidential Leadership Council charged STC leader Aidarous al-Zubaidi with treason, accusing him in a Facebook statement of "damaging the republic's military, political and economic standing," as well as "forming an armed gang and committing the murder of officers and soldiers of the armed forces."

Saudi coalition spokesperson Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said al-Zubaidi had been due to fly to Saudi Arabia with other officials but did not join the delegation. Al-Malki said intelligence indicated al-Zubaidi had moved "a large force — including armored vehicles, combat vehicles, heavy and light weapons, and ammunition" and had "fled to an unknown location."

Al-Bidh disputed that account, saying al-Zubaidi remained in Aden, Yemen's interim capital, to carry out his duties, because "an environment conducive to dialogue doesn't currently exist." The Saudi foreign ministry did not immediately respond to a request for comment, according to the Associated Press.

## Airstrikes and curfew in Aden

More than 15 Saudi airstrikes overnight hit al-Dhale governorate, where al-Zubaidi's village is located, targeting STC camps, according to STC leader Salah bin Laghir. Witnesses told the AP they saw armored vehicles affiliated with the STC leaving Aden overnight toward al-Dhale, and observed drones and flames as explosions shook neighborhoods there.

A curfew was imposed across Aden from 9 p.m. to 6 a.m. local time, with exemptions for medical and security personnel and emergency cases, announced by Presidential Leadership Council member Abu Zarae Al-Mahremy in a Facebook post. Schools were closed until further notice. Local security forces instructed shop owners and restaurants to close Wednesday night, witnesses told the AP, as checkpoints spread across much of the city.

The PLC also removed Ahmed Lamles from his post as governor of Aden and referred him for investigation for reportedly supporting al-Zubaidi's advances in Hadramout and Mahra governorates last month.

## How the coalition unraveled

The STC seeks to establish an independent nation in southern Yemen — a goal Saudi Arabia views as a violation of its national interests. Last week, the separatists announced a constitution for an independent state and demanded other factions accept the decision.

The crisis escalated in December, when the separatists seized two southern governorates from Saudi-backed forces and took over the Presidential Palace in Aden. Members of the internationally recognized government, which had been based in Aden, fled to Riyadh. Saudi forces then struck the port city of Mukalla, saying they were targeting weapons delivered from the United Arab Emirates to the STC. The anti-Houthi coalition demanded that the UAE — which has long backed the separatist group — withdraw its forces from Yemen; the UAE said over the weekend it had done so.

Al-Bidh said approximately 80 people affiliated with the STC had been killed since tensions rose in December, with most dying in Saudi bombings. On Sunday, Saudi-backed forces retook Mukalla, the capital of Hadramout governorate, following days of Saudi airstrikes.

## Decade of conflict

Yemen's civil war, fought on the southern edge of the Arabian Peninsula bordering the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden, has killed more than 150,000 people including fighters and civilians, according to the AP, and created one of the world's worst humanitarian disasters.

The professed goal of the coalition fighting the Houthis has long been to restore Yemen's internationally recognized government, which Houthi rebels drove from the north. The fighting among coalition members now challenges any unified campaign against those rebels.
