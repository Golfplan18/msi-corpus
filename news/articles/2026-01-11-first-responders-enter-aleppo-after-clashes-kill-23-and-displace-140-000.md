First responders entered a contested neighborhood in Aleppo, Syria, on Sunday after five days of fighting between Syrian government forces and the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces killed at least 23 people and displaced more than 140,000, the Associated Press reported. Kurdish fighters evacuated from the Sheikh Maqsoud neighborhood to northeastern Syria and declared a "partial ceasefire," saying they would continue to fight after wounded personnel and civilians were extracted.

The clashes, described by the AP as the most intense since former President Bashar Assad was ousted by insurgents in December 2024, erupted after negotiations over how to merge the U.S.-backed SDF into Syria's national army failed to advance — exposing the fragility of the country's post-civil-war transition.

## Fighting and Displacement

Fighting broke out January 6 in the predominantly Kurdish neighborhoods of Sheikh Maqsoud, Achrafieh, and Bani Zaid, the AP reported. Government security forces captured Achrafieh and Bani Zaid; Kurdish fighters subsequently withdrew from Sheikh Maqsoud to territory in northeastern Syria under SDF control.

Shelling and drone strikes drove more than 140,000 people from their homes during the five-day clash, according to the AP. The neighborhood appeared calm Sunday, the outlet reported.

The underlying dispute centers on the SDF's status in a reconstituted Syrian military. The U.S.-backed force has played a key role in combating the Islamic State group across large parts of eastern Syria and remains the largest armed faction not yet absorbed into the national army. Some units of the national army were previously Turkish-backed insurgent groups with a long history of conflict with Kurdish forces, the AP noted.

## Hospital Accusations

The SDF accused the Syrian government of targeting Khalid al-Fajer Hospital "dozens of times" before patients were evacuated. Damascus accused the Kurdish-led group of using the hospital and other civilian facilities as military positions.

Government security forces brought journalists to the area Sunday to tour the damaged hospital and an SDF military position that government forces had targeted, the AP reported. Both accusations remained unresolved as of the reporting.

## Mines, Aid, and the Displaced

Syrian Civil Defense first responders have been disarming improvised mines that they said were left by Kurdish forces as booby traps. Residents who fled were not permitted to return until the mines were cleared.

The United Nations said it was attempting to dispatch convoys to the neighborhoods carrying food, fuel, blankets, and other urgent supplies.

On one street, AP journalists found Syrian Red Crescent workers speaking with residents surrounded by charred cars and badly damaged residential buildings.

"We lived a night of horror. I still cannot believe that I am right here standing on my own two feet," said Ahmad Shaikho, a resident. "So far the situation has been calm. There hasn't been any gunfire."

Hoda Alnasiri, who had been displaced, appealed to be allowed to return. "I want to go back to my home, I beg you," Alnasiri said.

Some residents told the AP that SDF forces had not allowed their vehicles through checkpoints to leave during the fighting. The displacement drew comparisons to earlier rounds of forced movement during Syria's prolonged civil war.
