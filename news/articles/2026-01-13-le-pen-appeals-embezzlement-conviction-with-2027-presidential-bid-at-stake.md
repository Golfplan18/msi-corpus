PARIS — Marine Le Pen appeared before a Paris appeals court Tuesday to contest an embezzlement conviction that carries a five-year ban from holding elected office, placing her 2027 French presidential campaign in legal jeopardy. The National Rally leader, 57, denied wrongdoing, telling the three-judge panel that her party had not concealed its hiring practices and had not understood them to violate any rules.

"I'd like to tell the court that ... we did not feel we had committed any offence," Le Pen said. "We have never concealed anything."

The five-week appeal will determine whether France's most prominent opposition figure can stand in next year's presidential election — or faces a stiffer sentence, potentially up to 10 years in prison and a 1 million euro ($1.17 million) fine, if the conviction is upheld or the penalty stiffened.

## The conviction

A Paris court convicted Le Pen in March of misusing European Parliament funds by employing aides from 2004 to 2016 who instead performed duties for her party — known then as the National Front — in French domestic politics. The sentencing judge described Le Pen as the center of a "system" designed to divert EU funds, including to pay for her bodyguard and her chief of staff. The judge also found that Le Pen and her co-defendants did not personally enrich themselves.

Her sentence included two years of house arrest with an electronic monitoring bracelet, an additional two-year suspended sentence, and a 100,000-euro ($116,800) fine, alongside the five-year bar from elected office.

The appeal involves Le Pen, 10 other defendants, and the National Rally party as a legal entity. The case originated with a 2015 alert raised by Martin Schulz, then-president of the European Parliament, to French authorities.

## The Parliament's rebuttal

Patrick Maisonneuve, the European Parliament's lawyer, disputed Le Pen's characterization of the institution's oversight role.

"There's a contradiction in saying at the same time: 'I deny you the right to examine the content of my work as a member of parliament' and then saying: 'but the European Parliament didn't conduct a thorough review,'" Maisonneuve told reporters.

Le Pen had argued that European Parliament officials did not warn her party at the time that its hiring arrangements potentially violated EU rules.

## Political stakes

Le Pen was widely regarded as a potential front-runner to succeed President Emmanuel Macron in the 2027 election before last year's conviction. Her National Rally party holds the largest single bloc of seats in France's National Assembly and leads in national opinion polls.

Le Pen has alleged the proceedings amount to what she called "a democratic scandal" and has characterized the conviction as an attempt to prevent her from becoming president. Anti-corruption advocates have disputed that framing. Transparency France, an advocacy organization, noted that the conviction followed years of investigation and a trial in which Le Pen and other party members were able to freely defend their positions.

The three-judge panel is expected to announce a verdict possibly before summer. Outcomes range from acquittal to a new conviction that could bar her from the 2027 race.

## Succession

Le Pen took over the party from her father, Jean-Marie Le Pen, in 2011 and stepped down as party president in 2021, handing the role to Jordan Bardella, now 30. If she is ultimately barred from the 2027 race, Bardella is widely expected to serve as the party's presidential candidate.

Bardella said Monday in a New Year address that a conviction barring Le Pen from running would be "deeply worrying for (France's) democracy."
