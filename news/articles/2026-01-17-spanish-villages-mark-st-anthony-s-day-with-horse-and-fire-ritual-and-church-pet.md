Hundreds of spectators watched horses gallop through towering bonfires in a central Spanish village Friday night, hours before pet owners in Madrid lined up outside a church Saturday to have their dogs and cats blessed with holy water.

Both ceremonies mark St. Anthony's Day, the annual feast honoring St. Anthony the Abbott — the patron saint of domestic animals — observed each January across Spain. The contrasting traditions, one a centuries-old fire ritual in a village of 500, the other a quiet sprinkling of holy water at a city church, drew loyal crowds despite cold winter weather.

Las Luminarias and the urban pet-blessing ceremonies represent twin expressions of folk devotion that have persisted for centuries in Spain, even as the agricultural communities that gave rise to the fire ritual have largely emptied, and even as animal rights groups continue to object to open-flame horse jumping.

## Las Luminarias

Las Luminarias takes place each year in San Bartolomé de Pinares, a village of about 500 residents roughly 100 kilometers (60 miles) outside Madrid. Riders guide horses through bonfires lit in the middle of the street, in an act believed to purify the animals for the coming year.

Festivities begin around nightfall as residents stack giant bundles of tree branches along the street, sharing wine, beer and sweets while they wait. Hours later, the stacks are set alight, creating the fires horses must leap through — or around.

Antonio Patricio, 62, an attendee, described the tradition's agricultural roots. "In the old days, it was held because it was believed that the branches and the smoke blessed the horses and donkeys, which were used for farming, as a form of healing to prevent them from getting sick and to ensure they continued working in the fields," Patricio said.

Locals say Las Luminarias originated after a mysterious illness swept through the village's animals centuries ago, after which residents came to believe that smoke could purify and heal horses. The ritual has endured even as San Bartolomé and scores of other central Spanish villages have emptied; for one night each year, it draws families, friends and onlookers back.

## Preparations and controversy

Before the horses enter the flames, riders wrap the animals' tails in fire-resistant tape and apply a protective glaze to their manes. Some braid the manes tightly and adorn their horses with pink and red ribbons and decorative headpieces.

Animal rights groups have long criticized Las Luminarias. Locals say the horses are rarely, if ever, injured.

## Pet blessings in Madrid

On St. Anthony's Day, Catholic priests at St. Anthony's church in central Madrid blessed pets brought by their owners, sprinkling the animals with holy water as their owners waited patiently outside the church entrance. Many dogs arrived in winter vests. Cats, by observers' accounts, looked somewhat bewildered.

Madrid resident Rosa Gómez attended with her pointy-eared dog Kia. "I'm happy to be able to do it," Gómez said. "She is a little dog that was given to us six years ago by a family that couldn't take care of her, so we adopted her, and since then she has kept us great company."

The pet-blessing ritual, like Las Luminarias, is believed to bring the animals health and protection for the year ahead.
