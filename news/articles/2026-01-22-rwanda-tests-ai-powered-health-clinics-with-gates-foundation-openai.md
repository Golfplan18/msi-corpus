Rwanda will test artificial intelligence technology in more than 50 health clinics as part of a new initiative by the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation and OpenAI called Horizons1000. The two-year, $50 million program aims to improve health care services across Africa in regions where medical staff are severely stretched.

The project addresses an acute shortage of health workers. Rwanda has one health care worker for every 1,000 patients, far below the globally recommended ratio of 4:1,000. The Gates Foundation argues that AI can help expand access to quality care in regions with severe staffing shortages.

## Deployment begins across African clinics

The Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation and OpenAI announced Wednesday the launch of Horizons1000, a $50 million initiative designed to support 1,000 health clinics across Africa over two years. Rwanda will serve as an early test site, with the technology deployed in more than 50 clinics.

"In poorer countries with enormous health worker shortages and a lack of health systems infrastructure, AI can be a game changer in expanding access to quality care," Bill Gates said in a blog post announcing the initiative.

Andrew Muhire, a senior official with Rwanda's Ministry of Health, told the Associated Press that the technology is designed to strengthen clinical judgment rather than replace it, while improving operational efficiency within the country's stretched health system.

Muhire described the initiative as a "transformative opportunity" that will improve citizens' access to health care, "reduce administrative burden," and help medical professionals make "more accurate and timely decisions."

## Language compatibility emerges as central challenge

Digital experts have flagged a significant implementation barrier: current AI systems operate primarily in English, which is not widely spoken in Rwanda.

Audace Niyonkuru, CEO of the AI and open data company Digital Umuganda, told the AP that efforts are underway to develop AI technologies in Kinyarwanda, the language spoken by approximately 75 percent of Rwanda's population.

"Deploying AI technologies that do not operate in Kinyarwanda would pose a serious barrier to effective care," Niyonkuru said.

## Bridging critical gaps in care

The initiative targets regions where health worker shortages have created severe gaps in access to care. Rwanda currently has one health care worker for every 1,000 patients, far below the globally recommended ratio of 4:1,000. The Gates Foundation argues that AI can bridge this gap by enhancing the capacity of existing medical staff to diagnose and treat patients more efficiently.
