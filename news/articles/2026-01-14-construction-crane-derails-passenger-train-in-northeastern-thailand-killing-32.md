The accident struck a section of a planned Thai-Chinese high-speed rail project and renewed scrutiny of the main contractor, Italian-Thai Development, which was also a co-lead contractor on a Bangkok government building that collapsed during an earthquake in March, killing roughly 100 people.

A construction crane collapsed onto a moving passenger train in northeastern Thailand on Wednesday, triggering a derailment that killed at least 32 people and injured 64 more, including seven severely, the province's Public Health Provincial Office said. Three passengers remained missing as of Wednesday evening among the 171 believed to have been aboard.

The accident renewed scrutiny of the main contractor, Italian-Thai Development, which was also a co-lead contractor for a Bangkok government building that collapsed during an earthquake in March, killing roughly 100 people — and drew attention to a pattern of safety failures on major infrastructure projects in Thailand involving the same firm.

## The crash

The crane fell in Nakhon Ratchasima province, about 200 kilometers (135 miles) northeast of Bangkok, while the train was traveling from the capital to Ubon Ratchathani province, the Associated Press reported. The crane was a launching gantry crane — a self-supporting structure that typically runs on rails or wheels and is used to build elevated sections of roads and railways — and was being used on an elevated portion of a planned high-speed rail line at the time it fell.

Video from Thai public broadcaster ThaiPBS showed rescue workers standing atop overturned railway carriages, some with gaping holes torn in their sides. Images published in Thai media showed plumes of white and dark smoke rising from the site, with construction equipment dangling from girders between two concrete support pillars.

Samai Teechantuek, a 62-year-old whose house is about 100 meters from the site, told the AP of the moment the crane fell.

"The noises screeching, and then bam, bam, bam, all the way over there," she said. "When the dust settled, I saw the top of the train carriage. I heard people shouting 'save the children first!' A conductor pulled people out. I saw them pulling many people out. People from the shop over there also ran out to help."

"My legs were shaking. I was standing there shaking. I didn't dare going any closer," she said.

## Contractor scrutiny

The section of track where the accident occurred is part of a Thai-Chinese high-speed rail project linking Bangkok to Nong Khai province on the Laos border, a component of China's Belt and Road Initiative intended to eventually connect China with much of Southeast Asia. The total project has an investment cost of more than 520 billion baht ($16.8 billion). The section involved in Wednesday's accident carried a budget of more than 179 billion baht ($5.7 billion) and was originally expected to begin operating in 2027.

Anan Phonimdaeng, acting governor of the State Railway of Thailand, identified the main contractor as Italian-Thai Development, also known as Italthai, with a Chinese company responsible for design and construction supervision. Anan ordered the contractor to suspend operations at the site pending the investigation. He said the Railway Department plans to take legal action against the contractor as an initial step, and that authorities will examine the responsibilities of both parties.

Damage to the train was estimated at more than 100 million baht ($3.2 million), Anan said, while construction equipment suffered limited damage.

Italian-Thai Development posted a statement on its website expressing condolences to victims and said the company would take responsibility for paying compensation to families of those killed and for hospitalization costs for the injured.

The accident drew immediate attention because Italian-Thai Development was also the co-lead contractor for the State Audit Building in Bangkok, which collapsed during a major earthquake in March and killed approximately 100 people — the only major structure in Thailand to suffer such serious damage during that event. Dozens of executives were indicted in connection with that collapse, but none have yet been tried.

Involvement of Chinese companies in both projects has drawn additional scrutiny, as has Italthai's and Chinese companies' participation in the construction of several expressway extensions in and around Bangkok where multiple accidents, some fatal, had previously occurred.

In August 2024, a railway tunnel on the same planned high-speed rail route, also in Nakhon Ratchasima, collapsed, killing three workers. Days of heavy rainfall were believed to have contributed.

## Government response

Transport Minister Phiphat Ratchakitprakarn said he ordered an investigation into Wednesday's accident.

Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, who served as interior minister at the time of the State Audit Building collapse, said the Comptroller General's Department and Transport Ministry are responsible for blacklisting contractors, and that existing laws could not be amended in the brief time he held the interior minister post to expedite such action.

In Beijing, Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said the government was aware of the reports about the accident and had expressed condolences.

"The Chinese government attaches great importance to the safety of projects and personnel, and we are also learning about the situation," she said. "At present, it appears that the relevant section is being constructed by a Thai company, and the cause of the accident is still under investigation."
