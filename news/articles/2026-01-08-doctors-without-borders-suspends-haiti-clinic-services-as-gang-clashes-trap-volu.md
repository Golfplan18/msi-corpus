PORT-AU-PRINCE — Médecins Sans Frontières said Thursday it was suspending services at its clinic in the Bel-Air neighborhood of Port-au-Prince after violent clashes between police and armed men trapped seven community volunteers inside the building for hours and left one former volunteer dead at the clinic gate. The suspension eliminates the only medical services available in that area of Haiti's capital, where the organization served several thousand patients each month.

MSF, known in English as Doctors Without Borders, said the clashes occurred on Tuesday. One former volunteer was seriously injured during the violence and died at the building's gate.

"This situation is not an isolated case," MSF said in a statement.

Bel-Air is largely controlled by the Krache Dife gang, which is part of a large coalition of armed groups known as Viv Ansanm — Haitian Creole for "Living Together." MSF began working in the neighborhood in 2022 and has previously been forced to suspend services there because of violence.

## A shrinking health system

The Bel-Air closure follows MSF's announcement in October that it was shutting its emergency care center in another area of Port-au-Prince, also because of ongoing violence in the capital. Armed groups now control an estimated 90 percent of Port-au-Prince, according to the Associated Press, and around 60 percent of health facilities in the city are closed or nonfunctioning — including Haiti's general hospital.

The scale of the humanitarian toll is significant. From July to September, at least 1,247 people were killed across Haiti and another 710 were injured, according to the United Nations. More than 1.4 million people have been displaced across the country as violence has persisted.

## International mission falls short of targets

A United Nations-backed mission led by Kenyan police officers is helping an understaffed and underfunded Haitian national police force fight the armed groups. The mission launched in mid-2024, but by late 2025 it had assembled less than half the personnel originally envisioned and secured only 14 percent of the estimated $800 million it needed annually. The mission is in the process of transitioning into a gang suppression force with authority to arrest suspected gang members.
