CARACAS — Families of Venezuela's political prisoners spent Monday camped on sidewalks outside Caracas detention facilities, waiting for releases that the interim government of President Delcy Rodríguez had promised last week as a gesture of "peace." By Monday afternoon, the Venezuelan human rights group Foro Penal had verified the freedom of only 49 prisoners — a number that human rights advocates and a United Nations-backed investigative body said fell far short of what the government's statements had suggested.

CARACAS — Yaxzodara Lozada spent Monday night on a sidewalk outside a Caracas prison, one of many family members of political detainees who have gathered outside Venezuela's jails since last week waiting for releases that the interim government promised as a gesture of "peace."

By Monday afternoon, the Venezuelan human rights group Foro Penal had verified the freedom of only 49 prisoners — far fewer than advocates had expected. The group said those freed included nationals of Italy, Spain, Argentina, Israel, and Colombia. Human rights organizations estimate more than 800 people remain imprisoned in Venezuela on political grounds, according to the Associated Press.

The White House confirmed Monday that opposition leader María Corina Machado will meet with President Donald Trump on Thursday.

Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform over the weekend that Venezuela had begun "in a great way" the process of releasing its political prisoners, and said the releases came at Washington's request.

The UN-backed investigative mission on Venezuela welcomed the releases but said in a statement Monday that the number freed in recent days "is far below" the broader demand for the "immediate and unconditional release of all political prisoners."

## Families wait without answers

Interim President Rodríguez's government had promised the releases beginning Thursday, describing them as a gesture of "peace," but authorities did not disclose the names or numbers of prisoners they planned to free. That silence left rights groups searching for information and families in anguished uncertainty.

Lozada said her husband, a police officer, was arrested November 17 without explanation. She had not seen him since.

Jenny Quiroz's husband was arrested November 26 at his pharmacy in Caracas over allegedly criticizing the government in a WhatsApp group, she said. Quiroz, who had spent the night outside the prison, described the competing realities she saw playing out around her.

"They are two realities. They want the world to see that everything is normal. That nothing is happening here," Quiroz said, in comments translated from Spanish. "But it is a mix of anguish and desperation. Do you know what it means to go 48 days without knowing whether he eats, whether they have him isolated, whether they are subjecting him to psychological or physical torture?"

Quiroz said she wanted Trump to know that the information he receives about the prisoner releases is not entirely accurate.

## Government projects normalcy

Venezuela's interim government also deployed security forces to public schools across the country Monday for the first day of classes since the holiday break, as commerce, schools, and gyms reopened. The government has sought to project an image of returning normality in the weeks following the January 3 US military operation that resulted in the arrest of President Nicolás Maduro.

During a televised school tour, Rodríguez — surrounded by children — said Venezuela "actively resists" the United States while "we are writing a new page of Venezuelan history."
