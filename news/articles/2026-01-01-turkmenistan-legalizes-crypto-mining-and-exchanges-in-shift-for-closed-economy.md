ASHGABAT, Turkmenistan — Turkmenistan legalized cryptocurrency mining and exchanges on Thursday, with President Serdar Berdimuhamedov signing legislation that brings virtual assets under civil law in one of the world's most isolated nations.

The move represents a significant policy shift for a Central Asian country that has long maintained tightly controlled borders, a government-regulated internet, and an economy built almost entirely on natural gas exports.

## What the Law Does

The legislation places virtual assets within Turkmenistan's civil law framework and establishes a licensing scheme for cryptocurrency exchanges, according to the Associated Press. The licensing regime is overseen by the country's central bank, creating a formal government-supervised channel for cryptocurrency activity.

However, digital currencies will not be recognized as a means of payment, currency, or security under the law. That exclusion limits cryptocurrency's practical use in everyday commerce within Turkmenistan, even as the legal architecture for the industry takes shape.

Turkmenistan's internet also remains tightly regulated and controlled by the government — a condition that further constrains how widely residents can access the platforms and services on which cryptocurrency activity depends.

## A Gas-Dependent Economy

Turkmenistan, a former Soviet republic that gained independence in 1991, relies heavily on the export of its vast natural gas reserves. China is the country's main importer of gas. Turkmenistan is also working on a pipeline that would supply gas to Afghanistan, Pakistan, and India.

The country's closed economic posture has historically extended beyond energy policy. After independence, the nation typically placed strict entry requirements on would-be visitors, with many visa applications turned down for unclear reasons, the AP reported.

Turkmenistan adopted a law introducing electronic visas in April 2025, aimed at simplifying entry for foreigners — an earlier signal of incremental opening in a country long resistant to external contact. The cryptocurrency legislation follows in that vein, extending legal recognition to an industry the government had previously left unaddressed.
