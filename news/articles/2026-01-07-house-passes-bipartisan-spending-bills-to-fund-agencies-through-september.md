WASHINGTON (AP) — The U.S. House passed a bipartisan package of three spending bills on Thursday that would fund parts of the federal government through September, as lawmakers worked to avoid another government shutdown near the end of the month.

The bill passed the House by an overwhelming vote of 397-28. The measure would still need approval from the Senate before President Donald Trump could sign it into law.

Congress has passed only three of the 12 annual spending bills that fund federal agencies for the current fiscal year. Lawmakers said failure to pass the remainder before a Jan. 30 deadline risks another shutdown just weeks after a record-setting, 43-day shutdown late last year.

Leaders from both parties endorsed the latest measure, signaling that passage is likely in the Senate as well and leaving Congress “halfway home” in completing work on this year’s spending bills. The White House also endorsed the measure, calling it a “fiscally responsible bill.”

The package covers several agencies, including the Interior Department, the Environmental Protection Agency, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers and the Departments of Commerce and Justice. Democrats and Republicans differed in how they characterized the bill’s impact, with both sides highlighting what they described as changes from the Trump administration’s requests.

Republicans said the price tag of the bills, which they put at roughly $175 billion, comes in below current levels and generates savings for taxpayers. Democrats countered that they negotiated spending levels far above what the Trump administration had requested and removed scores of policy riders that they said would have weakened gun safety regulations, expanded oil and gas leasing on federal lands, and targeted LGBTQ and racial equity policies.

Democrats also said the measure includes legally binding spending requirements that restrain the White House’s ability to withhold or delay funds for programs Trump opposes. They pointed to Trump’s first year in office, when states, cities and nonprofits filed scores of lawsuits accusing the administration of unlawful power grabs.

Rep. Rosa DeLauro, ranking Democrat on the House Appropriations Committee, said: “This legislation is a forceful rejection of draconian cuts to public services proposed by the Trump administration and Republicans in Congress,” in urging support for the package.

Rep. Tom Cole, the Republican chairman of the House Appropriations Committee, urged colleagues to vote for the bill and said: “Republicans are strongest when we stay focused, Democrats are more effective when they negotiate in good faith, and the country is better off when Republicans and Democrats work together.”

Lawmakers also cited how far behind the spending process is this year. They said Congress has often bundled the 12 annual spending bills into one or two measures and voted before lawmakers left Washington for the holidays, which they said can make it easier to include provisions that could not pass on their own.

Democrats pointed to specific funding increases they said were maintained or expanded despite opposition from the Trump administration. They said a program meant to make homes more energy efficient for low-income Americans received a $3 million boost instead of being eliminated as proposed by Trump. They also said the Environmental Protection Agency would get $8.8 billion, which they said is more than double what Trump sought.

Republicans had raised concerns about earmarks included in the bill, which is now called community funding projects. To address those concerns, the bill removed a nearly $1.5 million earmark obtained by Rep. Ilhan Omar that would have funded a Somali-led organization’s efforts to provide job training and peer support services for people struggling with addiction.

The article said Republicans have been intensely focused on allegations of fraud by day care centers run by Somali residents, with those allegations still being investigated. Omar urged people not to blame an entire community for the actions of a relative few.
