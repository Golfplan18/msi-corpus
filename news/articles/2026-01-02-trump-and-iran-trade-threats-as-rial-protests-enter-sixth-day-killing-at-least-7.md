President Donald Trump and senior Iranian officials traded threats Friday over growing protests in Iran that have left at least seven people dead and entered their sixth day, further elevating tensions between Washington and Tehran months after U.S. forces struck Iranian nuclear facilities. Trump posted on Truth Social that Washington would come to protesters' rescue if Iran killed them violently and said the United States was ready to act, while Iran's foreign minister, senior security officials, and the country's UN ambassador warned that any outside interference would be met with force.

The confrontation is the sharpest public exchange between the two governments since the June 2026 conflict in which U.S. forces struck Iranian nuclear installations alongside an Israeli military campaign. The protests, sparked by the collapse of Iran's rial currency, have broadened to include calls against clerical rule, making them the most widely attended unrest Iran has seen since the 2022 demonstrations that followed the death of Mahsa Amini in police custody.

## Iranian officials respond

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi posted on X that Iran's armed forces were on alert and knew exactly where to aim if any nation violated Iranian sovereignty, according to the Associated Press.

Ali Larijani, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and a former parliament speaker, wrote on X that Israel and the United States were stirring up the protests — an accusation Iranian authorities have repeated during past rounds of unrest without providing supporting evidence. He warned that U.S. intervention in Iran's internal affairs would produce chaos across the region and damage American interests, and warned Trump that the American people should look after their own soldiers.

Ali Shamkhani, an adviser to Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei who previously served as secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, warned that any interventionist hand that came too close to Iran's security would be cut off, according to AP.

Iran's ambassador to the United Nations, Amir Saeid Iravani, wrote Friday evening to Secretary-General António Guterres and the Security Council. He asked the body to condemn U.S. rhetoric, called for affirmation of Iran's right to defend its sovereignty, and stated that the United States was fully responsible for any consequences stemming from what he described as illegal threats.

## No change in U.S. force posture

A U.S. official, speaking on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military planning, told AP that no significant changes had been made to U.S. force levels or preparations in the Middle East in the wake of Trump's social media posts.

## A calculated risk

Trump's direct expression of support for Iranian protesters departs from the approach of previous U.S. administrations. During Iran's 2009 Green Movement, President Barack Obama declined to offer public backing for protesters — a decision Obama later said was a mistake.

But open support from Washington carries risks of its own.

Naysan Rafati, an analyst at the International Crisis Group, a nonprofit focused on conflict prevention and resolution, told AP that while the grievances fueling the protests stem from the Iranian government's own policies, authorities would likely use Trump's statement as evidence that the unrest was being driven by external actors. Rafati said that using the statement to justify harsher repression carried the risk of provoking the very American involvement Trump had hinted at.

Iran's foreign ministry spokesman, Esmail Baghaei, cited longstanding Iranian grievances against the United States in response to Trump's post, including a CIA-backed coup in 1953, the 1988 U.S. downing of an Iranian passenger aircraft, and U.S. participation in the June conflict.

## Protests continue on sixth day

Videos circulating on social media Friday night showed demonstrations continuing in multiple Iranian cities, including at least three locations in southern and eastern Tehran. AP said it could not independently verify the footage.

Protesters also took to the streets in Zahedan, in the restive Sistan and Baluchestan province near the border with Pakistan. The funerals of several demonstrators killed in the unrest prompted additional marches.

Video reported to show the funeral of Amirhessam Khodayari, 21, showed mourners pushing back security forces who attended the burial. Khodayari died Wednesday in Kouhdasht, in Lorestan province, more than 400 kilometers southwest of Tehran. Iranian authorities said he had served in the Basij, the volunteer component of the Revolutionary Guards' paramilitary force — a claim his father denied. The semi-official Fars news agency subsequently reported doubts about the government's account.

## Economic roots of the unrest

The protests began as anger over the rapid fall of Iran's rial. A dollar now costs approximately 1.4 million rials, according to AP. Reformist President Masoud Pezeshkian has said his government wants to negotiate with demonstrators but has acknowledged a limited ability to stabilize the currency.

As the demonstrations have continued, crowds have added calls against the theocracy itself. Iran has not succeeded in shoring up its economy in the months since the June war.

The current protests are the most widely attended Iran has experienced since the 2022 demonstrations that followed the death of Mahsa Amini, a 22-year-old who died in police custody after being arrested for allegedly not wearing her hijab in conformity with official requirements. The current unrest has not yet spread across the country as broadly, nor has it been as intense as those demonstrations.

## June war's long shadow

The mutual threats between Washington and Tehran unfold against the backdrop of the June 2026 conflict in which the United States struck three Iranian nuclear installations in coordination with a 12-day Israeli military campaign against the Islamic Republic. Iran subsequently attacked Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar, a major U.S. military facility. No personnel were killed in that strike, though a missile hit a structure at the base.

Iran has recently announced that it no longer enriches uranium at any domestic facility, a signal AP characterized as intended to indicate to Western governments that Tehran remains open to nuclear negotiations in exchange for sanctions relief. Those talks have not materialized. Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu have both warned Iran against reconstituting its nuclear program.
