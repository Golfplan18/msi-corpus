The Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group held a symbolic burial Thursday for 22 people it says were killed in a Jan. 2 drone strike on its military positions in eastern Congo's Masisi territory. Hundreds of family members attended the ceremony at Goma's Unity Stadium alongside rebel administration officials and religious representatives, while the Congolese government denounced the event as fabricated propaganda.

The burial comes amid ongoing peace negotiations between M23 and Kinshasa to end decades of fighting in eastern Congo, where more than 7 million people have been displaced in what the United Nations describes as one of the world's most severe humanitarian crises.

GOMA, Congo — The Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group on Thursday held a symbolic burial for 22 people it says were killed in a Jan. 2 drone strike, drawing hundreds of family members to a ceremony at Unity Stadium in Goma that the Congolese government denounced as a fabricated spectacle.

The ceremony was attended by M23 administration officials, representatives of religious groups, and family members of the victims. M23 said the strike targeted its military positions in the Masisi territory of North Kivu province and was carried out by the Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of Congo, known as FARDC, and their allies, using what the rebel group described as "kamikaze drones." FARDC has not commented on the attack.

Théophile Kubuya Hangi, medical director of Masisi General Hospital, told the Associated Press that the facility received 47 people wounded in the explosions.

"Twenty-two people died from their injuries. Fourteen wounded are still hospitalized and receiving care," he said.

Promesse Hagenimana, who was injured in the strike, described the moment of the explosion.

"We were standing on the road in front of a concrete house. Moments later, the house exploded before our eyes," she said. "We threw ourselves to the ground. When I got up, my arm felt heavy. Next to me, a little boy was dead. I ran to the hospital before losing consciousness."

M23 said the bodies had been transferred from Masisi to Goma the day before the ceremony. Emmanuel Ndizeye, the administrator of the Masisi territory, said only victims from road-accessible areas could be brought to the capital.

"In some localities, insecurity and the state of the roads do not allow for the evacuation of victims, who have been buried on site," Ndizeye said. M23 said three additional people were buried near the site of the strike.

## Civilian Death Toll Disputed

M23's claim that all 22 victims were civilians was challenged by Stewart Muhindo, a researcher at the Kinshasa-based Ebuteli research center. Muhindo said the figure appeared inflated and that some coffins at the ceremony could contain the bodies of fighters killed in combat.

"The figure of 22 civilians killed is greatly exaggerated," Muhindo said, adding that sources on the ground in Masisi indicated actual civilian casualties did not appear to exceed a dozen.

Patrick Muyaya, spokesperson for the Congolese government, called the ceremony "the height of indecency and inhumanity" in a post on X.

"The mass graves, the bodies abandoned by the roadside, the villages emptied of their inhabitants," Muyaya said. "No ceremony, no staged event, no fabricated narrative can erase the reality of these crimes, nor absolve the father #Rwanda and the #M23 who planned, ordered, and executed them."

Erasto Musanga, named as the governor of North Kivu by the M23 administration, accused Congolese President Félix Tshisekedi of responsibility for the violence and threatened legal action through the rebel group's own justice system.

## A Region of Enduring Crisis

M23 has grown from hundreds of members in 2021 to approximately 6,500 fighters, according to the United Nations. More than 100 armed groups are active in the mineral-rich eastern Congo region near the Rwandan border, with M23 the most prominent. Congo, the United States, and U.N. experts accuse Rwanda of backing the rebel group. Rwanda denies the accusation.

The conflict has displaced more than 7 million people, according to the U.N. refugee agency, creating one of the world's most severe humanitarian emergencies.

The burial comes despite the signing of a U.S.-brokered deal between the Congolese and Rwandan governments and continuing negotiations between M23 and Kinshasa to end decades of fighting. Combat continues on multiple fronts across eastern Congo, claiming additional civilian and military casualties.
