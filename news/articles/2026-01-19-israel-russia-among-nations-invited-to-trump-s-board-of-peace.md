The board, whose final membership list is expected to be announced at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, would supervise an executive committee charged with deploying an international security force, disarming Hamas and rebuilding war-devastated Gaza. Israel's government said the formation of the executive committee was not coordinated with it and "is contrary to its policy," while France said it would not join "at this stage."

The Trump administration has invited Israel, Russia, Belarus, the European Union and dozens of other governments to join a new body called the Board of Peace to oversee the next phase of the Gaza ceasefire, officials said Monday, as questions mounted about the body's mandate, decision-making structure and potential rivalry with the United Nations.

Morocco's King Mohammed VI accepted a seat, becoming the first Arab leader and at least the fifth world leader to join, the Associated Press reported. Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Hungary and Argentina have also signed on.

Israel has been asked to join but it is not known whether Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has accepted the invitation, an Israeli official said, speaking on condition of anonymity because they were discussing a behind-the-scenes diplomatic matter.

## Israel's objections

Netanyahu's office said the formation of an executive board that will work to carry out the Board of Peace's vision was not coordinated with the Israeli government and "is contrary to its policy," without clarifying specific objections. Turkey, a key regional rival, is among those invited to join the committee.

Netanyahu said later Monday that while differences exist with the United States about the composition of the advisory committee for the next phase in Gaza, they would not harm his relationship with Trump.

"There will not be Turkish soldiers and Qatari soldiers in the (Gaza) Strip," he said.

Israeli far-right Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich dismissed the Board of Peace as a raw deal for Israel and called for its dissolution.

"It is time to explain to the president that his plan is bad for the State of Israel and to cancel it," Smotrich said. "Gaza is ours, its future will affect our future more than anyone else's. We will take responsibility for what happens there, impose military administration, and complete the mission."

Smotrich, who opposed the Gaza ceasefire, also suggested that Israel renew a full-scale offensive on the territory to destroy Hamas if it does not abide by what he called "a short ultimatum for real disarmament and exile."

## France holds out; Trump threatens tariffs

France does not plan to join the Board of Peace "at this stage" despite receiving an invitation, a French official close to President Emmanuel Macron said, speaking anonymously in line with the French presidency's customary practices. The issue raises questions particularly "with regard to respect for the principles and structure of the United Nations," the official said.

When told Monday that Macron was unlikely to join, Trump said, "Well, nobody wants him because he's going to be out of office very soon."

"I'll put a 200% tariff on his wines and champagnes and he'll join," Trump told reporters in Florida before a flight back to Washington. "But he doesn't have to join."

Russia received an invitation, and the Kremlin is "studying the details" and will seek clarity on "all the nuances" in contacts with the United States, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said. Trump confirmed Monday that Putin had been invited. Belarus President Alexander Lukashenko is ready to take part, according to the country's Foreign Ministry. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer said the United Kingdom is talking to allies about the Board of Peace.

European Commission spokesperson Olof Gill said that Commission President Ursula von der Leyen would speak with other EU leaders about Gaza, without confirming whether the commission's invitation had been accepted.

## How the board would work

Board members would oversee an executive committee charged with implementing the ceasefire's second phase, including deployment of an international security force, disarmament of Hamas and reconstruction of the war-devastated territory.

A $1 billion contribution secures permanent membership on the board, with the funds going to rebuild Gaza, according to a U.S. official who spoke on condition of anonymity because he was not permitted to speak publicly about the board's charter, which has not been made public. A three-year appointment carries no contribution requirement.

The World Bank has estimated that it will take $53 billion to rebuild Gaza.

A Trump reference in the invitation letters saying the body would "embark on a bold new approach to resolving global conflict" suggested the board could act as a rival to the U.N. Security Council. The U.S. is expected to announce its official list of members in the coming days, likely during the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland. The large number of invitations being sent out, including to countries that do not get along, has raised questions about the board's mandate and decision-making processes, as has the unclear role Israel would play on a board charged with implementing a ceasefire that directly involves it.

## Running Gaza

Egypt's foreign minister met Monday with the leader of a newly appointed committee of Palestinian technocrats who will run Gaza's day-to-day affairs during the second phase of the ceasefire. Foreign Minister Bader Abdelatty met with Ali Shaath, a Palestinian engineer and former official with the Western-backed Palestinian Authority, who was named last week as chief commissioner of the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza.

Abdelatty expressed the Egyptian government's "complete support" of the committee and affirmed its role in running Gaza's daily affairs until the Palestinian Authority takes over the territory, the Egyptian Foreign Ministry said in a statement.

## Humanitarian situation

The U.N. World Food Program said Monday it has "significantly expanded" its operations across Gaza 100 days into the ceasefire, reaching more than a million people each month with hot meals and food parcels, while warning the situation remains "extremely fragile." The WFP said malnutrition has been prevented for 200,000 pregnant and breastfeeding women, as well as children under 5.

An Integrated Food Security Phase Classification analysis that assessed conditions in October and November found that 77% of Gaza's population was facing crisis-level food insecurity.

## Israeli operations; Palestinians killed

Israeli military and security forces launched what they called a large-scale counterterrorism operation Monday in the West Bank city of Hebron to dismantle "terrorist infrastructure, eliminating illegal weapons possession and strengthening security." The Israeli military said the operation is expected to continue for several days. Hebron Mayor Khaled Dudin said Israeli forces targeted the area, home to 80,000 people, because it obstructs the construction of additional Israeli settlements.

Israeli forces on Monday killed three Palestinians in southern Gaza, including 17-year-old Hussein Tawfiq Abu Sabalah, who was shot and killed in the Muwasi area of Rafah, according to Nasser hospital. Two men crossed into Israeli-controlled areas east of Khan Younis before being shot dead. It was not immediately clear whether the teenager had crossed into or came close to the Israeli-controlled area.

More than 460 people have been killed by Israeli fire and their bodies brought to hospitals since the ceasefire went into effect just over three months ago, according to the Gaza Health Ministry. The ministry, which is part of the Hamas-led government, maintains detailed casualty records that are seen as generally reliable by U.N. agencies and independent experts.
