## WHO report and peer-reviewed studies consistently identify Wuhan market as spillover site

Sen. Rand Paul, R‑Ky., and former National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases director Dr. Anthony Fauci clashed over the origins of the COVID-19 pandemic during a Senate hearing Wednesday, with the scientific community pointing to an animal-to-human spillover as the best-supported hypothesis. Fauci, who led NIAID until 2022, has consistently backed the zoonotic theory, while Paul and the Trump administration assert the virus emanated from the Wuhan Institute of Virology. In 2025, the Trump administration removed several government websites containing COVID-19 information and replaced them with a page titled “LAB LEAK: The True Origins of COVID-19.”

The scientific evidence continues to point away from a laboratory origin. In a report issued in June 2025, the World Health Organization stated that “the weight of available evidence … suggests zoonotic spillover … either directly from bats or through an intermediate host.” The conclusion rested on three years of work by the WHO Scientific Advisory Group for the Origins of Novel Pathogens, a 27‑member panel of experts.

A 2024 study in the peer‑reviewed journal Cell examined multiple lines of evidence and found that coronavirus‑susceptible wildlife, the SARS‑CoV‑2 virus, and human beings were mingling in a very specific section of the Huanan wet market. Two earlier studies in Science, released in 2022, mapped the distribution of the earliest known cases and showed they formed a bull’s‑eye centered at the market. Evolutionary biologist Michael Worobey, who co‑authored the Science and Cell papers, told NPR in 2022 that the geographic clustering was extraordinarily tight. “It’s absurd how strong the geographical association is [to the market],” he said, estimating the odds that the virus came from a lab yet still produced that case cluster at “1 in 10,000.”

Worobey acknowledged that researchers have not found a closely related virus in an intermediate animal host. “But we have a bunch of other evidence,” he said. “The evidence is amongst the best we have for any emerging virus.”

Supporters of the lab‑leak theory note that scientific analyses have relied on incomplete data provided by the Chinese government and that no infected animal has ever been identified in the Wuhan market. Some U.S. intelligence agencies have expressed a low‑to‑moderate degree of confidence in the accidental lab‑leak scenario. Virologists who study pandemic origins, however, are far less divided. They describe the case for an animal origin as supported by “overwhelming evidence.”

WHO director‑general Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus and other experts continue to urge an open mind. “As things stand, all hypotheses must remain on the table, including zoonotic spillover and lab leak,” Tedros said. “We continue to appeal to China and any other country that has information about the origins of COVID-19 to share that information openly, in the interests of protecting the world from future pandemics.” Evolutionary virologist Jesse Bloom, writing in an email to NPR, said it is essential “to keep investigating this question with an open mind while also proactively taking steps to minimize the risks of both future research‑related and zoonotic outbreaks.”

Several scientists expressed concern that the political focus on pandemic origins is eroding public trust and weakening the country’s ability to confront the next outbreak. Felicia Goodrum, a virologist at Dartmouth College, said the goal of the ongoing investigations into Fauci’s personal notes is “to sow distrust in science and scientists.” Speaking to NPR on Monday from the American Society for Virology meeting in Minneapolis, Goodrum said funding cuts and the tension surrounding the origins debate are already disrupting research aimed at pandemic prevention. “We are less safe. We are less prepared for a pandemic right now than we were in 2019,” Goodrum said. “And this is terrifying to all of us.”
