The Paris summit produced the most detailed allied consensus yet on a post-ceasefire security architecture, but left unresolved the central obstacle — Russia has insisted there can be no ceasefire until a comprehensive settlement is agreed, and President Vladimir Putin has ruled out any deployment of NATO-country troops on Ukrainian soil. Participating nations also still must finalize binding commitments before any guarantees can take effect.

PARIS — Leaders from 27 European countries, Canada, the European Union and NATO declared "major progress" Tuesday on a multilayered framework to defend Ukraine if a peace deal is struck with Russia, at the 15th and largest meeting of the so-called "coalition of the willing" in Paris — the first to include U.S. envoys in person.

Plans discussed at the summit included having Washington lead any ceasefire monitoring effort, a role that would give the United States a direct stake in enforcing any eventual settlement. Allies said they were prepared to provide Ukraine's front-line forces with equipment and training, backed by air, land and sea support to deter any future Russian attack.

U.K. Prime Minister Keir Starmer said the meeting made "excellent progress" but cautioned that "the hardest yards are still ahead," noting that Russian attacks on Ukraine continue. He added that there can only be peace if Russia compromises: "Putin is not showing that he is ready for peace," Starmer said.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed that progress was made in the talks. "We determined what countries are ready to take leadership in the elements of security guarantees on the ground, in the air, and at sea, and in restoration," Zelenskyy told a news conference in Paris. "We determined what forces are needed. We determined how these forces will be operated and at what levels of command."

Participating nations still must ratify their individual commitments before any guarantees can take effect after a settlement.

## U.S. envoys endorse the framework

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff said the U.S. "strongly stands behind" security guarantees. Jared Kushner, President Trump's son-in-law, who also participated in the meeting, called it "a very, very, big milestone."

"This does not mean we will make peace, but peace would not be possible without the progress that we have made today," Kushner said.

French President Emmanuel Macron said the security statement endorsed by Ukraine's allies is a "significant step" toward ending Russia's invasion. The coalition's plans envision a Ukrainian army of 800,000 troops, Macron said.

In the event of a ceasefire, Starmer said the U.K. and France "will establish military hubs across Ukraine and build protected facilities for weapons and military equipment to support Ukraine's defensive needs."

A joint statement issued after the meeting said allies agreed to continue long-term military assistance and weapons supply to Ukraine's armed forces, which "will remain the first line of defense and deterrence" after any peace deal is signed.

## Binding commitments still unfinished

The joint statement said allies still must finalize "binding commitments" setting out what each country will do to support Ukraine. The post-ceasefire architecture also calls for rebuilding Ukraine's war-battered army — including replenishing its weapons stocks — to serve as the country's front-line deterrence against any resumption of fighting. Details of the plan, including the size of any supporting forces, were not made public.

Russia gave no immediate comment on Tuesday, which was the eve of Orthodox Christmas. Moscow has previously insisted there can be no ceasefire until a comprehensive settlement is agreed, and Russian President Vladimir Putin has ruled out any deployment of troops from NATO countries on Ukrainian soil.

## Diplomatic backdrop

The outcome of the meeting had been uncertain. Secretary of State Marco Rubio had initially been set to lead the U.S. delegation but changed plans following the U.S. military intervention in Venezuela, according to Macron's office.

European cohesion was also tested by President Trump's renewed call for the United States to take control of Greenland, a self-governing territory of Denmark. Leaders of France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain and the United Kingdom joined Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen on Tuesday in defending Greenland's sovereignty.

## Battlefield action continues

While diplomats convened in Paris, Ukraine's Security Service carried out drone strikes on a military arsenal in Russia's Kostroma region, triggering explosions that lasted for hours and forced the evacuation of nearby settlements, according to a security official who was not authorized to comment publicly and spoke on condition of anonymity. The site was described as a key logistics hub supplying ammunition in western and central Russia.

In a separate strike, Ukraine Security Service drones hit an oil depot in Russia's Lipetsk region, causing a large fire, the same official said.
