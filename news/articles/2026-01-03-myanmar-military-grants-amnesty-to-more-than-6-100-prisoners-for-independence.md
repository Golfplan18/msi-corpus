Myanmar’s military government granted amnesty to more than 6,100 prisoners Sunday and reduced sentences for other inmates to mark the 78th anniversary of the country’s independence from Britain, state-run media reported.

State-run MRTV television said Senior Gen. Min Aung Hlaing pardoned 6,134 prisoners, a move announced as the anniversary was marked in Naypyitaw with a flag-raising ceremony at City Hall. The AP reported it was not immediately clear whether the released prisoners included political detainees held for opposing military rule.

Officials also said 52 foreigners will be released and deported from Myanmar, according to the AP. No comprehensive list of who was freed was available, and the release process, which began Sunday, was expected to take several days to complete.

The AP reported that other prisoners received reduced sentences, with exceptions for inmates convicted of serious charges such as murder and rape and for those jailed on charges under various other security acts. The release terms also warned that if freed detainees violate the law again, they will have to serve the remainder of their original sentences in addition to any new sentence.

Buses took prisoners out of Yangon’s Insein Prison after 11 a.m., after friends and families had waited since morning for the announced releases. Among the first group freed from Insein, according to the pro-army outlet Popular News Journal, was Ye Htut, a former high-profile army officer who had served as information minister and presidential spokesperson in a previous military-backed government.

The AP reported that Ye Htut was arrested in October 2023 and sentenced to 10 years in prison the following month after being convicted of sedition and incitement for writing Facebook posts that allegedly spread false or inflammatory news. The AP said there was no sign that the prisoner release would include former leader Aung San Suu Kyi.

The AP reported that Suu Kyi was ousted in the 2021 military takeover and has been held virtually incommunicado since then. It said Suu Kyi is serving a 27-year sentence after being convicted in prosecutions supporters have called politically tinged, and that many political detainees have been held on a charge of incitement—a catch-all offense widely used to arrest critics of the government and punishable by up to three years in prison.

The AP cited the Assistance Association for Political Prisoners, an independent organization that tracks detentions and other effects of Myanmar’s conflicts, saying more than 22,000 political detainees, including Suu Kyi, were in detention as of last Tuesday. The AP also reported that following the 2021 takeover, massive nonviolent resistance has since become a widespread armed struggle.

The amnesty was announced as the military government proceeded with a monthlong, three-stage election process that critics say is intended to give the status quo a facade of legitimacy. In a statement Sunday, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called for the military to cease violence, allow unhindered humanitarian access, release those unjustly detained and engage in dialogue to pursue a peaceful and long-term end to the crisis.

Myanmar became a British colony in the late 19th century and regained its independence on Jan. 4, 1948.
