## Lawmakers override Lula vetoes, sending disputes to top court

Brazil's Supreme Court is set to weigh a series of cases in the coming days that could reshape protections for the Amazon rainforest and the Indigenous communities that depend on it. The cases span Indigenous land rights, recent rollbacks of environmental safeguards, and infrastructure projects. Despite their differences, the disputes share a common path to the court.

In recent years, Congress approved measures that environmentalists say violate constitutional rights. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, whose administration has pursued a more environmentally focused agenda, vetoed some measures outright and parts of others. Lawmakers overrode those objections, and the disputes ultimately reached the Supreme Court, which environmental advocates increasingly view as the last line of defense against the weakening of environmental protections in Brazil.

The cases illustrate the constraints facing the Lula administration. Despite achievements in reducing deforestation and directing resources to foster ecological investment, the government has often struggled to counter Congress, where agribusiness interests wield significant influence, according to the AP report.

Suely Araújo, a policy coordinator at the Climate Observatory, a network of environmental nonprofits, said the government's weakness in the legislature is the core problem. "The government is very weak in the legislative arena," Araújo said. "It often opposes anti-environmental bills, but it lacks the political strength to persuade Congress to vote with it."
