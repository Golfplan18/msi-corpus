The demonstration reflects enduring royalist sentiment in Nepal nearly two decades after the country became a republic, and marks a new mobilization by monarchy supporters as a fragile interim government faces criticism over its handling of corruption cases ahead of the March vote.

KATHMANDU, Nepal — Supporters of Nepal's deposed royal family gathered in the capital on Sunday, demanding restoration of a monarchy abolished in 2008 and calling for the return of ousted King Gyanendra ahead of parliamentary elections scheduled for March.

The rally was the first by royalist supporters since youth-led protests in September toppled the previous government and installed an interim administration, according to the Associated Press. Participants gathered around the statue of King Prithvi Narayan Shah, the 18th-century founder of the Shah dynasty, chanting "We love our king. Bring back the king."

"The last and only alternative for this country is king and monarchy only," said protester Samrat Thapa. "In the present context and the path country has taken after the Gen Z movement, there needs to be monarchy restored to manage the situation."

## Annual gathering turns peaceful after past violence

Sunday marked the birth anniversary of Prithvi Narayan Shah, a date that has in previous years drawn royalist rallies that turned violent. Two people were killed during a pro-king demonstration last March when clashes broke out between demonstrators and police. Sunday's gathering remained peaceful, with riot police maintaining a visible presence throughout, AP reported.

Gyanendra, Nepal's last Shah king, was forced to step down in 2008 when the monarchy was abolished and the country was declared a republic. Nepal's royal family has retained significant public support, according to AP.

## Interim government faces pressure on corruption

The current interim government, led by Sushila Karki — Nepal's first female prime minister and a retired Supreme Court judge — took office following the September protests. Those demonstrations were driven by Gen Z activists who cited corruption, a lack of economic opportunities, and poor governance among their grievances. The protests were initially sparked by the previous government's short-lived ban on social media.

Karki has been criticized for slow progress in filing corruption cases, AP reported — an issue that was central to the youth movement that brought her administration to power.
