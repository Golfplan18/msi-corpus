A Brazilian court sentenced former Catholic priest Bernardino Batista dos Santos to 24 years and nine months in prison for the rape of an underage person, according to a court document obtained by the Associated Press on Thursday. The court in the state of Minas Gerais also ordered the 78-year-old to pay 30,000 Brazilian reais — approximately $5,570 — in damages to the victim. The ruling was issued earlier this week; the case remains sealed.

The conviction marks a legal reckoning for dos Santos after the Archdiocese of Belo Horizonte removed him from his parish in 2021 following dozens of complaints spanning nearly five decades. His conviction, however, rests on a single 2016 incident made prosecutable under Brazil's revised statutes of limitation in similar cases. A lawyer representing more than 60 people who allege dos Santos raped them called the sentence a recognition that no institutional or religious position shields a perpetrator from accountability.

## The sentence and its basis

The 22-page ruling from a Minas Gerais court found dos Santos guilty based on a 2016 incident, according to the document the AP reviewed. A state police investigation reviewed by the AP showed that the family of the underage victim accused dos Santos of rape during a weekend at a farm he owned in the city of Tiros. At the time, dos Santos was serving as director of a kindergarten in Belo Horizonte.

His lawyer, Leonardo Diniz, said in a short statement that he is shocked by the court's decision and will appeal it.

## Decades of complaints, a single conviction

The Archdiocese of Belo Horizonte removed dos Santos from his position in the neighboring city of Contagem in 2021 following dozens of complaints going back to 1975. Brazilian law changes on statutes of limitation in sexual abuse cases ultimately enabled prosecutors to bring the 2016 incident to trial.

Dos Santos was first arrested in October 2024. He was later moved to house arrest with electronic ankle monitoring, according to Minas Gerais authorities.

## Lawyers for alleged victims respond

Ana Carolina Oliveira, a lawyer who represents more than 60 people who allege dos Santos raped them, said the ruling reflected the gravity of the conduct documented in the case.

"The court's decision recognizes the seriousness of the actions committed, the particular vulnerability of the underage victim, and the profound impact of the violations," Oliveira said in a statement.

"No social, institutional, or religious position can serve as a shield for impunity in crimes of this nature," Oliveira said.
