Finnish police opened a criminal investigation Wednesday into the severing of a critical undersea telecommunications cable in the Gulf of Finland, as authorities seized and detained the 14-member crew of a vessel found with its anchor lowered near the damage site. The ship, named the Fitburg and flagged in St. Vincent and the Grenadines, had been traveling from Russia to Israel when Finnish border guards discovered it in Finland's exclusive economic zone.

Helsinki police said the investigation covers aggravated criminal damage, attempted aggravated criminal damage and aggravated interference with telecommunications. The cable at issue belongs to Finnish telecom provider Elisa, which authorities described as critical underwater infrastructure. The damage occurred in Estonia's exclusive economic zone, police said.

The incident adds to a pattern of suspected attacks on undersea infrastructure crisscrossing the Gulf of Finland and Baltic Sea since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, and comes as Finnish prosecutors are already pursuing charges against the crew of a Russia-linked tanker that severed cables on Christmas Day 2024.

## Vessel Seized, Crew Detained

Finnish border guards found the Fitburg after the cable was damaged early Wednesday. The ship's crew of 14 — reported to hail from Russia, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Kazakhstan — was detained by Finnish authorities, according to local media.

Finnish National Police Commissioner Ilkka Koskimäki told local media that the Fitburg had been dragging its anchor for hours before authorities intervened. Koskimäki said investigators are not speculating on whether a state-level actor was behind the damage.

Finnish President Alexander Stubb wrote on social platform X: "Finland is prepared for security challenges of various kinds, and we respond to them as necessary."

## Three Cables Cut in 24 Hours

MSI previously reported that Finnish authorities announced the Fitburg's seizure alongside a broader set of cable damage in the Baltic region during the same period — <a href="/articles/2025-12-31-finland-seizes-vessel-as-multiple-undersea-cables-cut-in-baltic-region">see the full initial report on Finland seizing the vessel and the multiple cable cuts</a>.

A second cable, owned by Swedish telecom provider Arelion, was also damaged in the Gulf of Finland on Wednesday, according to Estonian officials. A third Arelion cable — this one running between Sweden and Estonia in the Baltic Sea — was cut on Tuesday.

Martin Sjögren, an Arelion spokesperson, confirmed the damage to both cables. "We are actively working with authorities in Sweden and other countries to investigate the cause of the cuts," Sjögren said in an email statement. "We cannot disclose any details about exact times or locations at this point with regard to the ongoing investigation."

It was not immediately clear whether the Arelion cable damage was linked to the Fitburg or the Elisa cable incident.

## Service and Repairs

Elisa said its telecom service was not affected by the damage to its cable. Sjögren said the vast majority of Arelion's customers also remained unaffected. Repair work on the Arelion cables is expected to begin once poor weather conditions clear, Sjögren said.

## Regional Context

The Gulf of Finland and Baltic Sea host dense networks of cables and pipelines linking Nordic, Baltic and central European countries — infrastructure that supports trade and energy security and, in some cases, reduces dependence on Russian energy resources.

Wednesday's damage is not the first of its kind in the region. Finnish prosecutors have been pursuing charges against the crew of the Eagle S oil tanker after it damaged cables between Finland and Estonia on Christmas Day 2024. The Finnish deputy prosecutor general said in August that charges of aggravated criminal mischief and aggravated interference with communications were filed against the Eagle S's captain and first and second officers; the officers denied the allegations. The Eagle S was flagged in the Cook Islands and has been described by Finnish customs officials and the European Union's executive commission as part of Russia's shadow fleet — aging vessels with obscure ownership acquired to evade Western sanctions. The Kremlin denied involvement in the Christmas Day incident.

Estonian authorities said they are cooperating with Finnish counterparts to determine whether to pursue a joint prosecution or a separate criminal case in the Elisa cable matter.
