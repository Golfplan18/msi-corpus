The sweep follows neighbors' reports of alleged subhuman conditions in an apartment where a mother and her two children were living at the territory's largest public housing project — a case now under investigation, with authorities noting the family has since fled.

Puerto Rico's Public Housing Administration announced Tuesday it will conduct compulsory inspections of all 328 public housing projects across the U.S. territory, deploying more than a dozen agents to assess living conditions in 56,000 units and determine whether occupants are authorized to reside there.

The sweep, which officials said would be completed by the first week of March, follows neighbors' reports of alleged subhuman conditions in an apartment where a mother and her two children were living at the territory's largest public housing project — a case now under investigation, with authorities noting the family has since fled.

Juan Rosario Hernández, director of the Public Housing Administration, told the local news station Tele11 that agents will check on the welfare of children and elderly residents, as well as verify whether each occupant holds legal authorization to live in the unit.

The inspections also address longstanding concerns about unauthorized occupancy. The agency seized 44 units last August it determined were illegally occupied, noting at the time that thousands of people remained on the waiting list for affordable public housing. Authorities additionally said some units have been used for drug trafficking operations.
