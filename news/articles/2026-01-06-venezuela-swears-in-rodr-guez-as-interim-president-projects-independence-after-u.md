CARACAS, Venezuela — Venezuela's National Assembly convened Monday for a scheduled swearing-in ceremony, installing Vice President Delcy Rodríguez as interim president as the government sought to show the country was operating independently following Saturday's U.S. military seizure of Nicolás Maduro, who had led Venezuela for nearly 13 years.

Rodríguez was sworn in by her brother Jorge Rodríguez, who was reelected as speaker of the chamber. In her remarks to assembled lawmakers, she said she came "with sorrow for the suffering inflicted upon the Venezuelan people following an illegitimate military aggression against our homeland."

The ceremony came as Maduro made his first U.S. court appearance the same day, pleading not guilty to narco-terrorism charges in New York and declaring himself "innocent" and a "decent man."

## Constitutional Succession

Venezuela's Supreme Court, which appointed Rodríguez as interim president on Saturday, declared Maduro's absence "temporary" rather than one of permanent unavailability — a legal distinction with significant consequences.

Venezuela's constitution requires a presidential election within 30 days whenever the president becomes "permanently unavailable" to serve. Under a declared temporary absence, the vice president takes over for up to 90 days, a period that can be extended to six months by vote of the National Assembly. The Supreme Court made no mention of a time limit in its ruling.

Rodríguez is also backed by Venezuela's military, which has historically served as the arbiter of power struggles in the country.

## Son Demands Father's Return

Maduro's son, Nicolás Maduro Guerra — also known as "Nicolasito" — made his first public appearance since Saturday at the legislative palace, demanding that his father and stepmother Cilia Flores be returned to Venezuela and calling for international support.

"If we normalize the kidnapping of a head of state, no country is safe. Today, it's Venezuela. Tomorrow, it could be any nation that refuses to submit," Maduro Guerra said. "This is not a regional problem. It is a direct threat to global political stability."

Maduro Guerra also denounced being named as a co-conspirator in the federal indictment that charges his father and Flores.

## Conciliatory Shift

Rodríguez struck a markedly softer tone toward Washington on Sunday than she had in the immediate hours after Maduro's capture. She said Venezuela was seeking "respectful relations" with the United States and extended an invitation for bilateral cooperation.

"We invite the US government to collaborate with us on an agenda of cooperation oriented towards shared development within the framework of international law to strengthen lasting community coexistence," Rodríguez said.

The conciliatory message came after President Donald Trump had threatened that Rodríguez could "pay a very big price" if she did not fall in line with U.S. demands.

## U.S. Posture

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on CBS' "Face the Nation" on Sunday that the United States would enforce an existing "oil quarantine" against Venezuela but would not govern the country day-to-day.

"We expect to see that there will be changes, not just in the way the oil industry is run for the benefit of the people, but also so that they stop the drug trafficking," Rubio said.

A State Department official said Monday that the Trump administration was making preliminary plans to reopen the U.S. embassy in Caracas in the event Trump decided to return American diplomats to the country. The official spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss internal administration deliberations.
