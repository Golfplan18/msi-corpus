Greece's Education Ministry removed 308,605 long-inactive students from state university rolls on Friday, cutting the country's official student population by nearly half in the most sweeping overhaul of Greek higher education enrollment in decades.

The removal formally ends a practice — abolished in recent legislation passed by the conservative government — that had allowed students to remain enrolled indefinitely, originally designed to facilitate lifelong learning and to accommodate lengthy breaks for work.

The students removed had been admitted to state-run four-year degree programs before 2017. About 35,000 of those affected successfully applied for reenrollment in 2025, the Education Ministry said.

"Student status is not valid for life in any modern European university," Education Minister Sofia Zacharaki said. "We want degrees with value, which reflect effort, skills and passion."

Greece's active student population now stands at just over 350,000, enrolled across 25 public higher education institutions, according to 2024 data from the Hellenic Authority for Higher Education.

Critics of the overhaul, drawn primarily from the academic community, said the government's second-chance reenrollment program fell far short of what was needed. Opponents argue the process failed to address the scale of disruption caused by Greece's severe financial crisis of the previous decade, which forced many students to abandon their studies mid-degree.

Education Ministry officials said dormant students imposed no direct financial burden on universities but created significant administrative difficulties that complicated planning and evaluation.

"With updated student lists, universities gain the ability to plan more precisely," Deputy Education Minister Nikos Papaioannou said. "That is a prerequisite for improving academic quality, daily operations and the criteria used to evaluate Greek universities in international rankings."

Greece has historically operated an exclusively public higher education system, with only state universities offering recognized degrees. Recognized private universities are gradually being introduced under the government's broader reforms to the sector.
