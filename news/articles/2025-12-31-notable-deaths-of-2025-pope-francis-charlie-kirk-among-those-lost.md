Pope Francis, history's first Latin American pontiff, died April 21 at age 88, setting in motion the election of the first American pope to lead the Roman Catholic Church and its 1.4 billion members worldwide. Conservative activist Charlie Kirk, 31, was fatally shot while speaking before a crowd at a Utah college in September. Virginia Giuffre, 41, an advocate for sex trafficking survivors who had accused financier Jeffrey Epstein and others of exploiting her as a teenager, died by suicide in April, according to her publicist.

They were among the deaths that drew wide public attention in a year that also claimed entertainment figures, political leaders, athletes, and activists whose careers shaped the latter decades of the 20th century. MSI previously reported on the institutional consequences of these and other losses in a [year-end compilation](/articles/2025-12-31-pope-francis-dick-cheney-among-notable-figures-lost-in-2025).

## Violent Deaths and Unusual Circumstances

Kirk's shooting, which occurred as he spoke before a crowd at a Utah college campus on September 10, prompted broad public conversation about political violence. He had risen from a teenage conservative campus activist to become a prominent podcaster and ally of President Donald Trump.

Giuffre's death drew renewed scrutiny to ongoing investigations of Epstein. She had been a central figure in Epstein's legal downfall and spent years advocating for sex trafficking survivors.

The deaths of Oscar-winning actor Gene Hackman, 95, and his wife Betsy Arakawa became a source of sadness and mystery after their bodies were found in their home in February. Authorities determined Hackman, who was in the advanced stages of Alzheimer's disease, died of heart disease — likely unaware that Arakawa had died of hantavirus a week earlier.

Director Rob Reiner, 78, was fatally stabbed along with his wife, Michele Singer Reiner, in their home on December 14. Reiner was known for films including "The Princess Bride," "When Harry Met Sally …" and "This Is Spinal Tap."

## Politics and Public Life

Dick Cheney, 84, died November 3. He served as vice president under President George W. Bush and was a leading advocate for the invasion of Iraq, widely regarded as one of the most powerful figures to hold that office. He spent his later years as a critic of President Donald Trump.

Former Supreme Court Justice David H. Souter, 85, died May 8. A New Hampshire Republican appointed by President George H.W. Bush, Souter spent nearly 20 years on the bench and became a consistent vote with the court's liberal bloc.

José Mujica, 89, Uruguay's former president and onetime Marxist guerrilla who led the country from 2010 to 2015, died May 13. His plainspoken philosophy and simple lifestyle as a flower farmer drew widespread attention.

Sam Nujoma, 95, who led Namibia's independence from apartheid South Africa in 1990 and served as the country's first president for 15 years, died February 8.

## Music

Ozzy Osbourne, 76, the lead singer of pioneering heavy metal band Black Sabbath, died July 22 — weeks after his farewell concert. Brian Wilson, 82, the Beach Boys' leader whose work included "Good Vibrations" and "California Girls," died June 11. Roberta Flack, 88, the Grammy-winning singer known for "Killing Me Softly With His Song," died February 24.

Sly Stone, 82, who led Sly and the Family Stone and helped transform popular music in the 1960s and '70s with hits including "Everyday People" and "Family Affair," died June 9. David Lynch, 78, the filmmaker behind "Blue Velvet," "Mulholland Drive," and the television series "Twin Peaks," died January 16.

## Film, Television, and the Arts

Robert Redford, 89, the Hollywood actor who became an Oscar-winning director and a prominent champion of independent cinema, died September 16. Giorgio Armani, 91, the Italian fashion designer, died September 4. Diane Keaton, 79, whose films included "Annie Hall" and "The Godfather," died October 11. Claudia Cardinale, 87, the Italian actor who starred in celebrated European films of the 1960s and 1970s, died September 23.

Mario Vargas Llosa, 89, the Peruvian Nobel literature laureate and a prominent figure in Latin American letters, died April 13.

## Science and Conservation

Jane Goodall, 91, the conservationist known for her groundbreaking chimpanzee field research and decades of environmental advocacy, died October 1 of natural causes while in California on a speaking tour, according to the Jane Goodall Institute.

James D. Watson, 97, who shared the 1962 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for co-discovering the double-helix structure of DNA in 1953, died November 6.

## Sports

George Foreman, 76, the heavyweight boxer who lost to Muhammad Ali in the "Rumble in the Jungle" before becoming world champion again at age 45 and a successful business owner, died March 21. Ryne Sandberg, 65, the Hall of Fame second baseman who starred for the Chicago Cubs, died July 28.

## A Year of Wide-Ranging Loss

The year also claimed Jean-Marie Le Pen, 96, founder of France's far-right National Front, on January 7; Viola Ford Fletcher, 111, one of the last survivors of the 1921 Tulsa Race Massacre in Oklahoma, who spent her later years seeking justice for the attack, on November 24; and Brigitte Bardot, 91, the French screen icon who became a prominent animal welfare activist, on December 28.

Khaleda Zia, 80, the first woman elected prime minister of Bangladesh, whose decades-long rivalry with another former premier defined the country's politics, died December 30.
