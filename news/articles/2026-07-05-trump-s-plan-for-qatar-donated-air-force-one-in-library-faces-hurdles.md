## Trump's plan for Qatar-donated Air Force One in library faces hurdles

**Subtype:** fact

- President Donald Trump wants the Qatari-gifted Boeing 747-8i that now serves as Air Force One to become the centerpiece of his planned presidential library in downtown Miami, according to The Wall Street Journal.
- The plan faces obstacles including possible objections from future presidential administrations, Democratic efforts in Congress to block a transfer, and the logistical challenge of moving a 250-foot aircraft into a midtown skyscraper.
- Rep. Joe Courtney (D., Conn.) filed an amendment to the fiscal 2027 National Defense Authorization Act that would bar the plane from being transferred to any individual or entity; Republican leadership blocked a vote on the House floor.
- The Air Force has not said how long it plans to keep the jet in its fleet, and retired presidential aircraft historically remain available for future presidents' use years after replacement, according to Air Force Gen. Dale White.

## Historical aircraft protocol, future administrations may block library display

President Trump flew on the newly gifted Air Force One for the first time July 1, a Boeing 747-8i the Qatari government transferred to the U.S. Air Force in spring 2025. The aircraft, valued at roughly $400 million, has since received about $400 million in taxpayer-funded communications gear and other military modifications, the Journal reported.

Trump has said he wants the jet to eventually form the centerpiece of his planned presidential library in downtown Miami, a vision similar to the Ronald Reagan Presidential Library and Museum in Simi Valley, Calif., which has housed a former Air Force One since 2005 — 16 years after Reagan left office and a year after his death.

But several factors could block the plan, according to the Journal. The Air Force has not set a retirement date for the aircraft, which it calls a VC-25B "bridge" aircraft intended to serve until new Boeing 747s arrive around mid-2028. Gen. Dale White, who oversees the Air Force One program, told the Journal that "the mission is going to dictate how long the bridge is going to be around."

The National Museum of the U.S. Air Force in Dayton, Ohio, is the primary arbiter of where retired military aircraft end up. It operates a loan program that allows other museums to display retired aircraft, but the Air Force secretary — a political appointee of the sitting president — ultimately decides which museums get historical aircraft. That means a future Democratic administration could block a loan to Trump's library.

"There is standard protocol for retiring military aircraft — first and foremost there are no more military requirements for it," Doug Birkey, executive director of the Mitchell Institute for Aerospace Studies, part of the Air and Space Forces Association, told the Journal.

On June 24, Rep. Joe Courtney (D., Conn.) filed an amendment to the fiscal 2027 National Defense Authorization Act that would block the Qatari plane from being transferred to any individual or entity. Republican leadership blocked the amendment from receiving a vote on the House floor. But Courtney signaled he would keep pushing.

"Rep. Courtney will continue working to ensure that the VC-25B Bridge aircraft, which President Trump described as practically 'brand new,' remains the property of the U.S. government, after substantial taxpayer investment, following the end of President Trump's term in office," a spokesman for Courtney told the Journal.

If Democrats win control of the House or Senate in November's midterm elections, Courtney and other lawmakers may have greater leverage to advance similar legislation.

Physical logistics pose another challenge. Placing a 250-foot-long, double-deck aircraft into the building lobby "is going to be a trick," Trump said in March, according to the Journal. The former Air Force One displayed at the Reagan library had its wings and vertical tail removed, was trucked from San Bernardino, Calif., to Simi Valley, and then had a pavilion built around it. Similarly, the Smithsonian Air and Space Museum required workers to cut a retired Boeing 747 into numerous pieces for reassembly.

The White House did not respond to the Journal's request for comment. Trump's library plan was first detailed in renderings earlier this year, and he has said the library property in downtown Miami would "most likely" include a hotel, with construction not beginning until after he leaves office.
