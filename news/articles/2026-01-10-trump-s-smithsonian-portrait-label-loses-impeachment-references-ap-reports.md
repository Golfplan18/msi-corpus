The Smithsonian’s National Portrait Gallery has removed references to President Donald Trump’s two impeachments from the “portrait label” that previously accompanied his photo in the museum’s “American Presidents” exhibition, the Associated Press reported.

The AP said the wall text that had summarized Trump’s first presidency and referenced his 2024 comeback victory was no longer displayed alongside the portrait. Instead, a different photo appeared without an accompanying text block, although the text remained available online, according to the report.

As photographed in the gallery on Sunday, the AP said Trump’s display was the only presidential portrait without any extended text. The AP reported that the White House did not indicate whether it sought the change, and that the Smithsonian did not respond to questions from the AP.

Trump ordered Smithsonian officials to review exhibits in August ahead of the July 4, 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence, according to the AP. The Republican administration said the effort would “ensure alignment with the president’s directive to celebrate American exceptionalism, remove divisive or partisan narratives, and restore confidence in our shared cultural institutions.”

The AP reported that Trump's original portrait label included biographical information such as his Supreme Court nominations and the administration's development of COVID-19 vaccines. The label text also stated: “Impeached twice, on charges of abuse of power and incitement of insurrection after supporters attacked the U.S. Capitol on January 6, 2021, he was acquitted by the Senate in both trials,” the AP reported.

The AP said the portrait label continued with additional election and historical comparison text, including: “After losing to Joe Biden in 2020, Trump mounted a historic comeback in the 2024 election.” It also reported that the label said Trump is “the only president aside from Grover Cleveland (1837– 1908) to have won a nonconsecutive second term.”

Asked about the display, White House spokesman Davis Ingle celebrated the new photograph, which shows Trump leaning over an Oval Office desk, the AP said. Ingle said it ensures Trump’s “unmatched aura ... will be felt throughout the halls of the National Portrait Gallery.”

The AP reported that the portrait was taken by White House photographer Daniel Torok and that the display credits Torok while including medallions identifying Trump as the 45th and 47th president. Similar numerical medallions appear with other presidents’ painted portraits that include more extended biographical summaries, the AP said.

The gallery said it had previously rotated two photographs of Trump from its collection before putting up Torok’s work, the AP reported. In a statement, the Smithsonian said it is beginning a planned update of the “America’s Presidents” gallery that will undergo a larger refresh this spring, and that for some new displays it has been exploring “quotes or tombstone labels,” providing only general information such as the artist’s name, according to the AP.

The AP reported that the gallery statement said references to impeachments involving Presidents Andrew Johnson and Bill Clinton remain on their portrait labels, along with President Richard Nixon’s 1974 resignation tied to the Watergate scandal. The statement also said the history of presidential impeachments continues to be represented in the National Museum of American History, the AP reported.

The AP said Trump has sought to reshape how federal cultural institutions document U.S. history and culture. In the months before the Smithsonian review order, the AP reported, Trump fired the head archivist of the National Archives and moved to remove the National Portrait Gallery’s director, Kim Sajet, who maintained the backing of the Smithsonian’s governing board before resigning.

At the White House, the AP said Trump also designed a “Presidential Walk of Fame” featuring gilded photographs of himself and predecessors and plaques describing their presidencies, and that the White House said Trump was a primary author of the plaques. The AP reported that Trump’s plaques praise him as a historically successful figure, while it said Biden’s plaque written under an autopen describes Biden as “by far, the worst President in American History” and says he “brought our Nation to the brink of destruction.”
