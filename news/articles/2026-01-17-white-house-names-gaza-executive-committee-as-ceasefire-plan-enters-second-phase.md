TEL AVIV — The White House this week named an international executive committee to supervise Gaza's postwar governance, as the Trump administration declared the U.S. ceasefire plan had entered its second phase. The committee is a broad collection of Trump confidants, veteran diplomats, financial leaders and regional officials whose appointments represent the clearest organizational step yet toward implementing the agreement that took effect October 10.

The committee will operate under the "Board of Peace," an overarching group of world leaders chaired by President Donald Trump. As of January 17, the White House had not announced who else would sit on the Board of Peace beyond Trump himself.

The second phase of the plan, as described by the White House, calls for deploying a multinational International Stabilization Force to Gaza, disarming Hamas and compelling it to yield governance authority, establishing a Palestinian technocratic committee to manage Gaza's day-to-day affairs, and rebuilding the war-battered territory.

## Trump's inner circle

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who also holds the title of national security adviser, sits at the center of American foreign policy on the committee.

Steve Witkoff, a real estate developer and close friend of Trump who served as the White House's Mideast envoy during Trump's second term, was described by the Associated Press as a key architect of the ceasefire agreement itself.

Jared Kushner, Trump's son-in-law and long-standing foreign policy adviser, also joins the committee. Kushner was a driving force behind the Abraham Accords — the diplomatic normalization agreements between Israel and several Arab nations concluded during Trump's first term — and has returned to a White House role to help advance the ceasefire's implementation.

Robert Gabriel, a policy adviser on Trump's first presidential campaign, currently serves as deputy national security adviser and rounds out the American representation.

## Regional powers

Turkey's Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, who has held the role since 2023 and is close to President Recep Tayyip Erdogan, represents a country that has strained ties with Israel but strong relationships with Hamas, positioning Ankara as a potential interlocutor in the disarmament process.

Ali Al-Thawadi, a Qatari diplomat whom Qatar's government described as playing a "central role" in the country's contribution to the U.S. ceasefire effort, represents Qatar's continuing mediation role. Qatar has served as a primary channel between Israel and Hamas throughout the war.

Gen. Hassan Rashad, director of Egypt's intelligence agency, brings Egypt's long-standing mediation role to the committee. Egypt shares borders with both Israel and Gaza and has worked alongside Qatar as a principal intermediary.

Reem Al-Hashimy, a Cabinet minister for the United Arab Emirates, represents a Gulf state with strong ties to Israel that is expected to play a significant role in financing Gaza's reconstruction.

## International and financial figures

Tony Blair, who served as British prime minister from 1997 to 2007, also sits on the committee. Blair later served as Middle East envoy for the Quartet of mediators — the United States, the European Union, Russia and the United Nations — a post that, according to the Associated Press, began with great promise but struggled to deliver results in its quest to advance Israeli-Palestinian peace.

Maj. Gen. Jasper Jeffers was appointed commander of the International Stabilization Force, the on-the-ground multinational security body tasked with enforcing the ceasefire. Jeffers previously headed the U.S. military's special operations command and oversaw implementation of the 2024 ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah.

Ajay Banga, president of the World Bank, and Marc Rowan, co-founder, chief executive and chairman of Apollo Global Management, the U.S. asset-management firm, join the committee alongside Sigrid Kaag, the Netherlands' former deputy prime minister who has served as the United Nations coordinator for humanitarian aid to Gaza since the war began in late 2023.

Yakir Gabay, an Israeli billionaire and major shareholder in Aroundtown SA, a global real estate group, gives Israel an unofficial presence on the executive committee. Gabay previously served as chief executive of the investment arm of Leumi, Israel's largest bank.

## On-the-ground leadership

Nickolay Mladenov, a Bulgarian politician and former U.N. envoy to the Middle East, was designated "high representative" for Gaza. He will serve as the on-the-ground link between the Board of Peace and the new Palestinian administrative committee.

Ali Shaath, a Gaza native, engineer and former deputy transportation minister for the internationally recognized Palestinian Authority in the West Bank, was named to lead that Palestinian committee, which will be responsible for administering Gaza's day-to-day affairs.
