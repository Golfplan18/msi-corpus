The price hike is part of a national differentiated-pricing policy now taking effect across major French cultural sites, but unions say it commercializes access to national heritage, and workers walked off the job again Monday over pay and working conditions — the latest in a series of strikes that have repeatedly closed the museum's doors.

The Louvre raised admission prices Wednesday for most non-European visitors by 45 percent, lifting the cost of entry to 32 euros ($37) from 22 euros, as the world's most visited museum moves to fund a modernization program while contending with repeated worker strikes and heightened scrutiny following a daylight theft of French Crown Jewels.

The change affects visitors from most non-EU countries — a group that includes Americans, who typically account for the majority of the Louvre's foreign tourists. Visitors who are citizens or residents of the European Union, Iceland, Liechtenstein, or Norway are not subject to the new rate.

## New pricing structure

Under the revised system, individual non-European visitors pay the full 32-euro rate. Guided groups from outside those countries pay 28 euros, with tours capped at 20 people "to maintain the quality of the visit," the museum said. Visitors under 18 remain eligible for free admission.

The Louvre said the new tariff will help finance investment under its "Louvre — New Renaissance" modernization project and could bring in as much as 20 million euros more per year.

The last price increase was in January 2024, when the standard entry fee rose from 17 euros to 22 euros.

## Part of a wider French policy

The museum said the hike is part of a national "differentiated pricing" policy announced early last year that is now taking effect across major French cultural sites, including the Versailles Palace, the Paris Opera, and the Sainte-Chapelle.

At Versailles, the "Passport" ticket will cost 35 euros in high season for visitors from outside the European Union, Iceland, Liechtenstein, and Norway, compared to 32 euros for those who are citizens or residents of those countries. At Sainte-Chapelle, the ticket rises to 22 euros for outside-EU visitors versus 16 euros for those within, according to heritage officials.

## Unions and visitors push back

The CGT Culture union denounced the policy, arguing it turns access to culture into a "commercial product" and creates unequal access to national heritage. French worker unions more broadly said the change undermines the universal mission of the museum — home to the "Venus de Milo" and the "Winged Victory of Samothrace."

Some visitors expressed similar reservations outside the Louvre on Wednesday. "Culture should be open to everyone — yes — at the same price," said Laurent Vallet, visiting Paris from Burgundy.

Others questioned the logic of charging visitors from abroad more than local residents. "In general, for tourists things should be a little bit cheaper than local people because we have to travel to come all the way here," said Darla Daniela Quiroz, visiting from Vancouver.

Some said they would pay regardless. "It's one of the main attractions here in Paris … We're still going to go," said Allison Moore, a Canadian tourist from Newfoundland visiting with her mother.

## Workers strike again

Despite the price increase, workers walked out again Monday in the latest strike over pay and working conditions, returning the museum's internal tensions to public view.

In June, a wildcat strike by gallery attendants, ticket agents, and security staff delayed the museum's daily opening, leaving thousands of visitors stranded beneath the glass pyramid. Workers cited unmanageable crowds, chronic understaffing, and deteriorating working conditions.

By December, unions said the heist and the state of the building had turned their long-running grievances into a broader national question. Louvre workers voted to continue striking until what they consider real change comes to the aging former royal palace.

## Context: the October heist

French museums had already been weighing higher fees for non-European visitors before the Oct. 19 theft of French Crown Jewels from the Louvre, which investigators valued at about 88 million euros. The robbery, carried out in daylight within minutes, intensified scrutiny of how France protects its most prized cultural treasures and fueled debate over how major landmarks should fund security upgrades and who should bear the cost.

## European comparisons

The standard entry to Rome's Colosseum, along with the Forum and Palatine Hill, is 18 euros ($20), and an adult ticket for Athens' Acropolis is 30 euros ($33), according to the Associated Press.
