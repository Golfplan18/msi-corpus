## State Department proposes $41M to reopen U.S. Embassy in Libya

**Subtype:** fact

- The State Department has informed Congress that it plans to move ahead with the potential reopening of the U.S. Embassy in Libya, proposing nearly $41 million to fund security-related activities for a phased resumption of operations.
- The department told lawmakers the reopening is "essential to advancing U.S. national security interests," citing Libya's strategic location on the Mediterranean and the growing involvement of Russia in the country.
- Libyan officials representing both the western and eastern regions of the country have met with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Massad Boulos, the senior adviser for Africa.
- The proposal would restore America's diplomatic presence in Libya after more than a decade.

## Libyan officials from east and west meet with Rubio and Trump Africa adviser

The department told lawmakers on Monday that the money would cover "a range of security-related activities associated with plans for the potential phased resumption of embassy operations in Libya," according to a copy of the notification obtained by the Associated Press.

The notification cited Libya's role in regional security, energy markets, and migration flows as making the country vital to U.S. interests. It singled out Russia's expanding influence as a particular concern.

"The country's instability has drawn increased involvement from near-peer competitors, notably Russia, which threatens U.S. and allied objectives in North Africa," the department said in the notification.

The proposed Libya reopening follows other Trump administration efforts to restore U.S. diplomatic presence at posts that had been shuttered. The administration has moved to reopen embassies in Syria and Venezuela, as [MSI reported in May](/articles/2026-05-18-trump-administration-moves-to-reopen-syria-embassy-after-14-years) and [March](/articles/2026-03-31-us-formally-reopens-embassy-in-caracas-after-relations-restored) respectively.

In Libya specifically, officials representing both sides of the country's division — the western government based in Tripoli and the eastern administration — have met with Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Massad Boulos, the administration's senior adviser for Africa, in recent diplomatic talks.

The proposal now moves to Congress, which would need to approve the allocation before the department could proceed with the phased reopening plan.
