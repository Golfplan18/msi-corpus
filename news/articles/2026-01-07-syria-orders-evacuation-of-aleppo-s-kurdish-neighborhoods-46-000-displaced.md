ALEPPO, Syria — Syria's military ordered residents of two predominantly Kurdish neighborhoods to evacuate by Wednesday afternoon, triggering the displacement of more than 46,000 people across Aleppo province as fighting between government forces and Kurdish-led fighters reached its deadliest level in recent months, the Associated Press reported.

Clashes on Wednesday killed seven people in areas controlled by the Kurdish-led, U.S.-backed Syrian Democratic Forces and five others in government-held areas, with dozens more wounded. Sounds of explosions were heard in the Sheikh Maqsoud and Achrafieh neighborhoods after Syria's military declared them a "closed military area" at the 3 p.m. (1200 GMT) deadline.

The violence comes as talks over a March agreement to merge the SDF with Syria's national army have made little progress, with government officials saying Sunday's Damascus meeting produced no tangible results.

## Government announces 'limited military operation'

Syria's Information Ministry said the army would launch "a limited military operation" in Aleppo, stating it came in response to SDF attacks on government-held parts of the city. The ministry said those attacks over recent months had killed more than 20 civilians, wounded more than 150 others, and resulted in the deaths of more than 25 soldiers.

The government said the Aleppo operations "are solely aimed at preserving security, preventing any armed activity within residential areas, and stopping their use as pressure card against Aleppo city" and "are carried out with full commitment to protecting civilians, ensuring their safety and safeguarding their property," according to state news agency SANA.

The SDF — which accused government forces of attacking civilian neighborhoods and infrastructure and of imposing a "suffocating and complete siege" on the Kurdish areas — said in a statement that government forces were using heavy weapons to attack Sheikh Maqsoud and Achrafieh, and that Kurdish fighters were engaged in "fierce resistance to repel" the assault.

## Civilians in flight

More than 46,000 people have been displaced across the province, according to Aleppo's Directorate of Social Affairs and Labor.

A Kurdish man who left with his wife and four children told the Associated Press that conditions inside the two neighborhoods had become harsh, including shortages of food and drinking water.

"I am ready to stay in a garden in order to protect my children," said the man, who did not give his name for fear of repercussions. He said he was originally from Afrin, the northern region captured by Turkish troops and Turkey-backed fighters in 2018.

Adiba Allo said a shell hit her home on Tuesday, forcing her to flee with her children. She added that they had received text messages with a telephone number to call if they had no place to stay.

## Stalled merger, renewed violence

The current clashes are the deadliest between the two sides to date and come as efforts to merge the SDF — which has tens of thousands of fighters — into Syria's national army have shown little progress.

Interim President Ahmad al-Sharaa signed a deal in March with the SDF, which controls much of Syria's northeast, for it to merge with the Syrian army by the end of 2025. Disagreements over how the merger would proceed have persisted. In April, scores of SDF fighters left Sheikh Maqsoud and Achrafieh as part of the agreement.

Officials from the central government and the SDF met again on Sunday in Damascus, but government officials said that no tangible progress had been made. The SDF and Syria's government have each accused the other of seeking to derail the March agreement.

The new Syrian army — formed after the fall of former President Bashar Assad in a rebel offensive in December 2024 — includes factions that were previously Turkey-backed insurgent groups with a history of clashing with Kurdish forces.

## Turkey's position

The SDF has for years been the main U.S. partner in Syria in fighting against the Islamic State group. Turkey considers the SDF a terrorist organization because of its association with the Kurdistan Workers' Party, or PKK, which has waged a long-running insurgency against the Turkish state. A peace process between Turkey and the PKK is now underway.

There was no immediate comment from the Turkish government on the Aleppo clashes. Turkey's pro-Kurdish Peoples' Equality and Democracy Party, known as DEM, called for an end to assaults on Kurdish neighborhoods in Aleppo, saying the attacks violated the March agreement, and urged Turkey and other states to support democratization in Syria by halting military operations.
