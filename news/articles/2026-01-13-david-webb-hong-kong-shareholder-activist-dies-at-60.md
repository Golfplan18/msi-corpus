David Webb, Hong Kong's most prominent shareholder activist and founder of the nonprofit investor-rights database Webb-site.com, died Tuesday in Hong Kong at age 60 from metastatic prostate cancer, according to a statement posted to his social media accounts.

"It is with great sadness that we share that David M. Webb MBE passed away peacefully in Hong Kong on Tuesday January 13th, 2026 from metastatic prostate cancer," the statement said. "David will be missed by his family, his many friends, and his supporters."

Webb spent nearly three decades using his platform to expose corporate misconduct and advocate for minority shareholders in one of Asia's most consequential financial markets, building a record that included uncovering a sprawling network of undisclosed corporate ties that triggered a criminal investigation.

## A platform for investor rights

Webb founded Webb-site.com in 1998 as a free-to-access database of company statistics and data. The nonprofit platform served journalists, shareholders, financial analysts, and lawyers, and became a widely used resource for tracking the corporate governance of listed Hong Kong companies, according to the Associated Press.

One of his most notable actions came in 2017, when he exposed dozens of companies in Hong Kong under what he called the "Enigma Network," advising investors not to own those stocks. Webb uncovered previously undisclosed ties and cross-shareholdings across the companies, which subsequently led to a criminal investigation.

## Background and recognition

Born in London in 1965, Webb was working for Barclays when he was sent to Hong Kong in the 1990s and remained in the city thereafter. He served as an independent director of Hong Kong's stock exchange from 2003 to 2008, when he resigned and accused the bourse of poor management.

In 2025, Webb was appointed a Member of the Order of the British Empire for his work raising corporate governance standards in Hong Kong.

## Diagnosis and final years

Webb first publicly disclosed that he had been diagnosed with metastatic prostate cancer in 2020 and said he hoped to live beyond the age of 60. He turned 60 in August.

At a farewell event held in his honor at Hong Kong's Foreign Correspondents' Club in May 2025, Webb reflected on his life and work.

"However long or short my life, unfortunately, it's shorter than I expected," he said. "I will die confident that I did my best and Hong Kong is my home."

Webb also noted in 2025 what he described as excess self-censorship among opinion writers following Beijing's imposition of a sweeping national security law on the territory in 2020, in the wake of citywide pro-democracy protests.

Kenneth Leung, a former lawmaker who had debated policy with Webb on radio programs, described the activist as sharp-minded and knowledgeable, telling the Associated Press that while Webb's scrutiny had been unwelcome in some corporate boardrooms, he was a person with a great sense of social responsibility.

"He did a lot for Hong Kong's small investors," Leung said.
