PRAGUE — Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš secured a mandatory parliamentary confidence vote Thursday, clearing the way for his coalition government to implement a sharp reorientation of Czech foreign policy away from supporting Ukraine and toward skepticism of key European Union policies. Lawmakers in the lower house of Parliament voted 108–91 in favor of the coalition.

The vote confirms a rightward populist turn in Prague that mirrors shifts in neighboring Hungary and Slovakia, aligning Babiš with Prime Ministers Viktor Orbán and Robert Fico in their resistance to EU-backed Ukraine support — and marking a break from the pro-Western government Babiš replaced.

## Coalition composition

Babiš, who served as prime minister from 2017 to 2021 before losing power, won decisively in October elections and formed a majority coalition with two smaller parties: the Freedom and Direct Democracy movement, which opposes immigration and rejects EU membership, and Motorists for Themselves. The three parties formed a 16-member Cabinet and, according to the Associated Press, share admiration for U.S. President Donald Trump.

## Ukraine policy

Unlike the previous government, Babiš has rejected financial aid for Ukraine and guarantees for EU loans to the country fighting Russia's invasion. His position aligns him with Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán and Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico, both of whom have resisted Western pressure to deepen financial commitments to Kyiv.

The new government will, however, continue a Czech-administered initiative that procured approximately 1.8 million artillery shells for Ukraine on non-EU markets last year. Under that arrangement, Czech officials administer the procurement but contribute no funds.

## Coalition partners' positions

The Freedom and Direct Democracy party holds more sweeping positions than ANO on both EU membership and the refugee question. The party sees no future for the Czech Republic in the EU or NATO and wants to expel most of the approximately 380,000 Ukrainian refugees currently residing in the country, according to the Associated Press.

Motorists for Themselves, which holds the environment and foreign ministry portfolios, has rejected the EU Green Deal and proposed reviving the country's coal industry.
