The trip marks a significant reorientation of Canadian trade strategy: Carney has pledged to double the country's non-American exports within a decade, and Beijing — Canada's second-largest trading partner — represents the most immediate avenue for that pivot.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney is in Beijing this week for the first visit by a Canadian prime minister to China in nearly a decade, seeking to rebuild fractured economic ties as U.S. President Donald Trump's tariffs and statements that Canada could become the "51st state" push Ottawa to diversify its trading relationships.

Carney is scheduled to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping on Friday. He will depart China on Saturday before visiting Qatar and attending the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, the following week.

"At a time of global trade disruption, Canada is focused on building a more competitive, sustainable and independent economy," Carney said in a statement. "We're forging new partnerships around the world to transform our economy from one that has been reliant on a single trade partner."

Canadian officials described the visit as an attempt to re-energize a dormant strategic partnership with China, which is Canada's second-largest trading partner after the United States. Officials said there would be progress on trade irritants with Beijing but not a definitive elimination of tariffs.

## Taiwan trip cut short

The visit generated a diplomatic ripple when two Liberal lawmakers, Helena Jaczek and Marie-France Lalonde, cut short a sponsored trip to Taiwan to "avoid confusion" about Canada's China policy. In a joint statement, they said their early return was based on "advice from the government" and did not alter Canada's stance on Taiwan, which China claims as its sovereign territory and has vowed to take by force if necessary.

Michael Chong, the opposition Conservative foreign affairs critic, said the decision was "nothing short of kowtowing to Beijing's authoritarianism."

## Frayed history

Canada-China ties deteriorated sharply beginning in late 2018, when Canada detained Huawei Technologies executive Meng Wanzhou at the request of the United States, which sought her extradition on fraud charges. China retaliated by arresting two Canadians — former diplomat Michael Kovrig and businessman Michael Spavor — on espionage charges. All three were released under a deal reached in 2021.

More recently, Canada imposed a 100% tariff on Chinese electric vehicles and a 25% tariff on Chinese steel and aluminum, following Washington's lead. China responded with tariffs on Canadian canola, seafood and pork and has indicated it would remove some of those measures if Canada lifts the EV tariff.

## Beijing's welcome, with caveats

An editorial in China's state-run Global Times newspaper welcomed Carney's arrival as "a new starting point" and called on Canada to lift "unreasonable tariff restrictions." Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said Monday that Beijing looks forward to the visit as an opportunity to "consolidate the momentum of improvement in China-Canada relations."

Zhu Feng, dean of the School of International Studies at Nanjing University, said Trump's trade protectionism had created new space for developing China-Canada ties, but cautioned against overestimating the shift. "Carney's visit does reflect the new space for further development in China-Canadian relations under the current U.S. trade protectionism," Zhu said, adding that Canada remains a U.S. ally bound by deep cultural ties and shared geography.

## A wider pattern

Carney is not the first Western leader to seek warmer ties with Beijing as relations with Washington have grown complicated. Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese reset China relations after his Labor Party took power in 2022, helping lift Chinese trade restrictions on Australian wine, beef and coal. British Prime Minister Keir Starmer has sought to repair ties since 2024 and is reportedly planning his own visit to Beijing.

Carney had already met with Xi on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in South Korea in late October. He has separately launched an effort to mend ties with India, where relations collapsed in 2024 after the Trudeau government accused New Delhi of involvement in the 2023 killing of a Sikh activist in Canada. Canadian Foreign Minister Anita Anand said in November that the two countries would move quickly to advance a trade deal.
