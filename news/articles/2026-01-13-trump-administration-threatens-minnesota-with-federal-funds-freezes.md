President Donald Trump’s administration is threatening to withhold billions in federal money flowing to Minnesota, targeting programs that support low-income families while also taking legal action against the state, according to Associated Press reporting published Wednesday.

The Trump administration has struck at Democratic-run states broadly, the report said, but Minnesota has been singled out amid criticism of Gov. Tim Walz, including a reference made last month to Minnesota’s Somali population. The AP story also said the administration has not laid out its exact plans in most cases, and it is not clear if the money will ultimately stop flowing or if delays are expected to be temporary.

Among the most immediate steps described by AP are Agriculture Department grant freezes affecting the state and Minneapolis; a planned Medicaid withholding affecting multiple programs deemed high risk; and a court dispute over federal child-care and other social-services money.

## Agriculture funding freeze linked to a fraud case
The U.S. Department of Agriculture said it is freezing funding in Minnesota and its largest city, Minneapolis, without providing extensive program-by-program detail. Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins announced the action in a Jan. 9 social media post and wrote: “No more handouts to thieves!”

In a letter to Walz and Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey, Rollins cited a major fraud case involving a USDA-funded pandemic relief program that broke in 2022, AP reported. Rollins said 78 people have been charged and 57 convicted in that case, and she told the state and city they must suspend current and future awards while providing payment justifications for transactions dating back to Jan. 20, 2025. Rollins also said those justifications would be required to receive future money.

Rollins said the awards at risk total more than $129 million, though her department did not specify which programs are included, AP said. The report added that it was not clear whether the amount includes payments to individuals or only to government entities.

## Medicaid payments: $515 million every three months
The federal government’s approach also includes a Medicaid reduction, AP reported. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services told Minnesota last week that it intends to withhold $515 million every three months from 14 Medicaid programs it said were high risk after rejecting a corrective action plan demanded by the federal government because of fraud allegations.

AP reported that the $515 million withholding is equivalent to one-fourth of the federal money for those programs. The programs identified as high risk include adult companion services, residential treatment services and nonemergency medical transportation.

Minnesota’s Department of Human Services said it is mounting an administrative appeal, arguing that the federal action is unlike anything it has seen in other states. Temporary Human Services Commissioner Shireen Gandhi said, “Minnesota cannot absorb the loss of more than $2 billion in annual funding for these programs without catastrophic consequences for the people we serve,” adding that, “It’s not corrective action,” and “It’s a punitive action.”

## Court pauses child-care and other social-services freezes
Another prong of the administration’s pressure on Minnesota involves child-care and other social-services funding. AP said the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services told Minnesota in late December that it was freezing funds in the child Care and Development Block Grant, which subsidizes child care for low-income families, and required the state to submit attendance records and other information before funds could resume.

The report said that last week the Trump administration halted payments not only for child care, but also for temporary aid under Temporary Assistance for Needy Families and for the Social Services Block Grant for Minnesota and four other states led by Democrats. The affected states listed by AP were Minnesota, California, Colorado, Illinois and New York.

The five states sued, the report said, with advocates and officials warning that stopping child-care subsidies could put many daycare providers at risk of layoffs or closures, which they said could leave families in difficult situations beyond those using the subsidies. AP reported that last week a federal judge ruled the administration cannot block the money for now.

## DOJ lawsuit challenges Minnesota hiring rules
AP also reported that the Justice Department on Wednesday launched another line of attack on Minnesota by suing the state in federal court over affirmative action hiring requirements. The lawsuit seeks to end programs intended to diversify the government workforce and says, according to the AP account, that Minnesota requires its hiring managers to “jump through additional hoops to hire employees with disfavored skin colors or sex chromosomes.”

The AP report said the lawsuit also seeks money for employees and prospective employees who were impacted, and that the state attorney general’s office said it would respond in court.

Minnesota agriculture officials said they are still sorting out how the grant freezes will play out. Thom Peterson told reporters Wednesday that it is not clear exactly which programs might lose funding, and he said the state has started receiving notices from some pauses, including to a University of Minnesota poultry testing lab.
