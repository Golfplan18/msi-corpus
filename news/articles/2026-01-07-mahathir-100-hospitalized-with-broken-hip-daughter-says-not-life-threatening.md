The hospitalization adds to a series of health scares for one of Southeast Asia's most consequential political figures, who governed Malaysia across two separate terms spanning more than two decades and became the world's oldest serving head of government.

Malaysia's centenarian former Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad was recovering at a Kuala Lumpur hospital Wednesday from a broken right hip he suffered in a fall at his home, according to his family. His daughter dismissed rumors that the 100-year-old statesman was dying, telling the Associated Press the injury was serious but not life-threatening.

"It's a hip fracture and we have to wait for it to heal which, due to his age, will take time," Marina Mahathir told the AP on Wednesday. "Otherwise he's as ok as anyone with a hip fracture can be."

She dismissed circulating rumors directly. "It's serious in anyone, more so a 100-year-old but it's not life-threatening," Marina Mahathir said.

Mahathir was rushed to the National Heart Institute on Tuesday after the fall. His office said he would remain hospitalized for the next few weeks for observation and treatment.

He fell while doing his normal morning exercise of brisk walking, according to his son Mukhriz Mahathir. Doctors ruled out surgery because of his age, Mukhriz said, meaning the recovery may take longer.

## A long record in government, and in hospitals

The hospitalization adds to a series of health scares for the former leader, who has been hospitalized several times in recent years for other conditions that were not critical. Mahathir has a history of heart disease, including a bypass surgery in 1989 following a heart attack and a quadruple bypass in 2007.

Mahathir served as prime minister from 1981 to 2003 and again from 2018 to 2020, becoming the world's oldest serving head of government during his second term. He turned 100 in July. Despite stepping away from frontline politics, he has continued to comment publicly on Malaysian political developments.

At the time of his 100th birthday, Mahathir attributed his longevity to staying mentally and physically active. "So long as I can function, I want to function. I want to behave almost as if I am not getting old. I try to live the same life that I lived when I was younger," he said.
