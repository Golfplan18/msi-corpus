Attorney General Pam Bondi said Tuesday night that Lindsey Halligan, the Trump-appointed interim U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia, is leaving her post as her 120-day tenure ends.

Bondi made the announcement in a social media post on X and said the circumstances leading to Halligan’s exit were “deeply misguided.” Bondi also said the Justice Department “will continue to seek review of decisions like this” that she said hinder its ability to keep the American people safe.

Halligan’s departure came after judges questioned whether she could legally continue in the role following a court ruling about two months earlier that declared her appointment illegal. She had been appointed in September to a 120-day stint that concluded Tuesday, and her time in the job had followed a period in which the Trump administration kept her in place despite the ruling.

The AP said the legal fight began with Halligan’s efforts to secure indictments against longtime Trump adversaries, including former FBI Director James Comey and New York Attorney General Letitia James. A judge later found Halligan’s appointment unlawful and dismissed the two cases, and the Justice Department appealed that dismissal.

In the days leading up to Tuesday’s outcome, the AP reported that two separate court orders signaled that judges believed Halligan’s tenure should end. It was not immediately clear who would next lead the U.S. attorney’s office in the Eastern District of Virginia, which has seen resignations and leadership turnover since last September, when veteran prosecutor Erik Siebert was replaced with Halligan.

One of Tuesday’s orders came from M. Hannah Lauck, chief judge of the Eastern District of Virginia. The AP reported that Lauck directed a clerk to publish a vacancy announcement on the court’s website and said she was “soliciting expressions of interest in serving in that position.”

In a separate order, U.S. District Judge David Novak struck the words “United States Attorney” from the signature block of an indictment in a case before him, the AP reported. Novak also barred Halligan from continuing to present herself with that title, and said he would initiate disciplinary proceedings if she violated his order and persisted in identifying herself as a U.S. attorney, with possible discipline for other signatories as well.

In the same order, Novak wrote that Halligan had “no legal basis to represent to this Court that she holds the position.” He added that any future representation could only be described as a false statement made in “direct defiance of valid court orders,” and he said the “charade” of Halligan masquerading as the United States Attorney for the district in defiance of binding court orders “must come to an end.”

The AP reported that Novak also criticized Justice Department leadership for what he characterized as an improperly antagonistic defense of Halligan in an earlier court filing, saying Halligan’s response—joined by Bondi and Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche—contained a level of “vitriol more appropriate for a cable news talk show.”

Halligan had been selected for the role amid pressure from Trump to charge Comey and Letitia James, the AP reported. The AP said Trump made his desire for indictments clear in a Truth Social post in which he implored Bondi to act swiftly.

After the court orders, Halligan acknowledged in her own statement that her 120-day tenure had come to an end Tuesday. She also lamented what she said was a legal limbo, saying judges “repeatedly treated my appointment as disqualifying” without removing her from the role. Halligan said, “The court’s remedy did not match its rhetoric,” adding that it treated her as if she had been removed by declaring her appointment unlawful and striking her name from filings while never taking what she said was the single step Judge Cameron McGowan Currie identified: appointing a replacement U.S. attorney.
