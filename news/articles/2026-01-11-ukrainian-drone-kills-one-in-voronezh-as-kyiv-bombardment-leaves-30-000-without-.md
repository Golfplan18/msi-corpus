A Ukrainian drone strike killed one person and wounded three others in the Russian city of Voronezh on Saturday, local officials said, as thousands of Kyiv residents remained without electricity following Russia's intense overnight bombardment of the Ukrainian capital.

A young woman died in a hospital intensive care unit after debris from a drone struck a house during the attack, Voronezh regional Governor Alexander Gusev said on Telegram. Russian air defenses shot down 17 of the incoming drones, Gusev said, but more than 10 apartment buildings, private houses and a high school were damaged. Voronezh, home to just over one million people, lies approximately 250 kilometers (155 miles) from the Ukrainian border.

The exchange illustrates the widening geographic scope of the drone war: Ukraine is striking deeper into Russian territory to deprive Moscow of energy export revenue, while Russia continues to batter Ukraine's civilian power grid in the middle of winter, deploying new weapons systems in what Ukrainian officials described as a deliberate warning to Kyiv and NATO.

## Ukraine Strikes Russian Energy Infrastructure

Separately, Ukraine's General Staff said Sunday its forces struck three drilling platforms operated by Russian oil giant Lukoil in the waters of the Caspian Sea. Ukraine's long-range drone campaign against Russian energy sites is aimed at depriving Moscow of the oil export revenue it uses to fund its continuing full-scale invasion, according to Ukraine's stated military rationale.

## Aftermath of Russia's Kyiv Bombardment

The weekend attacks followed a Russian barrage of Ukraine that sent hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles into Ukrainian territory overnight into Friday, killing at least four people in Kyiv, according to Ukrainian officials.

Ukraine's largest private energy supplier, DTEK, said Sunday that 30,000 people in Kyiv were still without power following the attack. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said Friday that approximately half of Kyiv's apartment buildings — nearly 6,000 — had been left without heat in daytime temperatures of about minus 8 degrees Celsius (17.6 degrees Fahrenheit).

## New Weapons Systems Deployed

The intense barrage included, for only the second time in the nearly four-year war, a nuclear-capable Oreshnik hypersonic missile, which struck western Ukraine. Ukraine's General Staff and Ukrainian officials characterized the deployment as a warning aimed at Kyiv and its NATO allies.

The missile strike followed reports of significant progress in talks between Ukraine and its allies on how to defend the country against further Russian aggression should a U.S.-led peace deal be reached.

Ukraine's Defense Ministry intelligence directorate said Sunday that Russia this month deployed the new jet-powered "Geran-5" strike drone against Ukraine for the first time. The Geran-5 is a Russian variant of the Iranian-designed Shahed. According to the directorate, the drone can carry a 90-kilogram (200-pound) warhead and has a range of nearly 1,000 kilometers (620 miles).

## Ongoing Drone Campaign

Ukraine's General Staff said Russia targeted Ukraine with 154 drones overnight into Sunday, and 125 were shot down.

## Peace Talks Continue

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Saturday in his nightly address that Ukrainian negotiators "continue to communicate with the American side." Chief negotiator Rustem Umerov was in contact with U.S. partners on Saturday, Zelenskyy said.
