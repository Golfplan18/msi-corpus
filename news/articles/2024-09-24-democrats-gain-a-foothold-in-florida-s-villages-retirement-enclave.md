THE VILLAGES, Fla. — Democrats in The Villages, one of the world's largest retirement communities and a longtime conservative stronghold in central Florida, are becoming more visible and organized following Vice President Kamala Harris' emergence as the Democratic presidential nominee, the Associated Press reported. The Villages Democratic Club has nearly doubled its membership since the 2020 election cycle, reaching about 1,500 members, according to club president Diane Foley.

The growth reflects how the Harris candidacy has energized previously reticent Democrats in politically unfavorable settings, though most residents in the 151,000-person community still avoid political discussion to preserve neighborly relations.

"They are beginning to realize that they have a voice and they can use it," Foley said.

In prior years, left-leaning residents kept their political views to themselves, wary of being excluded from golf groups, mahjong clubs, or neighborhood gatherings, the AP reported. "Democrats have been inclined to be quiet and to not broadcast their political preferences because we were so overwhelmed by the Republican party," Foley said.

## Signs of a shift

Parades of golf carts decorated in support of Harris have rolled through the streets of the community, officially named Wildwood-The Villages — a census-designated, master-planned metro area of more than 151,000 people. Second gentleman Doug Emhoff recently paid a visit as well. Democrats who wore Harris campaign T-shirts or put up lawn signs said neighbors approached them with relief, saying they believed they were the only ones.

Despite the increased activity, most residents said they still choose not to discuss politics openly. "You want to get along with your neighbors. They're nice people. So we just don't talk about it," Foley said.

## Former Republicans join the effort

Howard Underwood, 71, a retired lawyer, and his wife Janet, 74, were Republicans for four decades until Donald Trump became the party's candidate in 2016. Underwood, now unaffiliated with either major party, volunteered with the Democrats club to support Harris but said he would not put a bumper sticker on his car for fear of blowback.

"We've made common cause with the Democrats, even though I don't share a whole lot of Kamala's ideas on things," Underwood said.

He acknowledged the political atmosphere in The Villages had shifted, however slightly. "Democrats in The Villages has kind of become a thing," he said.
