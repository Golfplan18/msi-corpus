BEIRUT — A Catholic priest from northern Portugal known as Padre Guilherme led Mass at a Lebanese Catholic university Saturday afternoon, then walked onstage at a Beirut nightclub that evening to perform as a DJ for hundreds of cheering fans — capping his first appearance in Lebanon with both a sold-out crowd and a failed legal challenge by critics who called the show an insult to the faith.

Guilherme Peixoto, 52, performed at AHM nightclub after 18 people, including Christian religious officials, had petitioned Lebanon's judiciary to cancel the event. A judge rejected the petition. The club said it would have a security presence and would display no religious symbols inside the venue; Peixoto agreed to perform without his cassock, the traditional clerical coat, as part of the arrangement with organizers.

"For those that are objecting, if I, for them, I'm kind of scandal for them, I (am) sorry of course. And I only can ask (them) to pray for me," Peixoto said.

Peixoto frames his DJing as an extension of ministry, not a departure from it. "The Psalm asks us to praise the lord with all instruments, so now you have this new instrument that came that is electronic music," he said before delivering a sermon alongside a Lebanese priest at Saint Joseph University of Kaslik, where he held Mass in a packed auditorium of young people and older attendees.

What started as a fundraising tool for local churches has grown into a globe-spanning effort that Peixoto describes as a new form of youth evangelization. He has amassed 2.6 million Instagram followers and performs to large audiences around the world. He first gained wide attention at World Youth Day in 2023, performing before Pope Francis' open-air Mass, and appeared again at a 2025 event featuring Pope Leo XIV.

By nighttime Saturday at AHM nightclub, the crowd cheered as he took the stage. As he played and swayed to the music, images of the late Pope Francis, Pope John Paul II, and white doves were projected on large screens behind him. He played a song for Lebanon and waved a Lebanese flag to the audience.

Lebanon was, as Peixoto put it, a natural stop on his tour. Christians make up around a third of Lebanon's roughly 5 million people, giving the small Mediterranean nation the largest percentage of Christians in the Middle East, according to the Associated Press; Maronite Catholics are the largest Christian denomination. Pope Leo XIV visited Lebanon in November as part of his first official trip abroad, calling for peace and dialogue in the country and the broader region.

Peixoto echoed that call Saturday, through the music.

"The message is always: look to the dance floor, you see respect, you see something always beautiful … if this is possible for people with different race (and) clothes dancing together, why we cannot live like that in the world?" he said.

Reactions on social media were divided. "We who were raised to respect the word of God and the sanctity of the message in all its forms cannot accept turning faith into an entertainment show presented on a table of alcohol," one Lebanese user wrote on X. Others welcomed the approach. "The people attacking him just don't understand how powerful and needed his work is," another user wrote.
