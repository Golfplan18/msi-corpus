Myanmar's military-appointed election commission announced early results Saturday showing the military-backed Union Solidarity and Development Party leading in the first phase of a general election held Dec. 28, according to the state-run Myanma Alinn newspaper. The Union Election Commission, or UEC, said the USDP won 38 seats in the 330-seat lower house of parliament, with many constituencies still undeclared. Critics have characterized the vote as designed to provide a facade of legitimacy to military rule, and opposition groups called for voters to boycott the polls.

The election is Myanmar's first attempt to formalize a government since soldiers ousted the elected administration of Aung San Suu Kyi in February 2021, an event that triggered a civil war now in its fifth year. The vote's three-phase structure and the exclusion of 65 townships due to active armed fighting underscore how far the country remains from the stability the military has invoked to justify its rule.

## Early results

The UEC's partial announcement also named USDP leader Khin Yi as the winning representative from his constituency in the capital, Naypyitaw. A former general and police chief widely regarded as a close ally of military ruler Senior General Min Aung Hlaing, Khin Yi received 49,006 of the 68,681 votes cast in his race, according to the commission's statement. The Shan Nationalities Democratic Party and the Mon Unity Party each won one seat, the UEC said.

A senior USDP official told the Associated Press on Saturday that the party had won 88 of the 102 seats contested in the first phase. The official spoke on condition of anonymity, saying he was not authorized to release the information. The official also said the party ran unopposed in 29 constituencies and won 85% of contested seats in regional legislatures, though complete results will only be known after the second and third phases.

The military government said more than 6 million people — about 52% of the more than 11 million eligible voters in the first phase — cast ballots on Dec. 28, calling the turnout a decisive success.

## Structure of the vote

Voting is taking place in three phases because of ongoing armed conflicts across Myanmar. The Dec. 28 first round covered 102 of the country's 330 townships. The remaining phases are scheduled for Jan. 11 and Jan. 25, though 65 townships will not participate in any phase because of active fighting.

More than 4,800 candidates from 57 parties are competing for seats in national and regional legislatures. Only six parties are competing nationwide with the possibility of gaining meaningful representation in parliament, according to the AP. The USDP is by far the strongest contender among them.

Myanmar has a two-house national legislature totaling 664 seats. The party holding a combined parliamentary majority can select the new president, who may then name a cabinet and form a government. Under the military-drafted constitution, the armed forces automatically receive 25% of seats in each legislative house, a provision that ensures the military retains decisive institutional influence regardless of election outcomes.

## Critics and context

Critics of the electoral process charge that the polls are neither free nor fair, citing the exclusion of major parties and the government's repression of political dissenters. Opposition groups organized a voter boycott.

The military took power in February 2021 when soldiers ousted Aung San Suu Kyi's National League for Democracy government, blocking the NLD from serving a second term despite its landslide victory in the 2020 election. The coup triggered widespread popular opposition that has since grown into a civil war. The NLD was dissolved in 2023, along with 39 other parties, after refusing to register under new rules imposed by the military administration.
