China's People's Liberation Army said Wednesday it had "successfully completed" two days of military exercises in the waters around Taiwan, concluding maneuvers Beijing called "Justice Mission 2025" as the year drew to a close.

The PLA said the operation had "fully tested the integrated joint operations capabilities of its troops," according to an announcement released on New Year's Eve. The statement offered no details about what constituted success and did not specify exactly when the exercises ended.

The exercises drew formal objections from Japan and the Philippines and a measured response from U.S. President Donald Trump, who said he was not concerned, as Chinese President Xi Jinping declared in a New Year's Eve address that Taiwan's reunification with the mainland was inevitable.

## PLA announcement

The conclusion was announced in a video presentation accompanied by martial music. Senior Capt. Li Xi, spokesperson for the PLA's Eastern Theater Command, was quoted as saying the forces would remain on guard.

"Always on high alert, the troops of the Theater Command will keep strengthening combat readiness with arduous training, resolutely thwart the attempts of 'Taiwan Independence' separatists and external intervention, and firmly safeguard state sovereignty and territorial integrity," Li Xi said.

Xi, in a brief reference to Taiwan during his national New Year's Eve address, said Chinese people on both sides of the strait share "a bond of blood and kinship."

"The reunification of our motherland, a trend of the times, is unstoppable," Xi said.

## Regional responses

Japan's Foreign Ministry said Wednesday that Beijing's military exercises around Taiwan constitute "an act that escalates tension in the Taiwan Strait" and said Japan had conveyed that concern to Beijing. Japan's government said it expects cross-strait issues to be resolved peacefully through dialogue, a position it described as consistent.

"The peace and stability in the Taiwan Strait are important for the entire international community," the ministry said in a statement.

Philippines Defense Minister Gilberto C. Teodoro Jr. said he was "deeply concerned by China's military and coast guard actions around Taiwan," adding that the exercises undermine stability "in an already fragile geopolitical environment."

"This heightened scale of coercion has implications that extend beyond cross-Strait relations and into the broader Indo-Pacific community," Teodoro said. "Basic principles of self-restraint must be observed."

In November, Japan's prime minister said she would not rule out military intervention if Taiwan faced a direct attack by the PLA.

## U.S. position

Trump, speaking earlier this week, said he was not alarmed. "China has been doing naval exercises for 20 years in that area," Trump said, and noted his relationship with Xi as a stabilizing factor.

In mid-December, the United States announced a package of arms sales to Taiwan that, pending congressional approval, would represent the largest such package ever sent to the island — a move China sharply criticized.

## Background

Taiwan has operated as a self-governing island since 1949, when Chiang Kai-shek's Nationalist forces retreated there after losing a civil war to Mao Zedong's Communist forces. Beijing has since claimed sovereignty over the island and has said it would retake it by force if necessary.

The PLA sends warplanes and naval vessels toward Taiwan on a near-daily basis, according to the Associated Press, and has increased the scope and scale of such exercises in recent years.
