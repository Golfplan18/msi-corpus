Britain's Queen Camilla disclosed Wednesday that she was indecently assaulted on a train in the 1960s when she was a teenager, speaking publicly about the experience for the first time as part of her ongoing campaign against domestic violence.

Camilla, who has made fighting domestic abuse one of her signature royal causes, said she fought back against her attacker and kept the incident quiet for many years.

The disclosure came as Camilla used her royal platform to urge greater public awareness of what she called an epidemic of violence against women, meeting with surviving family members of three women murdered by a domestic abuser in 2024.

## The attack and its aftermath

Camilla described the assault in an interview with the BBC, saying she was reading when the man next to her attacked her.

"I was reading my book, and you know, this boy, man, attacked me, and I did fight back," Camilla told the BBC. "And I remember getting off the train and my mother looking at me and saying, 'Why is your hair standing on end?' and 'Why is a button missing from your coat?'"

The assault had previously been recounted in royal correspondent Valentine Low's book "Power and the Palace," published earlier in 2025, based on what Camilla told Boris Johnson when he served as mayor of London. According to that account, Camilla removed her shoe and struck her attacker before reporting the incident to a uniformed official at Paddington Station, where the man was arrested.

Camilla said the attack made her "furious" but that she kept quiet about it for many years until hearing other women recount their own experiences.

## Speaking from a royal platform

Camilla said she decided to speak out because domestic violence has been a "taboo subject" for so long that most people do not understand the scale of the problem.

"I thought, well, if I've got a tiny soapbox to stand on, I'd like to stand on it," she said. "And there's not a lot I can do except talk to people and get people together."

The BBC interview brought together Camilla and former racing commentator John Hunt and his daughter Amy, who have campaigned against domestic violence following the murders of three members of their family. John's wife Carol, 61, and their daughters Louise, 25, and Hannah, 28, were killed by Louise's ex-partner at their home outside London in July 2024.

Camilla praised both for their advocacy work.

"Wherever your family is now, they'd be so proud of you both," she told John and Amy Hunt. "And they must be, from above, smiling down on you and thinking, 'My goodness me, what a wonderful, wonderful father, husband, sister. They'd just be so proud of you both.'"
