Camilla has made fighting domestic abuse one of her signature causes as queen. Her public disclosure Wednesday marked the first time she had spoken directly about her own experience of sexual assault, adding personal weight to advocacy work she has carried out for years.

Queen Camilla spoke publicly Wednesday for the first time about being the victim of an indecent assault as a teenager in Britain in the 1960s, telling the BBC that a man on a train attacked her and that she fought back. Speaking alongside the surviving family members of three women killed by a domestic abuser, Camilla said violence against women has been treated as a "taboo subject" for so long that most people fail to grasp its scale.

"I was reading my book, and you know, this boy, man, attacked me, and I did fight back," Camilla said. "And I remember getting off the train and my mother looking at me and saying, 'Why is your hair standing on end?' and 'Why is a button missing from your coat?'"

Camilla said she kept the attack quiet for many years until she began hearing other women recount their own experiences. As MSI previously reported, the disclosure is the first time Camilla has spoken publicly about the incident and is part of her sustained [campaign against domestic violence](/articles/2025-12-31-queen-camilla-discloses-1960s-assault-as-she-campaigns-against-domestic-violence).

"I thought, well, if I've got a tiny soapbox to stand on, I'd like to stand on it," she said. "And there's not a lot I can do except talk to people and get people together."

## Details of the attack

The Associated Press reported additional details from the book "Power and the Palace," published earlier this year by Valentine Low, a former royal correspondent for the Times of London. According to Low's account — drawn from what Camilla told former Prime Minister Boris Johnson when he was mayor of London — Camilla was on a train to London's Paddington Station when the man seated next to her reached out and attempted to touch her. She fought him off by removing her shoe and hitting him in the groin. At Paddington, she found a man in uniform, told him what had happened, and the man was arrested.

Wednesday's BBC interview marked the first time Camilla spoke about the attack in public, though the incident had been described in Low's book.

## Interview with murder victims' families

Camilla's comments came in a group interview that also included surviving family members of Louise Hunt, 25, her sister Hannah, 28, and their mother Carol, 61. The three women were murdered by Louise's ex-partner at their home outside London in July 2024, according to the Associated Press.

The queen praised former racing commentator John Hunt and his daughter Amy for their advocacy work following the killings.

"Wherever your family is now, they'd be so proud of you both," Camilla told John and Amy Hunt.
