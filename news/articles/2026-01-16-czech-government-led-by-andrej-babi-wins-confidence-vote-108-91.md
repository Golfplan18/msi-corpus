PRAGUE (AP) — The Czech Republic’s new government led by populist Prime Minister Andrej Babiš won a mandatory confidence vote in the lower house of Parliament on Thursday.

Lawmakers voted 108-91 in favor of the coalition in the ballot that every new administration must win to govern, according to AP.

The coalition’s agenda includes aims to steer the country away from supporting Ukraine and to reject some key European Union policies, AP reported.

Babiš, who previously served as prime minister in two governments from 2017-2021, and his ANO, or YES, movement won big in the country’s October election and formed a majority coalition with two smaller political groups. AP said the partners were Freedom and Direct Democracy, and the right-wing Motorists for Themselves.

AP said the parties, which share admiration for U.S. President Donald Trump, created a 16-member Cabinet. The change is expected to significantly redefine the Czech Republic’s foreign and domestic policies, AP reported.

Unlike the previous pro-Western government, Babiš has rejected any financial aid for Ukraine and guarantees for EU loans to the country fighting the Russian invasion, AP said. The report said Babiš has joined Viktor Orbán of Hungary and Robert Fico of Slovakia in taking that approach.

AP said Babiš’s government would not abandon a Czech initiative that acquired about 1.8 million much-needed artillery shells for Ukraine last year on markets outside the EU. AP said the acquisition was done on condition that the Czechs would administer the shells but would not contribute money.

The Freedom party sees no future for the Czech Republic in the EU and NATO, AP said, and it wants to expel most of 380,000 Ukrainian refugees in the country.

AP reported that the Motorists are in charge of the environment and foreign ministries, and that they rejected the EU Green Deal while proposing a revival of the coal industry.
