Clashes between local residents and a gold mining company killed four people and wounded five others Tuesday in the Chah Ab district of Takhar province in northern Afghanistan, the Interior Ministry said Wednesday.

Three residents and one company employee died in the violence, Interior Ministry spokesman Abdul Mateen Qane said. He did not specify what triggered the clashes or identify the company's owner.

The bloodshed in Takhar highlights growing friction between rural Afghan communities and a Taliban-led mining expansion program that has attracted billions of dollars in investment contracts since the government seized power in 2021.

## Arrests made, operations suspended

Qane said a company security employee and a resident were arrested in connection with the violence. Security forces quickly restored order, and the deputy governor of Takhar visited the district to assess the situation, he said.

The mining company's operations have been suspended, Qane said.

Akbar Haqani, the provincial spokesman, said authorities had visited the area where the clashes erupted. Further details would be released after officials concluded their investigation, he said.

## Mining context

In 2023, Afghanistan's Taliban government said it had signed seven mining contracts totaling $6.5 billion in investment — the largest such round of deals since seizing power in 2021.

Afghanistan holds substantial mineral deposits, including coal, copper, iron ore, zinc, gold, and silver.
