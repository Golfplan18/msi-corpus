DUBAI, United Arab Emirates — U.S. President Donald Trump warned Iran on Friday not to harm protesters, posting on Truth Social that the United States was "locked and loaded and ready to go," as demonstrations against the Islamic Republic's government spread to more than 100 locations in 22 of the country's 31 provinces. Senior Iranian officials swiftly rejected Trump's statements as foreign interference, while Iran's United Nations ambassador asked the Security Council to condemn what he called unlawful U.S. threats.

The exchange marks the sharpest direct confrontation between Washington and Tehran since the United States struck Iranian nuclear sites in June, raising the stakes for a country battered by economic collapse and facing its largest domestic protests since the 2022 demonstrations that followed the death of Mahsa Amini in police custody.

## Trump's warning

Trump wrote on Truth Social that if Iran "violently kills peaceful protesters," the United States "will come to their rescue."

"We are locked and loaded and ready to go," Trump wrote, without elaborating.

As of Friday, no major changes had been made to U.S. troop levels in the Middle East or their preparations following Trump's posts, according to a U.S. official who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military plans.

Trump's direct show of support for the demonstrators departs from the approach taken by recent American presidents, who have generally avoided publicly backing protests in Iran out of concern that activists would be accused of working with the West. President Barack Obama held back from publicly supporting Iran's 2009 Green Movement demonstrations — a decision he called a mistake in 2022.

## Iranian officials respond

Ali Larijani, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council and a former parliament speaker, alleged on X that Israel and the United States were behind the demonstrations. He offered no evidence for the allegation, which Iranian officials have repeated during years of similar unrest.

"Trump should know that intervention by the U.S. in the domestic problem corresponds to chaos in the entire region and the destruction of the U.S. interests," Larijani wrote on X, which the Iranian government blocks. "The people of the U.S. should know that Trump began the adventurism. They should take care of their own soldiers."

Larijani's reference to U.S. soldiers appeared directed at America's wide military presence in the region. Iran attacked Al Udeid Air Base in Qatar after the June U.S. strikes on three nuclear sites; no one was injured, though a missile struck a structure there.

Ali Shamkhani, an adviser to Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei who previously served as the security council's secretary, separately warned that "any interventionist hand that gets too close to the security of Iran will be cut."

In a letter to UN Secretary-General António Guterres and the Security Council late Friday, Iran's UN Ambassador Amir Saeid Iravani asked the world body to condemn the U.S. rhetoric and reaffirm Iran's right to defend its sovereignty.

"The United States of America bears full responsibility for any consequences arising from these unlawful threats and any ensuing escalation," Iravani said.

## Risks of U.S. support

Analysts warned that Trump's public backing carries strategic risks even as it signals solidarity with demonstrators.

"Though the grievances that fuel these and past protests are due to the Iranian government's own policies, they are likely to use President Trump's statement as proof that the unrest is driven by external actors," said Naysan Rafati, an analyst at the International Crisis Group.

"But using that as a justification to crack down more violently risks inviting the very U.S. involvement Trump has hinted at," Rafati added.

## Protests spread

The demonstrations, now in their sixth day, have become the largest in Iran since the 2022 protests, though they have not yet reached the scale or intensity of those sparked by Amini's death. Amini, 22, died in police custody after being detained for not wearing her hijab to the satisfaction of authorities.

The current protests began over the collapse of Iran's rial currency — with $1 now costing approximately 1.4 million rials — but have expanded to include chants against Iran's theocracy. The U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency reported that demonstrations reached over 100 locations across 22 of Iran's 31 provinces.

At least eight people have been killed in violence surrounding the demonstrations. The most recent death reported was a demonstrator in Marvdasht in Iran's Fars province. In the city of Kouhdasht, in Lorestan province, the family of Amirhessam Khodayari — a 21-year-old killed Wednesday — disputed government claims that he had served in the Basij, the all-volunteer paramilitary force of Iran's Revolutionary Guard. The semiofficial Fars news agency later reported that questions had emerged about the government's account.

## Economic and diplomatic backdrop

Iran's reformist President Masoud Pezeshkian has signaled a desire to negotiate with protesters but acknowledged he has limited capacity to reverse the rial's depreciation. Tehran has had little success stabilizing its economy in the months since the June war.

Iran recently announced it had halted uranium enrichment at all sites in the country, a move interpreted as a signal of openness to negotiations over its nuclear program in exchange for sanctions relief. However, those talks have not materialized. Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu have both warned Iran against rebuilding its atomic program.
