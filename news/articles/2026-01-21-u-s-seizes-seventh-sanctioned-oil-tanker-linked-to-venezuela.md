U.S. military forces boarded and took control of a seventh oil tanker connected with Venezuela on Tuesday, the U.S. Southern Command said, as part of the Trump administration’s efforts to take control of the South American country’s oil.

The Southern Command said in a social media post that U.S. forces apprehended the Motor Vessel Sagitta “without incident” and that the tanker was operating in defiance of President Donald Trump’s “established quarantine of sanctioned vessels in the Caribbean.”

The military command did not say whether the U.S. Coast Guard took control of the tanker, as has been the case in prior seizures. The Pentagon and Southern Command said they had nothing to add when asked for more details.

The Sagitta is Liberian-flagged, and its registration says it is owned and managed by a company in Hong Kong. The ship last transmitted its location more than two months ago when exiting the Baltic Sea in northern Europe.

The tanker had been sanctioned by the U.S. Treasury Department under an executive order related to Russia’s invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The Southern Command post indicated the ship had taken oil from Venezuela. It said the capture “demonstrates our resolve to ensure that the only oil leaving Venezuela will be oil that is coordinated properly and lawfully.”

The military command posted what appeared to be aerial footage of the Sagitta sailing on the ocean, but the clip did not show U.S. forces flying toward it in helicopters or landing on the deck of the ship, unlike in prior videos.

The latest seizure follows a broader push by the Trump administration after a Jan. 3 surprise nighttime raid that ousted Venezuela President Nicolás Maduro. Since then, the administration has set out to control the production, refining and global distribution of Venezuela’s oil products.

Officials in Trump’s Republican administration have said seizing the tankers is a way to generate cash as they seek to rebuild Venezuela’s battered oil industry and restore its economy. Trump met with executives from oil companies nearly two weeks before Tuesday to discuss his goal of investing $100 billion in Venezuela to repair and upgrade its oil production and distribution. He said at the time the U.S. expected to sell at least 30 million to 50 million barrels of Venezuelan oil.

On Tuesday, Trump told reporters that the U.S. already has taken 50 million barrels of oil out of Venezuela. “We’ve got millions of barrels of oil left,” he said at the White House. “We’re selling it on the open market.” He added, “We’re bringing down oil prices incredibly.”

AP previously reported that the first tanker was seized off the coast of Venezuela on Dec. 10. Most of the other tankers were captured in waters near Venezuela, with the exception of the Bella 1, which was captured in the North Atlantic. AP said the Bella 1 was nearing the Caribbean when, on Dec. 15, it abruptly turned and headed north toward Europe, and was ultimately captured on Jan. 7.
