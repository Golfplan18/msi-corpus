TAIPEI, Taiwan — Taiwanese President Lai Ching-te vowed on Thursday to defend the self-ruled island's sovereignty against what he called China's "expansionist ambitions," delivering his New Year's address days after Beijing concluded live-fire military drills around Taiwan featuring rocket launches, aircraft, and warships.

"In the face of China's rising expansionist ambitions, the international community is watching to see whether the Taiwanese people have the resolve to defend themselves," Lai said.

Lai's address came as Taiwan advances an eight-year, $40 billion arms procurement program and Washington prepares its largest arms sale to the island to date, while Chinese leader Xi Jinping rang in the new year by calling Taiwan's eventual annexation "unstoppable."

## Beijing's Response

Beijing reacted sharply to Lai's remarks. A spokesperson for the State Council's Taiwan Affairs Office, Chen Binhua, called Lai "a saboteur of peace, a troublemaker and a warmonger," according to the official Xinhua news agency.

"No matter what Lai and the Democratic Progressive Party authorities say or do, they cannot change the fact that Taiwan is part of China," Chen said.

## Drills and the U.S. Arms Sale

China recently concluded live-fire exercises around Taiwan. Beijing had expressed anger at a planned U.S. arms sale to Taiwan valued at more than $11 billion — the largest to date — as well as at comments by Japan's new leader indicating that Tokyo could intervene in the event of a Chinese attack on Taiwan.

The arms package includes missiles, drones, artillery systems, and military software. The United States is obligated by its own laws to provide Taiwan with the means to defend itself.

On Wednesday, Xi said in his own televised New Year's address that Taiwan's eventual annexation was "unstoppable."

## Taiwan's Defense Program

"Facing China's serious military ambitions, Taiwan has no time to wait," Lai said.

Taiwan's $40 billion special defense budget is to be allocated from 2026 to 2033. The program includes construction of an air defense system called the Taiwan Dome, designed to provide high-level detection and interception capabilities. Lai has also pledged to raise overall defense spending to 5% of the island's GDP.

## Background

China claims Taiwan as its own territory and threatens to annex it by force if necessary. Taiwan has been governed independently from mainland China since 1949, when the Nationalist Party lost a civil war against the Chinese Communist Party and retreated to the island.
