The consolidation aims to relieve the country's 43 local police forces from handling complex cases that strain their resources while centralizing expertise in a single national agency.

The British government announced plans Sunday to create a new national police force, dubbed the "British FBI," to consolidate investigations into terrorism, fraud, online child abuse, and organized crime across England and Wales, according to Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood.

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said the new National Police Service will bring under one umbrella the work of existing agencies dealing with terrorism and crime, police helicopters, road policing, and regional organized crime units. The move aims to free the country's 43 local police forces to focus on day-to-day crime within their jurisdictions.

## The Case for Consolidation

"Some local forces lack the skills or resources they need to fight complex modern crime such as fraud, online child abuse or organized criminal gangs," Mahmood said in the announcement.

Mahmood said the current police system was "built for a different century" and that the new body will attract "world-class talent" while helping control costs by consolidating procurement into a single national operation.

## What Comes Next

The government will unveil full proposals to Parliament on Monday, with additional plans to reduce the number of police forces in England and Wales and reform recruitment and management practices.

London's Metropolitan Police, which takes the lead on counterterrorism investigations, welcomed the plan, saying the creation of a single national service aligns with the need for coordinated response to sophisticated security threats that cross regional boundaries.

## The Systemic Problem

The consolidation addresses long-standing fragmentation in British policing, where institutional divisions have hindered investigations into criminal enterprises and terrorism threats that operate across multiple jurisdictions.
