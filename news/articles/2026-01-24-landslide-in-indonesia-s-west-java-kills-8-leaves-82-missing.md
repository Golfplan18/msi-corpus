A landslide triggered by torrential rains killed at least eight people in Indonesia's West Java province early Saturday, with more than 80 residents feared buried under mud and debris. The 3 a.m. collapse in Pasir Langu village in West Bandung district buried about 34 houses, and rescuers worked through deep mud in search of survivors. Twenty-four residents managed to escape, according to Abdul Muhari, a spokesperson for Indonesia's National Disaster Management Agency.

The disaster underscores seasonal dangers Indonesia faces from October to April, when monsoon rains and high tides frequently trigger flooding and landslides across the archipelago of more than 17,000 islands, where millions live in mountainous areas or near fertile flood plains.

The worst-hit hamlet of Pasir Kuning was buried under thick mud, rocks, and uprooted trees. The debris tumbled down the mountainside, transforming the village's roads and green-terraced rice fields into brown sludge.

Rescuers reported the terrain made searching difficult. Television footage showed workers and residents digging through the debris in Pasir Langu as rescue operations continued.

## Official Response and Safety Measures

"Unstable soil and heavy rain continue to complicate search and rescue operations," said Teten Ali Mungku Engkun, who heads West Java's Disaster Management Office. He said local authorities rapidly assessed the damage and deployed emergency response teams immediately after the landslide.

Families living within 100 meters of the affected area were evacuated due to fears of further slope failures. Authorities urged residents in other landslide-prone areas to remain vigilant and evacuate immediately if they hear rumbling sounds, see soil movement, or believe conditions are unsafe.

## Recurring Seasonal Threat

These disasters strike seasonally in Indonesia. From October to April, monsoon rains and high tides frequently trigger flooding and landslides across the archipelago of more than 17,000 islands, where millions of people live in mountainous areas or near fertile flood plains.

A catastrophic disaster struck nearby in December, when floods and landslides hit Sumatra, Indonesia's largest island. That disaster killed at least 1,200 people and injured more than 7,000, according to the National Disaster Management Agency.

Central Java experienced a similar disaster last January, when more than 20 people were killed after being swept away in floods and landslides following torrential rains.
