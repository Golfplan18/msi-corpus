The coordinated international response reflects alarm among global policymakers that political pressure on the U.S. Federal Reserve could erode the institutional independence that central banks worldwide regard as essential for managing inflation and maintaining financial stability.

Central bankers from nine countries issued a coordinated statement Tuesday declaring they "stand in full solidarity" with Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell, as President Donald Trump escalated his confrontation with the central bank through Justice Department threats of criminal charges.

The statement, published on the European Central Bank's website, was signed by ECB President Christine Lagarde and Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey, along with seven other national central bank governors.

"Powell has served with integrity, focused on his mandate and an unwavering commitment to the public interest," the statement said.

The signatories added that "the independence of central banks is a cornerstone of price, financial and economic stability in the interest of the citizens that we serve. It is therefore critical to preserve that independence, with full respect for the rule of law and democratic accountability."

## The dispute

The confrontation is ostensibly about Powell's testimony to Congress in June over the cost of a major renovation of Federal Reserve buildings. In a statement Sunday, Powell — setting aside his previous practice of declining to engage Trump's criticism directly — called the administration's criminal charges threats "pretexts" in the president's campaign to seize control of U.S. interest rate policy from the Fed's professional staff.

Trump has repeatedly criticized Powell and the Fed for not moving faster to cut interest rates.

Economists warn that a politically compromised Fed that yields to presidential demands will damage its credibility as an inflation fighter and likely lead investors to demand higher returns before investing in U.S. Treasury securities.

## Why global central banks are watching

The U.S. dollar's dominant position in global trade and central bank reserves — the trade-weighted dollar index stood at 120.6 as of Tuesday — amplifies the reach of Fed decisions worldwide. Changes in U.S. interest rates affect the dollar's exchange rate against other currencies and the value of foreign investors' U.S. assets, transmitting effects far beyond American borders.

Politically independent central banks have become a cornerstone of the global economy because they can more readily take steps to fight inflation — including rate increases that are unpopular in the short term — while preserving price stability over longer periods.

## Signatories

Beyond Lagarde and Bailey, the statement was signed by Erik Thedeen, governor of Sweden's Riksbank; Christian Kettel Thomsen, chair of Denmark's central bank; Martin Schlegel, chair of the Swiss National Bank; Michele Bullock, governor of the Reserve Bank of Australia; Tiff Macklem, governor of the Bank of Canada; Chang Yong Rhee, governor of the Bank of Korea; and Gabriel Galipolo, governor of Brazil's Banco Central do Brasil.

François Villeroy de Galhau, board chair of the Bank for International Settlements, and Pablo Hernández de Cos, BIS general manager, also signed. The BIS, based in Basel, Switzerland, is an international organization of central banks.

The Bank of Japan was not among the signatories. The statement noted that additional names could be added. Bank of Japan officials could not immediately be reached for comment, the Associated Press reported.
