COTONOU, Benin — Benin's opposition parties won no seats in a legislative election Saturday, according to provisional results announced by the country's electoral commission, leaving the National Assembly entirely in the hands of parties aligned with President Patrice Talon weeks after a brief military coup attempt his government said it foiled.

The outcome deepens a political squeeze on the opposition ahead of an April presidential election: the main opposition party cleared the national vote threshold but failed the per-district requirement under a contested electoral code, while the leading opposition presidential candidate has already been barred from the race.

## All seats go to pro-Talon parties

Only two parties won seats under the provisional results: the Republican Bloc, which will hold 49 seats, and the Progressive Union for Renewal, which will hold 60. Both are aligned with Talon. The results must still be confirmed by the Constitutional Court.

Benin's electoral code requires a party to obtain at least 20% of the national vote and at least 20% in each of the country's 24 electoral districts to qualify for seat allocation. The Democrats, the main opposition party, won approximately 16% of the national vote but fell short of the per-district threshold, leaving it without representation.

Voter turnout was 36.73%, the electoral commission said.

## Opposition calls code 'exclusionary'

Guy Mitokpe, the spokesperson for The Democrats, said the results validated concerns his party had raised for roughly two years about the design of the electoral rules.

"These results confirm the struggle that (The Democrats) party has been waging for about two years," Mitokpe said. "We denounced this electoral code, saying that it heavily favored parties aligned with the president. It's an exclusionary electoral code. As proof, we won't have a candidate in the presidential election, and we were excluded from the municipal elections."

## Context: coup attempt and Africa's coup wave

The election took place weeks after a military takeover attempt against Talon that lasted a few hours before authorities announced it had been foiled. The attempt was among the latest in a series of coups across Africa in recent years, a pattern linked to disputed elections, constitutional disruption, security crises and youth discontent.

Benin gained independence from France in 1960 and endured a history of coups before experiencing relative stability over the past two decades.

## April presidential race

Talon, 67, is barred from seeking another term after a decade in office. Finance Minister Romuald Wadagni is seen as the front-runner for the April presidential election. The main opposition presidential candidate was barred from running for failing to meet required endorsements, leaving Talon's preferred successor without a major declared challenger.

Critics have accused Talon of clamping down on political opposition and human rights during his tenure, even as Benin recorded economic growth under his leadership.
