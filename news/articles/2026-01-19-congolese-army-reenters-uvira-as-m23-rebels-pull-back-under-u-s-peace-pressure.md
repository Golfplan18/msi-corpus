Uvira's return to government control restores Congo's presence in South Kivu province, which had been largely lost when the provincial capital, Bukavu, fell to M23. Despite a U.S.-brokered agreement reaffirmed by Presidents Félix Tshisekedi and Paul Kagame in Washington, fighting continues on multiple fronts in eastern Congo, where more than 7 million people have been displaced in what the United Nations describes as one of the world's most significant humanitarian crises.

Congolese soldiers and pro-government Wazalendo militia fighters reentered the strategic eastern city of Uvira on Sunday, the Congolese army and residents said Monday, retaking a key urban center more than a month after Rwanda-backed M23 rebels seized it in a rapid offensive. Sporadic gunfire continued in the city Monday morning, with the security situation still uncertain.

"The Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo are continuing their deployment in Uvira and surrounding areas to consolidate their positions and secure people and their property," army spokesperson Mak Hazukay said in a statement.

"Since yesterday, we have welcomed our soldiers back after more than a month away," Alain Ramazani, a resident of Uvira, told the Associated Press by phone. He said the soldiers entered the city alongside Wazalendo fighters.

## Fragile return, gunfire persists

M23 said it withdrew as a "unilateral trust-building measure" requested by the United States to facilitate the peace process. Congolese army vehicles were patrolling Uvira by Monday morning, but Ghislain Kabamba, director of the Observatory for Human Rights, Justice and Local Governance for Social Cohesion in Congo, said sporadic gunfire continued. Kabamba, who is based in Uvira, said it was unclear whether the shooting involved Wazalendo fighters, soldiers, or residents in a city he described as flooded with weapons.

More than 1,500 people have been killed and about 300,000 displaced in the conflict, Congolese government spokesperson Patrick Muyaya said Monday.

Uvira had been the Congo government's last major foothold in South Kivu province after the provincial capital, Bukavu, fell to the rebels. Its seizure by M23 allowed the rebel group to consolidate a broad corridor of influence across the east and brought the fighting to the doorstep of neighboring Burundi, which has maintained troops in eastern Congo for years, heightening fears of a broader regional spillover.

## Peace deal strained by continuing hostilities

M23's push into Uvira came days after Congolese President Félix Tshisekedi and Rwandan President Paul Kagame met with U.S. President Donald Trump in Washington to reaffirm a U.S.-brokered peace agreement. Despite that meeting, fighting has continued on several fronts across eastern Congo, the Associated Press reported, with ongoing civilian and military casualties.

Congo, the United States, and U.N. experts accuse Rwanda of backing M23. M23 has grown from hundreds of members in 2021 to around 6,500 fighters, according to the United Nations.

## A humanitarian crisis of regional scale

More than 100 armed groups are active in mineral-rich eastern Congo near the border with Rwanda, with M23 the most prominent. The broader conflict has created one of the world's most significant humanitarian crises, with more than 7 million people displaced, according to the U.N. agency for refugees.

Negotiations between Congo and the rebels continue, but fighting has not halted across the region.
