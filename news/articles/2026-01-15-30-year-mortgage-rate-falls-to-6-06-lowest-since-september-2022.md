The decline offers modest relief to prospective homebuyers, but the housing market remains in a deep slump now entering its fourth year, with home sales in 2025 finishing near a 30-year low despite monthly gains in the final four months of the year.

The average rate on a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage fell to 6.06% this week, its lowest point since September 2022, mortgage buyer Freddie Mac reported Thursday. The rate eased from 6.16% the prior week and stands well below the 7.04% average recorded a year earlier, Freddie Mac said.

The last time the benchmark rate was lower was Sept. 15, 2022, when it stood at 6.02%, according to Freddie Mac.

The decline extends a months-long retreat from rates that exceeded 7% and comes as the housing market enters its fourth year of slump, with home sales in 2025 finishing near a 30-year low despite monthly gains in the final four months of the year.

## Affordability improving, slump persisting

Lower rates have reduced monthly costs for buyers who can afford current prices. The median U.S. monthly housing payment fell to $2,413 in the four weeks ending Jan. 11, according to Redfin — a 5.5% drop from the same period a year earlier and near the lowest level in two years.

Even so, elevated home prices and uncertainty over the economy and job market are keeping many prospective buyers on the sidelines, according to the Associated Press.

Home sales remained stuck near a 30-year low last year despite the months-long rate decline, extending a slump driven by years of soaring prices and elevated borrowing costs that shut out many aspiring homeowners.

The average rate on 15-year fixed-rate mortgages, popular with homeowners refinancing existing loans, also fell, dropping to 5.38% from 5.46% the previous week. A year ago, the 15-year average stood at 6.27%, Freddie Mac said.

## Lock-in effect limits supply

A structural constraint is limiting the benefits lower rates can deliver to the broader market. Nearly 69% of U.S. homes with an outstanding mortgage carry a fixed rate of 5% or lower, and slightly more than half carry a rate at or below 4%, according to Realtor.com. That distribution means millions of existing homeowners have little financial incentive to sell and take on a new loan at a higher rate, constraining the supply of homes available for purchase.

Economists generally expect mortgage rates to ease further during 2026, though most recent forecasts show the 30-year rate remaining above 6% for the year — roughly twice the record lows reached earlier this decade.

## Refinancing surges on rate drop

The rate decline has set off a sharp increase in refinancing activity. Applications for mortgage refinancing loans soared 40% last week from the previous week, accounting for 60% of all home loan applications, the Mortgage Bankers Association reported. Applications for loans to buy a home climbed 16%.

"With mortgage rates much lower than a year ago and edging closer to 6%, MBA expects strong interest from homeowners seeking a refinance and would-be buyers stepping off the sidelines," said Bob Broeksmit, CEO of the Mortgage Bankers Association.

## Fed policy and a White House intervention

Mortgage rates began easing in July in anticipation of Federal Reserve rate cuts, which began in September and continued in December. The Fed does not set mortgage rates directly, but reductions in its short-term benchmark rate can signal lower inflation or slower economic growth, prompting investors to buy U.S. government bonds. That buying pressure can reduce yields on long-term Treasurys, which in turn can pull mortgage rates lower. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 4.15% on Jan. 15, according to Federal Reserve data.

The most recent rate decline followed President Donald Trump's announcement last week that the federal government would direct Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to buy $200 billion in mortgage bonds in a bid to push rates lower.

Lower rates drove a sharp increase in refinancing activity last fall, a trend that has continued into the new year, the Associated Press reported.
