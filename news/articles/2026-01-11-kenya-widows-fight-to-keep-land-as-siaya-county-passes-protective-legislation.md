Thousands of widows in western Kenya face eviction and property seizure by their late husbands' relatives despite a constitutional guarantee of land ownership rights for all citizens, the Associated Press reported. In November 2025, the local assembly in Siaya County unanimously passed a Widows Protection Bill that, if signed by the governor, would criminalize forced disinheritance and forced remarriage. Advocates say most widows lose their land because they do not know the legal protections already available to them under Kenyan law.

Kenya's succession laws recognize widows and children as the rightful inheritors of family property, but customary practices in Luo, Luhya, and Kisii communities often override those protections in rural areas. Advocates say the disinheritance of widows creates a cycle of poverty for hundreds of thousands of children across the country.

SIAYA, Kenya — Rebecca Anyango stood outside the house she has called home for 26 years, wondering how long it will remain hers.

Her late husband's family has threatened her with eviction for years, claiming she has no inheritance rights. This year they filed a lawsuit. Anyango, 70, has no legal representation.

She pointed toward her husband's burial site, a few steps from the door. "Where do I take the grave?" she asked.

Anyango is among thousands of widows in western Kenya who face losing their homes and land after their husbands die, according to the Associated Press. They are often in rural areas, with little education and scant awareness of their legal rights, advocates say.

In November, the local assembly in Siaya County — where Anyango lives — unanimously passed a Widows Protection Bill. If signed by the governor, it would criminalize forced disinheritance and forced remarriage of widows.

## A constitutional guarantee rarely enforced

Kenya's constitution guarantees the right to land ownership for all citizens, and the country's succession laws recognize widows and children as the rightful inheritors of family property. Advocates say those protections are rarely enforced in rural communities where customary norms dominate.

"If the woman is not aware of what protects her, then she will be disinherited," said Simiyu Waddimba, who teaches anthropology at the University of Nairobi and has authored research on wife inheritance.

In the Luo, Luhya, and Kisii communities, widowhood can come with customary expectations that are illegal under Kenyan law when imposed without consent. One practice, known as "sexual cleansing," requires a widow to have sex with another man — often a brother of her late husband — in the belief it will lift a perceived curse of widowhood. Another, "wife inheritance," involves a widow being taken in as a wife by her late husband's brother.

Women who refuse these practices are often isolated and stripped of their land, the AP reported.

## The Siaya bill and the legislator behind it

Scholastica Madowo, a county legislator and herself a widow, championed the Widows Protection Bill. She is one of four elected women in the 42-member local assembly.

"Those cultural practices are actually a violation of their rights unless the woman does it willingly," Madowo said.

During her own campaign for office, she said, opponents made insinuations about her status as a widow, including allegations that she had killed her husband.

The bill would establish welfare committees to help widows access legal aid to challenge disinheritance in the courts.

## A cycle of dispossession

Anne Bonareri, 60, of Kisii County knows firsthand how quickly the losses can accumulate. Within hours of her husband's death in 1997, her in-laws seized his possessions — photos, clothes, and the commercial property he had held in his name. They took her home as well. Bonareri was left with three young children and was pregnant with a fourth.

"They took everything, and I was left with one photo of the father," Bonareri said, her voice catching.

The day after the burial, she said, her husband's elder brother came to claim her as a wife. When she refused, she said, armed men were sent to attack her. She later worked three jobs to buy a small piece of land and build a new house.

Her daughter, Emma Mong'ute, founded the Amandla MEK Foundation in 2019 to provide legal advice and connect women in similar circumstances with pro bono lawyers. Mong'ute said the organization has had some success in helping women retain land, and that she would consider pushing for protective legislation in Kisii County similar to the Siaya bill.

Mong'ute, who is herself banned from the family land and cannot visit her father's grave there, said the disinheritance of widows creates a cycle of poverty for hundreds of thousands of children in Kenya.

## Legal training as a remedy

Easter Okech, with the Kenya Female Advisory Organization in Kisumu County, said most widows are disinherited because they do not understand the succession laws that protect them. She now offers legal training so women can represent themselves in ongoing cases. She also encourages the writing of wills — rarely done in rural areas — and the appointment of neutral executors.

## Some widows hold their ground

Not all widows in western Kenya have lost their land.

Marie Owino, 87, a former teacher, said she knew her rights and that her financial independence meant her in-laws "didn't dare" to disinherit her after her husband died 33 years ago. She still lives in the brick house she and her husband shared, set on 100 acres.

"Once you have established yourself that you can, then I'm telling you all those people will give you respect," Owino said.

## A regional pattern

Similar tensions between general and customary law extend across the continent, said Misheck Dube, a former associate professor at the University of Limpopo in South Africa who has researched widowhood in the region.

"While the general law protects the inheritance rights of surviving spouses and children, customary practices still allow different ethnic groups to administer estates according to their traditions, often to the detriment of widows," Dube said.
