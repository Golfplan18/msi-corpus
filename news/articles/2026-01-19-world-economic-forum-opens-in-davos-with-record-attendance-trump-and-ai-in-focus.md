The 2026 Davos gathering convenes as Trump's tariff threats, territorial pronouncements, and foreign-policy overhauls have unsettled longstanding relationships among many of the same governments represented at the forum, lending the event's theme — "A Spirit of Dialogue" — a sharper practical edge than its annual buzzword predecessors.

DAVOS, Switzerland — Nearly 3,000 high-level participants from business, government, and international institutions are converging on this Alpine ski town for the World Economic Forum's annual meeting, with U.S. President Donald Trump set to address what organizers describe as a record gathering of world leaders and artificial intelligence commanding a central place on the agenda alongside a geopolitical backdrop reshaped by his administration's early moves.

Organizers say nearly 400 top political leaders will attend — a record — including more than 60 heads of state and government, as well as nearly 850 chairs and chief executives of many of the world's leading companies. Trump is scheduled to deliver a speech Wednesday, joined by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and special envoy Steve Witkoff.

## A crowded guest list

Among the heads of state and government expected are French President Emmanuel Macron, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, Congolese President Felix Tshisekedi, Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng, and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, according to the Associated Press.

Organizers say the ministerial contingent includes 55 ministers for economy and finance, 33 for foreign affairs, 34 for trade, commerce, and industry, and 11 central bank governors. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte and World Trade Organization Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala are among the international institution officials expected to attend.

The technology sector is represented by Jensen Huang, chief executive of Nvidia; Satya Nadella of Microsoft; Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind; and Arthur Mensch, chief executive of France's Mistral AI.

## AI and geopolitics share the spotlight

More than 200 sessions are scheduled to tackle issues ranging from economic disparity and climate change to technology and global cooperation. Organizers have branded the gathering with the theme "A Spirit of Dialogue," organized around five sub-themes: cooperation, growth, investment in people, innovation, and building prosperity.

Artificial intelligence has become a central subject, with business executives examining how to apply it to boost efficiency and profits while labor leaders and advocacy groups are expected to warn of its threat to jobs and livelihoods, and policymakers navigating questions of regulation versus the right to innovate.

The geopolitical context has grown more complex than in recent years, according to the AP. Trump's positions on Venezuela, Greenland, and Iran — along with his aggressive tariff policies — have raised questions about America's role in international institutions and alliances, several of which are represented by senior officials at the forum.

## Forum background

The World Economic Forum is a Geneva-based think tank and event organizer whose flagship annual meeting debuted in 1971 in Davos, a ski-resort town of about 10,000 people situated at roughly 1,500 meters — nearly 5,000 feet — in the Alps of eastern Switzerland. The first edition, hosted by forum founder Klaus Schwab, featured a gathering of business executives. The meeting has since expanded into a conference spanning dozens of global issues.

Critics say the Davos gathering involves too much talk and not enough action to address inequality and climate change, the AP reported.
