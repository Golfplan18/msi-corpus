Hungary’s parliamentary election will be held on April 12, President Tamás Sulyok announced, urging people to vote freely ahead of the contest. In a message accompanying the announcement, Sulyok wrote, “One of the cornerstones of democracy is the right to vote freely.”

The announcement, made Tuesday, sets the date as Prime Minister Viktor Orbán prepares for what the report described as his toughest challenge in 16 years. Orbán and his nationalist-populist Fidesz party have been lagging behind the opposition Tisza party in most independent polls, the biggest disadvantage Orbán has faced since taking office in 2010, according to the report.

Tisza leader Péter Magyar, 44, emerged on Hungary’s political stage less than two years ago. The report said Magyar built his political movement through nearly non-stop tours of the rural countryside while drawing on dissatisfaction with Hungary’s chronically stagnant economy, high costs of living, and fraying social services. Magyar has also promised to dismantle Orbán’s system and put Hungary on a more prosperous, democratic track.

Orbán has been in the spotlight as the European Union’s longest-serving leader and returned to office in 2010 after serving one term as prime minister between 1998 and 2002. The report said Orbán has described his political project as transforming Hungary into an “illiberal democracy,” and it said he has centralized the country’s institutions and taken control of much of the media.

Critics, the report said, accuse Orbán of overseeing what they describe as a widespread network of official corruption. It said the governing coalition and allied media have framed the April election as a choice between war and peace, portraying Tisza as an EU project contrived in Brussels to topple Orbán’s government and install a puppet regime.

In that framing, the report said, Fidesz officials claim the Tisza-led direction would drain Hungary’s finances into the war in neighboring Ukraine and could even involve Hungary directly in the conflict. The report also said Fidesz politicians and loyal media outlets have accused Tisza of secretly plotting to raise taxes and institute “draconian austerity measures,” claims Tisza has strongly denied.

On the opposition side, Magyar has vowed that if elected he would introduce anti-corruption measures. He has also vowed to bring home billions of euros in funding the European Union has frozen to Budapest over judicial independence and corruption concerns, the report said.

Orbán has refused to hold a televised debate with Magyar, the report said. It added that Orbán’s last debate against an opponent was before the 2006 election, which he lost.

The official campaign period, under which signature collection for parliamentary candidates may begin and election posters can be put up, will start on Feb. 21, the report said.
