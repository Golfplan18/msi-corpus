Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš’s new government was set to face a mandatory confidence vote in Parliament as debate began Tuesday in the country’s 200-seat lower house, the Associated Press reported. Every new administration must win the vote to govern, AP said.

Babiš’s coalition holds 108 seats, giving it a majority in the chamber as lawmakers consider whether it will keep office. Babiš, who previously served as prime minister in two governments from 2017-2021, returned to the role after his ANO, or YES, movement won big in the country’s October election, according to AP.

The movement formed a majority coalition with two small political groups: the Freedom and Direct Democracy anti-migrant party and the right-wing Motorists for Themselves, AP reported. The parties created a 16-member Cabinet, AP said.

In his speech in the lower house, Babiš said, “I’d like to make it clear that the Czech Republic and Czech citizens will be first for our government,” AP reported.

AP said the political comeback by Babiš and the alliance with the two smaller parties is expected to redefine Czech foreign and domestic policies. The agenda is aimed at steering the country away from supporting Ukraine and rejecting some key European Union policies, AP reported.

The new government’s approach differs from that of the previous pro-Western administration, AP said. AP reported that Babiš rejected any financial aid for Ukraine and guarantees for EU loans to the country fighting the Russian invasion, and the story tied that stance to leaders including Viktor Orban of Hungary and Robert Fico of Slovakia.

AP also said the government would not abandon a Czech initiative that acquired some 1.8 million artillery shells for Ukraine last year. The initiative, AP said, was conducted on markets outside the EU on condition that the Czechs would administer it but would not contribute money.

On refugees and security, AP reported that the Freedom party sees no future for the Czech Republic in the EU and NATO and wants to expel most of 380,000 Ukrainian refugees in the country. For domestic policy, AP reported that Motorists for Themselves is in charge of the environment and foreign ministries, rejected the EU Green Deal, and proposed revivals of the coal industry.
