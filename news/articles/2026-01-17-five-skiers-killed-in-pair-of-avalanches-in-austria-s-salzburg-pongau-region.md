The twin disasters struck as rescue officials said avalanche danger in the region was already elevated, with warnings repeatedly issued to backcountry skiers before the slides occurred.

VIENNA — Five skiers died Saturday in two separate avalanches in the Salzburg Pongau region of western Austria, authorities said. A snow slide in the Gastein Valley, south of the city of Salzburg, swept away seven people and killed four of them; a female skier had died about 90 minutes earlier in the nearby Bad Hofgastein area.

"This tragedy painfully demonstrates how serious the current avalanche situation is," said Gerhard Kremser, district head of the Pongau mountain rescue service, citing the "clear and repeated warnings" about avalanche risk that had been issued before the slides occurred.

Of the seven people swept up in the Gastein Valley slide, two were injured — one seriously — and a third escaped unharmed, Austria's APA news agency reported.

The earlier fatality in Bad Hofgastein occurred at an altitude of about 2,200 meters (7,200 feet), according to APA.

Four rescue helicopters, mountain rescue and Red Cross dog teams, and a crisis intervention team responded to the Gastein Valley scene.
