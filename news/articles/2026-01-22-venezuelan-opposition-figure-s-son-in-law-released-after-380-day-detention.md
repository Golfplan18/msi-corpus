The release represents one of many political prisoner releases Venezuela has made since Maduro's capture, though hundreds more remain imprisoned on political grounds. Human rights organizations are documenting extensive calls for the release of the nearly 800 remaining prisoners they say are detained unjustly.

Mariana González said Thursday that her husband, Rafael Tudares Bracho, had returned home "after 380 days of unjust and arbitrary detention — having endured more than a year of the inhumane reality of enforced disappearance."

Tudares Bracho, whose wife is the daughter of exiled opposition leader Edmundo González, was held incommunicado throughout his detention. He was arrested just days before Maduro's inauguration for a third term and sentenced to 30 years in prison in November.

## One Year Detained, 12 Hours on Trial

The trial took place in a single 12-hour session on November 28, resulting in convictions for conspiracy, terrorism, and criminal association. Mariana González said it was not until January 16 that she was granted her first 25-minute visit with her husband since his arrest.

## Part of a Broader Pattern

His release reflects a pattern of prisoner releases by Venezuela's interim government following Maduro's capture to face drug-trafficking charges. The Trump administration has praised these releases as a gesture of goodwill to the United States.

Edmundo González, an exiled former diplomat widely regarded as the legitimate winner of Venezuela's 2024 presidential election, posted a video on social media hailing his son-in-law's freedom. "It would be a mistake to reduce this event to a personal story," González said. "There are still men and women who remain deprived of their liberty for political reasons, without guarantees, without due process, and in many cases, without truth."

## Hundreds Remain in Detention

Foro Penal, Venezuela's leading prisoner rights organization, verified the release of 145 people it considers to be political prisoners as of Tuesday. Yet at least 775 more remain in detention, according to the organization.

Families of Venezuelan prisoners continue to hold vigils outside prisons, demanding the release of imprisoned critics, journalists, and opposition members. The releases, while significant for individual families, represent only a fraction of those still detained on political grounds.
