## Profile traces Nixon's Jacksonville roots, DSA membership, general-election platform

Florida state Rep. Angie Nixon, a Democratic Socialists of America member who defeated combat veteran Alex Vindman 56.1% to 43.9% in the state's Democratic Senate primary, is preparing to face Republican Sen. Ashley Moody in November in a state where Republicans now hold a 1.5 million-voter registration edge over Democrats. If elected, the 42-year-old would become the first African American senator in Florida history.

Nixon said she "actually knew we were going to win" but did not know the margin would be so wide. "We put in the work and so super excited about the outcome," she said. "It shows that good old-fashioned grassroots organising will beat big money each and every day."

The result followed a primary in which the Democratic establishment had the money, the machinery and a candidate with national name recognition. Vindman, a combat veteran who testified against President Donald Trump during his first impeachment, had moved to Florida in recent years.

Nixon was born and raised in Jacksonville, primarily by a single mother who worked two jobs. Her father, an army veteran, was not always present. "None of my parents were involved in politics," she said. "My mom, though, was a union member and so that's where I learned about the power of organising."

Nixon said she remembers her mother telling her she was on strike and overhearing conversations about "scabs in the union and people crossing the picket line." "I am someone who grew up knowing the importance of solidarity with your fellow workers and people who were being oppressed and whatnot," she said.

Her childhood was shaped by the failures of public institutions. She was bussed across town to elementary school because her predominantly Black neighborhood school lacked resources. Her family later suffered a devastating tragedy when her favorite cousin was killed by gun violence.

After graduating from the University of Florida in 2007, Nixon became a union organizer with the Service Employees International Union. She was elected to the Florida House of Representatives in 2020, where she has pushed for expanding childcare and stopping a major grocery chain from "gouging their prices."

In May, Nixon protested the Republican-controlled legislature's redistricting of the state's congressional maps by shouting through a megaphone on the House floor, earning a reprimand from an ethics committee. After Gov. Ron DeSantis signed the legislature's redistricting plan, she staged a sit-in at his office and was arrested. Nixon also sued the state after being denied access to the South Florida migrant detention center known as "Alligator Alcatraz," and introduced legislation that would require Immigration and Customs Enforcement and other U.S. law enforcement agents to unmask and identify themselves on the job.

In June, Nixon joined the Democratic Socialists of America, whose more than 100,000 members include Zohran Mamdani, elected mayor of New York City last year. "They are an organisation that was pushing and fighting for the same things that I was fighting for," she said. "I don't agree with everything that they agree with, but they're fighting hard to ensure we have access to universal healthcare, access to free college and fully funded public schools."

Nixon resists the socialist label. "I would describe myself as a former single mama who is putting my family and my community first," she said. "I am a Democrat. I've always been a lifelong Democrat but I'm more of a social democrat who believes in strong safety nets." She described herself as aligned with what she called "the FDR Democrats," citing Franklin Delano Roosevelt's support for social programs.

Her Senate platform includes Medicare for All, universal childcare, a national rent freeze and moratorium on evictions, stronger union protections and a federal minimum wage of $25 an hour. She proposes higher taxes on billionaires, driving big money out of politics and reining in tech companies and data centers. Nixon performed strongly in working-class, majority-Black counties but was weaker in South Florida's Latino communities, where historical experiences with left-wing authoritarian governments make the socialist label highly polarizing.

A heated, shouting phone call between Nixon and Florida Democratic Party Chair Nikki Fried was reported shortly after the primary result. At one point, Nixon yelled, "Stop gaslighting me!" a source told Axios. Nixon did not deny the incident, describing the tension as a natural feature of a broad coalition.

"Sometimes you argue with your siblings because you want to do things a certain way and they may want to do things a certain way and at the end of the day you are all going to move in the direction to help benefit your family," Nixon said. "Our family is the community of Florida and so she and I are together. There's no ill will among us."

Democratic establishment figures have expressed concern that nominating a DSA member will backfire in conservative Florida. In the general election, Nixon faces Moody, a former state attorney general appointed by DeSantis to fill the seat vacated when Marco Rubio was elevated to Secretary of State. Whereas active registered Democrats outnumbered Republicans in the state in 2020, there are now 1.5 million more Republicans than Democrats.

Republicans have already begun framing Nixon's victory as a gift. Rep. Anna Paulina Luna told Fox News that Nixon's nomination "will bring Republicans out to vote specifically against her because of the fact they don't want her radical policies." Sen. Tim Scott of South Carolina called the Democratic left wing "dangerous, radical and, frankly, weird."

Progressives counter that a bold economic message is the only way to reverse the party's decline in the state. They point to the recent Miami mayoral race, where Eileen Higgins campaigned openly as a Democrat despite the race being officially nonpartisan and defeated Trump-backed Emilio Gonzalez, marking the first Democratic mayoral victory in Miami in nearly 30 years.

Trump, whose Mar-a-Lago estate is in Palm Beach, celebrated Vindman's defeat, calling him a "treasonous creep" and predicting "certain defeat" for "Radical Left Lunatic" Nixon in November.

Nixon said she is "concerned with whether this country is still going to be standing because we have someone who does not want to leave the White House. We have someone who is creating a paramilitary force and creating basically an underground ballroom-bunker type situation." She said she wants to go to Washington to "put a check on his corruption and his power to let him know that he is not a king. We did not appoint him to be a king."
