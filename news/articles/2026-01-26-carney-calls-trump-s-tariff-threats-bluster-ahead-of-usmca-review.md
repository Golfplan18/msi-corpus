The characterization represents Canada's effort to manage tensions escalating over Trump's broader trade agenda and his repeated suggestions that Canada might become a U.S. state or that the U.S. should acquire Greenland, which has strained the NATO alliance.

Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Monday that President Donald Trump's threats of 100% tariffs on Canada should be understood as negotiating positioning rather than settled policy, as the two countries prepare to review their shared trade agreement this year.

"The president is a strong negotiator, and some of these comments and positioning should be viewed in the broader context of that," Carney said.

Carney spoke to Trump on Monday, according to U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who told Fox News that Carney was "very aggressively walking back" some remarks he had made at the World Economic Forum in Davos last week.

## The Tariff Threat and USMCA Review

Trump threatened last weekend to impose a 100% tariff on Canadian goods if the country pursued a comprehensive trade deal with China. The threat came as Canada and the United States prepare for an annual review of the United States–Mexico–Canada Agreement, the trade pact that replaced NAFTA during Trump's first term.

Carney said Canada expects "a robust review" of the agreement but clarified that this year's talks are a review, not a full-scale renegotiation. "It's not six years ago," he said, referencing the first-term negotiations. "We talked about that. This is a review. It was built into the agreement. It's not a renegotiation."

## Canada's China Arrangement

Canada has no interest in negotiating a comprehensive trade deal with Beijing, Carney said. Dominic LeBlanc, Canada's minister responsible for Canada-U.S. trade, said he spoke with U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer on Sunday and clarified that Canada is negotiating only a "narrow trade arrangement" with China that mostly deals with "a few sectors" of the Canadian economy.

The arrangement followed Carney's recent visit to Beijing, where he cut Canada's 100% tariff on Chinese electric vehicles to 6.1% in return for lower Chinese tariffs on Canadian products including pork and seafood. Carney said the new strategic partnership "will make available tens of thousands of affordable electric vehicles in Canada."

The initial cap on Chinese EV exports to Canada is 49,000 vehicles annually at a tariff rate of 6.1%, growing to about 70,000 over five years. The initial cap represents about 3% of the 1.8 million vehicles sold in Canada annually. In exchange, Carney said China is expected to begin investing in the Canadian auto industry within three years.

## Tensions Over Greenland and Alliance Pressure

Trump's tariff threats came amid escalating tensions with Carney as the president's push to acquire Greenland strained the NATO alliance. Trump has repeatedly needled Canada over its sovereignty and suggested it might be absorbed into the United States as a 51st state.

At Davos last week, Carney emerged as a vocal advocate for countries to coordinate resistance to U.S. pressure. "Middle powers must act together because if you are not at the table, you are on the menu," he said. The remarks drew widespread attention and effectively upstaged Trump at the World Economic Forum, earning praise from other Western leaders.

LeBlanc said Canada is ready to move quickly on the USMCA review.
