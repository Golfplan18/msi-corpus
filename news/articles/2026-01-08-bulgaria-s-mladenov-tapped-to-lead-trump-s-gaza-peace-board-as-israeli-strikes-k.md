Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced Thursday that Nickolay Mladenov, a Bulgarian diplomat and former United Nations Mideast peace envoy, has been designated as director-general of President Donald Trump's Board of Peace for Gaza. Netanyahu made the announcement after meeting with Mladenov in Jerusalem. Israeli strikes across the Gaza Strip killed at least eight people the same day, according to Palestinian hospital officials and family members.

The appointment marks a concrete step in Trump's Mideast peace framework, which aims to supervise Gaza's reconstruction, the disarmament of Hamas, and the deployment of an international security force — though U.S. officials have reported little progress on any of those fronts since an October ceasefire ended more than two years of fighting between Israel and Hamas.

## Board mandate and appointments

A senior U.S. official, speaking on condition of anonymity because the appointment had not been officially announced, confirmed Mladenov as the Trump administration's choice to serve as the board's day-to-day administrator on the ground. Trump has said he will head the board personally. Additional appointments are expected next week, according to Israeli and American officials who both spoke on condition of anonymity pending a formal announcement.

Under Trump's plan, the Board of Peace is meant to supervise a new technocratic Palestinian government, the disarmament of Hamas, the deployment of an international security force, additional pullbacks of Israeli troops, and reconstruction of the territory.

Mladenov served as the U.N. Mideast peace envoy from 2015 to 2020, having previously served as the U.N. envoy to Iraq. He is a former Bulgarian defense and foreign minister. During his tenure as Mideast envoy, he maintained working relations with Israel and frequently worked to ease tensions between Israel and Hamas, according to the Associated Press.

## Strikes and ceasefire tensions

Israeli strikes across the Gaza Strip on Thursday killed at least eight people, including an 11-year-old girl and a teenage girl, according to Palestinian hospital officials and family members. Hamas called the deaths a "blatant violation of the ceasefire."

Israel's military said it was not aware of any strike-related casualties in northern Gaza's Jabaliya area, where the 11-year-old girl was killed, and did not immediately comment on the other deaths reported Thursday.

Israeli strikes since the October ceasefire have killed over 400 Palestinians, according to local health officials. Israel has said those strikes were in response to violations of the ceasefire agreement. Palestinian health officials say scores of civilians have been among the dead.

The phased ceasefire remains in its initial stage. Its first phase halted the fighting and secured an exchange of hostages held by Hamas for hundreds of Palestinians held by Israel, but Hamas has not returned the remains of Ran Gvili, a 24-year-old police officer killed in the October 7, 2023 attack that triggered the war. Israel's Hostages and Missing Families Forum said Wednesday that search teams had recommenced looking for Gvili's remains.

## International pressure

Egyptian and European Union leaders meeting in Cairo on Thursday urged the deployment of an international stabilization force in Gaza to oversee the ceasefire.

"The situation is extremely severe. Still, Hamas refuses to disarm. It blocks progress to the next stage of the peace plan at the same time Israel is also restricting the international NGOs that are putting humanitarian aid access at serious risk," said Kaja Kallas, the EU's foreign policy chief.

"There's no justification for the humanitarian situation in Gaza to have deteriorated to the current level," Kallas said.

Philippe Lazzarini, commissioner-general of the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees, warned Thursday that Israeli pressure on UNRWA risks creating a "huge vacuum" in health, education, and social services for Palestinians in Gaza and the West Bank.

"If the agency cannot or has to stop to operate in Gaza or in the West Bank, this will create a huge vacuum," Lazzarini told reporters in Ankara, where he was in talks with Turkish officials on improving humanitarian access in Gaza. He said a new UNRWA office in Turkey is expected to open "within weeks" as a liaison and advocacy hub.
