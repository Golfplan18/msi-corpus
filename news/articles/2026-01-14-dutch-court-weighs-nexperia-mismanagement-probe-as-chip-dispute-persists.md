The hearing exposes the fault lines of Western governments' efforts to assert national security controls over Chinese-owned technology companies, with global automakers caught between competing state interests in a critical semiconductor supply chain.

**THE HAGUE** — An Amsterdam appellate court heard arguments Wednesday over whether to launch a corporate mismanagement investigation into Dutch semiconductor maker Nexperia and its Chinese parent company Wingtech, in a case that has disrupted chip supplies to automakers in North America, Japan and South Korea. No immediate decision was expected.

The hearing at the enterprise chamber of the Amsterdam Court of Appeal comes as a standoff between Nexperia's Dutch headquarters and its Chinese manufacturing unit continues to unsettle global supply chains for the automotive industry.

Nexperia lawyer Jeroen van der Schriek told the three-judge panel that the behavior of Wingtech and Hong Kong-based holding company Yuching since October "makes it clear that they are willing to subordinate Nexperia's interests to other interests."

Lawyers for Wingtech and the company's founder and former Nexperia chief executive, Zhang Xuezheng, urged the court not to order an investigation, portraying Zhang as a businessman who sought to guide Nexperia through geopolitical turbulence. Zhang was not present at the hearing, according to the Associated Press.

## Background: seizure, export ban, and diplomatic fallout

The dispute entered public view in October, when the Dutch government said it had effectively seized control of Nexperia since late September on national security grounds. Zhang was replaced as chief executive following claims of mismanagement and concerns that intellectual property could be transferred to China.

Beijing responded by temporarily blocking the export of Nexperia chips from its plant in China, sending automakers scrambling for alternatives, the AP reported. Nexperia's chips are used widely by carmakers including those in North America, Japan and South Korea, according to analysts.

The export restriction was lifted after U.S. President Donald Trump met with Chinese leader Xi Jinping in late October. The Dutch government in November said it was relinquishing its control of Nexperia as a "show of goodwill."

## Standoff persists

The underlying dispute persisted. Nexperia's Chinese arm said its Dutch headquarters had interrupted shipments of wafers to its Chinese factory, which it said had hampered core production operations and its ability to deliver finished products. Nexperia's headquarters said the Chinese unit had ignored instructions from the head office.

Honda halted production of some vehicles during the crisis, and Mercedes-Benz was among carmakers seeking alternative chip sources, the AP reported.

China's Ministry of Commerce in late December demanded the Netherlands immediately take corrective steps, blaming Dutch actions for causing a global chip supply chain crisis.

## Company history

Nexperia was spun off from Philips Semiconductors roughly two decades ago and acquired by Wingtech in 2018. In 2023, the British government blocked Nexperia's bid to acquire Wales-based chipmaker Newport Wafer Fab, citing national security risks.
