Slovenia President Natasa Pirc Musar set a parliamentary election for March 22, choosing the date for a 90-member assembly, the Associated Press reported Tuesday.

Pirc Musar said she wanted the election to be conducted fairly and that she would pay attention to disinformation and manipulation “including from abroad,” according to her remarks in the announcement.

The election is viewed as a test for Slovenia’s liberal government, which the report said is facing a challenge from populist right-wing parties.

Slovenia, an Alpine country of about 2 million people, split from the communist-run former Yugoslavia in 1991 and joined NATO and the European Union in 2004, the report said.

The prime minister’s coalition government led by Robert Golob came to power in 2022 after defeating Janez Jansa, who was described as having pushed the traditionally moderate nation to the right.

Under Golob, Slovenia recognized a Palestinian state, the Associated Press said. The opposition, however, accused Golob of slowing down the economy.

The announcement also referenced the constitutional timeline for government formation. By law, Parliament must convene within 20 days of the election, and Pirc Musar then has another 30 days to appoint a prime minister-designate backed by a majority of lawmakers.

Pirc Musar also called on Tuesday for “respectful, responsible and tolerant communication during the election campaign,” the report said.

The Associated Press said the vote follows a November referendum in which Slovenians rejected a bill on assisted dying that would have allowed terminally ill patients to end their lives.
