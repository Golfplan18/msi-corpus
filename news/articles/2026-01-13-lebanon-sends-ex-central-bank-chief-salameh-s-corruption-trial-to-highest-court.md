Lebanon's corruption case against former central bank governor Riad Salameh has been transferred to the Court of Cassation, the country's highest judicial body whose rulings cannot be appealed, judicial officials told the Associated Press on Tuesday. Salameh, 75, faces charges of embezzlement and illicit enrichment alongside two legal associates; he was released on $14 million bail last September after a year in pretrial detention. No trial date has been set, and Salameh denies all charges.

The referral to the Court of Cassation marks a significant procedural step in a case that has become emblematic of Lebanon's catastrophic 2019 financial collapse, which wiped out depositors' savings and caused the local currency to lose most of its value. Salameh, once celebrated as the architect of Lebanon's post-civil-war economic recovery, now faces parallel financial-crimes investigations in several European countries and is subject to sanctions imposed by the United States, the United Kingdom, and Canada.

## Charges and court transfer

The trial will also cover two of Salameh's legal associates, Marwan Khoury and Michel Toueini, according to a copy of the court notice obtained by the AP. All three will be issued arrest warrants if they fail to appear before the court. Four officials confirmed the details on condition of anonymity, saying they were not authorized to speak with the media.

Salameh was charged in September 2024 with embezzling $42 million from Lebanon's central bank. Prosecutors later added charges of illicit enrichment, alleging he rented a Paris apartment from his former romantic partner for approximately $500,000 annually, purportedly to serve as an alternative office for the central bank if needed.

Salameh has repeatedly denied allegations of corruption, embezzlement, and illicit enrichment. He insists his wealth derives from inherited properties, investments, and his prior career as an investment banker at Merrill Lynch.

## Three decades in power, then collapse

Salameh served as Lebanon's central bank governor for 30 years, beginning in 1993, and was once credited with steering the country's economic recovery after its 15-year civil war. He kept the fragile economy afloat through extended periods of political turmoil and gridlock.

His reputation unraveled along with Lebanon's finances. A fiscal crisis that erupted in late 2019 wiped out depositors' savings and collapsed the value of the local currency. Much of the Lebanese public holds Salameh's policies responsible for the disaster.

He left his post in 2023 as France, Germany, and Luxembourg opened financial-crimes investigations, freezing accounts and assets linked to him and his associates. The three European countries have proceeded with little cooperation from Lebanese authorities, according to the AP. The United States, the United Kingdom, and Canada imposed sanctions on Salameh in August 2023.

## Current governor files new complaints, plans Paris trip

Lebanon's current central bank governor, Karim Souaid, announced last week that he is filing legal complaints against a former central bank governor and a former banking official who he said diverted funds to four shell companies in the Cayman Islands. Souaid did not name either individual.

Souaid said Lebanon's central bank would become a plaintiff in the country's investigation into Forry Associates. The U.S. Treasury, in connection with its August 2023 sanctions action, described Forry Associates as "a shell company owned by Raja," Salameh's brother, "in the British Virgin Islands" used to divert about $330 million in transactions related to the central bank.

Souaid said he plans to travel to Paris this month to exchange what he called "highly sensitive" information as France continues its inquiry into the matter.
