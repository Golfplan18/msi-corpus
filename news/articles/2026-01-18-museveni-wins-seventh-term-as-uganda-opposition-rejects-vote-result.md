KAMPALA, Uganda — President Yoweri Museveni of Uganda claimed a seventh term Sunday after official results gave him 71.6% of the vote in Thursday's presidential election, with his closest challenger Bobi Wine rejecting the outcome as fraudulent.

Museveni, 81, addressed the nation from his country home in western Uganda, where dignitaries gathered to hear him speak publicly for the first time since his declared victory. He said the result gave "a good taste of the strength" of his ruling National Resistance Movement party, which has governed the East African country for four decades.

"The opposition are lucky," Museveni said. "They have not seen our full strength."

Wine, a musician-turned-politician whose legal name is Kyagulanyi Ssentamu, received 24.7% of the vote according to official tallies he called fake. Wine, 43, posted footage Sunday on the social platform X of what he described as alleged incidents of ballot stuffing and the intimidation of his campaign representatives on the eve of voting. Authorities did not immediately respond to those specific allegations.

Wine may pursue a legal challenge before the courts, which have previously rejected opposition efforts to nullify Museveni's past victories while recommending electoral reforms.

## Election marred by shutdowns and machine failures

The vote was marred by a dayslong internet shutdown and widespread failures of biometric voter identification machines that caused delays in voting in several areas, including Kampala, the capital. The biometric machine failures are expected to form the basis of any legal challenge, according to reporting by the Associated Press.

Voter turnout stood at 52%, the lowest recorded since Uganda's return to multiparty politics in 2006. Museveni said he believed many of those who did not vote were members of his own party.

Security forces were a constant presence throughout the campaign period. Wine said authorities followed him and deployed tear gas against his supporters; he campaigned wearing a flak jacket and helmet, citing security concerns.

At least seven opposition supporters of a losing parliamentary candidate affiliated with Wine's party were killed by police after they attacked a polling station with machetes in the central Butambala district, Museveni said. Museveni accused parts of the opposition of attempting to foment violence during the vote and called Wine and others "traitors." Wine has previously rejected that characterization, saying he represents the aspirations of millions of young Ugandans seeking political change after decades under the same leader.

## Decades of consolidated rule

Museveni, described by the Associated Press as Africa's third-longest governing president, has remained in power by removing legal constraints on his continued rule. Constitutional term limits and age restrictions that once barred him from standing for election have since been eliminated. Some potential rivals have been jailed or sidelined, the AP reported.

Veteran opposition figure Kizza Besigye, a four-time presidential candidate, remained in prison on treason charges that Besigye has said are politically motivated.

A seventh term would bring Museveni closer to five decades in power. Museveni's supporters credit him with the relative stability that has made Uganda a destination for hundreds of thousands of people fleeing violence elsewhere in the region. Uganda has not witnessed a peaceful transfer of presidential power since independence from British colonial rule more than six decades ago.
