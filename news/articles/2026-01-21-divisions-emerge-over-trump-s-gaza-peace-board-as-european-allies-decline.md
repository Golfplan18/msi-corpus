Western European allies rejected invitations to President Donald Trump's Gaza Peace Board on Wednesday, citing concerns that the new initiative could replace the United Nations as a mediator in global conflicts. France, Norway, and Sweden announced they would not join the board, weakening Trump's hopes for broad international support for the Gaza peace plan.

The board, chaired by Trump, was initially conceived as a small group of world leaders to oversee the ceasefire in Gaza. But the Trump administration has since expanded its ambitions from that narrow focus to a broader concept, with Trump extending invitations to some 50 nations and suggesting a role as a conflict mediator globally, according to a White House official.

## France leads European rejection

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot stated the position Tuesday: "Yes to implementing the peace plan presented by the U.S. president, which we support with all our heart, but no to creating an organization as it has been presented, which would replace the United Nations."

When informed Monday evening that French President Emmanuel Macron would not accept the invitation, Trump responded: "Well, nobody wants it because he's going to be out of office very soon."

Norway's State Secretary Kristoffer Thoner indicated his country would not join the board because "it raises a series of questions that require additional dialogue with the United States." Sweden's Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson signaled his country would not join the board as currently drafted, according to Swedish news agency TT, though Sweden has not formally responded to the invitation.

## Israel joins as other nations accept

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced Wednesday that he would join the board, a reversal from an earlier position when his office criticized the composition of the committee. Netanyahu's move could put him at odds with some of his far-right coalition allies. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich has criticized the board and called on Israel to unilaterally assume responsibility for Gaza's future.

Israel and Egypt, the two nations essential to the Gaza ceasefire, announced they would join the board, along with Bahrain, Kosovo, and Azerbaijan.

A White House official said some 30 countries would ultimately join the board but provided no details. The official, speaking on condition of anonymity to describe internal plans not yet made public, said some 50 countries had been invited.

Among those accepting invitations were the United Arab Emirates, Armenia, Morocco, Vietnam, Belarus, Hungary, Kazakhstan, and Argentina.

## Board composition and role

The board's executive committee for Gaza will be responsible for implementing the difficult second phase of the ceasefire agreement. This includes deploying an international security force, disarming the Palestinian militant group Hamas, and rebuilding territory devastated by the war. The committee will also oversee a newly appointed committee of Palestinian technocrats who will administer day-to-day affairs in the Gaza enclave.

The White House said the board's members include Trump envoy Steve Witkoff; Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner; former British Prime Minister Tony Blair; Apollo Global Management Chief Executive Marc Rowan; Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan; Qatari diplomat Ali Al-Thawadi; Hassan Rashad, director of Egypt's General Intelligence Agency; UAE Minister Reem Al-Hashimy; Israeli businessman Yakir Gabay; and Sigrid Kaag, former vice prime minister of the Netherlands and Middle East expert.

Nickolay Mladenov, a former Bulgarian politician and former U.N. envoy for the Middle East, will serve as the board's representative overseeing daily affairs.

## Humanitarian toll continues

Months after the ceasefire began in October, more than 2 million Palestinians in Gaza continue to suffer the humanitarian crisis from more than two years of war. Israeli fire in the Gaza enclave on Wednesday killed at least five Palestinians, including a woman and two children, according to Gaza's Health Ministry.

A man and his 13-year-old son were killed along with a 22-year-old man while collecting firewood in eastern Gaza's central Bureij refugee camp, according to Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital, which received the bodies. The hospital said Israeli drones had fired on the men. The Israeli military did not respond to a request for comment, and it was unclear whether the men had crossed into military-controlled zones.

A 13-year-old boy was shot and killed by troops while collecting firewood in the eastern town of Bani Suhaila, according to Nasser Hospital, which received the body. In a video circulating online, the boy's father was seen weeping over his son's body on a hospital bed.

Nasser Hospital also reported receiving the body of a woman shot by Israeli troops in the Muwasi area of the southern Khan Yunis. The hospital said she was killed in an area not controlled by the military.

Since the October ceasefire began, more than 460 Palestinians have died from Israeli fire, according to Gaza's Health Ministry. The ministry, part of the Hamas-led government, maintains detailed casualty records that United Nations agencies and independent experts regard as generally reliable. The majority of Gaza's population depends on firewood for cooking and heating, as the enclave experiences a cold, humid winter.
