Families in Gaza are digging through garbage with bare hands for plastic and paper to burn for warmth, desperate for fuel as winter deepens months into an October ceasefire. The scene on January 22 in Khan Younis contrasted sharply with the vision presented at the World Economic Forum in Davos, where President Trump inaugurated a Board of Peace to oversee Gaza's development and claimed that "record levels" of humanitarian aid had entered the territory.

Despite increased aid flows since the ceasefire, the humanitarian crisis persists—hundreds of thousands remain displaced in tents and damaged buildings, prices for fuel remain prohibitive, and Israeli military operations continue to kill Palestinians who venture out to find firewood.

## The desperate search for warmth

At a garbage dump in Khan Younis on January 22, families searched through refuse with bare hands for plastic and paper to burn. The winter wind carried the cold deeper into a territory where shelter remained scarce three months into a ceasefire that began in October.

Sanaa Salah, who lives in a tent with her husband and six children, described the daily necessity of starting a fire. "Life is very hard," she said as her family threw plastic and paper into a flame. "We cannot even have a cup of tea." She added: "This is our life. We do not sleep at night from the cold."

Aziz Akel, whose house was destroyed and whose children were wounded, said his family cannot afford firewood or gas at the market price of 7 or 8 shekels—roughly $2.50. His daughter Lina Akel said he leaves the family tent early each morning searching for plastic in the garbage. "It's the basics of life," she said.

## The vision and the reality

The scene contrasted sharply with statements made the same day at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, where Trump inaugurated a Board of Peace intended to oversee Gaza's reconstruction.

Trump claimed that "record levels" of humanitarian aid had entered Gaza since the October ceasefire began. Jared Kushner, Trump's son-in-law, and envoy Steve Witkoff presented the devastated territory's development potential to the assembled leaders.

But in Gaza, the situation remained dire. Hundreds of thousands of Palestinians remained displaced, sheltering in tents and damaged buildings unable to protect them from winter cold.

Some Palestinians expressed skepticism about the Board of Peace. Rami Ghalban, displaced from Khan Younis, said Thursday: "This committee includes Israelis. I don't understand, as citizens, how can we understand this situation? The Israelis that inflicted suffering upon us."

Fathi Abu Sultan took a more resigned view. "We are in a position where there are no alternatives," he said. "Our situation is miserable."

## Crisis persists: Scarcity and violence

Aid has increased since the ceasefire began, but the framework's benefit remains limited. Fuel and firewood remain scarce despite the higher flow of supplies. Prices remain prohibitive for most families. The United Nations said Thursday that its partners managing displacement camps can provide support to only about 40 percent of the existing 970 sites across Gaza because of capacity and funding constraints. The organizations continue to distribute tents, mattresses, sleeping bags, blankets, warm clothes, cooking utensils, and solar lights.

Searching for fuel has become dangerous. Two 13-year-old boys were shot and killed by Israeli forces on Wednesday as they attempted to collect firewood, according to hospital officials.

Despite the ceasefire, deadly Israeli military strikes have continued. Israeli tank shelling on Thursday killed four Palestinians east of Gaza City, according to Mohamed Abu Selmiya, director of Shifa Hospital, where the bodies were taken. The Israeli military did not immediately respond.

Three Palestinian journalists, including Abdul Raouf Shaat, a frequent contributor to Agence France-Presse, were killed on January 21 when an Israeli strike hit their vehicle near a displacement camp in central Gaza. The Israeli military said the strike came after it identified suspects operating a drone that posed a threat to its troops. AFP said Shaat was not on assignment for the news agency at the time and demanded a full investigation.

More than 470 people have been killed by Israeli fire in Gaza since the ceasefire began in October, according to Gaza's health ministry. At least 77 have been killed by Israeli gunfire near the ceasefire line dividing Israeli-held areas from the rest of Gaza's Palestinian population.

## International responses and next steps

The Rafah border crossing on the Gaza-Egypt boundary is scheduled to open in both directions next week, according to Ali Shaath, head of the future technocratic government in Gaza. Israel said in early December it would open the Gaza side but has not yet done so. The opening would allow Palestinians to seek medical treatment or visit family in Egypt.

Russian President Vladimir Putin said Thursday he wants to send $1 billion to the Board of Peace for humanitarian purposes in Gaza if the United States unblocks the money. Putin met with Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas in Moscow on January 22.

"We believe that only forming and proper functioning of the Palestinian state can lead to a final settlement of the Middle East conflict," Putin said.
