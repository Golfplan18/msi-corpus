Discount grocery chain Aldi said Tuesday it plans to open more than 180 U.S. stores in 2026 and will add distribution centers in Florida, Arizona, and Colorado, expanding its footprint as persistently high food prices push more Americans away from traditional supermarkets and toward lower-cost alternatives.

The announcement came the same day the U.S. Labor Department reported that grocery prices rose 0.7% in December from the previous month, and that price hikes accelerated faster in 2025 than in either of the previous two years. Food costs have soared roughly 25% since the pandemic, according to government data. Overall U.S. consumer prices rose 2.65% year-over-year as of December 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, but specific grocery categories have climbed far more steeply.

Beef and veal prices rose 1% from November and are up 16.4% from a year earlier. Coffee prices climbed 1.9% in December and are up nearly 20% over the past year. Egg prices dropped 8.2% in December, continuing to fall after surging following a bird flu outbreak.

The vast majority of U.S. adults say they have noticed higher than usual prices for groceries and electricity in recent months, according to a survey by The Associated Press-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research.

## Aldi's expansion timeline

The 2026 openings will bring Aldi's total U.S. store count to nearly 2,800 by year's end, the company said, moving it closer to its goal of 3,200 stores by 2028. The company, whose U.S. operations are based outside Chicago, announced in 2024 that it planned to open 800 new stores by 2028 and recorded a record 225 openings in 2025.

Aldi said it plans to open more than 50 stores in Colorado over the next five years and intends to double its Las Vegas store count by 2030. The company said it remains committed to investing $9 billion in the U.S. through 2028.

## A shift in shopping behavior

Aldi's growth reflects a broader change in how Americans shop for food. Families have been dropping trusted name brands in favor of cheaper store brands and switching from longtime supermarket chains to discount and thrift stores, a shift retailers call "trading down." National bargain chains including Dollar General and Dollar Tree have also benefited from this trend, according to the Associated Press.

The shift began before President Donald Trump's trade war, but appears to have accelerated over the past year, according to the Associated Press.

## Competition from multiple directions

Traditional grocers face pressure from bargain chains, large-format retailers like Walmart, and online competitors. In December, Amazon said it had expanded same-day perishable grocery delivery to more than 2,300 cities and towns and said it has further expansion plans for 2026.
