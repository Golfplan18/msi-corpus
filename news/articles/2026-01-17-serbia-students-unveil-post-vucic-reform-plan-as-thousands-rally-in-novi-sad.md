The Novi Sad rally marked a shift from reactive protest to forward-looking governance planning, with student organizers presenting a post-Vucic policy agenda to a movement that has collected approximately 400,000 signatures demanding early elections — which Vucic has refused to call — and has sustained mass demonstrations for more than a year.

Thousands of people rallied Saturday in Novi Sad, Serbia, as university students announced a new stage in their campaign against President Aleksandar Vucic, presenting a concrete policy agenda for a post-Vucic government.

Student organizers told the crowd they would offer a blueprint to rid Serbia of corruption and restore the rule of law. Among the first proposed steps: banning officials found guilty of corruption from running for office and conducting investigations into their accumulated wealth.

The Novi Sad demonstration, which organizers dubbed "What victory will mean," drew crowds chanting "thieves" in the northern city where a November 2024 train station collapse killed 16 people and ignited the nationwide protest movement. Students said they had collected approximately 400,000 signatures in support of their demand for early elections.

Vucic has refused to call immediate early elections. Hundreds of people have been detained, or have reported losing their jobs or facing workplace pressure for opposing the government, the Associated Press reported. Organizers announced the next major rally for January 27 in Belgrade, the capital.

## A movement born from disaster

The protest wave began after a canopy collapsed at the Novi Sad train station in November 2024, killing 16 people. Demonstrators have accused the government of rampant corruption that, they argue, contributed to the infrastructure failure.

Vucic came to power more than a decade ago on a pledge to take Serbia into the European Union. He has since strengthened ties with Russia and China, while facing accusations of curbing democratic freedoms and allowing corruption and organized crime to flourish.

## Students against a political establishment

The student movement has drawn broad support from Serbs disillusioned with mainstream politicians, according to the Associated Press. Vucic has dismissed the students as acting under unspecified Western orders intended to "destroy Serbia."

Saturday's rally underscored the movement's evolution from spontaneous grief over the train station deaths into an organized political force capable of mobilizing sustained mass participation and articulating a governing vision — while Vucic's government has yet to offer a formal response to the students' election demand.
