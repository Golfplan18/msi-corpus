Gunmen abducted 177 church worshippers from three churches in Nigeria's northwestern Kaduna state on Sunday, authorities and residents said Thursday. The attack in Kajuru council area represents one of the largest mass abductions targeting religious sites in Nigeria in recent months, with 11 of the abducted managing to escape. The attackers are demanding 17 motorcycles—valued at approximately $1,000 each or $17,000 total—from families of hostages as ransom to begin negotiations for their release.

The abductions are part of a wave of kidnappings plaguing Nigeria's northwestern region, where remote communities with limited state security presence remain vulnerable to rogue armed gangs that carry out abductions for profit.

## Ransom demands and escape

The attackers contacted families on Thursday with their demand: 17 motorcycles to begin negotiations, residents told the Associated Press. Ishaku Dan'azumi, village head of Kurmin Wali, said the kidnappers made their position clear.

"They (abductors) said that they want 17 motorcycles (and) have not told us yet that they need money," Dan'azumi said.

Sebastine Barde, president of the Adara Development Association, explained the kidnappers' motivation. The attackers said they needed the vehicles to replace ones they had lost during operations in the region. Motorcycles allow gunmen to move swiftly through the forest reserves that blanket the remote areas, making pursuit by security forces more difficult.

## Security response

Nigerian security forces have deployed tactical teams to search for the kidnappers and locate the hostages. Kaduna Governor Uba Sani visited the affected areas in Kajuru on late Wednesday to assess the situation firsthand.

"Our administration will continue to pursue peace, security and inclusive development," the governor said.

The kidnappings follow a broader pattern of mass abductions in Nigeria's northwestern region. A week earlier, on January 4, gunmen in Niger State killed at least 30 people and abducted others in a village raid. The region's geography—vast forest reserves with limited government presence—has become a haven for rogue criminal gangs who finance operations through ransom abductions.

## International tensions

The kidnappings occur amid strained relations between Nigeria and the United States over religious violence. In recent months, the U.S. government has accused Nigeria's government of failing to protect Christians during the country's security crisis. Officials in Abuja have pushed back on that characterization, noting that attacks affect both Christian and Muslim communities.

The tensions escalated when the U.S. launched a military operation against alleged Islamic State group members on Nigerian territory in December. The Nigerian government said it was aware of the operation at the time.
