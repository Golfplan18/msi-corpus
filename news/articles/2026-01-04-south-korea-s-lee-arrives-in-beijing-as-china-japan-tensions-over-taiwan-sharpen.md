The summit gives Lee an opportunity to stabilize South Korea's ties with its largest trading partner at a moment of heightened regional pressure. On Sunday, North Korea also launched multiple ballistic missiles toward the sea, South Korea's military said, adding urgency to Korean Peninsula security discussions Seoul plans to raise with Beijing.

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung arrived in Beijing on Sunday for a four-day visit that will include a summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping, his first trip to China since taking office in June. China's state news agency Xinhua reported Lee's arrival in Beijing Sunday afternoon.

The visit comes as tensions between China and Japan over Taiwan, a self-governed island that Beijing claims as its sovereign territory, have sharpened following Chinese military exercises near the island and a Japanese prime minister's public statement about possible military involvement.

## Lee signals commitment to Beijing ties

Ahead of his departure, Lee gave an interview to China's state broadcaster CCTV at the presidential Blue House in Seoul. "This visit to China aims to minimize or eliminate these past misunderstandings or contradictions, to elevate and develop South Korea-China relations to a new stage," CCTV quoted Lee as saying.

Lee said South Korea consistently respects the "One-China" policy on Taiwan and called Xi "a truly reliable neighbor," according to CCTV. He also said Seoul's military alliance with the United States does not mean South Korea-China relations should move toward confrontation, CCTV reported, and acknowledged that past misunderstandings between the two countries had hindered bilateral relations.

## Summit schedule

South Korea's national security adviser Wi Sung-lac said Monday's summit will include "in-depth talks on substantive ways" to address security and economic issues facing the two countries. On Tuesday, Lee is to meet Zhao Leji, chairman of the National People's Congress, and Premier Li Qiang, Wi told reporters Friday.

The meetings mark Lee and Xi's second face-to-face encounter in two months.

South Korea will also request that China play "a constructive role" in promoting peace on the Korean Peninsula, Wi said. In their November talks, Lee already asked Xi to make greater efforts to persuade North Korea to return to negotiations.

## Regional pressure as backdrop

The visit unfolds against a backdrop of rising military pressure in the region. Last week, China staged large-scale military drills around Taiwan for two days. The Chinese Foreign Ministry said the exercises were intended to warn against what it described as separatist forces and "external interference." In December, planned U.S. arms sales to Taiwan prompted Beijing to sanction 20 U.S. defense-related companies.

Japan has also drawn Beijing's attention. Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi said in November that Japan's military could get involved if China were to take action against Taiwan — a statement that has added to existing strains between the two neighbors.

On Sunday, North Korea launched multiple ballistic missiles toward the sea, South Korea's military said, the latest weapons test ahead of the North's upcoming ruling party congress. South Korea and the United States have urged China, which serves as North Korea's main ally and economic lifeline, to use its influence to persuade Pyongyang to return to denuclearization talks or abandon its nuclear program.

South Korean officials said Lee's visit is intended to strengthen bilateral economic ties and to discuss ways to restore peace on the Korean Peninsula.
