The disappearance of the Indonesia Air Transport flight, which was carrying government maritime surveillance personnel in addition to its crew, adds to a pattern of transportation accidents in the archipelago nation, which depends on air and sea links to connect more than 17,000 islands.

JAKARTA, Indonesia — Indonesian search teams recovered one body Sunday from a ravine approximately 200 meters (about 660 feet) deep on the slope of Mount Bulusaraung on Sulawesi island, a day after an ATR 42-500 turboprop carrying 11 people vanished from radar during its approach to Makassar.

The plane, operated by Indonesia Air Transport, disappeared from radar Saturday afternoon shortly after air traffic controllers instructed the crew to correct its approach alignment, said Muhammad Arif Anwar, who heads Makassar's Search and Rescue Office. The aircraft was flying from Yogyakarta, on Indonesia's main island of Java, to Makassar, the capital of South Sulawesi province.

Eight crew members and three passengers from the Marine Affairs and Fisheries Ministry were aboard, conducting an airborne maritime surveillance mission, Anwar said.

Search teams found scattered debris near the recovered body, including parts of the aircraft frame and passenger seats. They also visually identified what is believed to be the turboprop's engine.

Additional bodies were spotted on a steep cliff, but crews had not been able to reach them before the search was suspended at nightfall, said Brig. Gen. Andre Clift Rumbayan, who leads the specialized search units. The operation was set to resume Monday morning.

Strong winds, heavy fog, and steep rugged terrain impeded the effort, said Maj. Gen. Bangun Nawoko, South Sulawesi's military commander. Visibility at the summit was approximately 5 meters (about 16 feet).

Indonesia relies on air transport and ferries to connect its more than 17,000 islands. The Southeast Asian nation has sustained a series of transportation accidents in recent years, from plane and bus crashes to ferry sinkings.
