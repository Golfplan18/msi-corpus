The accusations deepen a prolonged deterioration in Pakistan-Afghanistan relations that already produced cross-border airstrikes and a Qatar-brokered ceasefire last October, and come as Pakistan reported a near-doubling of militant attacks on its own territory in 2025.

ISLAMABAD — Pakistan's military warned Tuesday that Afghanistan is becoming a "hub for terrorists and non-state actors," accusing the Taliban government of patronizing al-Qaida, the Islamic State group and the Pakistani Taliban known as Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan.

Military spokesman Lt. Gen. Ahmad Sharif Chaudhry, speaking at a news conference, also alleged — without offering evidence — that about 2,500 foreign militants had recently entered Afghanistan from Syria following the December 2024 ouster of former President Bashar Assad. Chaudhry asserted that the militants were invited to Afghanistan.

"These terrorists are neither Pakistanis nor Afghan citizens and belong to other nationalities," Chaudhry said, adding that the reemergence of international militant groups could pose security risks beyond Afghanistan's borders.

## Taliban rejects the accusations

In Kabul, Taliban spokesman Zabihullah Mujahid dismissed the claims as "irresponsible and provocative" and urged Pakistan to avoid "unfounded propaganda."

"Afghanistan is an independent and stable country with a strong security system and decisive leadership that exercises full sovereignty over all its territory," Mujahid said, asking Pakistan to focus on its own internal challenges.

Chaudhry separately described the Afghan Taliban as an armed group that had "occupied Afghanistan" rather than a legitimate governing authority.

## Joint Pakistan-China pressure

Chaudhry's remarks came a day after Pakistan and China called for more "visible and verifiable" measures to eliminate militant organizations operating from Afghan territory and to prevent Afghanistan from being used to plan or carry out attacks against other countries.

Relations between Pakistan and Afghanistan have deteriorated sharply in recent months, with tensions occasionally producing violence. In October, Pakistan carried out airstrikes on what it described as Pakistani Taliban hideouts inside Afghanistan. Kabul retaliated by targeting Pakistani military posts. Qatar brokered a ceasefire to end the fighting.

Pakistan has long accused Afghanistan and India of backing Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan and the outlawed Baloch National Army. Both Kabul and New Delhi deny those allegations.

## Surge in militant violence inside Pakistan

Chaudhry said Pakistan killed 2,597 militants in 2025, up from 1,053 in 2024. The country also recorded 5,397 militant attacks in 2025, compared with 3,014 in 2024.

"Yes, this is a big number," Chaudhry said of the 2025 attack total. "Why? Because we are engaging them everywhere."

He added that Afghan nationals were involved in almost all major attacks inside Pakistan last year.

## Syria fighters in the region

Chaudhry's unsubstantiated claim about Syrian fighters came against the backdrop of Syria's nearly 14-year civil war, which ended with Assad's ouster in December 2024 but left behind a patchwork of armed groups shaped by years of foreign intervention. Fighters from Syria have since taken part in conflicts elsewhere in the region, including Turkish-backed combatants sent to Libya and militants recruited by Russia to fight in Ukraine.
