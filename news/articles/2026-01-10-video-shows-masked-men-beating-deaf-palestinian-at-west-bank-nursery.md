The attack is the latest in what rights groups describe as an escalating pattern of Israeli settler violence across the West Bank, where assaults have continued since intensifying during the Palestinian olive harvest in October. Israeli authorities have made limited arrests, and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has characterized the perpetrators as a fringe group, a description rights organizations dispute.

Security camera footage shows dozens of masked men attacking and beating Basim Saleh Yassin, a 67-year-old deaf Palestinian man, at a plant nursery in the northern West Bank village of Deir Sharaf on Thursday, according to two witnesses and video obtained by the Associated Press.

Yassin, who could not hear warnings from coworkers to flee because he is deaf, was left in the hospital with broken bones in his hand and injuries to his face, chest and back, the witnesses said. Four cars at the German-Palestinian-run nursery were also burned.

Two witnesses who are members of the family that owns the facility said Israeli settlers carried out the attack. Both spoke on condition of anonymity for fear of reprisal.

The video shows Yassin running from a group of masked men before falling to the ground. One man kicks him and another strikes him twice with what appears to be a stick. As the men leave, one kicks him in the head while others strike him again.

## Israeli military response

Israel's army said it dispatched soldiers to the Shavei Shomron junction — close to the area of the attack — following reports of dozens of masked Israelis vandalizing property. The army said it apprehended three suspects who were taken to police for questioning, and that security forces "condemn violence of any kind."

According to one family member who owns the nursery, it was the third attack on the facility in a year. A previous incident in September caused more than $600,000 in damage to offices and facilities, he said.

## Pattern of violence

Settler violence in the West Bank increased during the Palestinian olive harvest in October and has continued, according to the AP. Rights groups and Palestinians say attacks have become a daily phenomenon across the territory. Israeli authorities have done little beyond issuing occasional condemnations, according to the report.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has called the perpetrators "a handful of extremists" and urged law enforcement to pursue them "for the attempt to take the law into their own hands." Rights groups say the problem is more widespread than Netanyahu's characterization suggests.

## Settlement expansion

Israel captured the West Bank, east Jerusalem and Gaza in the 1967 war and has since settled over 500,000 Jews in the West Bank. Settlements are widely considered illegal under international law.

Last month, Israel's Cabinet approved a proposal for 19 new Jewish settlements in the West Bank. Israel has also cleared the final hurdle before starting construction on a settlement project near Jerusalem that would effectively cut the West Bank in two, according to a government tender reported this month.

## Gaza

Hospitals in the Gaza Strip reported at least two Palestinians killed in separate incidents. A man was killed in an Israeli strike in the Zaytoun neighborhood of eastern Gaza City, according to al-Ahly Hospital. A Palestinian was killed by Israeli gunfire near the southern city of Khan Younis, according to Nasser Hospital. Israel's military said its forces killed two individuals who had approached troops in northern Gaza and one in southern Gaza.

A ceasefire in Gaza has held since taking effect on Oct. 10. Mediators have been working to move Israel and Hamas into a second phase of the agreement, which covers Gaza's security and future governance. Both sides have accused each other of violations.
