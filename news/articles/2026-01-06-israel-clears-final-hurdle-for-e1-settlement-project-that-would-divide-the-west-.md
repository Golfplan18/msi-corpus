JERUSALEM — Israel published a government tender Tuesday clearing the way for construction of the contentious E1 settlement project near Jerusalem, a step that anti-settlement monitors say could trigger initial work within weeks and that critics argue would effectively cut the West Bank in two, foreclosing the possibility of a contiguous Palestinian state.

The tender, posted publicly on Israel's Land Authority website, solicits bids from developers to build 3,401 housing units on an open tract of land east of Jerusalem. The anti-settlement monitoring group Peace Now first reported the document's publication. Yoni Mizrahi, who oversees the group's settlement watch division, said initial construction work could begin within the month.

The E1 project has been under consideration for more than two decades but was frozen under U.S. diplomatic pressure during previous administrations. The international community overwhelmingly considers Israeli settlement construction in the West Bank to be illegal and an obstacle to peace.

The project runs from the outskirts of Jerusalem deep into the occupied West Bank. Critics say it would prevent the establishment of a contiguous Palestinian state in the territory.

Israeli Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, a far-right politician who oversees settlement policy, has been the project's most prominent champion. When Israel gave final formal approval to the E1 plan in August, he said: "The Palestinian state is being erased from the table not with slogans but with actions. Every settlement, every neighborhood, every housing unit is another nail in the coffin of this dangerous idea."

Peace Now said the publication of the tender "reflects an accelerated effort to advance construction in E1."

## Israel-Syria security talks in Paris

In a separate development Tuesday, Syrian and Israeli officials met in Paris for U.S.-mediated talks intended to broker a security agreement between the two countries. A joint statement issued after the meeting said discussions centered on Syrian sovereignty, Israeli security, and economic cooperation. The two sides agreed to establish a joint communication cell to coordinate intelligence sharing and military de-escalation, the statement said.

Syria's new interim leadership came to power in December 2024, when insurgents led by Ahmad al-Sharaa ousted longtime leader Bashar Assad in a rapid offensive. Israel subsequently seized a U.N.-patrolled buffer zone in southern Syria and conducted hundreds of airstrikes on Syrian military facilities. Syrian officials have said their priority is the withdrawal of Israeli forces. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office said Israel emphasized the importance of security for its citizens and of protecting the Druze minority in Syria.

## Gaza: food supply and NGO access

The United Nations said Tuesday that aid groups have enough food on hand to sustain people in Gaza for the first time since the war began more than two years ago. U.N. spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric said the January round was "the first since October 2023 in which partners had sufficient stock to meet 100% of the minimum caloric standard."

Dujarric's statement came against a backdrop of mounting access restrictions. Israel has revoked the operating licenses of more than three dozen organizations in Gaza, including Doctors Without Borders, the Norwegian Refugee Council, and Oxfam.

Kaja Kallas, the European Union's foreign policy chief, called on Israel Tuesday to lift the restrictions to avert deaths from exposure, hunger, and lack of medicines. "To deliver aid rapidly, safely and at the scale required, international NGOs must be able to operate in a sustained and predictable way," Kallas said in a statement from the 27-nation bloc.

## Raid at Birzeit University

The Palestinian Red Crescent said 11 people were injured during an Israeli military raid Tuesday at Birzeit University in the West Bank. Talal Shahwan, the university's president, said at a press conference that about 20 Israeli military vehicles stormed the campus gate and entered the grounds.

"Unfortunately, targeting the university is a recurring event," Shahwan said, adding that the forces displayed "clear brutality."

Israeli officials said military and border troops were deployed to break up an anticipated gathering and encountered a crowd of hundreds of people, some of whom they said threw rocks from rooftops. Officials said they used targeted fire toward the "main violent individuals."

## Journalists barred from Gaza

The Foreign Press Association, which represents major international media organizations including the Associated Press, on Tuesday criticized Israel's continued refusal to allow foreign journalists into Gaza despite a three-month ceasefire. The ban has been in effect since the war began in October 2023. The FPA has asked Israel's Supreme Court to end the restriction; the Israeli government told the court this week it remains opposed to foreign journalist access, citing security concerns.
