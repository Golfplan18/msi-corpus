President Donald Trump on Tuesday criticized the United Kingdom's agreement to transfer sovereignty of the Chagos Islands to Mauritius, calling the plan an act of stupidity that demonstrated the need for the United States to acquire Greenland. The attack marked a reversal from the U.S. government's prior support for the deal and heightened tensions with Prime Minister Keir Starmer's administration over Trump's territorial ambitions in the Arctic and Indian Ocean.

The confrontation escalates tensions as Trump reasserts pressure on European allies and the United States moves toward a more expansionist foreign policy posture.

## Trump's Criticism

Trump posted to Truth Social on Tuesday with criticism of the UK's agreement to transfer the Chagos Islands to Mauritius. "Shockingly, our 'brilliant' NATO Ally, the United Kingdom, is currently planning to give away the Island of Diego Garcia, the site of a vital U.S. Military Base, to Mauritius, and to do so FOR NO REASON WHATSOEVER," he wrote. "There is no doubt that China and Russia have noticed this act of total weakness."

He continued: "The UK giving away extremely important land is an act of GREAT STUPIDITY, and is another in a very long line of National Security reasons why Greenland has to be acquired."

The posts marked a reversal from the administration's earlier support for the deal. The U.S. government had previously stated that the agreement "secures the long-term, stable, and effective operation of the joint U.S.-U.K. military facility at Diego Garcia."

## The Chagos Agreement

The United Kingdom and Mauritius signed the sovereignty-transfer agreement in May 2025 after two centuries of British control. Under the deal, Britain will pay Mauritius at least 120 million pounds ($160 million) annually to lease Diego Garcia, the island hosting the U.S. military base, for at least 99 years.

The agreement came after United Nations pressure on Britain to return the islands to Mauritius. British officials said the arrangement protects the security of the base from international legal challenges.

A British government spokesperson said Tuesday that "the U.K. will never compromise on our national security" and that the deal "secures the operations of the joint U.S.-U.K. base on Diego Garcia for generations, with robust provisions for keeping its unique capabilities intact and our adversaries out."

## Political Response

Trump's attack prompted interpretation from British officials that the president was venting frustration over European resistance to his Greenland ambitions rather than expressing genuine concern about Chagos.

Cabinet Minister Pat McFadden said: "I don't really believe this is about Chagos. I think it's about Greenland."

Opposition figures in Parliament, however, agreed with Trump's criticism. Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch said in a post that Trump was correct, describing the agreement as "a terrible policy that weakens UK security and hands away our sovereign territory."

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage, an ally of Trump, stated: "Thank goodness Trump has vetoed the surrender of the Chagos islands."

The agreement faced parliamentary resistance; while the House of Commons approved it, the House of Lords passed it only alongside a "motion of regret" expressing opposition.

Prime Minister Keir Starmer called Trump's statements about acquiring Greenland "completely wrong" and urged that tensions be "resolved through calm discussion."

## Strategic and Historical Context

Diego Garcia hosts approximately 2,500 military personnel, mostly American. The U.S. military has described the base as "an all but indispensable platform" for security operations in the Middle East, South Asia, and East Africa.

The Chagos Islands have been under British control since 1814, when France ceded them. Britain separated the islands from Mauritius in 1965 to facilitate U.S. military construction, displacing as many as 2,000 residents.

An estimated 10,000 displaced Chagossians and their descendants now live primarily in Britain, Mauritius, and the Seychelles. Some have fought in U.K. courts for decades for the right to return to their homes. The sovereignty-transfer agreement includes provisions for creating a resettlement fund to assist displaced islanders in relocating to the islands, though Diego Garcia itself would remain unavailable to them.
