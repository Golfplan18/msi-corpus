Indonesian authorities raised the alert level for Mount Bur Ni Telong volcano in Aceh province to its second-highest level Tuesday evening, as more than 2,500 residents were evacuated from four villages most at risk, officials said. The spike in seismic activity at the 2,624-meter stratovolcano in Bener Meriah regency follows months of intensifying underground unrest.

The heightened alert compounds an already dire humanitarian situation: the Bener Meriah area is still recovering from catastrophic floods and landslides earlier this month that killed at least 1,154 people across 52 cities and regencies on Sumatra island, with 165 residents still missing and more than 7,000 injured, according to the National Disaster Management Agency.

## Seismic activity

Seismographs recorded at least seven earthquakes felt roughly 5 kilometers from the crater on Tuesday evening, along with seven shallow volcanic earthquakes, 14 deep quakes, and two tectonic quakes, said Lana Saria, the acting head of the Geological Agency at the Energy and Mineral Resources Ministry.

"Aftershocks following local tectonic events indicate magma activity is easily triggered by tectonic disturbances," Saria said. She added that the increase in seismic activity has been ongoing since July and became more intense and shallow in the past two months.

Visual monitoring showed the volcano clearly visible with no crater smoke. Scientists nonetheless warned of possible eruption, including phreatic blasts and hazardous volcanic gases near fumarole and solfatara zones — openings in the Earth's crust that emit steam and gases.

## Evacuations and safety restrictions

Authorities urged residents and visitors to stay at least 4 kilometers from the crater and to avoid fumarole and solfatara zones during cloudy or rainy weather, warning that gas concentrations in those conditions can be life-threatening.

Tagore Abubakar, the regent of Bener Meriah, said authorities fear that heavy rains combined with volcanic activity could worsen conditions and complicate evacuation efforts. Some residents chose to shelter with relatives in the neighboring Central Aceh regency rather than use government facilities.

## A region still reeling from flooding

In Bener Meriah regency alone, 31 people died and 14 remain missing from the earlier floods and landslides, which disrupted access to remote villages and displaced more than 2,100 residents.

The combination of lingering flood damage and an active volcanic threat leaves emergency managers contending with overlapping crises in the same mountainous terrain.

## Indonesia's volcanic landscape

Indonesia sits along the Ring of Fire, a horseshoe-shaped series of seismic fault lines encircling the Pacific Ocean. The country, an archipelago of more than 280 million people, has more than 120 active volcanoes.
