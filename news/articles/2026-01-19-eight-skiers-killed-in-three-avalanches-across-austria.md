Three avalanches killed eight skiers across Austria on Saturday, authorities said, as slides struck mountain terrain near Salzburg and in central Austria within hours of each other.

The deaths came despite what rescue officials described as clear and repeated prior warnings about elevated avalanche risk in Austrian mountain regions.

The first avalanche struck the Bad Hofgastein area in western Austria at approximately 12:30 p.m., killing a female skier at an altitude of about 2,200 meters (7,200 feet), according to the Pongau mountain rescue service.

Roughly 90 minutes later, a second avalanche swept through the nearby Gastein Valley, south of the city of Salzburg, carrying away seven people. Four were killed, two suffered serious injuries, and one escaped unharmed, authorities said.

In Pusterwald, a town in central Austria, a third avalanche killed three Czech skiers shortly before 4:30 p.m., police said. Four of their companions were evacuated to safety.

"This tragedy painfully demonstrates how serious the current avalanche situation is," said Gerhard Kremser, district head of the Pongau mountain rescue service, citing "clear and repeated warnings" about avalanche risk that had been issued in advance of the incidents.
