BEIRUT — Officials from Syria and Israel were set to resume U.S.-mediated talks in Paris on Tuesday, with Damascus seeking to reactivate a 1974 disengagement agreement and secure the withdrawal of Israeli forces from a United Nations-patrolled buffer zone Israel seized more than a year ago, two officials said Monday.

Syria's delegation will be headed by Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shibani and Hussein Salameh, head of the General Intelligence Directorate, a Syrian official told the Associated Press on condition of anonymity because he was not authorized to speak publicly.

The renewed talks come after negotiations stalled last year, with Syria pressing for Israeli withdrawal as a condition of reasserting full sovereignty over its southern territory and Israel maintaining a presence it describes as temporary but open-ended.

## Syrian aims

Syria's central objective in the talks is to reactivate the 1974 disengagement agreement that established the buffer zone and to secure Israeli withdrawal, the Syrian official said.

In the new round of discussions, Damascus will seek "the withdrawal of Israeli forces to the lines prior to Dec. 8, 2024, within the framework of a reciprocal security agreement that prioritizes full Syrian sovereignty and guarantees the prevention of any form of interference in the country's internal affairs," the official said.

## French role

A French diplomat confirmed that the Syria-Israel talks would take place Tuesday in Paris, with the United States mediating. France's foreign minister also met with his Syrian counterpart Monday evening, the diplomat said. The diplomat spoke on condition of anonymity because he was not authorized to give details about the discussions publicly.

## Background: Assad's fall and Israel's moves

In December 2024, insurgents led by Syria's now interim President Ahmad al-Sharaa ousted Assad in a lightning offensive, ending his family's decades-long grip on the country.

Israel was suspicious of the new Islamist-led leadership and quickly moved to take control of the buffer zone. It has since launched hundreds of airstrikes on Syrian military facilities and conducted periodic incursions into villages outside the buffer zone, which have at times led to violent confrontations with residents.

Israel has said its presence is temporary — aimed at clearing out pro-Assad remnants and militants to protect Israel from attacks — but has given no indication its forces would leave anytime soon.

Al-Sharaa has said he has no desire for a conflict with Israel.

Israeli officials did not respond to requests for comment on the talks. A spokesperson for Tom Barrack, the U.S. envoy to Syria, declined to comment.
