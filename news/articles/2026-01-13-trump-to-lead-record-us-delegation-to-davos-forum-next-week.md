The Davos meeting marks Trump's third in-person attendance as U.S. president. His delegation will include five Cabinet secretaries and other senior officials at a forum that, for the first time, will not be chaired by its founder, Klaus Schwab, who resigned last year.

President Donald Trump will attend the World Economic Forum's annual meeting in Davos, Switzerland, next week, leading what organizers said Tuesday is the largest-ever U.S. delegation to the gathering. The event runs Jan. 19 through Jan. 23 at the Alpine resort and is expected to draw 3,000 participants from 130 countries, including a record 64 heads of state or government.

Forum President Borge Brende said the U.S. delegation will include Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, and U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, along with Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner and special envoy Steve Witkoff — representing five Cabinet secretaries and other senior officials in total.

## Record attendance expected

Six of the Group of Seven leading industrial nations, including the United States, will have their leaders present, Brende said. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Syrian President Ahmad al-Sharaa, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres, and World Trade Organization Director-General Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala are among the other high-profile attendees confirmed.

The 64 heads of state or government expected so far also marks a record, Brende said, though that figure could rise before the forum opens. The 3,000 expected participants include 850 chief executives and chairs of major companies, the forum said.

China's delegation will be led by Vice Premier He Lifeng, Beijing's top trade official, according to forum officials.

Tech industry leaders also figure prominently on the attendance list. Jensen Huang, chief executive of Nvidia, and Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella are among those expected, the forum said.

## New leadership at the helm

The 2026 meeting is the first annual session not chaired by forum founder Klaus Schwab, who resigned last year. Larry Fink, chairman and chief executive of the New York-based investment management firm BlackRock, and Andre Hoffmann, vice chairman of Swiss pharmaceuticals company Roche Holdings, are serving as interim co-chairs.

## Context

Trump has attended the forum twice before as president and was beamed in by video in January 2025, days after his second inauguration. The World Economic Forum held its first annual meeting in 1971 and has long served as a venue for dialogue and deal-making among business, political, and cultural figures.

The forum says its stated goal is "improving the state of the world" and that advocacy groups, academics, and cultural leaders play an important role alongside government and business delegations. Critics have described the gathering as a venue for elites to conduct business that can sometimes come at the expense of workers and people on the margins of society, the Associated Press reported.
