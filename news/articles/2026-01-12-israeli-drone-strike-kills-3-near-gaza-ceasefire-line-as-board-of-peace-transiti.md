The strike came as Gaza's ceasefire — agreed last October after more than two years of war — remained in its initial phase, with the Gaza Health Ministry reporting more than 440 people killed since the agreement and the governance arrangements intended to replace Hamas's administration still unsettled.

An Israeli drone strike killed three Palestinians on Monday near the Morag corridor in central Gaza, hospital officials said, as the territory awaited an expected announcement of a U.S.-backed governing body to oversee its post-war administration.

Israel's military said the three had crossed the ceasefire line, approached troops, and posed an immediate threat. Soldiers later found weapons and intelligence-gathering equipment on them, the military said.

## Governance transition still taking shape

The strike came as Gaza awaited an expected announcement this week of a "Board of Peace," the body that would supervise Gaza's new Palestinian government under the Trump administration's peace plan. Under the plan, the board would also oversee the disarmament of Hamas, the deployment of an international security force, additional pullbacks of Israeli troops, and reconstruction. Officials said Trump would announce his appointments to the board in the coming days. The United States had reported little progress on any of these fronts so far.

Hamas said it will dissolve its existing government once the new Palestinian technocratic committee takes control, as mandated under the U.S.-brokered peace plan. Hamas and the rival Palestinian Authority had not announced the names of who would sit on the committee as of Monday, and it remained unclear whether candidates would be cleared by Israel and the United States.

Hamas spokesperson Hazem Kassem, in a Telegram post on Sunday, called for faster establishment of the Palestinian technocratic committee set to govern Gaza.

## Ceasefire under strain

The Gaza Health Ministry reported that more than 440 people have been killed since Israel and Hamas agreed last October to suspend their two-year war. Each side has accused the other of violating the ceasefire, which remained in its initial stage as efforts continued to recover the remains of the final Israeli hostage in Gaza.

Israel's military controls a buffer zone covering more than half of Gaza, while the Hamas-run government retains authority over the rest.

Also on Monday, Lt. Col. Mahmoud al-Astal, a senior Hamas police officer, was killed in the Muwasi area of Khan Younis, the Hamas-run Interior Ministry said. An Israel-supported armed group in southern Gaza claimed responsibility for the shooting. Throughout the war, Israel has supported anti-Hamas groups in the territory, the Associated Press reported.

## Diplomatic talks on second stage

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan participated on Monday in a video conference with U.S., Egyptian, and Qatari officials to discuss preparations for the second stage of the ceasefire agreement, according to Turkish officials. The talks were held as a continuation of a meeting in Miami at the end of December.

## Prisoner deaths, protests

Dozens of Palestinians, including medical workers, held a protest in Gaza City on Monday outside the International Committee of the Red Cross building, demanding the release of thousands of Palestinian prisoners still held in Israeli prisons. The protest was organized by the Palestinian Prisoners Committee.

Palestinian prisoner groups announced Sunday that Hamza Abdullah Abdelhadi Adwan, 67, a father of nine with serious health problems, died in an Israeli prison on September 9, based on information his family received from the Israeli military. Adwan had been detained at a checkpoint on November 12, 2024. Two of his children were killed in the Gaza war.

Israel's military said Monday that its police force would investigate the death and send findings to the military prosecutor's office for review.

The Palestinian Prisoners' Affairs Commission reported that 87 Palestinian detainees have died in Israeli prisons since the start of the war, including 51 from Gaza. Palestinian prisoner groups said more than 100 detainees — some not yet identified — had allegedly died of torture, starvation, medical neglect, and abuse.
