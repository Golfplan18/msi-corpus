The crash highlights transportation safety concerns in South Africa, where minibus services provide school transport for many students. The driver's upgraded charges reflect authorities' determination that his conduct reflected intent to kill rather than negligence alone.

A school bus carrying 16 students collided with a truck in South Africa's Gauteng province on Monday morning, killing 12 of the children at the scene. Seven people were injured in the collision—five students, the bus driver, and a passenger in the truck. Three of the injured were discharged from the hospital, while two students remain hospitalized, according to authorities.

## Rising Death Toll

Two more teenage students died Thursday from injuries sustained in the collision, raising the death toll to 14. One injured student was in intensive care at Sebokeng Hospital when she died Thursday morning, the Gauteng health department said. Another female student died at a private hospital.

## Criminal Charges

The minibus driver, Ayanda Dludla, 22, was initially slated to face several counts of culpable homicide—the South African equivalent of negligent homicide. However, the National Prosecuting Authority upgraded the charges Thursday as two more of the injured students succumbed to their wounds. Dludla now faces 14 counts of murder, three counts of attempted murder, and a charge of driving without a professional license, according to National Prosecuting Authority spokesperson Lumka Mahanjana.

## Court Appearance

Dludla appeared before the Vanderbijlpark Magistrate's Court on Thursday, where he declined to seek bail. The case was adjourned until March 5 for further investigation.
