Hundreds of thousands of Bangladeshis gathered Wednesday in Dhaka for funeral prayers for former Prime Minister Khaleda Zia, who died a day earlier at age 80 after a prolonged illness, with dignitaries from India, Pakistan, Nepal and Bhutan joining the national mourning.

The prayers were held at Manik Mia Avenue outside the country's national parliament building. People from Dhaka and surrounding areas arrived early; witnesses said many cried, calling Zia their "mother" as they reached the venue. Some had traveled overnight from rural areas. In neighborhoods kilometers away, crowds spilled into major streets, raising their hands together in prayer.

Zia's death ends a 41-year political career that made her Bangladesh's first female prime minister. Her passing comes as the country prepares for elections in February, with the Bangladesh Nationalist Party — now led by her son Tarique Rahman — positioned as the front-runner.

## Regional attendance

Dignitaries from India, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Nepal traveled to Dhaka for the services. Local media reported that foreign envoys and representatives from 32 countries attended the ceremony.

India's External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar, after arriving in Dhaka, met Zia's elder son Tarique Rahman and delivered a personal letter from Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Pakistan's National Assembly Speaker Sardar Ayaz Sadiq, Nepal's Foreign Minister Bala Nanda Sharma and Bhutan's Minister for Foreign Affairs and External Trade Lyonpo D. N. Dhungyel also attended to show their respect.

## State honors

Bangladesh's interim government, headed by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Muhammad Yunus, declared a three-day national mourning period and designated Wednesday a public holiday. Flags flew at half-staff across the country.

Zia was buried in the late afternoon with state honors. Soldiers formed an honor guard and saluted as her family members stood nearby. She was interred beside the grave of her husband, a former president who was assassinated in a military coup in 1981, in a park outside the parliament building. Authorities said about 10,000 security officials, including soldiers, were deployed around the venue to maintain order.

## Political career and context

Zia entered politics after her husband's death and rose to prominence as an opposition leader during a nine-year movement against a former military dictator ousted in a mass uprising in 1990. She became prime minister for the first time in 1991 after a landslide victory as Bangladesh introduced parliamentary democracy, and served two full terms as well as a third brief term. She led the Bangladesh Nationalist Party until her death.

Zia maintained a long-running political rivalry with former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina, who headed the Bangladesh Awami League and ruled the country for 15 years before being ousted in a mass uprising in 2024. Hasina has been in exile in India since August 5, 2024. In November, she was sentenced to death on charges of crimes against humanity related to that uprising.

Rahman, Zia's son and the acting head of her party, is the front-runner ahead of Bangladesh's next elections, scheduled for February.
