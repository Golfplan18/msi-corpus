President Donald Trump has enlisted golf champion Jack Nicklaus to overhaul the Courses at Joint Base Andrews — the military facility about 15 miles from the White House long known as the "president's golf course" — in what the White House described as the most significant renovation in the courses' history.

Plans are in early stages, and the cost of — and funding for — the project have not been determined, the White House said. Trump said only that it will require "very little money."

"President Trump is a champion-level golfer with an extraordinary eye for detail and design," White House spokesman Davis Ingle said in a statement. "His vision to renovate and beautify Joint Base Andrews' golf courses will bring much-needed improvements that service members and their families will be able to enjoy for generations to come."

The Andrews project adds to a broad set of construction initiatives Trump has pursued since returning to office, and comes the same week the administration separately ended a lease with a nonprofit managing three public golf courses in Washington.

## A tour, but no recorded rounds

Trump toured the base by helicopter with Nicklaus before Thanksgiving. Trump called Andrews "a great place, that's been destroyed over the years, through lack of maintenance." Other golfers, however, describe the courses as in good shape despite some dry patches; online reviews praise the facility's mature trees, water hazards, and tricky roughs.

The White House and base have no record of Trump playing the courses, despite prior visits to the base. As of Jan. 2, Trump had spent an estimated 93 days of his second term golfing, according to an Associated Press analysis of his schedules — primarily at courses his family owns in Virginia and Florida, and at his golf club in Bedminster, New Jersey.

## A long presidential tradition

The Courses at Andrews — inside the secure grounds of Joint Base Andrews in Maryland, better known as the home of Air Force One — include two 18-hole courses and a 9-hole course. Gerald Ford was the first president to golf there, in 1974. Gerald Ford, Ronald Reagan, George H.W. Bush, Bill Clinton, George W. Bush and Joe Biden all visited, and Barack Obama played the courses roughly 110 times in eight years, more than any president.

Michael Thomas, who served as general manager from 1981 until his retirement in 2019 and golfed with four presidents during that time, said presidential rounds at Andrews required an elaborate security operation.

"It's a Cecil B. DeMille production every time," Thomas said, noting that Secret Service over the years deployed as many as 28 golf carts — alongside the president's usual 30-car motorcade — to maintain the perimeter.

Thomas said the commanders in chief generally shared one enthusiasm amid the logistical choreography: "They all like to drive the cart because they never get an opportunity to drive."

## Part of a wider construction agenda

The White House said the courses and clubhouse need improvements due to age and wear, and that discussions include a possible multifunctional event center as part of the project. The base's golf course opened in 1960; its facilities have undergone past renovations, including alterations prompted by a 2018 construction project to replace aging presidential aircraft and build a new hangar.

The Andrews renovation joins other Trump construction projects: demolishing the White House's East Wing for a ballroom now expected to cost $400 million, renovating the bathroom attached to the Lincoln Bedroom, and replacing the Rose Garden's lawn with a patio area. Trump has also led building projects at the Kennedy Center, proposed a Paris-style arch near the Lincoln Memorial, and said he wants to rebuild Dulles International Airport in northern Virginia.

On Wednesday, the Trump administration ended a lease with a nonprofit that had managed three public golf courses in Washington. The White House said that action is unrelated to the Andrews plans.
