Tuesday's power failure stranded passengers for hours aboard trains left without heat, lighting or phone-charging capacity, forced thousands to scramble for alternative flights and buses, and upended end-of-year getaway plans for travelers across northwestern Europe.

PARIS — Trains resumed running in both directions through the Channel Tunnel on Wednesday after operator Eurotunnel said the 50-kilometer (32-mile) undersea link was restored to "full capacity" overnight, but Eurostar warned passengers to expect continued disruptions from the previous day's severe travel chaos.

The power fault inside the tunnel was repaired overnight Tuesday, Eurotunnel said in a brief statement that did not detail the cause of the failure. Eurostar, which runs passenger trains through the tunnel, said "knock-on impacts" from Tuesday's cascading cancellations would continue to affect London-Paris, London-Brussels and London-Amsterdam services Wednesday, with early morning cancellations and delays in both directions.

As MSI previously reported, the initial power failure on Tuesday stranded travelers for hours and forced thousands to seek alternative flights and buses for their end-of-year journeys. ## A night without heat or light

Tuesday's disruptions were compounded by a separate power malfunction on the U.K. side that Eurostar said was related to the electrical fault inside the tunnel. An overhead power cable fell onto a Eurostar train traveling from London to Paris near the tunnel entrance, Eurostar said, and an effort to move the train with its passengers inside proved "very complex."

The power failure also caused severe delays to two Eurostar trains bound for Brussels, Eurostar said.

Passenger Ghislain Planque told French broadcaster BFMTV that his Eurostar journey Tuesday evening from London to France was meant to take just under 90 minutes but instead lasted around 11 hours, with passengers stuck overnight aboard the train.

"We were left without electricity, so with no heating, no air-conditioning, no possibility to charge phones," Planque said. "We were in total darkness for some of the time."

## Knock-on effects expected to persist

With trains again moving through the tunnel Wednesday, Eurostar's focus shifted to clearing the backlog of passengers affected by the previous day's disruptions. The company's website continued to show delays across its primary routes as crews worked to restore normal scheduling.

Eurotunnel's brief statement provided no explanation for what caused the initial power fault or when normal schedules were expected to fully resume.
