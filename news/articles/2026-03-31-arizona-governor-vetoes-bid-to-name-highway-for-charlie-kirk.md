Arizona Gov. Katie Hobbs vetoed a bill on Friday that would have added Charlie Kirk’s name to Loop 202, a highway circling the Phoenix area, setting off fresh debate over whether lawmakers can use naming decisions to memorialize political figures.

The proposal would have honored Kirk, a conservative activist whose Turning Point USA organization is based in Arizona. Republicans in the state passed legislation seeking to attach Kirk’s name to the road, but Hobbs rejected it in a veto message to state lawmakers.

In her veto, Hobbs denounced political violence while also arguing that Republicans had improperly injected politics into a function she said should stay nonpartisan. “I will continue working toward solutions that bring people together, but this bill falls short of that standard by inserting politics into a function of government that should remain nonpartisan,” Hobbs wrote.

Hobbs also suggested that the bill should have been handled through the state’s process for naming historic highways, a role she said belongs to a state board. The veto, in that framing, shifted the question from whether Kirk should be honored to whether the Legislature should be the one driving that recognition.

Republican state Senate President Warren Petersen, who sponsored the legislation, disputed Hobbs’s reasoning. Petersen said the governor was the one practicing politics by breaking with what he described as a long-standing Arizona practice of recognizing people who made an impact on society.

Petersen said the veto “tells people that recognition now depends on political alignment, not contribution,” adding that “That’s not how Arizona has ever approached these decisions, and it’s a disappointing shift for our state.” The statement left open the question of whose version of “tradition” should govern the naming process.

The national debate is not limited to Arizona. According to an Associated Press analysis using Plural bill-tracking software, lawmakers in more than 20 states have introduced over five dozen bills seeking to honor Kirk, including measures to name roads after him or create official days of remembrance.

The bills described by the analysis range beyond highway and memorial road proposals, including proposals that invoke Kirk’s name in connection with measures that would protect free speech rights on college campuses or encourage schools to teach about the role of Judeo-Christian values in American history.

Arizona and Florida were among the first states to give final approval to Kirk-inspired legislation. In Florida, Republican Gov. Ron DeSantis has not yet acted on a bill that would designate a road in Miami-Dade County as “Charlie Kirk Memorial Avenue” while also designating a road in Broward County as “President Donald J. Trump Boulevard.”
