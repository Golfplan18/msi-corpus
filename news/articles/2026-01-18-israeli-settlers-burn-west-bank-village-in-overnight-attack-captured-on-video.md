The assault comes as settler violence in the occupied West Bank has spiked in recent months. Around 500,000 Israelis have settled in the territory since Israel captured it in the 1967 Mideast war — a presence most of the international community views as illegal and a major obstacle to peace.

Israeli settlers burned at least eight homes and two vehicles in a Bedouin hamlet north of Jerusalem on Saturday night, according to the Palestinian Authority, in an attack that security camera footage obtained by the Associated Press captured in detail. The Palestinian Authority's Jerusalem governorate said dozens of settlers descended on the village of Khirbet al-Sidra, injuring two Palestinians, who were hospitalized, and two activists.

## Footage of the attack

The AP footage, time-stamped at around 10 p.m. Saturday, shows several structures in the village going up in flames as sounds of gunfire, screaming and barking dogs echo in the background. As the fires grow, they illuminate bands of settlers dressed in black moving freely through the village.

Israel's military said soldiers dispatched to Khirbet al-Sidra found an Israeli vehicle with clubs inside, and confirmed that Palestinians, Israelis and foreign nationals had been injured. Troops were searching the area to make arrests. As of Sunday afternoon, no arrests had been reported.

Israel has characterized such violence as the work of a small, radical minority. Its far-right government, dominated by settlers and their supporters, has done little to stop the attacks, the Associated Press reported.

The Palestinians seek the West Bank, along with east Jerusalem and the Gaza Strip, for a future state.

## Israel moves to cut UNRWA water and electricity

Also on Sunday, Israeli Energy Minister Eli Cohen said he had issued formal notices to disconnect water and electricity from facilities belonging to UNRWA, the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees.

UNRWA said the shutdown could take effect within two weeks. The move follows a bill Israel's parliament passed in December to cut the supply of electricity and water to the agency's facilities. An earlier ban has already closed many of UNRWA's services in east Jerusalem, though the agency continues to operate a vocational training center there.

UNRWA provides aid and services — including health care and education — to around 2.5 million Palestinian refugees in Gaza, the West Bank and east Jerusalem, and to around 3 million more in Syria, Jordan and Lebanon. Israel has accused the agency of being deeply infiltrated by Hamas; UNRWA rejects that accusation.

## Board of Peace grows

Four more countries announced Sunday that they had received invitations to U.S. President Donald Trump's Board of Peace: Jordan, Greece, Cyprus and Pakistan. Albania, Egypt, Paraguay, Argentina and Turkey had previously announced invitations to the body.

The board, composed of world leaders, was initially conceived as a mechanism to end the Israel-Hamas war in Gaza. It is taking shape with a broader mandate; Trump's invitation letters described a body that would "embark on a bold new approach to resolving global conflict."

A U.S. official, speaking on condition of anonymity about the body's charter — which has not been made public — said there is no requirement to contribute money to join. The official confirmed that a $1 billion contribution secures a permanent membership rather than a three-year appointment, and said money raised would go to rebuilding Gaza.
