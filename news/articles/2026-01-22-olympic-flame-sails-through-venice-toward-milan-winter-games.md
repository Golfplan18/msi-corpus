The Olympic flame passed through Venice on Thursday, carried on traditional Venetian boats down the Grand Canal and across St. Mark's Basin to the Doge's Palace. The torch relay, 46 stops into its 63-day journey across Italy, reached the lagoon city with thousands gathering in St. Mark's Square to watch the passage toward the Milan Cortina Winter Games opening February 6.

The flame's route through Venice, which started in Rome and will pass through all 110 Italian provinces over 12,000 kilometers, marks Italy's first hosting of the Winter Olympic torch in nearly two decades. Two official cauldrons will burn during the Games in Milan and Cortina as athletes compete through February 22.

## The Passage Through Venice

The Olympic flame arrived near dusk at Venice's Piazzale Roma, the main bus terminal for the city. It crossed the Ponte delle Guglie in the Cannaregio district before reaching the arched Rialto Bridge, where it was loaded onto the Serenissima, a long traditional Venetian boat that once carried Venice's rulers. Flanked by smaller traditional boats and police on Jet Skis, it made its way through the Grand Canal toward the Accademia Bridge. From there, torchbearers carried the flame on foot to Punto della Salute, opposite St. Mark's Square, for a brief tour of St. Mark's Basin. A cauldron was lit at St. Mark's Basilica as thousands cheered.

## A Torchbearer's Emotion

Francesco Lamon, an Olympic gold medal-winning cyclist, was among Thursday's torchbearers. "It's an indescribable emotion," he told the Associated Press.

Amid the celebration, a small group of anti-war demonstrators called for Israel and the United States to be excluded from the Games.

## The Broader Torch Relay

Venice represents the 46th stage of the torch relay's 63-day journey covering 12,000 kilometers, or nearly 7,500 miles. The flame started in Rome and will pass through all 110 Italian provinces before reaching Milan's San Siro Stadium for the opening ceremony on February 6.

Two official cauldrons will burn during the Games—one in Milan at Arco della Pace in Sempione Park and one in Cortina in Dibona Square. The Games run through February 22, when the closing ceremony will take place in the Veneto city of Verona.

## Regional Significance

Venice historically has served as a crossroads between Eastern and Western civilizations, evident in its Byzantine architecture and its historical role in the trade of spices, silks, and art. In the modern era, it is the capital of the Veneto region, which includes the host city Cortina nestled in the Dolomites to the north. On clear days, snow-capped mountains can be seen from Venice's historic center.

The Games' presence in Italy marks the first time the country has hosted the Olympic flame in nearly two decades, since the 2006 Winter Games in Turin.
