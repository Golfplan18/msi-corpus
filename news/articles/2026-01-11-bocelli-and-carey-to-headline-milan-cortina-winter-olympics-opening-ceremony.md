The announcement fills out the entertainment program for what organizers say will be the most widely viewed moment of the Games — a ceremony notable for spanning four alpine venues simultaneously and including a tribute to the late fashion designer Giorgio Armani, who shaped the Italian Olympic team's look for decades.

Crossover tenor Andrea Bocelli will join previously announced pop star Mariah Carey as headline performers for the Milan Cortina Winter Olympics opening ceremony on Feb. 6, organizers said Sunday. The three-hour spectacle will unfold at Milan's landmark San Siro soccer stadium before an expected crowd of 60,000, with millions more watching on television.

Bocelli's appearance "will constitute one of the most iconic moments of the event, uniting the spectacle with the essence of Olympic values," organizers said in a statement, adding that his inclusion will "contribute a contemporary and global tone to the narrative."

## A ceremony across four venues

Reflecting the Milan Cortina Games' status as the most spread-out Olympics in history, elements of the ceremony will also be conducted in three remote venues and beamed to the televised audience: Cortina in the heart of the Dolomite mountains, Livigno in the Italian Alps, and Predazzo in the autonomous province of Trento. The multi-site approach allows athletes from all far-flung disciplines to participate in the opening event regardless of where their competition venues are located.

Marco Balich, a veteran producer of more than a dozen Olympic ceremonies, is directing the production. Details about the ceremony have been released gradually over recent months.

## Bocelli's global profile

Bocelli, a classically trained singer whose recordings span classical and pop music, has performed at the coronation of King Charles III and the 75th anniversary of the Italian Constitution in Rome. He will appear at San Siro during a break from a world tour, according to his official calendar, traveling the following day to perform at the Schottenstein Center in Columbus, Ohio on Feb. 7, followed by six other U.S. dates.

Carey, the first international star named to perform at San Siro and a six-time Grammy award winner, announced her participation in an Instagram post with the phrase "Ci vediamo a Milano" — Italian for "See you in Milan."

## Additional performers and an Armani tribute

Italian actors Sabrina Impacciatore, who appeared in the second season of HBO's "The White Lotus," and Matilde De Angelis, who starred in the Netflix series "The Law According to Lydia Poet," will also participate in the ceremony, organizers said.

The program will include a tribute to the late Italian fashion designer Giorgio Armani, who died in September at age 91. Armani had long designed the Italian Olympic team's uniforms, and his legacy as one of the founders of Italian ready-to-wear is closely tied to Milan, where he endowed the Armani theater, the Silos museum, and the EA7 Emporio Armani Milano basketball team.

The closing ceremony, scheduled for Feb. 22 at Verona's ancient Roman Arena, will be headlined by internationally acclaimed ballet star Roberto Bolle.
