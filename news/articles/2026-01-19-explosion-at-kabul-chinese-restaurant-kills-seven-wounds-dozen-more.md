The blast struck a restaurant in Kabul's Shahr-e-Naw district that police said was popular with Chinese Muslims, drawing condemnation from Pakistan as tensions between the two neighbors — already close to open conflict in recent months — sharpened further.

An explosion at a Chinese restaurant in downtown Kabul killed at least seven people and wounded about a dozen more on Monday, according to city police and an Italian medical charity operating near the blast site. Afghan authorities said the cause remained under investigation, while Pakistan's president attributed the blast to a bomb and accused Afghanistan's Taliban government of allowing its territory to be used for terrorism.

The explosion struck in the Shahr-e-Naw district of the Afghan capital, according to Kabul police command spokesman Khalid Zadran. He said the restaurant was jointly owned by an Afghan man, a Chinese national and his wife, and was popular with Chinese Muslims. Zadran said one Chinese national and six Afghans were killed, and that the blast occurred near the restaurant's kitchen.

The Italian charity EMERGENCY said its surgical center in Kabul, located near the blast site, received 20 people from the explosion, including seven who were already dead. The charity said the casualty count was still provisional.

"The wounded, some of whom are being assessed for surgery, have suffered lacerations and bruises," said Dejan Panic, EMERGENCY's country director in Afghanistan. Those injured included four women and a child, he said.

Chinese state broadcaster CCTV separately reported that two Chinese people were seriously injured and a security guard was killed in the blast.

## Pakistan condemns blast, accuses Taliban of enabling terrorism

Pakistan's President Asif Ali Zardari issued a statement strongly condemning what he described as a bomb blast. He accused Afghanistan's Taliban government of failing to honor ceasefire agreements, "particularly the obligation to prevent the use of Afghan soil for the export of terrorism," adding that "no terrorist groups should be allowed safe havens in Afghanistan."

Afghan officials have repeatedly said they do not allow their territory to be used as a base by terrorist groups.

Relations between Pakistan and Afghanistan had already been severely strained in the months before the blast. In October, the two countries came close to all-out conflict, with cross-border clashes leaving dozens dead before Qatar brokered a ceasefire. Zardari on Monday also accused Taliban-governed Afghanistan of enabling attacks on other neighboring countries, and singled out Tajikistan.

His statement came after Tajik authorities said their border guards had killed four men who crossed into Tajikistan from Afghanistan overnight Monday. Afghan authorities said the four were drug smugglers.
