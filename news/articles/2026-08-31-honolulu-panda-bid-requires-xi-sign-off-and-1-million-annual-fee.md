## Honolulu panda bid requires Xi sign-off and $1 million annual fee

**Subtype:** fact

- Chinese President Xi Jinping's personal sign-off is required for any agreement to send giant pandas from China to the Honolulu Zoo, the Associated Press reported.
- The arrangement carries an estimated annual fee of at least $1 million and expectations that Honolulu will not cross political red lines with Beijing.
- Honolulu Mayor Rick Blangiardi traveled to China this past spring in a roughly $30,000, taxpayer-funded trip to advance the zoo's panda bid.

## Mayor likens effort to 'a peace treaty'

Any panda arrangement would come with "strings attached," according to the AP. The personal sign-off of Chinese President Xi Jinping is required, the annual fee is estimated at at least $1 million, and Honolulu is expected not to cross political red lines with Beijing.

Blangiardi and his wife, who was born in Beijing, spent 10 days in China this past spring at a cost to city taxpayers of roughly $30,000, the AP reported. The itinerary included travel between three cities, a visit to Fuzhou Panda World, an 18-course meal and photo stops at the Great Wall of China.

The trip followed advice the mayor said he received after approaching the Chinese Consulate-General in Los Angeles about his interest in securing pandas for the Honolulu Zoo. The advice, according to the AP: go to China. "You would've thought we were doing a peace treaty," Blangiardi told Honolulu radio host Rick Hamada four days after he returned. That comparison may not be far off from reality — the mayor's interest has set in motion a process whose outcome depends on approval from the top of the Chinese government.

The story was reported by Ben Angarone of Honolulu Civil Beat and distributed by the Associated Press.
