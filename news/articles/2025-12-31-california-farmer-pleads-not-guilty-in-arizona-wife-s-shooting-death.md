HOLBROOK, Ariz. — Michael Abatti, 63, a prominent California farmer, pleaded not guilty Wednesday to a murder charge in the shooting death of his estranged wife, Kerri Ann Abatti, at her home in Pinetop, Arizona. Abatti made his first appearance in Navajo County Superior Court after being extradited from El Centro, California, where Arizona authorities had arrested him on the charge.

As [MSI previously reported](/articles/2025-12-31-california-farmer-pleads-not-guilty-to-killing-estranged-wife-in-arizona), Abatti was arrested in El Centro in connection with Kerri Ann Abatti's death and transferred to Arizona to face the murder charge. Investigators say he traveled from El Centro to the eastern Arizona mountain community of Pinetop on Nov. 20, 2025, carried out the killing, and returned to California early the following morning.

Prosecutors say Abatti is being held in the Navajo County jail on a $5.5 million bond. Owen Roth, one of his attorneys, said his client is able to post bond.

Kerri Ann Abatti was found dead at a home in Pinetop, where she had moved after separating from her husband. She had filed for divorce, with proceedings pending in California at the time of her death. The couple married in 1992, had three children, and split in 2023; they were in dispute over finances in the divorce proceedings at the time of the killing.

She was a descendant of early Latter-day Saints settlers who helped found Pinetop in the 1880s.

Michael Abatti comes from a multigenerational farming family in California's Imperial Valley, the region that is the largest user of Colorado River water and is known for growing leafy greens, melons, and forage crops. His grandfather, an Italian immigrant, was among the region's early settlers, and his father helped start the Imperial Valley Vegetable Growers Association.
