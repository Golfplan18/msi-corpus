MEXICO CITY — Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum said Monday she had "a very good conversation" with U.S. President Donald Trump and that the two governments will continue cooperating on security without U.S. military intervention against drug cartels. The approximately 15-minute call followed a week in which Trump had publicly offered to send U.S. forces to confront the cartels and repeatedly claimed the cartels were "running Mexico."

The call came as Trump's rhetoric about intervention across Latin America had gained new weight following the recent U.S. military operation that ousted Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, leading Sheinbaum to request direct dialogue. Analysts said U.S. military intervention in Mexico remained unlikely given Mexico's status as a critical economic partner and its ongoing security cooperation with Washington.

Sheinbaum said Trump again raised the possibility of U.S. military assistance against the cartels during the call, which she rejected. "We told him, so far it's going very well, it's not necessary, and furthermore there is Mexico's sovereignty and territorial integrity and he understood," Sheinbaum said.

She said she addressed the Venezuela question directly with Trump. "He asked me my opinion about what they had done in Venezuela and I told him very clearly that our constitution is very clear, that we do not agree with interventions and that was it," Sheinbaum said.

Trump had staked out his position the previous week. In a Fox News interview aired Thursday, Trump said: "We've knocked out 97% of the drugs coming in by water and we are going to start now hitting land, with regard to the cartels. The cartels are running Mexico. It's very sad to watch."

## Rubio demands 'tangible results'

The Monday presidential call came a day after Mexico's Foreign Affairs Secretary Juan Ramón de la Fuente spoke Sunday with U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio. According to a U.S. State Department statement, Rubio asked for "tangible results" and more cooperation to dismantle the cartels.

Sheinbaum said Mexico had shared those results with the United States, citing a significant drop in homicides, falling U.S. fentanyl seizures, and fentanyl overdose deaths.

Analysts still see U.S. military intervention in Mexico as unlikely because Mexico is a critical economic partner that is doing what the United States asks, according to the Associated Press, but they expect Trump to continue using such rhetoric to maintain pressure on Mexico to do more.

## Cuba not on the agenda

Sheinbaum said the two leaders did not discuss Cuba during the Monday call, even though Trump had threatened Cuba on Sunday. Mexico is an important ally of Cuba, including selling it oil that Cuba will need more urgently now that the Trump administration says it will not allow oil shipments from Venezuela to Cuba.

The two leaders agreed to continue working together, Sheinbaum said.
