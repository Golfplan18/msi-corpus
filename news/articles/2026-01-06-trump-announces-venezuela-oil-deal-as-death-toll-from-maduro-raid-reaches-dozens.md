The oil announcement — worth as much as $2.8 billion at current prices — came as the Trump administration sought to reshape Venezuela's energy industry while managing the political fallout of a military operation that drew bipartisan scrutiny at home and expressions of alarm from allies in Europe and Latin America.

CARACAS/WASHINGTON — President Donald Trump said Tuesday that Venezuela would provide 30 million to 50 million barrels of oil to the United States at market price, pledging to use proceeds from the sale "to benefit the people" of both countries, as Venezuelan officials reported that at least 24 of the country's security forces were killed in the U.S. military raid that captured former President Nicolás Maduro.

Venezuela's Attorney General Tarek William Saab said the overall death toll from the weekend strike in Caracas was "dozens" when including both officers and civilians, and called the operation a "war crime." Cuba's government confirmed separately that 32 Cuban military and police officers — personnel belonging to the Revolutionary Armed Forces and the Ministry of the Interior — were killed while working in Venezuela at the time of the raid.

Seven U.S. service members were also injured in the operation, according to the Pentagon. Five had returned to duty; two were still recovering from gunshot wounds and shrapnel injuries, according to a U.S. official not authorized to speak publicly who spoke on the condition of anonymity.

## The oil deal

With oil trading at roughly $56 a barrel, the transaction Trump announced late Tuesday could be worth as much as $2.8 billion, according to the Associated Press. The 30 million to 50 million barrels would represent the equivalent of as much as two and a half days of U.S. oil consumption, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration, which tracks an average U.S. demand of roughly 20 million barrels per day of oil and related products.

Despite holding the world's largest proven crude oil reserves, Venezuela produces on average about one million barrels per day — far below the U.S. average daily production of 13.9 million barrels recorded in October, according to EIA data cited in the AP report.

The White House was organizing a meeting on Friday with executives from Exxon, Chevron, and ConocoPhillips to discuss Venezuela, according to a person familiar with the matter who requested anonymity to discuss the plans. The Trump administration has pressed to open Venezuela's oil industry more widely to American investment and expertise.

## Venezuela's acting president responds to threats

Venezuela's acting President Delcy Rodriguez pushed back Tuesday against earlier warnings from Trump, who told her she would face an outcome worse than Maduro's if she did not "do what's right" and overhaul Venezuela to align with U.S. interests.

Delivering an address before government agricultural and industrial sector officials, Rodriguez said: "Personally, to those who threaten me: My destiny is not determined by them, but by God."

Venezuela's military, in a video tribute posted to Instagram honoring the slain security personnel, said their deaths affirmed "our unwavering oath not to rest until we rescue our legitimate President, completely dismantle the terrorist groups operating from abroad, and ensure that events such as these never again sully our sovereign soil."

## Trump pushes back on Democratic criticism

Trump, speaking before a House Republican retreat in Washington on Tuesday, pushed back against Democratic criticism of the military operation. He noted that his Democratic predecessor Joe Biden had also called for Maduro's arrest on drug trafficking charges, and that Biden's administration in its final days raised the reward for information leading to Maduro's arrest after Maduro assumed a disputed third term. The Trump administration doubled that reward to $50 million in August.

Maduro was indicted in the United States in 2020 on charges of narco-terrorism and international cocaine trafficking conspiracy. He pleaded not guilty to federal drug trafficking charges in a U.S. courtroom on Monday, according to the AP.

"You know, at some point, they should say, 'You know, you did a great job. Thank you. Congratulations.' Wouldn't it be good?" Trump said.

## What polls show

Americans were divided over the Maduro capture, with many still forming opinions, according to a poll conducted by The Washington Post and SSRS using text messages over the weekend. About 4 in 10 approved of the U.S. military being sent to capture Maduro, while roughly the same share were opposed; about 2 in 10 were unsure.

Nearly half of Americans — 45% — opposed the U.S. taking control of Venezuela and choosing a new government for the country. About 9 in 10 said the Venezuelan people should decide the future leadership of Venezuela.

## Colombia protests; European allies push back on Greenland

Colombia's Foreign Affairs Minister Rosa Villavicencio said Tuesday she would meet with the U.S. Embassy's charge d'affaires in Bogota to present a formal protest over U.S. threats against the country. On Sunday, Trump said he was not ruling out an attack on Colombia.

"It is necessary for the Trump administration to know in more detail about all that we are doing in the fight against drug trafficking," Villavicencio said.

In Europe, the leaders of France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain and the United Kingdom joined Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen in defending Greenland's sovereignty after Trump renewed calls for an American takeover of the Danish territory.

"Greenland belongs to its people," the joint statement said. "It is for Denmark and Greenland, and them only, to decide on matters concerning Denmark and Greenland."
