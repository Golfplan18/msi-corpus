Timkat — recognized by UNESCO as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2019 — is considered by the faithful nearly as significant as Easter and Christmas, drawing pilgrims from across Ethiopia for ritual re-enactments of baptism and processions of the tabots, revered symbols of the Ark of the Covenant.

ADDIS ABABA, Ethiopia — Millions of Ethiopian Orthodox Christians gathered Monday for Timkat, the annual Epiphany festival commemorating the baptism of Jesus Christ by John the Baptist, with celebrations held across the country from lakesides in the Oromia Region to ancient fortresses in Gondar, the Associated Press reported.

In Batu, a town on the western shore of Lake Dambal (Hora-Dambal) in the Oromia Region, thousands of pilgrims entered the lake waters after priests blessed them, symbolically reenacting baptism and embracing the festival's themes of purification, renewal, and faith. In the capital, Addis Ababa, crowds gathered to receive the tabots — symbols of the Ark of the Covenant used to consecrate churches — which priests carried from churches to nearby public spaces for overnight prayers before returning them the following day, surrounded by millions of the faithful.

Celebrations were also held inside the 17th-century Fasilides Fortress in Gondar, in the Amhara region, as well as in towns and cities across the country.

"We are celebrating the baptism of Jesus Christ, and our hearts are filled with joy," said Bereket Adugna, 43, who traveled from Addis Ababa with his wife and two children to Batu for the occasion.

Priests dressed in colorful robes carried ceremonial umbrellas in procession from churches to lakeshores or rivers amid chants and hymns, escorting the tabots through crowds of worshippers.

"For us Ethiopians, Epiphany is an opportunity to share what we have with those who need our help," said Asnake Dechasa, a deacon, speaking to the Associated Press.

Timkat, seen by the faithful as nearly as significant as Easter and Christmas, was inscribed by UNESCO as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2019.

The celebrations came as Ethiopia faces mounting pressures. Tensions have escalated with neighboring Eritrea, and famine conditions have worsened across parts of the country. Humanitarian organizations warn that millions of Ethiopians need emergency food aid, amid declining humanitarian support from Western nations.
