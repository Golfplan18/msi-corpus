The board, whose creation was endorsed by the U.N. Security Council as part of the U.S. 20-point Gaza ceasefire plan, is intended to manage the ceasefire's second phase — including a new Palestinian committee in Gaza, an international security force, Hamas disarmament, and reconstruction of the war-damaged territory — and Trump's invitation letters signal ambitions for a broader mandate in global affairs.

At least a dozen countries have received invitations to join President Donald Trump's newly formed Board of Peace, an international body designed to oversee Gaza's postwar transition, the Associated Press reported Sunday. A permanent seat on the board costs $1 billion, according to a U.S. official who spoke on condition of anonymity about the charter, which has not been made public; nations without the billion-dollar contribution are eligible for three-year appointed seats with no financial requirement, the official said.

Hungary and Vietnam confirmed they have accepted. Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán's acceptance was announced by Foreign Minister Péter Szijjártó on state radio Sunday. Vietnam's Communist Party chief To Lam accepted via a foreign ministry statement.

India received an invitation, a senior government official said, speaking on condition of anonymity because the information had not been made public. Australia was also invited and will consult with the United States "to properly understand what this means and what's involved," Deputy Prime Minister Richard Marles told the Australian Broadcasting Corp. on Monday. Jordan, Greece, Cyprus and Pakistan said Sunday they had received invitations. Canada, Turkey, Egypt, Paraguay, Argentina and Albania had already confirmed receiving invitations in earlier days. It was not clear how many countries had been invited in total, the AP reported.

The United States is expected to announce its official list of board members in the coming days, likely during the World Economic Forum meeting in Davos, Switzerland.

## Board's mandate and UN Security Council shadow

The Board of Peace will oversee the challenging second phase of the Gaza ceasefire, which took effect October 10. That phase includes establishing a new Palestinian committee in Gaza, deploying an international security force, disarming Hamas and rebuilding the war-damaged territory.

In letters sent Friday to world leaders inviting them as "founding members," Trump said the board would "embark on a bold new approach to resolving global conflict."

The board could become a rival to the U.N. Security Council, which endorsed the U.S. 20-point Gaza ceasefire plan that includes the board's creation. The 15-seat council has been blocked by U.S. vetoes from acting to end the Gaza war, while the U.N.'s broader influence has been curtailed by major funding cuts from the Trump administration and other donors.

## Israel objects to executive committee

Israel on Saturday objected to a related White House announcement — the creation of an executive committee tasked with carrying out the board's vision. A statement from Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office said the committee "was not coordinated with Israel and is contrary to its policy," without details. The AP described the statement as a rare public criticism by Israel of its close ally in Washington.

The executive committee includes Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Trump envoy Steve Witkoff, Jared Kushner, former British Prime Minister Tony Blair, World Bank President Ajay Banga, Trump's deputy national security adviser Robert Gabriel and Israeli billionaire Yakir Gabay. Representatives of ceasefire monitors Qatar, Egypt and Turkey also hold seats. Turkey, which has a strained relationship with Israel but maintains good relations with Hamas, could play a significant role in persuading the group to yield power in Gaza and disarm, the AP reported.
