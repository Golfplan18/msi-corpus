Brazilian film "The Secret Agent" earned four Oscar nominations Thursday, tying Brazil's record for Academy Award nominations and signaling a breakthrough moment for the country's cinema. The film — directed by Kleber Mendonça Filho and starring Wagner Moura — received nominations for best picture, best actor, best international film, and achievement in casting, matching the 2002 film "City of God" for the most nominations by a Brazilian production.

The nominations represent a remarkable rise in Brazilian cinema's global visibility, following the 2025 success of "I'm Still Here," which became Brazil's first Oscar-winning film. Both works have sparked nationwide discussion about Brazil's military dictatorship from 1964 to 1985, a period when people were tortured and disappeared.

Director Kleber Mendonça Filho said more than one million spectators have seen the film. "The nominations are recognition of our culture and of Brazil's ability to tell stories that move the world," he said in a video posted to social media Thursday.

## Memory and Reckoning

Both films have sparked broader conversation about Brazil's dictatorship. The period from 1964 to 1985 was marked by torture and disappearances. Sabrina Guimarães, a 20-year-old university student in Rio de Janeiro who saw the film Thursday, said learning about the history through cinema was more effective than classroom instruction alone.

"Even though we learn this stuff at school, we don't spend much time on it and it's not very specific. Feeling like you're there in the person's shoes, knowing what was happening at the time is very interesting," she said. "It's good to understand what happened in the past so we don't repeat these things in the future."

Director Mendonça Filho said the film emerged as a reaction to Brazil's past decade of political turmoil, including the far-right administration of former President Jair Bolsonaro. Bolsonaro was sentenced to 27 years and three months in prison last year for attempting a coup.

## The Film's Universal Resonance

In an interview, Mendonça Filho discussed the film's global relevance. "The film is very Brazilian, but it's also universal, so it can be used to discuss issues in the United States, in Europe or in Brazil," he said. "The theme of power being used to crush people and social classes is a theme that's current, not just historical."

## Industry Recognition

Gabriel Domingues, a casting director nominated in the newly created category of "achievement in casting," said the enthusiasm around the film reflects broader momentum in Brazilian cinema. "Brazilian cinema is really in a moment of intense emotion, beyond just excitement. People get very moved, with this participation in international events and awards and everything," he told the Associated Press.

## A New Pride in Cinema

The success of both films is changing how Brazilians view their film industry, according to Ana Paula Sousa, a cinema expert and teacher at ESPM University in São Paulo. Brazil has historically had low movie attendance rates. "People are talking about Brazilian cinema and thinking it's cool to talk about it," Sousa said. "That's something we didn't see before, and it's really great."

She expressed hope that the successes would encourage more consistent attendance. Lúcia Espírito Santo, a 78-year-old retired lawyer in Rio de Janeiro, said the films resonated with her personal experience during the dictatorship. "What we see in the film happened a lot. People would disappear and you didn't know why," she said after a screening. "Friends of mine from college disappeared because they spoke out, they advocated for freedom and democracy."

Espírito Santo said Brazil's international film recognition brought her pride. "We're showing up, stepping onto the red carpet abroad," she said. "Brazil is starting to look like a producer of films, of well-told stories."

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva said the nominations represent "recognition of our culture and of Brazil's ability to tell stories that move the world." He added that the local industry is "experiencing one of the best moments in its history."
