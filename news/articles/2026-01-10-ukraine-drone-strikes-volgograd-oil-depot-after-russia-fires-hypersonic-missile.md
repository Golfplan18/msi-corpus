KYIV — Ukrainian drones set fire to an oil depot in Russia's southern Volgograd region overnight Saturday, Ukrainian military officials said, one day after Russia launched a massive barrage that killed at least four people in Kyiv and marked only the second use of Moscow's nuclear-capable Oreshnik hypersonic missile in the nearly four-year-old war.

The exchange of long-range strikes underscored the war's deepening infrastructure dimension: Ukraine targets Russian energy sites to reduce the oil export revenue Moscow uses to fund its invasion, while Russia has been systematically attacking Ukrainian power and heating systems in what Kyiv officials describe as an attempt to "weaponize winter."

## Volgograd depot struck, evacuation possible

Ukraine's General Staff said it struck the Zhutovskaya oil depot overnight, adding in a statement posted to Telegram that the facility supplies fuel to Russian forces and that damage was still being assessed. Volgograd Gov. Andrei Bocharov said no casualties were immediately reported but that residents near the depot may need to be evacuated; he did not specify the extent of the damage, according to a post on his regional administration's Telegram channel.

## Russia's Friday barrage: drones, missiles, and a hypersonic weapon

The Volgograd strike came in response to a broad Russian assault on Ukraine that unfolded overnight into Friday. Russia struck Ukraine with 121 drones and one Iskander-M ballistic missile, according to Ukraine's air force, which said it shot down 94 of the drones. The barrage killed at least four people in Kyiv, Ukrainian officials said.

Also in the Friday attack, Russia launched the Oreshnik ballistic hypersonic missile at western Ukraine — reported to be only the second wartime use of the weapon — in what Kyiv officials characterized as a clear warning to Ukraine's NATO allies. The Oreshnik is nuclear-capable.

Russia's Defense Ministry said separately that 59 Ukrainian drones were neutralized overnight over Russia and occupied Crimea, and that Russian forces struck Ukrainian energy facilities and fuel-storage depots using aviation, drones, missiles and artillery.

## UN: millions deprived of essential services

Stéphane Dujarric, spokesman for the U.N. secretary-general, said Friday's attacks "have resulted in significant civilian casualties and deprived millions of Ukrainians of essential services, including electricity, heating and water at a time of acute humanitarian need."

## Restoration efforts in Kyiv

Ukrainian Prime Minister Yulia Svyrydenko said heat supply would be fully restored in Kyiv by the end of Saturday. She said areas on the right bank of the Dnieper River would gradually lift emergency blackouts and return to scheduled power outages, but that restoring supply on the left bank — where Russian attacks were concentrated — would take longer due to significant damage to the power grid.

## Additional Ukrainian strikes

Beyond the Volgograd oil depot, Ukraine's military said it struck a drone storage facility belonging to a unit of Russia's 19th Motor Rifle Division in the Zaporizhzhia region of southern Ukraine, as well as a drone command and control point near the eastern city of Pokrovsk.

The intense Russian barrage and the Oreshnik launch followed reports of progress in talks between Ukraine and its allies on defense arrangements should a U.S.-led peace process advance, according to the Associated Press.
