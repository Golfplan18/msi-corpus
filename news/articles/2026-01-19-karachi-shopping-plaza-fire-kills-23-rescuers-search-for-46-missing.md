A fire that burned for nearly 24 hours through a multistory shopping plaza in Karachi, Pakistan, killed at least 23 people and left 46 others unaccounted for as of Monday, city police said. Rescue teams entered the badly damaged building after firefighters extinguished the blaze late Sunday, recovering additional bodies as authorities warned the death toll would likely rise. Most of the dead could not be identified — police and medical officials said the bodies were beyond recognition and would require DNA testing.

The disaster is the latest in a long history of deadly fires in Karachi, Pakistan's largest city, where authorities have repeatedly cited poor safety standards and illegal construction as contributing factors. A garment factory fire in the city in 2012 killed 260 people.

Rescue teams in Karachi entered a gutted multistory shopping plaza Monday after firefighters extinguished a blaze that burned for nearly 24 hours, recovering more bodies as 46 people remained unaccounted for, police said. The death toll reached 23, according to city police chief Asad Raza, who warned authorities feared the number would rise. Local media reported a higher count of at least 26 dead.

Identifying the victims has proved difficult. Only six bodies had been identified as of Monday, police surgeon Dr. Summaiya Syed said. She said the remaining bodies were "beyond recognition" and would require DNA testing. Doctors were collecting DNA samples from relatives of the missing.

## Rescue operation under way

The fire broke out Saturday and spread rapidly through shops stocked with cosmetics, garments and plastic goods, said Dr. Abid Jalal Sheikh, Karachi's chief rescue officer. Those killed included a firefighter, Sindh provincial Chief Minister Murad Ali Shah said at a news conference Monday.

Shah announced that the government would provide 10 million rupees — approximately $36,000 — in compensation to the family of each person killed.

City Mayor Murtaza Wahab said the rescue operation would continue until all missing persons had been accounted for. As night fell Sunday, crews struggled to reach parts of the severely damaged building where some people were believed to be trapped after losing contact with their families.

## Relatives wait for news

Some relatives of the missing gathered outside the burned-out plaza throughout the day, waiting for information.

Qaiser Ali said his wife, daughter-in-law and sister went shopping for an upcoming wedding event Saturday and were inside the building when the fire broke out. He told the Associated Press he had spoken with all three by mobile phone Sunday before they went silent.

"I don't know what has happened to them or whether they are alive," Ali said. "We are praying that all those missing come out safely."

Saifur Rehman said he escaped the building as the fire spread but that his brother Mohammad Abrar, who owns a shop in the plaza, was left behind.

## Cause under investigation

The cause of the fire was not immediately known. Police said an investigation was underway.

Karachi, Pakistan's largest city and the capital of Sindh province, has a history of deadly fires often attributed to poor safety standards and illegal construction. A fire at a shopping mall in the city in November 2023 killed 10 people and injured 22 others. A garment factory fire in Karachi in 2012 killed 260 people.
