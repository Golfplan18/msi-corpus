## Nikkei and Kospi rise on chipmaker buying as oil climbs on U.S.-Iran escalation

**Subtype:** fact

- Asian shares traded mixed on Monday, with chipmaker buying lifting Japan's Nikkei 225 by 1.7% to 66,104.93 and South Korea's Kospi by 3.3% to 6,907.53.
- Samsung Electronics gained 4.5% and SK Hynix surged 6.2% on the session.
- Brent crude rose 67 cents to $96.95 a barrel on further escalation in the U.S.-Iran conflict.
- Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.1% to 25,377.12 and the Shanghai Composite shed 0.2% to 3,920.70; Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was virtually unchanged at 9,005.30.

## Brent crude rises to $96.95

Asian shares opened the week on a divided footing, with semiconductor-heavy benchmarks in Tokyo and Seoul rallying while oil prices climbed on further escalation in the U.S.-Iran conflict.

Japan's Nikkei 225 added 1.7% to close at 66,104.93, and South Korea's Kospi jumped 3.3% to 6,907.53, both propelled by buying of computer chipmakers' stocks. Shares in Samsung Electronics rose 4.5%, and memory chipmaker SK Hynix surged 6.2%.

"The AI complex continues to provide the local equity markets with its most dependable growth pulse," Stephen Innes, a former trader, said about the Asian markets. He added: "The AI complex is firmly in the leadership seat and reminding investors that, for all the hand-wringing around valuation, the hardware trade is still refusing to roll over."

Elsewhere in the region, Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost 1.1% to 25,377.12, while the Shanghai Composite shed 0.2% to 3,920.70. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was virtually unchanged at 9,005.30.

In energy markets, Brent crude, the international benchmark, added 67 cents to $96.95 a barrel. The AP attributed the move to further escalation of what it described as the six-month-long U.S. war with Iran.
