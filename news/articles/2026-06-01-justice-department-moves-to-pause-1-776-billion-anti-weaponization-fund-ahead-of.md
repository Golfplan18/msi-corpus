## Summary

**Subtype:** fact

- The Department of Justice announced it will comply with a federal judge's order to temporarily halt work on the Trump administration's $1.776 billion anti-weaponization fund ahead of a mid-June hearing.
- Congressional leaders raised objections to the compensation fund, warning that the allocation structure threatened to derail the president's broader legislative push for immigration enforcement funding.
- A mid-June judicial review will evaluate the legality of the executive branch's authority to distribute the nearly $1.8 billion in payouts without a direct congressional appropriations mandate.

The Department of Justice announced on June 1 that it will abide by a federal judge's recent order to temporarily pause operations of the anti-weaponization fund, stepping back from the program's payout structure while the court schedules arguments for mid-June. The nearly $1.8 billion initiative, established earlier this year to process financial compensation claims from individuals aligned with the president, triggered an immediate legislative standoff. Members of Congress from both parties intervened to question the funding mechanism, noting that its continued rollout threatened to derail separate negotiations required to pass immigration enforcement measures.

Administrative attorneys confirmed in a court filing that the Justice Department would not proceed with disbursements while the judicial review is pending. The administration's compliance signals a tactical retreat in a rapidly escalating fight over executive spending, as lawmakers weigh whether to formally block the initiative through appropriations riders or allow the courts to determine its statutory limits. 

The temporary halt follows a wave of legal challenges initiated in mid-May, when a coalition of former prosecutors and acquitted academics filed suit to block the fund's disbursements. The judge's subsequent order effectively froze the allocation process, setting the stage for the mid-June hearing to focus on whether the executive branch exceeded its authorized spending power when designing the compensation framework. If the court finds the fund violates congressional appropriations authority, the Justice Department would face additional legislative hurdles before any payments could be processed.
