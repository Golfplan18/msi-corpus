The visit deepens a new diplomatic alignment between Israel and Somaliland along a strategically sensitive stretch of coastline overlooking the Gulf of Aden, while placing Israel in direct conflict with much of Africa and the Arab world over the status of a territory that, until last month, no country had ever formally recognized.

HARGEISA, Somaliland — Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar visited Somaliland on Tuesday and promised Israel would open an embassy and appoint an ambassador there, cementing the country's status as the first nation ever to recognize the breakaway territory's independence from Somalia.

Saar's trip to Hargeisa — his first official visit to Somaliland — came eleven days after Israel's Dec. 26 decision to extend recognition to the territory, a move the African Union, the Organization of Islamic Cooperation, and more than 20 mostly Middle Eastern and African nations rejected.

"Nobody will determine for Israel who we recognize and who we maintain diplomatic relations with," Saar said, according to a transcript shared with the Associated Press.

Somaliland President Abdirahman Mohamed Abdullahi said the visit "carries historic significance" and marks an important milestone for the two countries.

## A shared sense of regional isolation

Somaliland's foreign minister, Abdirahman Dahir Adan, told the AP his country was "very grateful" for Israel's recognition and described the two nations as natural partners, each facing hostility from neighboring states and sharing an interest in security along the Red Sea.

"Israel is in a region where many of its neighbors are enemies. And Somaliland is in an area where many of their neighbors are hostile to them," Adan said, calling their struggles "very, very similar."

Adan predicted other nations would follow Israel in recognizing Somaliland in the near future.

## Somalia and the region push back

Somalia's federal government said Tuesday it "condemns in the strongest terms the unauthorized incursion," describing Saar's visit as "an unacceptable interference in the internal affairs" of the country. The federal government insists Somaliland remains an integral part of Somali territory and has warned that foreign recognition undermines its sovereignty and territorial integrity.

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said in a statement that Israeli recognition of Somaliland was a "dangerous precedent that threatens regional and international peace and security."

Three key regional players — Ethiopia, Kenya, and the United Arab Emirates — did not join the chorus of countries rejecting the recognition.

## Background: 35 years without recognition

Somaliland declared independence from Somalia in 1991 during a descent into conflict that continues to leave the East African country fragile. Despite having its own government and currency, the territory had never before been recognized by any nation until Israel's December decision.

The territory lies along a major shipping lane across the Gulf of Aden from Yemen and is home to a port coveted by regional powers including Ethiopia and the United Arab Emirates.

## Gaza resettlement proposal denied

The question of Palestinian displacement also surfaced during the visit. U.S. and Israeli officials previously told the AP that Israel had approached Somaliland about taking in Palestinians from Gaza as part of a plan by U.S. President Donald Trump to resettle the territory's population. Adan said Tuesday that such a proposal had neither been discussed nor considered.

The United States has since abandoned that resettlement plan. The State Department says it continues to recognize the territorial integrity of Somalia, "which includes the territory of Somaliland."

Video footage shared on social media purported to show fighter jets flying over Hargeisa during Saar's visit; it was not immediately clear which country's aircraft they were.
