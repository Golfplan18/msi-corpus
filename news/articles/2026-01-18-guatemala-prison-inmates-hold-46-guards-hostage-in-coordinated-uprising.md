Inmates in three Guatemalan prisons seized 46 guards as hostages Saturday in what authorities described as apparently coordinated disturbances, the country's interior ministry said. National Police were deployed around the affected facilities, and Interior Minister Marco Antonio Villeda said he was willing to engage with inmates but would not meet their demands.

The ministry said the uprising was a direct result of prison administrators' decision to strip privileges from some imprisoned gang leaders. There were no reports of injuries or deaths, according to authorities.

The incident adds to pressure on President Bernardo Arévalo's government, which has been working to reassert control over a prison system authorities acknowledge has longstanding ties to organized crime operating outside prison walls.

## Government's position

"In Guatemala, we don't negotiate with terrorists nor with organized crime," the interior ministry said in a statement. "We also don't allow groups that have sown fear to impose their conditions."

Villeda said he remained willing to talk with the inmates while holding firm against their conditions for releasing the hostages, according to the Associated Press.

Videos circulated on social media showed some prisoners demanding transfer, the AP reported.

## Recent security setbacks

The standoff follows a significant security failure for the Arévalo administration. In October, the president accepted the resignations of three top security officials, including Villeda's predecessor, after authorities acknowledged that 20 gang members had escaped from custody over a period of days.

Arévalo told the Associated Press in an interview Thursday that severing the connection between incarcerated gang members and criminal networks outside prison walls remains a central priority.

"The link between the prison system and the criminality outside has to be cut," he said. "That's why all this effort to regain control of the prison system is very important."
