The announcement reflects a growing international movement to restrict teenagers' access to social platforms. The United Kingdom is considering similar restrictions, and Australia already implemented a ban on social media for those under 16, which has resulted in approximately 4.7 million accounts being revoked.

French President Emmanuel Macron announced plans Saturday to accelerate legislation banning social media for children under 15, with the goal of implementing the prohibition by September 2026, at the start of the next school year. In a video released by broadcaster BFM-TV and addressed to the lawmaker sponsoring the bill, Macron said the measure was necessary to protect young people's mental health and autonomy.

"The brains of our children and our teenagers are not for sale," Macron said. "The emotions of our children and our teenagers are not for sale or to be manipulated. Neither by American platforms, nor by Chinese algorithms."

Macron's government also plans to ban mobile phones in high schools. "I believe this is a clear rule," he said. "Clear for our teenagers, clear for families, clear for teachers, and we are moving forward."

## International Momentum for Restrictions

The announcement reflects a broader push to restrict teenagers' access to social platforms. The United Kingdom government said it will consider similar restrictions. Australia has already implemented a ban on social media for those under 16, resulting in approximately 4.7 million accounts being revoked since the law took effect.

## Health Watchdog Documents Risks

France's health watchdog reported that one in two teenagers spends between two and five hours per day on a smartphone. According to a December report, 90% of children aged 12 to 17 use smartphones daily to access the internet, with 58% using their devices for social networks.

The watchdog documented a range of harmful effects from social media use, including reduced self-esteem and increased exposure to content associated with self-harm, drug use, and suicide. Several families in France have sued TikTok over teen suicides they say are linked to harmful content on the platform.

The proposed legislation will be examined in a public session of the French legislature Monday.
