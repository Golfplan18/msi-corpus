The British government's action represents the first major statutory response to a documented wave of non-consensual deepfake images attributed to Grok Imagine, xAI's AI image-generation feature, and signals a shift toward criminal enforcement — targeting both individual users and the companies that supply nudification tools — rather than reliance on platform self-regulation.

The United Kingdom moved Thursday to fast-track legislation criminalizing AI-generated non-consensual intimate images, as Prime Minister Keir Starmer demanded that Elon Musk's platform X immediately comply with British law following a global backlash over Grok, xAI's chatbot, being used to generate sexualized deepfakes of women and children.

xAI announced late Wednesday that it had introduced measures to prevent Grok from allowing the editing of photos of real people to portray them in revealing clothing in jurisdictions where doing so is illegal. Starmer said the move was welcome but stressed that oversight of the platform would not relent.

"Free speech is not the freedom to violate consent," Starmer said Thursday. "I am glad that action has now been taken. But we're not going to let this go. We will continue because this is a values argument."

## What the new laws cover

The government said it is fast-tracking provisions of the Data (Use and Access) Act — passed by Parliament last year but not yet in force — to make it a criminal offense to create or request deepfake intimate images. Those provisions are set to take effect on February 6.

Separately, the government said it is also criminalizing so-called "nudification" apps as part of the Crime and Policing Bill, which is currently before Parliament. The new criminal offense would make it illegal for companies to supply tools designed to create non-consensual intimate images.

"The content which has circulated on X is vile. It is not just an affront to decent society, it is illegal," Technology Secretary Liz Kendall said. Kendall cited a report from the Internet Watch Foundation that said the deepfake images included the sexualization of 11-year-olds and depictions of women subjected to physical abuse.

Justice Secretary David Lammy addressed those responsible for the images directly. "Let this be a clear message to every cowardly perpetrator hiding behind a screen: you will be stopped and when you are, make no mistake that you will face the full force of the law," Lammy said.

## Ofcom investigation and potential penalties

Britain's media regulator, Ofcom, has launched an investigation into whether X has breached UK laws over Grok-generated images, including depictions of children being sexualized and people being digitally undressed. Ofcom said such images — and similar productions made by other AI models — may constitute pornography or child sexual abuse material.

Kendall said X could face a fine of up to 10% of its qualifying global revenue depending on the outcome of the Ofcom investigation, as well as a possible court order blocking access to the site in the UK.

Musk, in a post on X, said Grok was already operating within the law. "When asked to generate images, it will refuse to produce anything illegal, as the operating principle for Grok is to obey the laws of any given country or state," he posted. "There may be times when adversarial hacking of Grok prompts does something unexpected. If that happens, we fix the bug immediately."

## Background on Grok Imagine

The backlash stems from Grok Imagine, an AI image generator launched last year that allows users to create images and videos from text prompts. The feature includes a "spicy mode" capable of generating adult content. Thousands of images emerged in recent weeks that digitally undressed people without their consent, including depictions of women and children in bikinis or in sexually explicit poses, the Associated Press reported.

Critics said laws regulating generative AI tools have been long overdue. Starmer has also faced calls to stop his government from using X; Downing Street said this week it was keeping its presence on the platform "under review."
