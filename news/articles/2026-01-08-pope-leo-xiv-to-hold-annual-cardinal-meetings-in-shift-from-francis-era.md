VATICAN CITY — Pope Leo XIV said Thursday he will convene annual meetings of the world's Catholic cardinals, marking a significant shift in Vatican governance after years of complaint that his predecessor had sidelined the church's senior leadership.

At the close of his first consistory — the formal term for such gatherings — Leo asked the assembled cardinals to return to Rome for a follow-up session June 27–28 and then on an annual basis for three to four days each year, Vatican spokesman Matteo Bruni said. The next session falls before the June 29 feast day.

The decision responds directly to grievances that cardinals raised before the May conclave that elected Leo, history's first American pope. Many among the 245 cardinals had complained that Pope Francis largely bypassed the full College of Cardinals, relying instead on a hand-picked advisory group of nine cardinals who met every few months at the Vatican to advise him. Leo convened his first consistory the day after he closed the 2025 Holy Year — a timing cardinals interpreted as signaling the formal start of his pontificate now that Francis' Jubilee obligations had been fulfilled.

## A chance to take stock

For the cardinals gathered, the sessions offered a first sustained look at how Leo intends to lead. History's first U.S. pope has remained, by many accounts, something of an unknown figure since his election; he tends to listen more than he speaks and has not announced major priorities or sweeping decisions.

Cardinal Pablo Virgilio David, bishop of Kalookan in the Philippines, said Leo took extensive notes as cardinals presented their views — though what the pope plans to do with them remains unclear. "Your guess is as good as mine," David said. "All I know was he was taking down notes very, very seriously. So he must be up to something."

Cardinal Stephen Brislin, archbishop of Johannesburg, South Africa, said the regular meetings reflected a collegial instinct in the new pope. "I think the pope wants to be collegial, and he wants to draw on the experience and the knowledge of different cardinals coming from all the different parts of the world," Brislin told a press conference after the meeting.

"Because obviously the Catholic Church is a universal body, and the richness that comes from the experiences of people in other parts of the world really helps, I think, the successor of Peter in order to give leadership to the church," he said.

## Cardinals getting to know one another

The gatherings also serve a practical internal purpose: allowing cardinals who rarely meet to develop working relationships. Before the conclave, some cardinals noted that certain members of the college were strangers to them — a complication when facing the task of electing a pope.

Cardinal Cristóbal López Romero, archbishop of Rabat, Morocco, said the first consistory had already addressed that gap. "It was very fruitful because it allowed us to know one another better, to be in contact with the pope and take the pulse of how things are going," he said.

Cardinal Jean-Paul Vesco, archbishop of Algiers, Algeria, said he was struck by the spirit Leo had brought to the gathering. "There is something being built, a willingness to work together," Vesco said. "And what really struck me, more than anything that was said, was this figure of the pope, whom we are now discovering as pope. And in his simplicity, we feel loved, we want to love him, and then there is the brotherhood between us."
