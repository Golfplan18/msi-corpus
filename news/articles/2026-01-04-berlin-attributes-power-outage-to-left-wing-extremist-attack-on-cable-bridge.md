BERLIN — Berlin city authorities on Sunday attributed a fire that cut power to more than 45,000 households and 2,200 businesses in southwest Berlin to a politically motivated attack by left-wing extremists. The blaze broke out Saturday morning on a cable bridge over the Teltow Canal, near the Lichterfelde power plant, knocking out electricity to four city districts along with heating and internet services.

Many residents and businesses face days without power. Authorities estimated that those still without electricity would likely remain so until Thursday, as snowy weather and freezing temperatures slowed restoration work.

The incident is under investigation as a possible act of arson. Authorities compared it to a similar power outage in southeast Berlin last September, when radical activists claimed responsibility for that attack. Officials said Sunday they were working to confirm the authenticity of a letter claiming responsibility for the latest incident.

"It is unacceptable that once again clearly left-wing extremists have attacked our power grid and thereby endangered human lives," Berlin Mayor Kai Wegner said, according to a German news agency. Wegner said the perpetrators were "clearly left-wing extremists."

Franziska Giffey, the city's Senator for Economic Affairs, described the incident as "a particularly severe power outage affecting tens of thousands of households and businesses, including care facilities, hospitals, numerous social institutions and companies."

Power was restored to some households by Sunday, but the combination of damaged high-voltage infrastructure and winter conditions complicated full recovery. Care facilities and hospitals were among those affected during the outage.

The attack follows a pattern that Berlin authorities have now seen twice within months. Investigations into both incidents remain ongoing.
