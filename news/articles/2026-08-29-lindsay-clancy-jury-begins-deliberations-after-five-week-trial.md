## Defense cites postpartum psychosis; prosecutors argue killings were intentional

Jurors in the Lindsay Clancy murder trial began deliberating this week in Plymouth, Massachusetts, weighing whether the former labor and delivery nurse should be held criminally responsible for killing her three young children at her Massachusetts home in 2023. By the end of the day Friday, the panel had not reached a verdict.

The deliberations close a five-week trial in which the jury heard testimony from doctors, first responders, relatives and Clancy's former husband. The factual conduct at the center of the case is not in dispute: both sides agree that Clancy carried out the killings. What the jury must resolve is whether her mental state at the time absolves her of criminal responsibility, or whether, as prosecutors contend, she planned the acts deliberately.

Her attorneys argue that Clancy was suffering from postpartum psychosis at the time and that the poor mental healthcare she received before the killings exacerbated her condition. Prosecutors argue she intentionally planned the killings.

If the jury convicts Clancy, her sentence could be as severe as life in prison. If she is found not guilty, she may be released or remain confined in a mental health facility.

Deliberations are expected to continue in the coming days.
