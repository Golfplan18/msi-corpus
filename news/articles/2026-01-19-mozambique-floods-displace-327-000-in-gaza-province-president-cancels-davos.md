MAPUTO, Mozambique — Flooding has displaced more than 327,000 people in Mozambique's southern Gaza province, with roughly 40 percent of the province submerged by water, provincial governor Margarida Mapandzene Chongo said Monday. Mozambican President Daniel Chapo canceled his trip to the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, as authorities declared a countrywide red alert — the highest warning level — and ordered evacuations from the lower portions of Xai-Xai, a city of about 115,000 people on the Limpopo River.

The flooding, driven by weeks of torrential rain across parts of southern Africa, has killed more than 100 people in Mozambique, South Africa, and Zimbabwe combined. Cabinet minister and government spokesperson Inocencio Impissa said nearly 600,000 people have been affected across Gaza and the neighboring Maputo province — more than triple the 200,000 impact that humanitarian organizations had initially feared earlier this month.

## Evacuations and Conditions on the Ground

Chongo said the approximately 327,000 displaced residents were sheltering in dozens of temporary sites, including schools and churches, after fleeing or being evacuated from flooded or flood-threatened areas. Gaza province has a total population of about 1.4 million.

Authorities called for the complete evacuation of residents from the lower parts of Xai-Xai, the provincial capital, as additional flooding threatened the city. Videos posted on the city's official Facebook page showed streets in Xai-Xai resembling rivers as floodwater surged through parts of the city. Images from the nearby town of Chokwe — which had already seen evacuations — showed floodwater nearly completely covering houses and other buildings, with only rooftops visible above the surface.

The National Institute for Disaster Risk Reduction, which is coordinating rescue operations, said around 110 people were rescued by helicopter Sunday while trapped in trees or on other elevated points. Among those rescued were children, elderly people, and a pregnant woman about to go into labor.

## Conditions Expected to Worsen

Chongo said "the situation is likely to worsen" in Gaza because of heavy rains in southern Zimbabwe that would ultimately flow toward her province. Mozambican authorities said severe flooding in northern South Africa was also affecting Gaza province, which borders South Africa, as rivers flowing into Mozambique had burst their banks.

Transport Minister João Matlombe said roughly 40 percent of Gaza was submerged, and reported that 152 kilometers (94 miles) of roads across the country had been completely destroyed, with more than 3,000 kilometers (1,864 miles) of additional roads damaged.

## Regional Scale and Costs

Authorities described conditions in three Mozambican provinces as "critical." The recovery cost for Mozambique could run to hundreds of millions of dollars, according to the Associated Press. The premier of one province in South Africa affected by the storms said damage there could total around $250 million.

Mozambique, a nation of 34 million people on the southeastern coast of Africa, has faced devastating cyclones and prolonged drought in recent years. Several provinces have been hit by the current floods.
