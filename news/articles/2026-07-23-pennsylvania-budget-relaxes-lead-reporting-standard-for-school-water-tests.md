## Pennsylvania budget relaxes lead-reporting standard for school water tests

**Subtype:** fact

- Pennsylvania lawmakers inserted language into the state budget's final 48 hours that relaxes rules for publicly reporting lead test results on school drinking water.
- The change raises the reporting threshold from any detectable lead to levels above five parts per billion, according to records reviewed by Spotlight PA.
- State law does not require schools to test for lead, as long as the topic is discussed at a public meeting annually.
- Several school associations had complained that the previous reporting requirement was unrealistic without more state funding.

## No state mandate requires Pennsylvania schools to test for lead

The provision was inserted into the final 48 hours of budget negotiations earlier this month, according to records reviewed by Spotlight PA. Under the new language, schools that choose to test their drinking water for lead must report results to the state only when levels exceed five parts per billion. Previously, any detection above zero required reporting for public posting.

Pennsylvania law does not require schools to test for lead at all. Schools need only hold an annual public discussion of the topic. The change reduces the amount of information the state makes public when testing does occur.

The relaxation followed complaints from several statewide associations representing school boards, principals, and other school officials. The associations told education leaders that the state's prior requirement — reporting any lead detection — was unrealistic without additional funding, the records show.

The budget containing the provision passed earlier this month.
