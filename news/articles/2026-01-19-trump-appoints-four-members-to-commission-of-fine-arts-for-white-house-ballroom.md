President Donald Trump this week quietly appointed four new members to the Commission of Fine Arts, a federal panel that is one of two reviewing the administration’s plan for a White House ballroom. The new members were identified in court papers filed Thursday as the National Trust for Historic Preservation sought to stop related work. The commission is scheduled to meet Jan. 22, according to the court filings.

The Commission of Fine Arts normally has seven members but had been unable to meet for months, the AP reported. Trump dismissed six commissioners last fall after the East Wing was torn down, and a seventh commissioner—who had been the chair—had resigned after Trump took office because the commissioner’s term had expired.

One of the newly appointed members is James McCrery, an architect who had led the now $400 million ballroom project until Trump replaced him late last year, AP reported. The White House has said the project would be financed with private donations, including from Trump himself, and the East Wing has already been demolished to make space for the ballroom, according to the report.

Aside from McCrery, the commission’s new lineup will include Mary Anne Carter of Tennessee, Roger Kimball of Connecticut and Matthew Taylor of Washington, D.C., the AP said. Carter is chair of the National Endowment for the Arts, a role she also held during Trump’s first term, and she previously worked as a staffer to Rick Scott, the report said.

Kimball is described by AP as an art critic and conservative commentator. Taylor is listed by AP as the Washington, D.C. appointee, with the report identifying the four new members as enough to create a quorum for the panel’s Jan. 22 meeting.

In the court papers, Heather Martin, a deputy assistant to the president, said the White House is tentatively scheduled to formally present the project to the commission on Feb. 19 and March 19. Martin also said that at that point the panel could complete its review, AP reported.

The National Trust for Historic Preservation is challenging the project in federal court. The group accused the Trump administration of violating federal laws by starting the ballroom work before submitting the plan for independent reviews by the commissions, Congress and the public, according to AP.

The other federal panel involved in overseeing construction on federal land, including the White House grounds, is the National Capital Planning Commission. That commission heard an initial presentation about the ballroom at its meeting on Jan. 8, AP reported.
