The shipment underscores the diverging trade strategies of two self-described ideological allies: while Trump has waged trade wars, Argentine President Javier Milei has systematically dismantled the trade protections that defined his country's economy for decades, with Chinese manufacturers emerging as the primary beneficiaries.

ZÁRATE, Argentina — A cargo vessel carrying more than 5,800 Chinese electric and hybrid vehicles unloaded at Zárate Port in Buenos Aires province Monday, marking Argentina's first large-scale shipment of Chinese cars and a conspicuous counterpoint to U.S. President Donald Trump's tariff campaign against Chinese imports.

The BYD Changzhou arrived after a 23-day voyage from Singapore, its cargo the first to take advantage of a new Argentine government measure allowing up to 50,000 electric and hybrid vehicles priced under $16,000 into the country tariff-free this year — down from a 35% import levy that previously applied.

"For decades people in Argentina had this vision that everything here must be manufactured here," said Claudio Damiano, a professor at the Institute of Transportation at Argentina's National University of San Martin. "The boat has a symbolic value as the first step for BYD. Everyone's wondering how far it will go."

## Milei and Trump: allies diverging on trade

Argentine President Javier Milei, who came to power in 2023 on a promise to dismantle economic statism, has pursued a free-trade agenda that runs counter to the approach of his self-described ideological ally in Washington. Speaking Wednesday at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Milei said his deregulation measures "allow us to have a more dynamically efficient economy" and invoked Trump's signature slogan: "This is MAGA, 'Make Argentina Great Again.'"

The leaders' differences were visible at the same forum, where Trump had moments earlier laid out his vision for American primacy through tariffs and territorial demands.

For the last two years, Milei has flung open Argentina's doors to imports, slashing trade barriers, unwinding customs red tape, and shoring up the local currency to make foreign goods more affordable. Argentina logged a record 30% increase in imports last year compared to the year before, according to the Associated Press, with Chinese imports to Argentina surging over 57% in the same period.

The ideological bond between the two leaders has yielded concrete benefits for Milei. Trump offered Milei a $20 billion credit swap last year to boost his friend's chances in a crucial midterm election, according to the AP — a deployment of U.S. economic influence on behalf of an ally rather than in the form of coercive demands, as Trump has used elsewhere in the region.

## A symbolic reversal

The scene on the muddy riverbank in Buenos Aires province carried weight beyond the vehicles themselves. Argentina under the Kirchner governments — the leftist movement led by former Presidents Néstor Kirchner and Cristina Fernández de Kirchner — had become one of the most closed economies in the region, shielding domestic industry behind high tariffs and import restrictions.

Government spokesperson Javier Lanari framed Monday's shipment as a clean break with that era. "Argentina has rejoined the world," Lanari said. "Very soon, the Cuban-made vehicles left to us by Kirchnerism will be part of a sad and dark past."

The same day the BYD Changzhou unloaded in Zárate, European Union lawmakers voted to delay ratification of a landmark free trade agreement with the Mercosur bloc of South American countries, including Argentina — a deal that would have reduced barriers for European industrial imports and boosted sales of German electric vehicles in the region.

"For the Europeans, there's just no possibility of competing with the Chinese," Damiano said.

## China positioned to dominate Argentina's EV market

The new tariff-free quota applies only to vehicles priced under $16,000 — a threshold that, according to auto-sector economists, effectively advantages Chinese manufacturers over European and American rivals.

"Chinese manufacturers have the technology and the ability to meet the price limits set by the government," said Andrés Civetta, an economist specializing in the auto sector at the Argentine consulting firm Abeceb. "China has won the race."

Western car manufacturers in Argentina have raised alarms about unfair competition. Opposition lawmakers have criticized the tariff exemption, and Argentina's comptroller general posted on social media: "Trump is right: China must be stopped."

Chinese investment has also poured into Argentina's energy and mining sectors under Milei, and BYD and similar Chinese automakers have already established a significant presence across Latin America, drawing controversy from Mexico City to Rio de Janeiro.

## Infrastructure limits constrain the near-term outlook

For all the symbolic import of the shipment, Argentina faces practical constraints on how quickly Chinese electric vehicles gain traction. Pablo Naya, the creator of Sero Electric — described as Argentina's only domestic electric car manufacturer — said the country's aging power grid is not ready for a mass wave of EVs, and no dealer service centers currently exist capable of performing internal repairs on Chinese models.

"Honestly, we're not worried," Naya said from the Sero Electric factory in the Buenos Aires suburb of Castelar.

But Naya acknowledged the arithmetic could shift once Argentine infrastructure and consumer demand catch up to Chinese supply.

"Then that would get complicated for us," he said. "We'd have a problem."
