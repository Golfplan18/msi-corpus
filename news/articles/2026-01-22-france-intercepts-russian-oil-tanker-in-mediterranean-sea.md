France's navy, working with intelligence provided by the United Kingdom, intercepted an oil tanker in the Mediterranean Sea on Thursday that departed from Russia, according to officials familiar with the operation. The ship, identified as the Grinch, is suspected of operating with a false flag as part of Russia's effort to evade international sanctions on its oil trade.

The interception is part of an intensifying effort by Western nations to disrupt Russia's "shadow fleet," a network of aging vessels and tankers used to circumvent sanctions tied to Moscow's war in Ukraine. Oil revenue remains a critical source of funding for Russia's military operations.

French maritime authorities said the Grinch, which departed from Murmansk in northwestern Russia, is suspected of operating under a false flag from the Comoros Islands, an archipelago off East Africa. The vessel's crew is Indian, according to French military officials who discussed the operation on condition of anonymity.

The interception occurred in the western Mediterranean off the southern Spanish coastal town of Almería. French maritime authorities said the navy is escorting the ship to anchorage for additional checks.

"We are determined to uphold international law and to ensure the effective enforcement of sanctions," President Emmanuel Macron said in a post accompanying a photograph of a French helicopter hovering over the vessel.

Macron added: "The activities of the 'shadow fleet' contribute to financing the war of aggression against Ukraine."

## The Shadow Fleet Strategy

Russia deploys what is described as a "shadow fleet"—a collection of aging vessels and tankers owned by nontransparent entities with addresses in non-sanctioning countries and sailing under flags from such nations—to evade sanctions tied to its invasion of Ukraine. Experts estimate the shadow fleet numbers over 400 ships. France and other countries have vowed to crack down on the operations and are negotiating deals with flag-carrying nations to make vessel boarding easier.

Oil revenue is critical to Russia's economy, allowing President Vladimir Putin to fund the war effort without triggering inflation or currency collapse.

## Previous Enforcement

In September, French naval forces boarded another oil tanker off the French Atlantic coast that Macron also attributed to the shadow fleet. That vessel, known as "Pushpa" or "Boracay"—its name was changed several times—had sailed from the Russian oil terminal in Primorsk near Saint Petersburg and was sailing under a Benin flag.

Russian President Vladimir Putin denounced that earlier interception as an act of piracy and alleged that Macron initiated the operation to deflect attention from French domestic problems.

## Legal Proceedings

According to French judicial authorities, the captain of the Grinch will go on trial in February over the crew's alleged refusal to cooperate.
