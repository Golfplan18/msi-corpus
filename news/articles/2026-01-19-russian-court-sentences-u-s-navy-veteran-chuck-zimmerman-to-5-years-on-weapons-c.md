A Russian court has sentenced Chuck Zimmerman, 58, a U.S. Navy veteran, to five years in prison on charges of illegally transporting weapons, court officials announced Monday. Authorities found a firearm on Zimmerman's yacht when it arrived at the Black Sea resort city of Sochi in June. His family says the case is a fabrication intended to create leverage for a future prisoner exchange with the United States.

Zimmerman is one of a small number of Americans remaining in Russian custody as arrests of U.S. citizens in Russia and subsequent prisoner swaps have grown more frequent amid the worst deterioration in U.S.-Russia relations since the Cold War.

Russian authorities found the weapon while inspecting Zimmerman's yacht upon its arrival in Sochi, according to the Krasnodar regional courts' press service. Court records seen by the Associated Press showed Zimmerman was convicted in October; the Sochi court verdict was upheld two months later by the Krasnodar regional court.

Zimmerman told the court he had traveled to Russia to meet a woman he had previously contacted online and that he carried the firearm for self-defense, unaware of Russian laws, the court's press service said. He fully admitted guilt, according to the statement.

His sister, Robin Stultz, disputed that account and cast doubt on the admission.

"He was sailing from the U.S. to New Zealand, so of course he had a firearm on board," Stultz said in a statement to the AP. "You can't just call 911 if something goes wrong out at sea. He voluntarily disclosed it to them and they charged him with arms smuggling. This is an obvious set-up to get another American they can trade. He needs to be declared wrongfully detained."

Stultz said her brother was intercepted while sailing in international waters with no intention of entering Russia. She added that he has not been able to meet with anyone from the U.S. Embassy since his arrest.

"I wouldn't trust any 'confession' the Russians claim he's given," she said.

There was no immediate comment from U.S. officials.

A website set up in support of Zimmerman describes him as a father of two and an electrician. Several high-profile prisoner exchanges between Russia and the United States have taken place in recent years.
