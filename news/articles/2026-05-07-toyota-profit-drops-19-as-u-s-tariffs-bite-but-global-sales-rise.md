## Summary

**Subtype:** fact

- Toyota Motor Corp. reported a 19% profit decline for the fiscal year ended March 2026, posting 3.85 trillion yen ($25 billion) versus nearly 4.8 trillion yen a year earlier, and said U.S. tariffs erased about 1.4 trillion yen ($9 billion) from its operating income.  
- Toyota sold nearly 9.6 million vehicles worldwide during the year, up from roughly 9.4 million the prior year, and its quarterly profit rose 23% to 817 billion yen ($5.2 billion).  
- The automaker forecast fiscal‑year profit of around 3 trillion yen ($19 billion), citing lingering uncertainty from Middle‑East supply‑chain disruptions linked to the Strait of Hormuz blockage.  
- Toyota noted that higher oil prices and longer shipping routes caused additional cost pressures after the Iran war closed the strait.  
- Following the earnings release, Toyota shares fell about 2.2% in trading.

Toyota’s earnings dip underscores how trade policy and geopolitical tension can quickly affect the margins of a major global automaker.

Toyota said it will continue its “mobility company” transformation, expanding into boats, planes, robotic arms for store shelving and medical‑equipment transport while pursuing leaner operations and greater local procurement.
