An attack by the Allied Democratic Force, an Islamic State-linked militant group, killed at least 25 people in eastern Congo early Sunday, according to a human rights organization. The violence marks the latest in a series of attacks by armed groups in the mineral-rich region, where the ADF and other organizations have caused recurring civilian casualties.

The ADF operates in the border region between Uganda and Congo and has been a persistent source of violence in the region. The group has escalated its attacks in recent months, contributing to instability and displacement in eastern Congo.

The Allied Democratic Force attacked a village in eastern Congo's Ituri province early Sunday, killing at least 25 people, according to a human rights organization monitoring the region.

## Details of the Attack

The assault by the ADF, an Islamic State-linked militant group, killed 15 men burned alive in a house and seven shot in the village of Apakulu in Irumu territory. Three others were killed in the Walese Vonkutu administrative area. The attack occurred around 4 a.m.

"This tragedy occurred around 4 a.m. and claimed the lives of at least 25 people," said Christophe Munyanderu, president of the Convention for the Respect of Human Rights. "This incursion by the ADF is a true massacre."

## Who Is the ADF?

The ADF operates in the border region between Uganda and Congo, growing out of an insurgency against Ugandan President Yoweri Museveni's rule. The group was pushed into Congo following military assaults by Ugandan forces. Last July, it carried out a series of attacks that killed more than 100 people.

## Escalating Violence in the Region

The eastern region of Congo has witnessed several attacks in recent months by armed groups including the ADF and the Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group. The Ugandan and Congolese armed forces have been conducting joint military operations against the ADF and other armed groups active in the region. The ADF did not issue an immediate statement about the attack.
