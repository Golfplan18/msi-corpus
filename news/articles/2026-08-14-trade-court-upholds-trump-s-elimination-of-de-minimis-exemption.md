## Supreme Court previously voided Trump's global tariffs

A federal trade court threw out a legal challenge to President Trump's executive order scrapping the so-called "de minimis" exemption, under which goods worth less than $800 could enter the United States duty free. Three judges sitting in the U.S. Court of International Trade ruled Thursday that Trump had the legal authority to invoke the International Emergency Economic Powers Act to eliminate the exemption.

The ruling contrasts with the trade court's earlier treatment of Trump's April 2025 "liberation day" global tariffs, which it ruled were unlawful — a decision the Supreme Court subsequently upheld. In the global tariffs case, the Supreme Court came to the opposite conclusion, ruling that Trump could not act without approval from Congress.

In their judgment, the three judges wrote: "In reaching this conclusion, we find that the President's power to 'nullify [or] void . . . exercising any . . . privilege' does not run afoul of separation of powers principles." The court also found that the removal of the exemption did not constitute "an exercise of the power of the purse" and "is not an exercise of the power to legislate."

Trump hailed Thursday's ruling as a "big win" for his resolve to get rid of what he called a "ridiculous giveaway... one of the most DESPICABLE loopholes in American Trade Policy" costing the U.S. Treasury more than $10 billion in revenue from tariffs that it would otherwise receive. In a statement, Trump said: "For years, Foreign Shippers could send packages worth up to $800 into our Country, DUTY FREE, NO TARIFF, far less scrutiny. It became a giant loophole for TARIFF Cheats -- and a Pipeline exploited by Fentanyl Traffickers, Counterfeiters, and other Criminals shipping dangerous and illegal products into America. The numbers were staggering. In 2024 alone, de minimis cost America an estimated 10.8 BILLION DOLLARS in foregone TARIFF Revenue, and an astonishing share of narcotics and counterfeit seizures came through the de minimis channel."

Detroit Axle, a Michigan-based auto-parts distributor, brought the de minimis case last year on the grounds that Trump had overstepped his authority under IEEPA. The firm's legal counsel did not immediately comment on the ruling.

The decision clears the way for U.S. Customs and Border Protection to continue collecting tariff revenue on low-value packages. The agency netted more than a billion dollars in 2025 from the ending of de minimis, and the court ruling lets that revenue flow continue until the exemption is permanently eliminated, when Trump's One Big Beautiful tax cut and spending bill kicks in next July.
