Iran's government appointed Abdolnasser Hemmati as central bank governor on Wednesday after the rial fell to a record low against the U.S. dollar, triggering mass protests in Tehran and other cities that merchants and traders sustained by shuttering their shops.

President Masoud Pezeshkian's Cabinet named Hemmati, 68, a former minister of economic and financial affairs, to replace Mohammad Reza Farzin, who resigned Monday — one day after the protests began, according to the official IRNA news agency.

Iran's currency crisis, compounded by a roughly 40% inflation rate and Western sanctions over the country's nuclear program, has undercut household budgets and sharpened public anger at the government's economic stewardship.

## Currency freefall

The rial has collapsed since Farzin took office in 2022, when the dollar traded at 430,000 rials. By Wednesday the dollar was trading at 1.38 million rials, according to the Associated Press. The deterioration marks a stark departure from the 32,000-rial rate that prevailed at the time of the 2015 nuclear accord, which lifted international sanctions in exchange for tight controls on Iran's nuclear program.

A gasoline price change introduced in recent weeks is expected to worsen inflation further, the AP reported. The combination of rapid depreciation and inflationary pressure has driven up the prices of food and other daily necessities, adding to strain on households already facing restricted incomes under Western sanctions.

## New governor, contested record

Hemmati carries a complicated history into the role. He previously served as minister of economic and financial affairs under Pezeshkian but was dismissed by parliament in March for alleged mismanagement and accusations that his policies hurt the strength of the rial against hard currencies.

Government spokeswoman Fatemeh Mohajerani said on X that Hemmati's agenda would include controlling inflation, strengthening the currency, and addressing mismanagement at state banks.

## Protests and official warnings

Protests that began Sunday were described as among the largest in Iran in three years. Traders and merchants kept their shops closed in the main bazaars of Tehran, the southern city of Shiraz, and the western city of Kermanshah, according to witnesses cited by the AP.

Iran's Prosecutor General Mohammad Movahedi Azad warned that efforts to turn protests over economic issues into insecurity, property damage, or what he called "foreign scenarios" would face a strong reaction, Mizanonline reported.

In the southern city of Fasa, the head of the local justice department, Hamed Ostevar, denied that a young man had been killed during a protest. He said crowds had broken into the governor's office and injured three policemen; four protesters were arrested, he said.

## Sanctions and the nuclear deal's collapse

The rial's long-term decline traces in significant part to the collapse of the 2015 nuclear agreement. President Donald Trump withdrew the United States from the deal in 2018, during his first term, reimposing sanctions that have constrained Iran's access to international financial markets and suppressed oil revenue.
