The Associated Press in December 2025 released its annual collection of 100 photographs documenting a year of conflict, displacement, political upheaval, and resilience across the globe, drawing from a photojournalist corps that operates in more than 200 locations worldwide.

The collection spans events in Gaza, Syria, Ukraine, Nepal, Venezuela, the Philippines, Haiti, and the United States, and includes photographs taken by AP photographer Mariam Dagga, who died in an Israeli airstrike while covering the same hospital she had been photographing in Gaza.

## A photographer lost in the conflict she documented

Among the images that carried particular weight was a photograph Dagga took on April 16, 2025, showing Shaima Seiam lying wounded at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, southern Gaza, beside the body of her infant daughter Ahlam, who was days from her first birthday after their home was struck by an Israeli airstrike. Weeks after taking the photograph, Dagga herself was killed in another airstrike while covering the same hospital, according to AP.

## The year in frames

Several photographs in the collection document events that came to define 2025.

On Jan. 5, a toppled statue of the late Syrian President Hafez Assad, defaced during the overthrow of the Assad regime, lay in ruins on a mountain in Dayr Atiyah, Syria, captured by photographer Ghaith Alsayed. On Jan. 30, Israeli captive Arbel Yehoud — held hostage by Hamas since Oct. 7, 2023 — was escorted by fighters and handed over to the Red Cross in Khan Younis.

On Feb. 23, Venezuelan migrants were photographed near Gardi Sugdub on Panama's Caribbean coast after abandoning attempts to reach the United States amid the Trump administration's immigration crackdown.

On March 1, Palestinians gathered around a table surrounded by rubble in Rafah, southern Gaza, for iftar — the fast-breaking meal on the first night of Ramadan — photographed by Abdel Kareem Hana.

On July 3, Republican members of Congress reached to shake hands with House Speaker Mike Johnson after the signing of a bill of tax cuts and spending reductions at the Capitol. One week later, on July 10, a memorial wall in Kerrville, Texas, displayed photographs of victims from a catastrophic flood that struck the region, killing more than 130 people, according to AP. On July 22, a Venezuelan migrant deported to El Salvador by the Trump administration was documented being welcomed home by relatives in Caracas.

On Sept. 9, a protester wearing a flak jacket and carrying a shield taken from a police officer shouted outside the Singha Durbar — the seat of Nepal's government — during demonstrations against corruption and a ban on social media in Kathmandu.

## War, migration, and moments apart

The collection also includes photographs of Rwanda's cycling team competing in the road cycling World Championships in Kigali on Sept. 24; Catholic pilgrims dancing at the annual pilgrimage to the shrine of the Virgin of El Rocio in Spain on June 8; and aerobatic displays above the Bucharest International Air Show in Romania on Aug. 30.

AP described its photographers as "journalists, explorers, artists, understanders," expert in "vantage point and light, in people skills and storytelling." The agency, which has won 36 of its Pulitzer Prizes since the award was established in 1917, employs photojournalists it described as trusted eyewitnesses across the globe.

The gallery was curated by AP photo editors Benjamin Snyder, Enric Martí, and Jacqueline Larma. Text was by Ted Anthony.
