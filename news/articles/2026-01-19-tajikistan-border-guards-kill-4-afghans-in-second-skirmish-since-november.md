The incident adds fresh strain to a tentative diplomatic thaw between two countries that severed ties after the Taliban's takeover of Afghanistan in 2021, with Kabul and Dushanbe offering sharply conflicting accounts of who the men were and how many were killed.

Tajikistan's border guards killed four men who crossed from Afghanistan overnight near the Badakhshan border, Tajikistan's National Security Committee announced Monday. The two governments immediately offered conflicting accounts of who the men were — and even how many died.

The incident is the second violent episode along this stretch of the Afghanistan-Tajikistan frontier since late November, adding fresh strain to a diplomatic relationship that remains fragile more than four years after Tajikistan severed ties with Kabul following the Taliban's seizure of power.

## Conflicting accounts

Tajikistan's National Security Committee said its guards spotted the men near a village close to the border with Afghanistan's Badakhshan province. The men refused an order to surrender, offered armed resistance, and were killed in the ensuing skirmish, the committee said in a statement. It described the four as "members of a terrorist organization" but did not elaborate.

Afghan government spokesman Zabihullah Mujahid rejected that characterization. "Investigations are underway to determine who they were and what they were smuggling, but they are smugglers," he told the Associated Press. He said the area has long had problems with smuggling and that the Taliban government had already taken steps to address them.

The Afghan count also differed. Ehsanullah Kamgar, a spokesman for the Badakhshan provincial police command, said three people were killed — one fewer than Tajikistan's figure — and identified them as drug smugglers.

## Second incident in two months

The skirmish follows a reported cross-border attack in late November in which three Chinese workers were killed and a fourth was wounded. Tajikistan said that attack was carried out from inside Afghanistan with a drone equipped with grenades and firearms.

Afghan Foreign Ministry deputy spokesman Hafiz Zia Ahmad Takal said at the time that the November attack appeared to have been caused by "elements that are trying to create chaos, instability, and distrust between countries in the region." He said Kabul would offer "full cooperation with the government of Tajikistan."

## Uneasy diplomacy

Tajikistan cut ties with Afghanistan after the Taliban's takeover in 2021. Since then, both sides have taken incremental steps toward normalization: border markets reopened in 2023, and a Tajik delegation visited Kabul in November. Monday's skirmish and the competing official accounts it produced illustrate how much tension persists along a border where, as Mujahid put it, "there have always been problems."
