MARATHON, Greece — A priest cast a wooden cross into the visibly depleted waters of Lake Marathon on Tuesday as Orthodox Christians in Greece used Epiphany ceremonies to draw attention to a deepening water crisis threatening Athens. Authorities declared a water emergency in the Greek capital in November after reservoir levels fell sharply for a third consecutive year.

Water stored across Athens's four reservoirs has dropped from more than 1 billion cubic meters in 2022 to about 390 million cubic meters, according to George Stergiou, chairman of the greater Athens water utility EYDAP, who attended the ceremony at Lake Marathon, an artificial reservoir north of the capital.

"This is the third consecutive year of a significant drop in reservoir levels. This means that the problem is right in front of us," Stergiou told the Associated Press.

Stergiou warned that prolonged droughts are becoming more frequent and are often followed by intense rainfall that does little to replenish supplies.

## Investment program targets losses and reuse

EYDAP has announced a 2.5 billion euro ($2.9 billion), decade-long investment program to reduce water losses and boost water security. The plan includes pipe replacements, smart meters and expanded wastewater reuse.

"What we want to do, as a first step, is replace all those uses that today could be met with non-potable water by recycled water," Stergiou said.

Sofia Nalpantidou, the reservoir's manager, said Lake Marathon's low level should be understood in the context of its role within the wider supply network. The lake is connected to the broader system and partly managed for seasonal flood control.

"We usually keep it lower in winter because we want to maintain a buffer," Nalpantidou said.

## Residents note the change

Antonis Stamou, a 78-year-old retiree who attended Tuesday's service, said the shift in rainfall is unmistakable to those who have lived in the area for decades.

"In the past, it used to rain a lot more, sometimes daily. That doesn't happen anymore," Stamou said.
