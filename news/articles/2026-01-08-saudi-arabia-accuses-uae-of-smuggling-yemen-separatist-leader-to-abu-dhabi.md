The allegation marks the most direct public confrontation between Riyadh and Abu Dhabi in decades, as what was once a joint military coalition in Yemen has fractured into competing pressures over the fate of southern Yemen and its separatist movement.

Saudi Arabia accused the United Arab Emirates on Thursday of smuggling Aidarous al-Zubaidi — the leader of the Southern Transitional Council and a man charged with treason by Yemen's governing authority — out of the country and into Abu Dhabi, further straining a partnership between the two Gulf nations that has defined the yearslong Yemen war.

A Saudi military statement attributed to Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said al-Zubaidi left Yemen by boat to Somalia before being flown to the UAE capital on an Ilyushin Il-76 cargo aircraft. The UAE did not immediately respond. The STC said Wednesday — a day before the Saudi statement — that al-Zubaidi had remained in Aden, directly contradicting the Saudi account.

## The accusation and its unusual details

The Saudi statement named a UAE major general as involved in the alleged operation, identifying him by his nom de guerre — a level of public accusation rarely seen in Gulf Arab relations. It also noted that the Il-76 had previously been deployed to Ethiopia, Libya, and Somalia, routes along which the UAE military has faced prior accusations of funneling weapons. The UAE has denied running guns into those areas.

Saudi state media appeared to coordinate coverage of the incident. The satellite news channel Al Arabiya aired what it described as intercepted telephone calls related to al-Zubaidi's alleged escape. The state-backed newspaper Arab News published a front-page image of al-Zubaidi under a "WANTED" headline and a scathing editorial saying his refusal to travel to the kingdom was "cementing his image as a traitor to his country."

"Al-Zubaidi chose narrow self-interest, aligning with foreign powers at the expense of his homeland and attempting to impose southern secession by force," the editorial said.

## The split behind the accusation

The Southern Transitional Council, long backed by the UAE, has been a major armed faction in the coalition fighting Iran-backed Houthi rebels who have controlled Yemen's capital, Sanaa, for years. That coalition began fracturing in recent days after STC fighters advanced in two governorates in a move Saudi Arabia interpreted as a preparation for secession from Yemen.

Saudi Arabia responded with airstrikes against the STC and intercepted what the kingdom said was an arms shipment from the UAE. Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council — the governing body that has administered southern Yemen since 2022 and included al-Zubaidi — expelled him and charged him with treason after he declined to fly to Riyadh for talks.

## Diplomacy continues in Riyadh

Despite the public accusations, diplomatic activity continued Thursday. Saudi Arabia's ambassador to Yemen, Mohammed al-Jaber, said on X that he had met with the STC delegation that had landed in Riyadh a day earlier to discuss al-Zubaidi's recent actions, which he said "harmed the southern cause and didn't serve it."

"We also explored ways to work in the future to address what happened in a manner that serves the Southern cause, the Coalition's efforts to achieve security and stability in Yemen," al-Jaber said.

Mohamed al-Ghaithi, an STC member and head of the negotiation and reconciliation committee supporting the Presidential Leadership Council, called the meeting "fruitful" and said the delegation "rejected everything that harms unity." He added that the group had "heard clear commitments from our brothers in Riyadh toward our people's cause and ensuring a secure and stable future."

The STC had said Wednesday that it lost contact with its delegation after they landed in Riyadh, saying that members' phones were either switched off or ringing with no one answering.

## Somalia opens investigation

Somalia's Immigration and Citizenship Agency said Thursday it was launching an investigation into "the alleged unauthorized use of Somalia's national airspace and airport," calling it unacceptable and a violation of its sovereignty. The agency did not name an external actor in its statement but said any attempt by al-Zubaidi to evade Saudi Arabia's call for dialogue through alleged external support would be a breach of bilateral agreements.

## International attention

Hans Grundberg, the U.N. special envoy for Yemen, met Thursday with Presidential Leadership Council members in Riyadh. Grundberg said an anticipated conference expected to be hosted by Saudi Arabia "offers a timely opportunity to reduce tensions, address long-standing grievances through political means, and move discussions towards stabilization."

Saudi Arabia's foreign minister met with U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio in Washington on Wednesday. The United States has conducted airstrikes against Houthi rebels under both former President Joe Biden and President Donald Trump.

## Background on the conflict

The war in Yemen, on the southern edge of the Arabian Peninsula bordering the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden, has killed more than 150,000 people, including fighters and civilians, according to the Associated Press, and created one of the world's worst humanitarian disasters. The Houthis have also launched attacks on commercial shipping in connection with the Israel-Hamas war in Gaza, disrupting a vital route for global commerce.

Saudi Arabia and the UAE are OPEC members and neighboring states that have increasingly competed over economic issues and regional politics, particularly in the Red Sea area. The Yemen dispute has become their most serious confrontation in decades.
