Deep in the Ecuadorian Andes, the Yanacocha Reserve shelters one of the world's most threatened birds. The Black-breasted puffleg, measuring just 3.5 inches long, has dwindled to between 150 and 200 individuals globally, according to the International Union for the Conservation of Nature. The emblematic hummingbird of Quito occupies a narrow elevation band between 9,800 and 11,400 feet, where high-altitude forests are rapidly disappearing to agricultural conversion.

The Black-breasted puffleg's isolation in this narrow elevation band makes it especially vulnerable, and conservationists say its fate exemplifies larger threats to Andean biodiversity from agricultural pressure.

The Yanacocha Reserve, founded 25 years ago by the Jocotoco Foundation on Pichincha volcano's slopes about 27 miles northwest of Quito, now shelters nearly all remaining Black-breasted pufflegs.

## The Reserve's Broader Mission

"We realized we were conserving an entire ecosystem, not just one species," conservationist Paola Villalba said.

## A Specialized Species

The bird displays distinctive white "trousers" of feathers around its legs, contrasting sharply with its deep, metallic black chest and bronze-green wings. It occupies an exceedingly narrow ecological niche, found only between 3,000 and 3,500 meters (9,800 and 11,400 feet) above sea level.

## Agricultural Pressure

Shirley Farinango of the Birds and Conservation Foundation notes that this specific elevation range constitutes "prime territory" for agricultural conversion. High-altitude forest clearing across the region for grazing and agriculture has steadily shrunk the habitat available to a species that cannot survive at lower elevations.

## Restoration Efforts

Conservationists working on the reserve are now racing to restore cloud-shrouded forest habitat as the window for saving the species narrows. The restoration effort extends work that began with the reserve's founding, when the foundation recognized that protecting the puffleg meant protecting the entire cloud forest ecosystem it inhabits.
