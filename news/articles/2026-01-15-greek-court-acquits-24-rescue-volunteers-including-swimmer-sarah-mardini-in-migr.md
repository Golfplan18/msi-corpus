A Greek court on the island of Lesbos acquitted 24 humanitarian rescue volunteers Thursday of charges related to aiding migrants at sea, ending a case that international human rights organizations had criticized for more than seven years. Among those cleared was Syrian competitive swimmer Sarah Mardini, whose story of fleeing war and rescuing fellow refugees at sea inspired the 2022 Netflix film "The Swimmers." The court found all defendants not guilty of facilitating illegal entry and forming a criminal organization — charges the prosecutor had already urged be dropped for lack of evidence.

The ruling comes as Greece and several other European Union governments are intensifying crackdowns on irregular migration and expanding deportation programs, prompting rights groups to call on governments to protect, rather than prosecute, humanitarian rescue work at sea.

LESBOS, Greece — A Greek court acquitted 24 humanitarian rescue volunteers Thursday of charges related to aiding migrants on the island of Lesbos, closing a case that human rights organizations had criticized for subjecting aid workers to criminal prosecution for saving lives at sea.

The court found all defendants not guilty of facilitating illegal entry and forming a criminal organization. The prosecution had already urged acquittal before the verdict, saying the charges lacked evidence and noting that one defendant routinely alerted Greek port authorities to approaching vessels.

Among those cleared was Syrian competitive swimmer Sarah Mardini, who was arrested in 2018 and whose ordeal inspired the 2022 Netflix sporting drama "The Swimmers." Mardini spent more than three months in pre-trial detention following her arrest.

"We didn't do anything illegal," Mardini said. "If helping people is a crime, then we are all guilty."

Supporters clapped and cheered as the acquitted volunteers emerged from the courthouse. Many of the group, after spending hours inside the courtroom, went for a celebratory nighttime swim in the island port.

The 24 defendants were volunteers with Emergency Response Centre International, a Greek nonprofit. Thursday's ruling follows an earlier acquittal on related charges in separate trials.

Eva Cosse, a senior researcher at Human Rights Watch, welcomed the decision but called it sobering. "The acquittals are a vindication for the defendants but are also bittersweet: Two dozen people were subjected to a seven-year legal ordeal on baseless charges for saving lives," she said.

Mardini's case drew particular attention because of her own experience as a refugee. She fled Syria in 2015 with her younger sister Yusra Mardini, both competitive swimmers. The two survived a dangerous sea crossing from Turkey to Lesbos, during which they described swimming for hours to keep an overcrowded dinghy afloat.

Yusra Mardini went on to compete in the first refugee team at the 2016 Olympic Games in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. After settling in Germany, Sarah Mardini returned to Lesbos for volunteer work before her 2018 arrest.

The verdict was announced as Greece and several other European Union governments are intensifying crackdowns on irregular migration, with plans to expand deportations for people whose asylum claims are rejected.

Eve Geddie, a senior official at Amnesty International, urged European governments to protect humanitarian assistance. "We hope today's decision sends a strong signal to Greece and other European countries that solidarity, compassion and defending human rights should be protected and celebrated, not punished," she said.
