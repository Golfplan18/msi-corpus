## Body

The Trump administration’s strategy against Cuba is being discussed as closely aligned with the playbook the United States used to pressure Venezuela, a comparison that hinges on repeated threats, criminal indictments, and coercive energy measures. But experts cited by Associated Press said the United States may not get the same results because Cuba and Venezuela differ in ways that affect how pressure lands, including how leadership functions and what kind of military posture the U.S. is able to sustain.

Brian Finucane, a senior adviser with the International Crisis Group and a former State Department lawyer, said Trump’s approach to Venezuela was treated internally as a success and that the administration has tried to repeat the model. Finucane told AP that Trump “viewed the Venezuelan intervention as a fantastic success,” and that he has sought to replicate it “elsewhere,” though Finucane cautioned that Cuba is “a very different country than Venezuela.” <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

Finucane also pointed to what he described as the political differences that would matter if U.S. planners aimed to remove Cuba’s leadership. He said there is no obvious successor in Cuba who would work with the Trump administration, contrasting that with Venezuela, where U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro in January and where Delcy Rodríguez stepped in with U.S. approval and remains in power. Cuban officials who were not authorized to comment publicly and spoke on condition of anonymity told AP, however, that “there is no Delcy in Cuba.” <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

The U.S. threats phase also overlaps in the comparison laid out by AP. In the Venezuela campaign, Trump began laying groundwork months before military action, including public warnings aimed at Caribbean leaders to “get in line or face American might,” AP reported. AP said Trump stood with senior national security advisers in Florida before an operation that removed Maduro from power, and that in December he warned Maduro with a conditional threat, saying: “If he wants to do something, if he plays tough, it’ll be the last time he’ll ever be able to play tough.” <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

After Maduro was taken to the United States for trial, AP reported that Trump’s focus shifted to other countries in the region, including Cuba. AP said that on Jan. 5 Trump told reporters, “Cuba is ready to fall. Cuba looks like it’s ready to fall. I don’t know if they’re going to hold out.” AP also reported that Trump threatened tariffs on any country that sells or supplies oil to Cuba, and said the U.S. might have “the honor of taking Cuba” following military operations in Venezuela and Iran; on Thursday, AP reported, he repeated threats while calling Cuba “a failed country.” <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

Energy pressure forms the other major similarity in AP’s side-by-side account, but with a key difference in how each target is squeezed. AP reported that U.S. oil embargoes were designed to produce intense pressure on ruling elites, while pushing through diametrically opposite pathways: in Venezuela, the administration initially targeted oil exports to starve Maduro of revenue, then shifted after Maduro’s ouster to limiting Venezuela’s ability to export oil to certain countries, with much of its crude sent through U.S. refineries. <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

For Cuba, AP said the embargo is aimed at starving the energy-strapped country of oil imports, while the U.S. has allowed some limited shipments to reach the island. AP reported that the broader U.S. blockade, which has been in place for decades, has made it harder for the Cuban government to provide electricity and gasoline. The measures could also carry risks beyond the intended economic squeeze, Finucane told AP, warning that destabilization could contribute to a potential refugee crisis and drawing parallels to Cubans who traveled to Florida in makeshift boats in the 1990s. <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

AP said the two pressure campaigns also share a pattern of bringing federal charges against figures at the center of power. During Trump’s first term, the Justice Department charged Maduro with narco-terrorism conspiracy and other counts in 2020, and AP reported that the case was used to justify capturing Maduro. In that account, charging and pursuing Maduro changed how Venezuela related to the United States, allowing the sale of previously sanctioned Venezuelan oil to U.S. companies and global markets after years of broad blocking of dealings with Venezuela’s government and oil sector. <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

In Cuba, AP reported that an indictment against Raúl Castro over the 1996 shootdown of civilian planes flown by Miami-based exiles marks an escalation step in the Trump administration’s campaign. William LeoGrande, a professor who specializes in Latin American politics at American University, told AP that the immediate aim of charging Castro “over the 1996 shootdown of civilian planes flown by Miami-based exiles” is to take another step up the ladder of escalation. But LeoGrande said charging Castro would not change how the Cuban government operates day to day, adding that Castro “still has influence and the leadership seeks his opinion on major decisions, but he is not running the government on a day-to-day basis.” <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

Finally, AP said the military footprint element in the comparison is real but not equal. For Venezuela, AP reported that in the months before Maduro was captured, the U.S. dispatched a fleet of warships near Venezuela in what became its largest military buildup in Latin America in generations, including the USS Gerald R. Ford aircraft carrier and three amphibious assault ships carrying around 2,000 Marines, with fighter jets flying over the Gulf of Venezuela and months of strikes against small boats accused of smuggling drugs. AP said the actual mission to capture Maduro involved more than 150 aircraft launched across the Western Hemisphere. <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>

In Cuba, Finucane told AP that the U.S. currently has a smaller force in the Caribbean Sea. AP reported that the Caribbean now includes two amphibious assault ships with Marines and that the U.S. touted the arrival of the USS Nimitz aircraft carrier and accompanying warships on the same day charges against Castro were announced this week. AP said the Nimitz is on its last-ever tour, taking part in maritime exercises in the region before being decommissioned. Finucane summed up the mismatch in scale and assumptions, telling AP: “They’re very different situations, and it’s very difficult to see similar outcomes,” adding that a “snatch-and-grab raid” against Raúl Castro or another leader would not be expected to produce the same results in Cuba as in Venezuela. <a href="https://apnews.com/article/cuba-strategy-venezuela-trump-pressure-campaign-a7555abe7f38de0e94129ca6abc3afcf">src_001</a>
