The landslide adds to an acute humanitarian emergency in eastern Congo, where decades of armed conflict — including a recent escalation by the Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group — has displaced more than 7 million people and left the region's emergency-response capacity severely stretched.

A landslide struck the village of Burutsi in North Kivu province of eastern Congo at about 1 a.m. Tuesday, killing at least 13 people and leaving more than 30 missing, local authorities said. The disaster followed several hours of intense rainfall and severed the main road connecting Goma, the region's major city, to the provincial capital of Walikale, blocking emergency access to the site.

"Nature acted terribly, and the entire hillside collapsed into the village of Burutsi while ... people were asleep," Descarte Akilimali, the sector chief of the Burutsi area, told The Associated Press.

Local officials said they had requested assistance from the national government, but the road closure complicated the government's ability to respond.

## A region under compounded strain

The disaster struck one of the world's most acute humanitarian zones. More than 100 armed groups compete for control of mineral-rich eastern Congo near the border with Rwanda, and more than 7 million people have been displaced by the protracted conflict, officials said.

Walikale was captured in an offensive by the Rwanda-backed M23 rebel group last year, and the surrounding area has continued to experience violence. M23 forces also seized Goma and Bukavu, two key cities in eastern Congo, in what the Associated Press described as a major escalation of the conflict.
