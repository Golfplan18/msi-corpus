Brazil's President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva on Thursday vetoed a bill that would have reduced the 27-year prison sentence of his predecessor Jair Bolsonaro, who was convicted over a failed 2023 coup attempt.

Lula, who had pledged to block the proposal, announced the decision at a ceremony at the presidential palace in Brasília. The event fell on the third anniversary of the Jan. 8, 2023 riots, in which Bolsonaro's supporters stormed and destroyed Congress, the Supreme Court, and the presidential palace itself.

"We don't have the right of forgetting our past. That's why we don't accept dictatorships, civilian or military," Lula told members of Brazil's three branches of power assembled for the ceremony. "Jan. 8 is marked by history as the day of our democracy's victory. It is victory against those who tried to seize power through force, ignoring popular will expressed in the polls."

## What the bill would have done

Had Lula not acted, the charge of attempting a coup d'état would have been absorbed into a separate charge under the bill's terms, effectively reducing Bolsonaro's sentence. Other convicts connected to the coup trial could have seen their jail time cut by two-thirds.

Brazil's Senate passed the bill in December. Congress retains the ability to override Lula's veto, though analysts said doing so would be politically risky for lawmakers ahead of October's general elections. Lula, 80, is considered a front-runner in a likely contest against Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro, one of the former president's sons.

## Supreme Court weighs in

Brazil's Supreme Court Chief Justice Luiz Edson Fachin, speaking at a separate ceremony at the court, said the 2023 riots illustrated how "the democratic rule of law is in crisis in the contemporary world." Fachin did not name Bolsonaro or the military officials jailed in connection with the plot.

"It is necessary to resist, always within democratic boundaries, and our pathway must be our institutions. Brazil has given a great example of resilience," Fachin said.

The Supreme Court said it has opened more than 1,700 lawsuits against participants in the riots, including both organizers and individual protesters.

## Political divisions on display

Lower House Speaker Hugo Motta and Senate President Davi Alcolumbre did not attend Lula's ceremony.

Sen. Esperidião Amin, a Bolsonaro supporter, said hours after the event that he would seek an amnesty bill for those involved in the coup attempt — a move Brazilian Supreme Court justices have already said would be unconstitutional.

Bolsonaro, 70, began serving his sentence in November 2025 following his conviction, which extended his ban from running for office until 2033. He had already been ruled ineligible to run until 2030 for abuse of power during the 2022 campaign. Bolsonaro's lawyers have urged authorities to place him on house arrest because of his poor health; he has been hospitalized repeatedly since being stabbed during the 2018 campaign. Neither Bolsonaro nor his lawyers made public comments about the veto.
