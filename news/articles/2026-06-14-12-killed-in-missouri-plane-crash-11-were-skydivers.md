## Summary

**Subtype:** fact

- A private plane crashed near Butler, Missouri, shortly after departing Butler Memorial Airport on June 14, killing all 12 people on board.
- Eleven of the victims were skydivers; the 12th was the pilot, according to sources cited by Fox 4.
- Multiple agencies responded, including the Missouri State Highway Patrol, Butler police, Bates County sheriff's office, the FAA, and the NTSB.
- The aircraft turned back toward the airport before crashing near Business 49 Highway, Bates County emergency management reported.

The crash occurred just before 11:30 a.m. Sunday, according to Bates County emergency management, after the plane departed Butler Memorial Airport. The aircraft turned back before going down near Business 49 Highway, Fox 4 reported, citing emergency management officials.

The Missouri State Highway Patrol confirmed the fatalities in a social media post, stating that "all occupants (12 total) have perished." Troopers were on scene assisting the Butler Police Department and the Bates County Sheriff's Office, the patrol said.

The National Transportation Safety Board and the Federal Aviation Administration are among the agencies responding to the scene, according to KMBC. The NTSB will lead the investigation into the cause of the crash.

Videos posted online appeared to show a heavily damaged aircraft in a grassy area, with plumes of white smoke rising from the wreckage.

The Guardian has sought additional comment from the Butler Police Department, the NTSB, and the FAA.
