Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Wednesday that he will travel to China from Jan. 13 to 17 to meet with President Xi Jinping, the first visit by a Canadian prime minister to China in more than eight years. The trip comes as Ottawa moves to reduce its economic reliance on the United States, which has threatened Canada with tariffs and claimed it could become the "51st state."

The visit marks a significant diplomatic pivot for Canada, which sends more than 75 percent of its exports to the U.S. and now faces pressure to diversify as the North American free trade agreement comes up for review this year.

"We're forging new partnerships around the world to transform our economy from one that has been reliant on a single trade partner, to one that is stronger and more resilient to global shock," Carney said in a statement on Wednesday.

Xi invited Carney to Beijing when the two leaders met at an Asia-Pacific summit in October. China is Canada's second largest trading partner, and Carney has set a goal of doubling Canada's non-U.S. exports in the next decade.

## A Strained Relationship

Relations between the two countries deteriorated sharply in late 2018 after Canadian authorities arrested a senior executive of Chinese tech giant Huawei Technologies as part of an extradition agreement with the United States. China arrested two Canadian citizens in retaliation.

More recently, Canada levied a 100 percent tariff on electric vehicles, batteries, and other goods from China in 2024, acting in tandem with the United States. China responded with steep tariffs on Canadian canola, seafood, and pork.

China has since offered to remove its import taxes on some Canadian products if Canada drops the EV tariff, the Associated Press reported.

## Davos to Follow

After the Beijing visit, Carney is scheduled to attend the World Economic Forum Annual Meeting in Davos, Switzerland from Jan. 19 to 21, extending a foreign-engagement push as Ottawa recalibrates its trade relationships.
