A Fulton County judge on December 31, 2025, dismissed racketeering charges against 61 defendants accused of conspiring to halt construction of an Atlanta-area police and firefighter training facility that critics call "Cop City," ruling that Georgia's Republican attorney general lacked authority to bring the case without the governor's approval.

Judge Kevin Farmer said Attorney General Chris Carr needed permission from Gov. Brian Kemp before securing the 2023 indictments under Georgia's Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations law, The Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported. Carr's office said it plans to appeal.

The ruling came in what legal experts described as the largest criminal racketeering case ever filed against protesters in U.S. history, one in which each of the 61 defendants faced up to 20 years in prison.

## Scope of the case

Allegations against the 61 defendants ranged from throwing Molotov cocktails at police officers to providing food and other support to protesters. Five defendants also face separate charges of domestic terrorism and first-degree arson stemming from a 2023 incident in which masked activists burned a police car in downtown Atlanta and threw rocks at a skyscraper occupied by the Atlanta Police Foundation.

Farmer ruled that Carr also lacked authority to pursue the arson charge, but said the domestic terrorism charge can likely stand, The Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported.

Amanda Clark Palmer, an attorney for one of the defendants, said the ruling confirmed procedural failures in the prosecution's approach.

"The prosecution did not follow the law when filing these charges," Clark Palmer said, according to The Atlanta Journal-Constitution.

Clark Palmer said the defendants' relief remained incomplete while the appeal question remained open.

"We are relieved the dismissal order has been entered but our relief is not complete yet as we wait to see whether the Attorney General will appeal," she said in a statement.

Carr's office expressed firm opposition to the ruling. "We strongly disagree with this decision and will continue to vigorously pursue this domestic terrorism case to ensure that justice is served," the office said in a statement.

## Background

The dispute over the 85-acre (34-hectare) training facility — which ultimately cost more than $115 million — intensified in January 2023 when state troopers killed a 26-year-old activist known as "Tortuguita" during a sweep of the South River Forest near the construction site. Authorities said Tortuguita had fired at troopers while inside a tent. A prosecutor found the troopers' actions "objectively reasonable." Tortuguita's family filed a lawsuit alleging his hands were in the air and that troopers used excessive force when they initially fired pepper balls into the tent.

Protests followed the killing. Masked vandals at times targeted police vehicles and construction equipment in efforts to stall the project and pressure contractors to withdraw. Opponents also pursued civic channels, packing City Council meetings and mounting a referendum effort that became entangled in the courts.

## Political context

Carr, who is running for governor, had pursued the case. Kemp had called the prosecution an important step to combat "out-of-state radicals that threaten the safety of our citizens and law enforcement." Critics had described the indictment as a politically motivated, heavy-handed effort to suppress the movement against the facility.
