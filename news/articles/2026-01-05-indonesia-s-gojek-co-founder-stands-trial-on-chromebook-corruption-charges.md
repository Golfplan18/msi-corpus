Jakarta's Corruption Court on Monday opened the trial of Nadiem Anwar Makarim, co-founder of Indonesian ride-hailing and payments giant Gojek and a former education minister, on charges that he manipulated a nationwide government procurement of Google Chromebook laptops for schools to benefit himself and Gojek's parent company.

Makarim, 41, served as education, culture, research and technology minister from 2019 to 2024 under former President Joko Widodo. He was arrested September 7 after the attorney general's office in Jakarta opened an investigation into alleged corruption linked to the government's "digitalization of schools" initiative.

## The indictment

Prosecutors alleged Makarim steered the Chromebook procurement in 2020 and 2021 "entirely for personal business interests," pressing Google to invest in PT Aplikasi Karya Anak Bangsa — known as PT AKAB — the parent company of Gojek.

"The procurement ignored proper pricing benchmarks and technical needs, especially for remote or under-resourced regions," lead prosecutor Muhammad Fadli Paramajeng told a three-judge panel at the Corruption Court on Monday.

The purchase of more than 1.2 million Chromebooks was, according to prosecutors, designed to strengthen Google's position in Indonesia's education technology market and was tied to subsequent Google investments of about $787 million in PT AKAB through Google Asia Pacific.

Prosecutors claimed Makarim received about 809 billion rupiah — approximately $48.2 million — in connection with the program. He faces a possible sentence of life imprisonment under Indonesia's 2001 Corruption Law for allegedly causing state losses and misusing public funds.

Prosecutors said Makarim's own ministry research team had refused to recommend the Chromebook model, concluding it was ineffective in regions lacking reliable internet access. Makarim overrode those objections, according to the indictment.

## Background and alleged conflicts

Makarim, a Harvard University graduate, co-founded Gojek in 2009. The company was valued at more than $10 billion when he left in 2019 to join the Widodo cabinet.

Prosecutors alleged that Makarim's departure from PT AKAB and Gojek before taking ministerial office was a "strategic concealment" to mask conflicts of interest. They said he appointed close associates as directors and "beneficial owners" of the company, allowing him to maintain indirect control over company decisions despite formally divesting.

## Defense

Makarim has denied the allegations, saying he did not personally receive funds from the Chromebook procurement or related services.

"The defendant was not involved in the procurement process, as his role was limited solely to formulating policy," defense lawyer Ari Yusuf Amir told the court.

Amir called the indictment "unclear, inaccurate and incomplete," arguing it conflated Makarim's ministerial authority with decisions made by other government officials. Defense attorneys also argued that Google's investment in PT AKAB largely predated Makarim's ministerial tenure and represented routine corporate activity unconnected to the laptop deal. They noted Makarim divested from PT AKAB upon taking office and that his personal wealth fell by more than 50% during his term.

Two former education ministry officials and a former technology consultant were also charged in connection with the case. A fourth staff member is wanted by authorities but remains at large.
