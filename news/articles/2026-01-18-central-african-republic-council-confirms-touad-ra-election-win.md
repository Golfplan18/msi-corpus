The Central African Republic’s Constitutional Council on Monday confirmed President Faustin-Archange Touadéra’s election victory and granted him a third term, rejecting an appeal by the opposition that alleged widespread fraud and other irregularities.

The council said Touadéra secured 77.9% of the vote, a modest increase from 76.15% reported in provisional results earlier this month. It said the main opposition candidate, Anicet Georges Dologuélé, received 13.1%, down slightly from 14.66% previously announced.

The council also dismissed Dologuélé’s appeal to annul the election, citing a lack of supporting documents.

Dologuélé had challenged the results on Jan. 6, citing instances of alleged malpractice by the National Elections Authority and widespread fraud. Earlier this month, he proclaimed himself the winner of the election.

The opposition’s Union for Central African Renewal said in a Facebook statement that it accepted the ruling in “a” spirit of republican responsibility, while adding that its struggle would continue. Yves Sanghamy, the spokesperson for Dologuélé, said: “Our fight has never been one of disorder. It is about electoral truth, the sovereignty of the people and respect for democratic rules.”

Touadéra, 68, faced challenges from six candidates, but the main opposition coalition did not participate after denouncing what it called an unequal political environment. Analysts said Touadéra has consolidated power within state institutions.

The Associated Press reported that a referendum removing term limits was approved in 2023, allowing Touadéra to run for a third term. It also reported that some 2.4 million Central African Republic citizens were registered to vote in the election, described as the first of its kind in the country, with voters choosing simultaneously for presidential, legislative, regional and municipal seats.

The Central African Republic has been embroiled in conflict since 2013, when mostly Muslim rebels seized power and forced then-President François Bozizé out of office. The conflict was de-escalated by a 2019 peace deal between the government and 14 armed groups, with six of those groups later withdrawing from the agreement.

The AP said the country has been among those where Wagner, a Russian mercenary group, was first active in Africa, and that Wagner has been responsible for the president’s security. It reported that tensions have grown between Touadéra and Russia after Moscow demanded that Wagner be replaced with the Russian government-run Africa Corps.

Touadéra’s inauguration is scheduled for March 30.
