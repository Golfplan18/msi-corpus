VICTORIA, British Columbia — British Columbia will not renew the federal exemption underpinning its drug decriminalization experiment, Health Minister Josie Osborne announced Wednesday, ending a three-year pilot set to expire Jan. 31.

Osborne said the project's aim had been to make it easier for people to come forward and seek help, but it "hasn't delivered the results" officials hoped for.

The decision makes British Columbia the second North American jurisdiction within roughly a year to end a decriminalization experiment. Oregon voted in 2020 to become the first U.S. state to decriminalize small amounts of hard drugs, then recriminalized that possession in 2024.

## How the pilot worked

The project began in January 2023 after Health Canada granted British Columbia an exemption under the federal Controlled Drugs and Substances Act. The exemption initially allowed adults to possess up to 2.5 grams cumulatively of opioids, crack and powdered cocaine, methamphetamine and MDMA.

The scope narrowed significantly in 2024, when the exemption was amended to restrict decriminalized possession to private homes, locations where homeless people are legally sheltering, and designated health-care clinics, overdose prevention sites, drug checking sites and supervised consumption sites.

## What the province says comes next

Osborne said the government is focused on "strengthening the approaches that are helping people get timely, appropriate care."

"We are building a more complete and comprehensive system of mental-health and addictions care in B.C., including prevention, treatment and recovery, harm reduction and aftercare," she said in a statement.

## International context

Portugal became the first country in the world to decriminalize the consumption of all drugs in 2001 and has maintained that policy since, offering a longer-running reference point for jurisdictions weighing similar approaches.
