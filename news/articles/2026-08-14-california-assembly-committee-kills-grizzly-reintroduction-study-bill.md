## California Assembly committee kills grizzly reintroduction study bill

**Subtype:** fact

- The California Assembly Appropriations Committee killed Senate Bill 1305 on Thursday.
- SB 1305 would have directed the California Department of Fish and Wildlife to study grizzly bear reintroduction.
- Democratic Sen. Laura Richardson of Inglewood authored the bill.
- Committee chair Buffy Wicks cited gray wolf reintroduction and subsequent cattle losses as a cautionary example.

## Wicks cited gray wolf reintroduction and cattle losses

The California Assembly Appropriations Committee on Thursday killed Senate Bill 1305, a measure that would have tasked the California Department of Fish and Wildlife with creating a "roadmap that evaluates whether, and under what conditions, reintroduction of the grizzly bear is feasible and advisable."

The bill was authored by Democratic Sen. Laura Richardson of Inglewood. The proposal would have directed the wildlife agency to evaluate the feasibility of returning grizzly bears to California, rather than to carry out any reintroduction directly.

As is typical with the Legislature's appropriations committees, lawmakers did not discuss the bill or take public testimony before Democratic Assemblymember Buffy Wicks of Oakland, the committee's chairperson, announced the decision.

Wicks later told reporters that before intentionally reintroducing grizzly bears, the state must figure out how humans can best coexist with predatory wildlife. She pointed to the controversial return of gray wolves to California and the subsequent loss of cattle.
