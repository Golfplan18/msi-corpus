DODOMA, Tanzania — Tanzania President Samia Suluhu Hassan on Thursday addressed the country's foreign diplomatic corps for the first time since a disputed October election, expressing sympathy over a six-day internet shutdown imposed amid postelection violence that rights groups say left hundreds dead.

Hassan's remarks came three months after an election in which she won more than 97 percent of the vote — a contest foreign observers said failed to meet democratic standards after the two main opposition candidates were barred from running and the country's leading opposition figure remained jailed on treason charges.

Speaking to ambassadors, high commissioners, and representatives of international organizations in the capital, Hassan said the government would strive to prevent a recurrence of the disruption.

"To our partners in the diplomatic community and foreigners residing here in Tanzania, I express my sincere sympathy for the uncertainty, service restrictions and internet shutdowns you experienced," Hassan said.

Violence broke out on election day and continued for days as the internet remained cut amid a heavy police crackdown. Rights groups say hundreds of people were killed and property worth millions of shillings was destroyed in turmoil that struck a country that had enjoyed relative calm for decades, according to the Associated Press.

Hassan blamed the violence on foreigners and pardoned hundreds of young people who had been arrested, saying they had acted under peer pressure.

The president on Thursday defended the shutdown and security measures, saying her administration had taken them to preserve constitutional order and protect citizens. She also sought to reassure envoys of their physical safety going forward.

"I assure you that we will remain vigilant to ensure your safety and prevent any recurrence of such experiences," Hassan told the assembled diplomats.

Since the October vote, Tanzania has established a commission of inquiry to investigate the violence and its causes.
