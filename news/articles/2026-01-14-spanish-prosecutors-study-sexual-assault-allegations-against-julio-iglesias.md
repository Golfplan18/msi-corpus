Spanish prosecutors are examining allegations that Grammy-winning singer Julio Iglesias sexually assaulted two former employees at his residences in the Dominican Republic and the Bahamas, the country's National Court prosecutors' office confirmed to the Associated Press on Wednesday. The allegations, brought on behalf of the two women by a nongovernmental organization, cover alleged conduct between January and October 2021 and include charges of sexual harassment, human trafficking for forced labor, and systematic labor abuses.

BARCELONA, Spain — Spanish prosecutors are examining allegations that 82-year-old singer Julio Iglesias sexually assaulted two former employees at his residences in the Dominican Republic and the Bahamas, the country's National Court prosecutors' office confirmed to the Associated Press on Wednesday.

The case invokes a provision of Spanish law allowing the National Court in Madrid to try crimes alleged to have been committed abroad by Spanish citizens, offering the two women a potential legal path even though the alleged incidents occurred in the Caribbean.

## The allegations

Women's Link Worldwide, a nongovernmental organization representing the two women, said it filed a formal complaint with the Spanish prosecutors' office on Jan. 5. The group said the women accuse Iglesias of "crimes against sexual freedom and indemnity such as sexual harassment" and of "human trafficking for the purpose of forced labor and servitude."

The women also alleged in their testimony that Iglesias regularly checked their cellphones, prohibited them from leaving the house where they worked, and demanded they work up to 16 hours a day with no contract or days off, according to the NGO. The alleged conduct occurred between January and October 2021.

Jovana Ríos Cisneros, executive director of Women's Link Worldwide, said Spanish prosecutors have agreed to take statements from the two women and granted them the status of protected witnesses.

"Being heard by the Prosecutor's Office is a very important step in the search for justice," Ríos Cisneros said.

## Legal path chosen

Gema Fernández, senior attorney at Women's Link Worldwide, said at an online press conference Wednesday that "Spanish legislation regarding sexual violence, gender-based violence and trafficking could be an interesting option" for the two women.

"Listening to what (the two women) are seeking and their definitions of justice, it seems to us that filing a complaint with the Public Prosecutor's Office of the National Court of Spain was the path that best suited their definition of justice. That is why we are supporting them along this path," Fernández said.

Prosecutors have not set a date to take statements from the women. Under Spanish law, prosecutors have up to six months — extendable to one year — to determine whether the information warrants a criminal prosecution, Fernández said.

Women's Link Worldwide said it did not contact authorities in the Bahamas or the Dominican Republic and did not know whether those nations had initiated investigations. Panky Corcino, spokesman for the Dominican Republic attorney general's office, declined to comment, saying he could not confirm or deny an investigation.

## Official response in Spain

The allegations, first published in a joint investigation by Spanish online newspaper elDiario.es and Univision Noticias, drew a response from Spain's left-wing government.

Spanish government spokeswoman Elma Saiz said Tuesday, after the media reports were published, that they "demanded respect."

"Once again I can reaffirm this government's firm and complete commitment to take on any act of violence, harassment or aggression against women," Saiz said.

Culture Minister Ernest Urtasun said Wednesday that the government is studying whether to strip Iglesias of the state's Gold Medal of Merit in the Fine Arts, which he received in 2010.

"It is something we are studying and evaluating, because evidently we feel obliged to do so when faced by such a serious case," Urtasun said.

## Iglesias has not responded

Iglesias has not spoken publicly regarding the allegations. Russell L. King, a Miami-based entertainment lawyer who lists Iglesias as a client on his website, did not respond to a request for comment from the AP.

## Background

Iglesias, born in Spain in 1943, is one of the world's most commercially successful recording artists, having sold more than 300 million records in more than a dozen languages. He built his international following in the 1970s and '80s, winning a Grammy in 1988 for Best Latin Pop Performance for his album "Un Hombre Solo" and receiving a Lifetime Achievement Award at the Grammys in 2019. He is the father of pop singer Enrique Iglesias.
