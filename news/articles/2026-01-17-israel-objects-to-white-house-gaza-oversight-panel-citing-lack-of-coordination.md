Israel's government on Saturday publicly objected to the White House's announcement of an international executive committee to oversee next steps in Gaza, saying the panel "was not coordinated with Israel and is contrary to its policy." Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu directed the foreign ministry to contact Secretary of State Marco Rubio over the dispute. The rebuke came one day after the White House unveiled the committee, which includes senior Trump administration officials, a former British prime minister, and diplomats from several Middle Eastern states.

The dispute signals friction between close allies at a pivotal moment as the Gaza ceasefire moves toward its second phase, which calls for Hamas disarmament, deployment of an international security force, and reconstruction of the war-damaged territory — steps that require cooperation Israel has not yet committed to provide.

## Committee composition

The White House announced the executive committee on Friday as part of what it described as a Trump-led "Board of Peace," whose full membership has not yet been named. The committee as announced includes Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Trump envoy Steve Witkoff, presidential son-in-law Jared Kushner, former British Prime Minister Tony Blair, Apollo Global Management CEO Marc Rowan, World Bank President Ajay Banga, and Trump's Deputy National Security Adviser Robert Gabriel.

Regional representation includes a diplomat from Qatar, an intelligence chief from Egypt, Turkey's foreign minister, and a Cabinet minister from the United Arab Emirates — all countries that served as ceasefire mediators. Israeli billionaire businessman Yakir Gabay is the sole Israeli-linked figure on the panel; no Israeli government official was included.

The White House also announced the members of a new Palestinian committee to run Gaza's day-to-day affairs, with oversight from the executive committee. That Palestinian committee met for the first time on Thursday in Cairo.

Netanyahu's office did not respond to questions Saturday about its specific objections to the committee.

## Israeli right-wing reaction

Minutes after Israel's statement, far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir backed Netanyahu and, in a separate statement, urged the military to prepare to return to war. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich, another far-right Netanyahu ally, said on social media that "the countries that kept Hamas alive cannot be the ones that replace it."

Palestinian Islamic Jihad, Gaza's second-largest armed group after Hamas, also expressed dissatisfaction Saturday with the committee's makeup and claimed in a statement that it reflected Israeli "specifications."

## Second phase and Hamas position

The Trump administration said on Wednesday that the U.S.-drafted ceasefire plan was moving into its second phase, which includes the new Palestinian administrative committee, deployment of an international security force, disarmament of Hamas, and reconstruction of the territory. Hamas has said it will dissolve its government in Gaza once the new Palestinian committee takes office, but it has shown no sign that it will dismantle its military wing or security forces.

Turkey, which holds strained relations with Israel but good relations with Hamas, was included on the executive committee and could play a role in persuading Hamas to yield power and disarm, according to the Associated Press.

## Ceasefire context and casualties

The ceasefire took effect on Oct. 10. Its first phase focused on the return of all remaining hostages in exchange for the release of hundreds of Palestinian detainees, along with a surge in humanitarian aid and a partial withdrawal of Israeli forces from Gaza.

The war began with the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on Oct. 7, 2023, which killed some 1,200 people, mostly civilians, and took more than 250 hostage. Israel's retaliatory offensive has killed over 71,400 Palestinians, according to Gaza's Health Ministry; more than 460 of those deaths occurred after the ceasefire took effect. The ministry, part of the Hamas-run government, maintains detailed casualty records that U.N. agencies and independent experts consider generally reliable.

Ran Gvili, who was killed during the Oct. 7 attack, is the last hostage whose remains are believed to be held in Gaza. His parents, Talik and Itzik Gvili, said Saturday in a statement that they were "deeply concerned by moves toward the rehabilitation of Gaza" while Hamas had not fulfilled its obligation to return everyone.
