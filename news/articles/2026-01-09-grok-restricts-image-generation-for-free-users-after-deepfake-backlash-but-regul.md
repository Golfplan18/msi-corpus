Elon Musk's AI chatbot Grok on Friday restricted image generation and editing to paying subscribers after weeks of criticism over sexualized deepfakes — including images that researchers said in some cases appeared to depict children — but regulators in Europe and the United Kingdom said the change did not resolve the underlying problem.

The episode has drawn regulatory scrutiny across three continents, with the European Commission ordering X to retain all internal documents and data related to Grok through the end of 2026 under the EU's digital safety law, and the United Kingdom's media and privacy regulators both contacting X and Musk's AI company xAI for compliance information.

## Restriction falls short of regulators' demands

As of Friday, Grok displayed the message "Image generation and editing are currently limited to paying subscribers" in response to image-altering requests on the X platform. Paying subscribers, those holding X blue checkmarks, receive access to the feature for $8 per month.

The Associated Press confirmed Friday afternoon, however, that image editing remained available to free users on the standalone Grok website and app, limiting the restriction's scope to X's social media platform.

European and British officials rejected the change as inadequate.

"This doesn't change our fundamental issue. Paid subscription or non-paid subscription, we don't want to see such images. It's as simple as that," said Thomas Regnier, a spokesman for the European Commission. The commission had previously described Grok's behavior as "illegal" and "appalling."

Geraint Ellis, a spokesman for British Prime Minister Keir Starmer, called the change "not a solution."

"In fact, it is insulting to the victims of misogyny and sexual violence," Ellis said, adding that the episode demonstrates that X "can move swiftly when it wants to do so." Ellis said the government expects "rapid action" and that "all options are on the table."

Starmer, speaking to Greatest Hits Radio, said X needs to "get their act together and get this material down. We will take action on this because it's simply not tolerable."

An X spokesperson did not respond to a request for comment.

## International scrutiny widens

France, Malaysia and India have also been scrutinizing the platform, and a Brazilian lawmaker has called for an investigation.

The European Commission's data-retention order covers all internal documents and data relating to Grok and forms part of a wider investigation under the EU's digital safety law.

## Background on Grok and its image tools

Grok, accessed through X, launched in 2023. Last summer, the company added an image generator feature called Grok Imagine, which included a "spicy mode" capable of generating adult content.

In recent weeks, researchers said the chatbot had been granting a wave of requests to modify images, including placing women in bikinis or sexually explicit positions. Researchers warned that in a few cases some images appeared to depict children, and governments around the world condemned the platform and opened investigations.

Musk has pitched Grok as an edgier alternative to competing AI chatbots with more restrictive safeguards. Because Grok's generated images are publicly visible on X, they can spread rapidly across the platform.
