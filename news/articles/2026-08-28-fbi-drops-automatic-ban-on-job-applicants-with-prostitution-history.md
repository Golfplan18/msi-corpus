## FBI still rejects applicants with recent or repeated encounters

The FBI's new hiring guidance on applicants with prostitution history carves out three categories that still trigger automatic dismissal — encounters within the last 10 years, three or more encounters overall, or any such conduct while in a position of trust — and routes other candidates into a contextual review.

The thresholds and the new holistic-review pathway were described by a person familiar with the matter who was not authorized to discuss internal decision-making and spoke on condition of anonymity. For applicants whose conduct falls outside the three disqualifying categories, the FBI will allow consideration of the context of the conduct rather than treating it as grounds for automatic rejection from the hiring process.

The change falls along a continuum of evolving recruitment standards within federal law enforcement and at the bureau, which several years ago relaxed its approach to candidates' past marijuana use.

The bureau's hiring has drawn scrutiny from current and former agents who have voiced concerns about what they describe as a loosening of recruitment requirements as the bureau works to fill its ranks.

The reporting was based on accounts from people familiar with the matter, some of whom spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to discuss internal FBI decision-making.
