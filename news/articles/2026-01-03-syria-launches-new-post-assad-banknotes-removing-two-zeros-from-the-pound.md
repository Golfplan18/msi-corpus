DAMASCUS, Syria — Syria began circulating new currency bills Saturday as the country's new government moves to stabilize an economy battered by years of war and Western sanctions. A presidential decree signed by President Ahmad al-Sharaa ordered the gradual withdrawal of old Syrian pounds in exchange for new banknotes that strip two zeros from each denomination's face value, making every 100 old pounds equal to one new pound.

The redenomination is one of the first concrete steps taken by Syria's post-Assad authorities to restore economic order since Hayat Tahrir al-Sham-led forces ended the Assad family's 54-year rule by taking Damascus in December 2024.

Central Bank Governor Mokhles Nazer said on X that after months of preparations, the exchange of old Syrian pounds with new banknotes officially began Saturday morning. The timetable for fully withdrawing old bills — some of which bear the images of deposed President Bashar Assad and his late father and predecessor, Hafez Assad — will be set by the central bank and carried out through designated exchange centers.

Under the new system, the largest denomination drops from 5,000 old Syrian pounds to a 500-pound note.

## Currency's collapse in figures

The scale of Syria's monetary deterioration is visible in the exchange rate. At Damascus shops Saturday, one U.S. dollar fetched 11,800 old Syrian pounds. At the start of Syria's conflict in mid-March 2011, one dollar bought 47 Syrian pounds.

## Sanctions relief and recovery

The United States and the European Union have removed most of the sanctions imposed on Syria during Assad's rule, clearing one barrier to economic recovery. Syria's new authorities have described stabilizing the currency as a prerequisite for broader economic normalization after more than a decade of war.
