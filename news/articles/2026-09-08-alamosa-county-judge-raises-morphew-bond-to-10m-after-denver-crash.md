## Alamosa County judge raises Morphew bond to $10M after Denver crash

**Subtype:** fact

- Alamosa County District Judge Amanda Hopkins raised bond for Barry Morphew to $10 million on Tuesday after prosecutors argued he was a flight risk.
- Morphew had been free on a $3 million surety bond with GPS location monitoring and travel restricted to work or medical emergencies.
- Prosecutors cited an Aug. 26 crash in Denver — after Morphew picked his daughter up at the airport and fled the scene — as evidence he was not honoring his release terms.
- Morphew was arrested Friday in Boulder County; the same judge initially amended his bond to $3 million cash only before raising it further on Tuesday.

## Prosecutors argued Aug. 26 crash showed Morphew violated release terms

The crash in Denver on Aug. 26 occurred after Morphew had picked his daughter up at the airport, authorities said, and he then fled the scene. Prosecutors told the court the episode showed Morphew was not honoring the conditions of his release, which allowed him to leave home only for work or a medical emergency and required GPS location monitoring.

Morphew is charged with killing his wife, Suzanne Morphew. The case has proceeded in Alamosa County, where the charges were filed.

After Morphew's arrest Friday, Hopkins amended his bond to $3 million cash only — meaning he could be released only by paying the full amount rather than having it guaranteed by a bondsman. At Tuesday's hearing, she raised the amount to $10 million, according to the Associated Press, as prosecutors renewed their argument that Morphew posed a flight risk given the alleged bond breach.
