The unrest, which has spread to more than 280 locations across 27 of Iran's 31 provinces, is being driven by the rial currency's collapse to a record low and the government's removal of subsidized exchange rates, a policy shift that has pushed food prices sharply higher and left some store shelves empty.

Security forces fired tear gas Tuesday to break up a sit-in by protesters inside Tehran's Grand Bazaar, Iran's most storied commercial hub, as the surrounding market largely shut down and demonstrations entered their tenth day, witnesses said.

At least 36 people have been killed since the protests began Dec. 28, with more than 2,000 others detained, according to the U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency. The group, which relies on an activist network inside Iran, said the dead include 30 protesters, four children and two members of Iran's security forces.

Demonstrations have reached more than 280 locations across 27 of Iran's 31 provinces, the group said.

## Sit-in at the Grand Bazaar

In Tehran's Grand Bazaar — a labyrinth of covered passages and alleyways that has served as a center of Iran's economic and political life for centuries — demonstrators sat down in one passage in front of security forces as nearby shops closed, according to online videos and witness accounts.

Authorities subsequently fired tear gas to disperse the crowd. Iran's state-run media did not immediately acknowledge the incident, a pattern repeated throughout the days since the demonstrations began. Later footage purportedly showed tear gas deployed at a hospital and a metro station in Tehran.

## Rial hits new low as food prices spike

The sit-in came as Iran's rial fell to 1.46 million to the dollar on Tuesday, a new record low. Prior to Iran's 1979 Islamic Revolution, the rial had traded at roughly 70 to the dollar. At the time of Iran's 2015 nuclear agreement with world powers, the rate stood at approximately 32,000 rials to the dollar.

Iran's Central Bank compounded consumer pressure by ending a preferential, subsidized exchange rate for all imported goods except medicine and wheat. Many firms had taken advantage of the difference between official and market rates, according to the Associated Press, pocketing profits while ordinary Iranians watched their savings lose value. With the subsidy gone, merchants are expected to pass costs directly to consumers.

The average bottle of cooking oil doubled in price, Iran's state-run IRNA news agency reported. Cheese and chicken prices also spiked. Some shops reported empty shelves as suppliers held back goods rather than sell at a loss.

President Masoud Pezeshkian, Iran's reformist leader, acknowledged in a televised speech Tuesday that the crisis may be moving beyond official control.

"We should not expect the government to handle all of this alone," Pezeshkian said. "The government simply does not have that capacity."

He also warned of further economic pain. "If we do not make realistic decisions, we ourselves will push the country toward crisis and then complain about the consequences," Pezeshkian said.

## Hospital raid in Ilam draws U.S. condemnation

Late Monday, Pezeshkian ordered the interior ministry to form a special team to conduct what his office called a "full-fledged investigation" of events in Ilam province, roughly 515 kilometers southwest of Tehran, where online videos purportedly showed security forces firing on civilians in Malekshahi County.

The presidency also acknowledged an "incident in a hospital in the city of Ilam." Online video purportedly showed security forces in riot gear raiding the facility, where activists said authorities were searching for protesters. Ilam province is mainly home to Iran's Kurdish and Lur ethnic groups and faces severe economic hardship.

The U.S. State Department, in a Farsi-language post on X, called the hospital raid "a crime."

"Storming the wards, beating medical staff and attacking the wounded with tear gas and ammunition is an clear crime against humanity," the State Department posted. "Hospitals are not battlefields."

The semiofficial Fars news agency, believed close to Iran's paramilitary Revolutionary Guard, reported that a crowd gathered at a funeral for two of the dead later stormed and damaged three banks in Malekshahi, resulting in one person killed and several wounded. Fars had earlier alleged, without providing evidence, that demonstrators in the area had carried firearms and grenades.

## Trump warning, Iranian threats

U.S. President Donald Trump warned Iran on Friday that if Tehran "violently kills peaceful protesters," the United States "will come to their rescue." Iran's Supreme Leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, responded on Saturday, saying "rioters must be put in their place."

Iranian officials threatened to target American troops in the Middle East following Trump's statement. How or whether the United States would intervene remained unclear.
