## Pennsylvania child death review gets $242,000, far below requested $2.5M

**Subtype:** fact

- The Pennsylvania Department of Health received $242,000 for child death reviews, far below the $2.5 million Gov. Josh Shapiro proposed earlier this year, according to the Associated Press.
- State law requires all deaths of individuals under 21 to be examined by county teams, but only half of cases have been reviewed since 2020.
- County officials say the mandate has overwhelmed local resources.
- Some advocates say the limited funding will not be enough to make major improvements to the system.
- The Shapiro administration said it was disappointed and that its broader plan for state-level staff, grants, and a public education campaign appears unachievable.

## Mandate leaves half of child deaths unreviewed since 2020

The $242,000 allocated for the Child Death Review program in the latest state budget is a fraction of the $2.5 million Shapiro had sought, the Associated Press reported. The program is designed to help counties fulfill a legal requirement to review all deaths of people under age 21.

County teams are required to analyze the circumstances surrounding each death to identify patterns that could help prevent future deaths, the AP reported. But since 2020, only half of all eligible deaths have been reviewed. County officials said they are overwhelmed by the demands of the mandate, a requirement many have struggled to keep up with.

The Shapiro administration said it was disappointed by the funding level, according to the AP. It had planned to revamp the program with new state-level staff, grants for county teams, and a public education campaign. Much of that plan appears unachievable given the limited funding available, the administration said.
