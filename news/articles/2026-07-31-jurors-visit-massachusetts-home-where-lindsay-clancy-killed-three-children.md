## Jurors visit Massachusetts home where Lindsay Clancy killed three children

**Subtype:** fact

- Jurors in the murder trial of Lindsay Clancy visited the Duxbury, Massachusetts, home where she killed her three children in January 2023, according to the Associated Press.
- Court officers escorted the jury in groups of six for a roughly 40-minute viewing of the interior, including the basement where the children died, and the yard where Clancy was found after jumping from a second-story window.
- Journalists were kept away by a police barricade, but court officers were visible through trees as jurors moved along the former driveway; the home now belongs to a new owner.
- Clancy herself did not attend the site visit, and her lawyers argue she was in the grip of postpartum psychosis, a rare mental illness tied to the stress, sleep deprivation, and hormonal changes after childbirth.

## Defendant did not attend site visit; lawyers cite postpartum psychosis

The jury in the Duxbury murder trial traveled by bus to the home Friday, driven past two businesses relevant to the January 2023 killings before being escorted to the house, the Associated Press reported. The visit, a rare step in a criminal proceeding, gave the jurors a firsthand view of the rooms and the yard where the events unfolded.

During the 40-minute examination, the six-person panel walked through the property in small groups, according to the AP. They saw the basement, where Clancy strangled her three children, and the yard where she was discovered after jumping from a second-story window. Journalists were kept behind a police barricade, but court officers could be seen through the trees transporting jurors to and from the bus along what had been the Clancy family’s driveway.

The home now has a new owner, the wire service noted.

The site visit followed two days of testimony in which police described finding the three dead children and the prosecution played the 911 call, as [MSI previously reported](/2026-07-30-police-describe-discovering-three-dead-children-in-duxbury-home-as-trial-hears-9). Clancy’s trial opened with her entering a plea of not guilty by reason of mental illness, and the defense has argued that the killings were a tragic consequence of an unrecognized and untreated psychiatric crisis, not a criminal act.

The jury will now return to the courtroom to hear further evidence as the trial continues.
