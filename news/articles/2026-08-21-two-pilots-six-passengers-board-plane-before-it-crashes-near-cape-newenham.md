## Two pilots, six passengers board plane before it crashes near Cape Newenham

**Subtype:** fact

- A charter plane carrying eight people crashed Thursday near a remote radar site in western Alaska, the NTSB's Alaska region chief said.
- Clint Johnson, the National Transportation Safety Board's Alaska region chief, said two pilots and six passengers were on board and he did not immediately have details on their status.
- The plane departed Anchorage and is believed to have crashed early Thursday afternoon on approach to Cape Newenham, according to Johnson.
- The Alaska Rescue Coordination Center launched a search and rescue mission after receiving a report of an "aircraft incident" near the Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site.

## Alaska Rescue Coordination Center launches search and rescue mission

The plane departed Anchorage and was believed to have crashed early Thursday afternoon on approach to Cape Newenham, a remote radar site in western Alaska, Johnson said. He described the information received so far as preliminary and said he did not immediately have details on the status of those aboard.

The Alaska Rescue Coordination Center said it launched a search and rescue mission after receiving a report of an "aircraft incident" near the Cape Newenham Long Range Radar Site. The center said additional information would be released as it became available and did not respond to questions.
