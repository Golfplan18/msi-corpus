BEIJING — Chinese President Xi Jinping and South Korean President Lee Jae Myung pledged Monday to expand trade and safeguard regional stability during a summit in Beijing, even as North Korea launched several ballistic missiles into the sea hours before the two leaders sat down.

Lee, who took office in June, made the four-day trip his first to China as South Korea's president. Xi hosted Lee at the Great Hall of the People, where the Chinese president stressed the two countries' "important responsibilities in maintaining regional peace and promoting global development," according to a readout broadcast by China's state-run CCTV.

Lee called for opening "a new chapter in the development of Korea-China relations" during "changing times," saying "the two countries should make joint contributions to promote peace, which is the foundation for prosperity and growth."

The summit carried broad regional significance: China-Japan tensions are sharpening over Taiwan, a U.S. military operation in Venezuela has drawn condemnation from both Beijing and Pyongyang, and North Korea's missile launches set a pointed backdrop to talks that both governments described as a new diplomatic opening.

## North Korea missile launches

Just hours before Lee landed in Beijing, North Korea fired several ballistic missiles into the sea. Pyongyang said the launches included hypersonic missiles, which are designed to travel at more than five times the speed of sound and are very difficult to detect and intercept. Foreign experts doubt that North Korea has developed such a functioning hypersonic weapon, AP reported.

South Korea's national security adviser Wi Sung-lac told a briefing that the two countries agreed during the summit to "continue to explore creative ways to ease tensions on the Korean Peninsula" and confirmed China's resolve to play "a constructive role" in efforts to promote peace.

China is a major ally of North Korea and provides it with an economic lifeline. Beijing and Moscow have repeatedly blocked U.S.-led efforts to tighten United Nations sanctions on Pyongyang.

## Trade and cooperation agreements

China and South Korea maintain substantial economic ties, with bilateral trade reaching approximately $273 billion in 2024, according to AP. At the summit, Xi and Lee oversaw the signing of 15 cooperation agreements covering technology, trade, transportation, and environmental protection, CCTV reported.

Earlier Monday, Lee attended a business forum in Beijing with representatives from major South Korean and Chinese companies, including Samsung, Hyundai, LG, and Alibaba Group. That gathering produced additional agreements in consumer goods, agriculture, biotechnology, and entertainment, presided over by Lee and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng.

## China-Japan frictions and Taiwan

Lee's visit coincided with rising tensions between China and Japan. Japan's new leader recently said Tokyo could intervene in a potential Chinese attack on Taiwan, the island democracy that China claims as its own. Last week, China staged large-scale military drills around the island for two days, which Beijing said were intended to warn against separatist and "external interference" forces.

In his meeting with Lee, Xi invoked the two countries' shared history against Japan, calling on them to "join hands to defend the fruits of victory in World War II and safeguard peace and stability in northeast Asia."

Lee said, in a CCTV interview before the trip, that South Korea's military cooperation with the United States should not mean that Seoul-Beijing relations "should move toward confrontation." He said his visit aimed to "minimize or eliminate past misunderstandings or contradictions" and "elevate and develop South Korea-China relations to a new stage."

## Venezuela backdrop

The summit also unfolded against the backdrop of a U.S. military operation in Venezuela that resulted in the removal of President Nicolás Maduro. North Korea, which has long feared the U.S. might seek a change of government in Pyongyang, criticized the action as a "wild violation" of Venezuela's sovereignty and cited it as an example of what it called the "rogue and brutal nature of the U.S." China also condemned the operation, saying it violated international law and threatened peace in Latin America.
