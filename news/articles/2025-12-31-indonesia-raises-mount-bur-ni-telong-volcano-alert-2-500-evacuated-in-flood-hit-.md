The volcanic emergency places Bener Meriah regency — where 31 people died and more than 2,100 were displaced by recent flooding — at heightened risk, as authorities warn that heavy rain combined with volcanic activity could worsen conditions and complicate efforts to reach isolated communities.

BANDA ACEH, Indonesia — Indonesian authorities raised the alert level for Mount Bur Ni Telong volcano in Aceh province to its second-highest tier Tuesday evening after a surge of seismic activity signaled heightened eruption risk, ordering thousands of residents to evacuate in a region still reeling from one of Sumatra's deadliest recent flood disasters.

The 2,624-meter (8,600-foot) stratovolcano in Bener Meriah regency recorded at least seven earthquakes Tuesday evening felt about 5 kilometers (3 miles) away, according to Lana Saria, acting head of the Geological Agency at Indonesia's Energy and Mineral Resources Ministry. Seismographs also detected seven shallow volcanic earthquakes, 14 deep quakes, and two tectonic quakes.

"Aftershocks following local tectonic events indicate magma activity is easily triggered by tectonic disturbances," Saria said. She added that increased seismic activity had been ongoing since July and had become more intense and shallow in the past two months.

Scientists raised the alert from the third to the second-highest level based on visual and instrumental monitoring results, Saria said. Visual monitoring showed the volcano clearly visible with no crater smoke, but she warned of possible eruption, including phreatic blasts and hazardous volcanic gases near areas with fumaroles and solfataras — openings in the Earth's crust that emit steam and gases.

Authorities urged residents and visitors to stay at least 4 kilometers (2.4 miles) from the crater and to avoid fumarole and solfatara zones, particularly during cloudy or rainy weather when gas concentrations can reach life-threatening levels.

## Evacuations under way

More than 2,500 residents in the four villages nearest the crater were evacuated to government shelters, said Tagore Abubakar, the regent of Bener Meriah. Some residents also fled to relatives' homes in neighboring Central Aceh regency, he said.

As MSI reported Tuesday, the evacuation order was issued as the alert level was raised, with authorities warning that heavy rains combined with volcanic activity could worsen conditions and further complicate efforts to reach isolated communities. <a href="/articles/2025-12-31-indonesia-raises-volcano-alert-in-aceh-orders-2-500-residents-to-evacuate">MSI's earlier report covers the initial alert escalation and official response.</a>

## A region already in crisis

The volcanic emergency strikes a regency still in acute recovery. Catastrophic floods and landslides struck 52 cities and regencies across Sumatra island earlier this month, leaving 1,154 people dead, 165 still missing, and more than 7,000 injured, according to the National Disaster Management Agency.

Bener Meriah was among the hardest hit. Thirty-one people died and 14 remained missing after flooding disrupted access to remote villages and displaced more than 2,100 residents in the regency alone.

## Indonesia's volcanic landscape

Indonesia sits along the Ring of Fire, a horseshoe-shaped series of seismic fault lines encircling the Pacific Ocean. The archipelago nation of more than 280 million people has over 120 active volcanoes, making volcanic emergencies a recurring feature of life across its islands.
