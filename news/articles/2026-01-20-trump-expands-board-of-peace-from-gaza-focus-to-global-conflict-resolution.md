President Donald Trump is expanding a 'Board of Peace' initially designed to oversee the Gaza ceasefire into a broader initiative aimed at resolving global conflicts, inviting dozens of nations to join the effort. Trump, who will serve as chairman, is headed to the World Economic Forum in Davos this week, where more details are expected to emerge. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Wednesday that he has agreed to join the board, despite earlier criticism of its structure.

The initiative represents a significant expansion of Trump's original vision. The draft charter, obtained by The Associated Press, would concentrate substantial power in Trump's hands, including authority over membership invitations, vote-breaking, and decisions about the board's operations.

The Board of Peace, originally envisioned as a small oversight committee for the Gaza ceasefire, has grown into a sprawling initiative with global ambitions, according to documents obtained by The Associated Press. The expansion comes as President Trump prepares to present the initiative at the World Economic Forum in Davos.

A draft charter of the board, which Trump says he will chair, outlines his intention to concentrate significant power in his position. He will have authority to invite member states, break tie votes, decide meeting frequency, and create or dissolve subsidiary entities.

## Membership Structure and Financial Requirements

Countries that have agreed to participate include Israel, Azerbaijan, the United Arab Emirates, Morocco, Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Hungary, Argentina, Armenia and Belarus.

Permanent membership—requiring a contribution of more than $1 billion in cash during the first year—distinguishes a higher tier of participation. Standard members serve three-year terms. The draft charter remains under revision and has not been finalized, according to a U.S. official.

The scope of the board's mandate has become substantially broader. Letters sent to various world leaders describe the board embarking "on a bold new approach to resolving global conflict," suggesting a role as an alternative to the U.N. Security Council.

## International Response

Invitation letters have been sent to Britain's Prime Minister Keir Starmer, Paraguay's Santiago Peña, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, Egyptian President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi and Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan. Russia, Ukraine, China, Kosovo, India, Slovenia, Croatia, Thailand and the European Union's executive arm have also said they received invitations.

The United Kingdom said it was considering membership but expressed concerns about Russia's potential involvement. Germany shares "the aim of serving peace in the world" but said the United Nations remains "the central multilateral framework for international crises and conflict management."

Russia is "studying the details" and will seek clarity on "all the nuances" through contacts with the United States, according to Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov.

Norway announced it will not join, citing questions the proposal raises that require further dialogue with the United States. Swedish Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson said his country is not signing up "as the text currently stands."

France, which has had disagreements with the Trump administration over other issues, appears unlikely to join. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said: "Yes to implementing the peace plan presented by the president of the United States, which we wholeheartedly support, but no to creating an organization as it has been presented, which would replace the United Nations."

When asked about French President Emmanuel Macron's apparent reluctance, Trump said: "Well, nobody wants him because he's going to be out of office very soon. I'll put a 200% tariff on his wines and Champagnes and he'll join. But he doesn't have to join."

## Supporting Boards for Implementation

The White House announced an executive board to carry out the Board of Peace's vision. Members include Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Trump envoy Steve Witkoff, Jared Kushner, former British Prime Minister Tony Blair, Apollo Global Management CEO Marc Rowan, World Bank President Ajay Banga, and Trump's deputy national security adviser Robert Gabriel.

A separate Gaza Executive Board will oversee implementation of the ceasefire's second phase. This board, according to the agreement, is responsible for deploying an international security force, disarming Hamas, and rebuilding Gaza. Nikolay Mladenov, a former Bulgarian politician and U.N. Mideast envoy, will serve as the board's representative overseeing day-to-day operations.

The Gaza Executive Board also includes Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Qatari diplomat Ali Al-Thawadi, Egypt's Hassan Rashad, Emirati minister Reem Al-Hashimy, Israeli businessman Yakir Gabay, and Sigrid Kaag, a former deputy prime minister of the Netherlands and Middle East expert.

The boards will supervise a newly appointed committee of Palestinian technocrats responsible for Gaza's day-to-day governance.
