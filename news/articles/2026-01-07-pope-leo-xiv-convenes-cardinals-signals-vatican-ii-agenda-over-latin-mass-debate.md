VATICAN CITY — Pope Leo XIV gathered approximately 170 cardinals at the Vatican on Wednesday for the first consistory of his papacy, asking them to set priorities for his next two years as head of the Catholic Church. The cardinals signaled continuity with the agenda of Pope Francis, choosing to focus on making the church more missionary and responsive to ordinary Catholics rather than on the divisive question of the traditional Latin Mass.

"This day and a half together will point the way for our path ahead," Leo said at the opening of the two-day meeting, known as a consistory.

The roughly 170 red-capped cardinals — around two-thirds of the total College of Cardinals — gathered in the Vatican's audience hall for the session. Leo originally offered four agenda items but asked the cardinals to select just two. The majority chose to focus on the missionary nature of the church and on making the church more "synodal," or responsive to rank-and-file Catholics, Vatican spokesman Matteo Bruni said.

The cardinals declined to make the liturgy, including the question of the old Latin Mass, one of the two main themes of discussion. The ancient rite had been a source of tension in parts of the church after Francis greatly restricted it. Individual cardinals remain free to raise the subject in discussion, Bruni said.

## A call for Vatican II implementation

Earlier Wednesday, Leo gave what the Associated Press described as the strongest signal yet of his pontificate's direction, calling for full implementation of the reforms of the Second Vatican Council — the 1960s meetings that modernized the Catholic Church and remain a source of debate among its members.

Leo announced he would devote his weekly catechism lessons to a rereading of key Vatican II documents.

"Therefore, while we hear the call not to let its prophecy fade, and to continue to seek ways and means to implement its insights, it will be important to get to know it again closely, and to do so not through hearsay or interpretations that have been given, but by rereading its documents and reflecting on their content," he said.

Leo cited popes from Vatican II forward on the council's importance, adding: "Indeed, it is the magisterium that still constitutes the guiding star of the church's journey today."

Vatican II, held between 1962 and 1965, allowed for the use of vernacular languages rather than Latin for Mass, called for greater participation by lay Catholics in church life, and revolutionized Catholic relations with Jews and members of other faiths. Its reforms have remained a line of division between traditionalist and progressive wings of the church in the decades since.

## A new governing approach

Leo called the consistory the day after formally closing the 2025 Holy Year Jubilee, suggesting he viewed the Jubilee's conclusion as the moment to begin setting his own agenda. His first months as pope had been dominated by fulfilling Jubilee obligations and wrapping up the outstanding matters of Francis's pontificate, according to AP.

The decision to convene the full College of Cardinals also marked a departure from Francis's approach. Francis had relied on a small, hand-picked group of nine cardinals who met every few months at the Vatican rather than on the broader College to help him govern. Cardinals had reportedly complained of being sidelined during Francis's 12-year pontificate; Leo obliged by bringing them together.

The original four-point agenda included discussion of two of Francis's key reform documents — his inaugural statement on the missionary nature of the church and the 2022 document reforming the Vatican bureaucracy — as well as the synodal church concept and a liturgy discussion. After the cardinals narrowed the focus Wednesday, individual cardinals were expected to speak to the selected themes on Thursday, Bruni said.
