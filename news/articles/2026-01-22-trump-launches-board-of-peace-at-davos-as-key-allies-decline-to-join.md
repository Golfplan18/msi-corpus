President Donald Trump announced a Board of Peace at the World Economic Forum in Davos on Thursday, saying it will oversee the ceasefire in Israel's war with Hamas. But several major U.S. allies—including Norway, Sweden, France, and Canada—have declined to participate, signaling skepticism about the initiative. Trump withdrew Canada's invitation after Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney criticized his administration's approach to international order.

The new board, which Trump said could eventually rival the United Nations, has already encountered resistance over its membership and undefined mandate. The charter remains unpublished, and questions persist about how the board will function and what authority it will exercise.

Trump said the board will grow to include more than 50 countries and could become the most prestigious assembly of world leaders ever created. At the launch event in Davos, however, officials from only 19 countries attended. "You're the most powerful people in the world," Trump told the assembled group.

## Allied Skepticism and Withdrawal

Trump withdrew Canada's invitation to join the board after Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney delivered unusually blunt criticism of the Trump administration's approach to international order. "Please let this Letter serve to represent that the Board of Peace is withdrawing its invitation to you regarding Canada's joining, what will be, the most prestigious Board of Leaders ever assembled," Trump wrote to Carney on social media.

Some world leaders have indicated interest but said they need approval from their legislatures before formally committing. Trump specifically named Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni and Polish President Karol Nawrocki as leaders in that position.

The charter for the board has not yet been made public. Asked whether he would retain the title of chairman after leaving office, Trump said he would have the right to remain chairman "in theory, for life—but I'm not sure I want that."

## Gaza Ceasefire and Border Opening

At the event, Ali Shaath, described as the head of a new future government in Gaza, announced that the Rafah border crossing would open in both directions next week. Israel said only that it would consider the matter next week, offering no confirmation of the planned opening.

Trump said the board would work alongside the United Nations rather than replace it. "This isn't the United States, this is for the world," he said, adding, "I think we can spread it out to other things as we succeed in Gaza."

## Ukraine Peace Negotiations

On the sidelines of the forum, Trump met privately with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy for about an hour. Trump described the meeting as productive but expressed ongoing frustration about the lack of resolution. "I had a good meeting—but I've had numerous good meetings with President Zelenskyy and it doesn't seem to happen," he said.

Trump noted that "the same things that have been holding it up for the past year" continue to be obstacles. He indicated that both Putin and Zelenskyy now want to reach a deal but remained uncertain about prospects. "There were times when Putin didn't want to make a deal, times when Zelenskyy didn't want to make a deal. Now I think they both want to make a deal—but we'll find out," he said.

Zelenskyy said trilateral meetings involving the United States, Ukraine, and Russia would take place in the United Arab Emirates starting Friday. Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff and his son-in-law Jared Kushner traveled to Moscow on Thursday for talks with Putin.

## Pressure Campaign on Iran

Regarding Iran, Trump said he is moving a fleet of ships toward the country "just in case" he decides to take action in response to the Islamic government's violent crackdown on street protests. He said he received assurances that Iran would not carry out the planned executions of more than 800 protesters. Trump said he is maintaining a threat of 25 percent tariffs on countries conducting business with Iran. "We're doing that," he said.
