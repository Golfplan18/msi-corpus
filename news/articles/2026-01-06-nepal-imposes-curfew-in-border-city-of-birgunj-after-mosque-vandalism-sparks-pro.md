BIRGUNJ, Nepal — Nepalese authorities imposed a curfew Tuesday on Birgunj, a key southern border city, after vandalism of a mosque in a nearby town sparked dueling protests between Hindu and Muslim groups, officials said.

The Parsa District Administration ordered all residents off the streets and banned gatherings and demonstrations, warning that violators could be shot by security forces. Armed soldiers and police officers were patrolling the city, located 130 kilometers (80 miles) south of the capital Kathmandu.

The unrest is unusual for Nepal, a predominantly Hindu nation where open confrontations between Hindu and Muslim communities are rare. Most of Nepal's Muslim population lives in the country's southern border regions — the same corridor that serves as a vital conduit for the oil, goods, and supplies Nepal imports from India, with Birgunj as the main land-entry point.

## How the protests began

Muslim groups began protesting in Birgunj's streets on Sunday against the vandalism of a mosque in a nearby town, officials said. Hindu followers held a separate demonstration the same day. The protests from both communities continued through Tuesday.

As of Tuesday, there had been no major clashes between the two groups and no major injuries, officials said. Only scuffles with riot police were reported.

## Birgunj's strategic importance

Birgunj is Nepal's primary border crossing for goods arriving overland from India, making disruption there of particular concern to the country's supply chains. The city's roads and customs infrastructure channel much of Nepal's imported fuel and commercial goods before distribution to the rest of the country.

Hindu-Muslim tensions in Nepal are historically uncommon. The country's Muslim communities are concentrated largely in the Terai — the low-lying southern belt along the Indian border — where Birgunj sits.
