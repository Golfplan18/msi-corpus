The departures are the latest in what B'Tselem documents as a broader pattern: since the war between Israel and Hamas erupted in October 2023, more than 2,000 Palestinians from at least 44 entire communities have been expelled by settler harassment and violence in the area, the rights group said. United Nations officials have warned the trend is changing the map of the West Bank.

JERUSALEM — Twenty-six families fled the Palestinian Bedouin village of Ras Ein el-Auja in the central West Bank on Thursday, and more families were packing to leave on Sunday, according to residents and the Israeli human rights group B'Tselem. Residents said Jewish settlers from unauthorized outposts nearby had been entering homes, taking livestock, and intimidating women and children on a daily basis.

"We have been suffering greatly from the settlers. Every day, they come on foot, or on tractors, or on horseback with their sheep into our homes. They enter people's homes daily," said Nayef Zayed, a resident, as neighbors dismantled sheep pens and tin structures.

The village was originally home to approximately 700 people from more than 100 families that have lived there for decades, the Associated Press reported. Those who have not yet left are now among the last Palestinians remaining in the area, said Sarit Michaeli, international director at B'Tselem, who has been working with the residents.

## The December turning point

The turning point came in December, when settlers erected an outpost roughly 50 meters from Palestinian homes on the northwestern flank of the village, Michaeli and Sam Stein, an activist who had been living in the village for a month, told the AP.

After that, settlers were reported walking through the village at night, entering homes to film women and children, and tampering with the village's electricity, Michaeli said. International activists began escorting children to school.

"The settlers attack us day and night, they have displaced us, they harass us in every way," said Eyad Isaac, another resident. "They intimidate the children and women."

Residents said they called police frequently to ask for help, but it seldom arrived.

## Families scatter without a clear destination

Displaced families dispersed to villages near Jericho and near Hebron further south, residents said. Some sold their livestock and moved into cities. Others dismantled their structures without knowing where they would go.

"Where will we go? There's nowhere. We're scattered," said Zayed. "People's situation is bad. Very bad."

## Broader pattern of displacement

The village sits in a corridor running from Ramallah west to Jericho along the Jordanian border — part of the roughly 60 percent of the West Bank that has remained under full Israeli military control under interim peace accords signed in the 1990s.

Since the war between Israel and Hamas erupted in October 2023, more than 2,000 Palestinians from at least 44 entire communities have been expelled by settler harassment and violence across that corridor, B'Tselem said. Neighboring Palestinian hamlets in the area have already been emptied, Michaeli said. United Nations officials have warned the pattern is changing the map of the West Bank.

Israel's military and the local settler governing body did not respond to requests for comment, the AP reported.

## Settlement expansion under Netanyahu government

Settlement expansion has been promoted by successive Israeli governments over nearly six decades. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's government, which has placed settler leaders in senior positions, has made it a top priority, according to the AP.

That growth has been accompanied by a rise in settler harassment, much of it carried out by residents of unauthorized outposts. Rights groups and Palestinians say these outposts often begin with small farms or shepherding operations used to establish a foothold on land.

Some 500,000 Israelis have settled in the West Bank since Israel captured the territory, along with east Jerusalem and the Gaza Strip, in the 1967 Mideast war. The presence of Israeli settlements is viewed by most of the international community as illegal and a major obstacle to peace, according to the AP. The Palestinians seek all three areas for a future state.
