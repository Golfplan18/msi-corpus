## Abbott pauses Texas data center approvals pending an audit

**Subtype:** fact

- Gov. Greg Abbott on Monday ordered a pause on Texas data center approvals until regulators audit proposed facilities seeking connections to the state's electric grid.
- Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas to verify developer disclosures covering tax breaks, power use and generation, water use and cooling operations, community-impact mitigation efforts, and facility ownership.
- Projects that fail the "comprehensive verification and audit" should be denied connections to the grid, Abbott said.
- The length of the audit was not announced.

## Order directs verification of tax breaks, power use, water, ownership

Under the order, the Public Utility Commission of Texas and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas must verify what developers disclose before new data centers can connect to the state's electric grid. Abbott, who announced the pause Monday, said any project that does not pass the "comprehensive verification and audit" should be denied a grid connection.

The disclosures the agencies will check span the tax breaks a project would receive, its projected power use and generation, water use and cooling operations, steps intended to limit impacts on surrounding communities, and who owns the facility. The governor did not say how long the audit would take.
