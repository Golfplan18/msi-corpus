The agreement marks a deliberate shift in Canada's trade orientation as U.S. tariffs continue to weigh on the Canadian economy, with Carney having said ahead of the visit that his government is focused on building economic ties less reliant on the United States at what he called 'a time of global trade disruption.'

BEIJING — Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Friday that Canada will cut its 100% tariff on Chinese electric vehicles in exchange for reduced Chinese tariffs on Canadian canola seeds, a deal concluded after two days of meetings with Chinese leaders and that departs from the tariff posture Canada had shared with the United States.

Under the terms Carney described at a news conference, China will be allowed to export an initial 49,000 electric vehicles annually to Canada at a 6.1% tariff rate, a cap that will grow to about 70,000 vehicles over five years. In return, China agreed to reduce its total tariff on Canadian canola seeds from 84% to about 15%, Carney said.

"Our relationship has progressed in recent months with China," Carney said. "It is more predictable and you see results coming from that."

## Xi meeting and broader context

Carney, the first Canadian prime minister to visit China in eight years, met earlier Friday with Chinese President Xi Jinping at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing. Xi told Carney he is willing to continue working to improve ties, noting that bilateral talks have been underway since the two met on the sidelines of a regional economic conference in South Korea in October, according to the Associated Press.

Carney told Xi that better relations between the two countries could help improve a global governance system he described as "under great strain." At the news conference, he said the existing system may give way at least in part to country-to-country or regional agreements. "The question is: What gets built in that place? How much of a patchwork is it?" he said.

Carney had traveled to Beijing saying his government is focused on building an economy less reliant on the United States at what he called "a time of global trade disruption." U.S. President Donald Trump's tariffs have weighed on both the Canadian and Chinese economies, and Trump has previously said he would like Canada to become the 51st state.

## U.S. reaction

Trump said Canada striking a deal with Beijing was appropriate. "Well, it's OK. That's what he should be doing and it's a good thing for him to sign a trade deal," Trump said. "If you can get a deal with China, you should do that."

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer offered a cooler assessment. Greer told CNBC that Canada's decision to allow Chinese EV imports at a low tariff is "problematic" and said Canada may regret that in the long term.

## Domestic opposition

Ontario Premier Doug Ford, whose province is home to Canada's auto sector, criticized the agreement in a social media post. "Make no mistake: China now has a foothold in the Canadian market and will use it to their full advantage at the expense of Canadian workers," Ford said. He also warned that the deal risks "closing the door on Canadian automakers to the American market, our largest export destination."

Carney sought to address concerns from Canadian automakers and workers by noting that the initial cap represents about 3% of the 1.8 million vehicles sold in Canada annually. He said China is expected to begin investing in the Canadian auto industry within three years as part of the arrangement.

"We're building (a) new part of our car industry, building cars of the future in partnership, bringing affordable autos for Canadians at a time when affordability is top of mind," Carney said.

## Background

Canada had aligned with the United States under former Prime Minister Justin Trudeau by imposing 100% tariffs on Chinese EVs and 25% tariffs on Chinese steel and aluminum. China responded by placing 100% duties on Canadian canola oil and meal, 25% duties on pork and seafood, and added a steep tariff on canola seeds last August. An industry group said those combined measures effectively closed the Chinese market to Canadian canola. China's imports from Canada fell 10.4% last year to $41.7 billion, according to Chinese trade data.

## Analysts and business observers

Nelson Wiseman, professor emeritus of political science at the University of Toronto, said the deal serves the interests of both sides. "Canada is diversifying its bets economically," Wiseman said. "And China is succeeding in driving a small wedge between Canada and the U.S."

Jacob Cooke, chief executive of WPIC Marketing + Technologies, a company that helps exporters navigate the Chinese market, said the visit reestablished a basis for engagement that had been absent for years. "These three things we didn't have," Cooke said, referring to dialogue, respect, and a framework between the two nations. "The parties were not talking for years."

Carney is scheduled to leave China on Saturday. He will visit Qatar on Sunday before traveling to the annual gathering of the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, the following week, where his office said he will meet business leaders and investors to promote trade and investment.
