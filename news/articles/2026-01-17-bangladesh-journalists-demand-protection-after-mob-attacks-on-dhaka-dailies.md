Journalists, editors, and media owners in Bangladesh on Saturday demanded that authorities protect the press, following mob attacks in December that burned the offices of two leading Dhaka newspapers and trapped reporters inside as elections approach in February.

The demand for protection came as Bangladesh's interim government, led by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Muhammad Yunus, faced criticism from global human rights organizations for failing to prevent the attacks and, separately, for jailing journalists linked to the country's previous administration — a combination that press-freedom advocates warned could inflict lasting damage on independent media ahead of elections.

## The Conference

The Editors Council and the Newspapers Owners Association of Bangladesh jointly organized the Saturday conference in Dhaka, drawing editors, journalist union leaders, and reporters from across the country. Participants demanded that authorities uphold press freedom as the country prepares for elections scheduled for February.

Nurul Kabir, president of the Editors Council and editor of the English-language New Age daily, said attempts to silence media and democratic institutions form a dangerous pattern.

"Those who want to suppress institutions that act as vehicles of democratic aspirations are doing so through laws, force and intimidation," Kabir said.

## The December Attacks

In December, angry mobs stormed and set fire to the Dhaka offices of the Daily Star, Bangladesh's leading English-language newspaper, and Prothom Alo, the largest Bengali-language daily, shortly after the death of a prominent Islamist activist. Journalists and other staff were trapped inside as the buildings burned. Those who sheltered on the roof of the Daily Star were rescued hours later. Both buildings were looted, and a leader of the Editors Council was manhandled by the attackers when he arrived at the scene. Liberal cultural centers in Dhaka were also attacked the same day.

The newspaper authorities said the Yunus-led administration failed to respond adequately to the attacks despite repeated requests for assistance in dispersing the mobs.

The motive for the newspaper attacks was not immediately clear, according to the Associated Press. The editors of both papers are known to have close connections to Yunus. In the months before the attacks, Islamist groups had organized protests outside the offices of both papers, accusing them of ties with India.

## UN Warning

After the December attacks, a United Nations expert said the mob assaults on leading media outlets and cultural centers were deeply alarming and required prompt and effective investigation.

"The weaponization of public anger against journalists and artists is dangerous at any time, and especially now as the country prepares for elections. It could have a chilling effect on media freedom, minority voices and dissenting views with serious consequences for democracy," Irene Khan said in a statement.

## Background

Yunus came to power after former Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina fled Bangladesh during a mass uprising in August 2024. Human Rights Watch and Amnesty International have since criticized the interim government for failing to uphold human and civil rights.

Dozens of journalists face murder charges linked to the 2024 uprising, on the grounds that their reporting encouraged the Hasina government to use lethal weapons against protesters. Several journalists with documented ties to Hasina have been arrested and jailed since Yunus assumed leadership of the interim government.
