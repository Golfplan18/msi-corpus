## Trump says $1.8B compensation fund 'dead' as GOP senators block nominee

- President Donald Trump said Friday that his $1.8 billion anti-weaponization fund is "dead," the Associated Press reported.
- Trump told reporters at Camp David that administration officials had "agreed not to have a fund" that compensates his political allies, while making clear he disagrees with that decision, according to the AP.
- In a social media post Friday morning, Trump said people prosecuted by the Justice Department for their involvement in the Jan. 6 attack on the Capitol "are suffering still, many ruined, and I felt that they should be given compensation for what has been done to them."
- Two Republican senators are blocking the president's attorney general nominee in protest over the fund, the AP reported.

## President tells reporters officials agreed not to have payouts for allies

Trump told reporters at Camp David on Friday morning that administration officials had "agreed not to have a fund" that compensates his political allies, yet made clear that he disagrees with that decision, according to the Associated Press.

The comments came hours after an early morning social media post in which Trump said people who had been prosecuted by the Justice Department — many of them for their involvement in the Jan. 6 attack on the Capitol — "are suffering still, many ruined, and I felt that they should be given compensation for what has been done to them," the AP reported.

Two Republican senators are blocking the confirmation of Trump's attorney general nominee in protest over the fund, according to the AP.
