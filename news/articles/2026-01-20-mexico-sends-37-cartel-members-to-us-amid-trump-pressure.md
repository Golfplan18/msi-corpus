Mexico transferred 37 cartel members to the United States on Tuesday, marking the third such transfer in less than a year as the Trump administration ratchets up pressure on the country to combat cross-border drug trafficking. Mexican Security Minister Omar García Harfuch said the transferred individuals were "high impact criminals" that "represented a real threat to the country's security."

The transfer represents Mexico's calculated effort to demonstrate compliance with the Trump administration's hardened stance on drug enforcement, even as Mexico's president has publicly stated that U.S. military intervention is unnecessary.

Video released by Mexican authorities showed handcuffed prisoners surrounded by heavily armed masked officers being loaded onto a military jet at an airport outside Mexico City. García Harfuch posted the announcement on social media platform X.

The three transfers have sent a total of 92 cartel members to the United States, García Harfuch said.

## Cartels represented

The transfer included members from the Sinaloa Cartel, the Beltrán-Leyva organization, the Jalisco New Generation Cartel, the Northeast Cartel, and a remnant of the Zetas based in the Mexican border state of Tamaulipas, across from Texas. Mexican authorities said all had pending cases in the United States.

Among those transferred was María Del Rosario Navarro Sánchez, whom Mexican authorities identified as the first Mexican citizen to face charges in the U.S. for providing support to a terrorist organization, following accusations of cartel conspiracy.

## Trump's escalating pressure

Trump has publicly entertained military action against Mexican cartels, with rhetoric intensifying following a U.S. military operation in Venezuela that deposed former President Nicolás Maduro in early January.

"We've knocked out 97% of the drugs coming in by water and we are going to start now hitting land, with regard to the cartels," Trump said in an interview with Fox News.

Mexico's President Claudia Sheinbaum spoke with Trump last week and told him that U.S. military intervention in Mexico was "not necessary," though she emphasized the two governments would continue collaborating on drug-trafficking matters.

## Previous transfers

Mexico sent its first batch of cartel figures to the United States in February 2025, transferring 29 individuals including Rafael Caro Quintero, a drug lord who was behind the killing of a U.S. Drug Enforcement Administration agent in 1985. A second round in August saw Mexico send 26 additional cartel figures, though none carried the profile of Caro Quintero.

García Harfuch previously characterized these transfers as public safety decisions, saying Mexico did not want the transferred individuals to continue operating drug trafficking operations from inside Mexican prisons.

David Mora, a Mexico analyst at the International Crisis Group, said that as pressure increases from the White House, Mexico's government "needs to resort to extraordinary measures, such as these transfers."

The U.S. State Department and Justice Department did not immediately respond to requests for comment on the latest transfer.
