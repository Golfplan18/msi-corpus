HARBIN, China — Sculptors gathered this weekend in Harbin to compete at the annual Ice and Snow Festival, as a Wolf Supermoon rose over the frozen cityscape of northeastern China's Heilongjiang province on Sunday and families traveled from distant locations to view illuminated works carved in what is already one of China's coldest cities.

Competitors put final touches on their entries Sunday, Jan. 4, a day after AP photographer Ng Han Guan documented sculptors preparing works for the competition and visitors approaching the icy steps of an obelisk sculpture glowing in yellow and purple. A security guard stood dwarfed by one of the larger ice structures on the same day.

The festival is held each January in Harbin, the provincial capital, located not far from China's border with Russia. Ice sculptures are lit from within, and the event draws competitors and spectators to a section of the city that the Associated Press described as a spectacle running through mid-February. For visitors who wish to, swimming amid the ice is also an option the festival offers.
