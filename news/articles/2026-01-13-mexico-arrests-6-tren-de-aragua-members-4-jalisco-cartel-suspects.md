Mexican authorities announced Tuesday they had arrested six alleged members of the Venezuelan-origin Tren de Aragua gang in Mexico City on charges related to drug trafficking, extortion, and human trafficking, and separately arrested four alleged members of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel in western Mexico. The dual enforcement actions were announced hours after Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum and U.S. President Donald Trump spoke about the two governments' ongoing collaboration against drug cartels.

Mexican authorities on Tuesday announced two separate enforcement actions targeting criminal organizations the Trump administration has designated as foreign terrorist organizations, arresting six alleged members of the Tren de Aragua gang in Mexico City and four alleged members of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel in western Mexico.

The announcements came a day after Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum and U.S. President Donald Trump spoke about their governments' ongoing collaboration against drug cartels, as Washington continues to press Mexico for concrete results against cross-border criminal networks.

## Tren de Aragua arrests

Mexico's Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch announced the Tren de Aragua arrests on social media Tuesday. The six suspects — one woman and five men — were taken into custody following surveillance on several buildings in the capital, authorities said. Drugs, a gun, and a notebook recording extortion activity in the city were seized during the operation. García Harfuch did not provide the nationalities of those arrested.

The suspects face charges related to drug trafficking, extortion, and human trafficking, authorities said.

## Jalisco cartel arrests

The Attorney General's Office announced the four Jalisco New Generation Cartel arrests separately later Tuesday. Spokesman Ulises Lara said one suspect was detained in Tepic, in Nayarit state along Mexico's Pacific coast, where the individual allegedly coordinated flights of small aircraft from Central America to clandestine landing strips in Nayarit, Jalisco, and Zacatecas states.

Three additional suspects were arrested in Zapopan, just outside Guadalajara, including the cartel's boss overseeing operations in Mexico's second-largest city and a man allegedly coordinating the movement of drug shipments from Central America, Lara said. He did not specify when those arrests occurred.

Guadalajara is one of the host cities for this summer's FIFA World Cup.

## Context

The Trump administration designated both Tren de Aragua and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel as foreign terrorist organizations. U.S. officials have alleged that Tren de Aragua maintained ties to Venezuela's now-deposed President Nicolás Maduro, and the U.S. government has targeted vessels in the Caribbean it alleges were carrying drugs for the gang.

Tren de Aragua originated more than a decade ago inside a Venezuelan prison and expanded its reach as nearly 8 million Venezuelans fled economic turmoil, migrating to other Latin American countries and the United States. Trump placed the gang at the center of his anti-immigrant political messaging, elevating its international profile.
