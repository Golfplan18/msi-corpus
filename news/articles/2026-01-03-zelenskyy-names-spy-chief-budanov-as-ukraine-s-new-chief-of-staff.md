KYIV, Ukraine — President Volodymyr Zelenskyy appointed Gen. Kyrylo Budanov, Ukraine's military intelligence chief, as his new chief of staff Friday, elevating one of the country's most prominent wartime figures to the heart of the presidential administration as diplomatic efforts to end Russia's invasion intensify.

Budanov, 39, who has led Ukraine's military intelligence agency — known by its acronym GUR — since 2020, replaces Andrii Yermak, Zelenskyy's longtime chief aide who resigned in November after anti-corruption officials raided his apartment in an investigation into alleged graft in Ukraine's energy sector.

The appointment places a battlefield intelligence architect with direct experience in Russia-related negotiations at the top of Ukraine's government as Zelenskyy has said a peace deal is "90% ready" — with the remaining points, believed to include territorial questions, still unresolved and carrying what the president called consequences for "the fate of peace, the fate of Ukraine and Europe."

## A career forged after Crimea

Budanov built his career in Ukraine's defense establishment following Russia's illegal annexation of Crimea in 2014, taking part in special operations and intelligence missions linked to the fighting with Moscow-backed separatist forces in eastern Ukraine and Crimea before Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022. He was reportedly wounded during one such operation.

Since that invasion, Budanov became a prominent public face of Kyiv's intelligence effort, appearing regularly in interviews and briefings that mixed strategic signaling with psychological pressure on Russia. He warned frequently of Moscow's long-term intentions toward Ukraine and the broader region, portraying the war as an existential struggle for the country's statehood.

Officials credited the GUR under Budanov with operations targeting Russian command structures, logistics hubs, energy infrastructure and naval assets — including attacks deep inside Russian territory and occupied areas of Ukraine.

## A high-profile target

Budanov's role and public status made him a target. He has reportedly survived multiple assassination attempts by Russian security services. In November 2023, his wife, Marianna, was hospitalized in Kyiv with heavy metals poisoning, according to the Associated Press.

## An uncertain role in the peace process

Budanov served as part of the Ukrainian delegation that worked with the U.S. negotiating team, and has had contact with the Russian side on issues such as coordinating prisoner exchanges. It was not yet clear what role, if any, he would take in the peace process in his new position as chief of staff.

In his first comments after the appointment was announced, Budanov thanked Zelenskyy for his trust and wrote on his Telegram page: "I continue to serve Ukraine. For me, it is both an honor and a responsibility — at a historic time for Ukraine — to focus on the critically important issues of the state's strategic security."
