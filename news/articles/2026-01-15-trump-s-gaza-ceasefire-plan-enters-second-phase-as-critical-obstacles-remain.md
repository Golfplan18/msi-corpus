JERUSALEM — The White House said Wednesday it is moving into the second phase of President Donald Trump's Gaza ceasefire plan, announcing a transitional Palestinian governing committee to run daily affairs in the territory as core requirements of the plan — Hamas disarmament, an international security force, and reconstruction financing — have not been resolved.

Trump's Mideast envoy, Steve Witkoff, announced the new phase in a post on X, saying it will include establishing the committee and beginning "the complicated tasks of disarming Hamas and reconstruction." The announcement contained few details about the committee's powers or full composition.

The move marks the first concrete governance step since the ceasefire halted more than two years of fighting in October 2025, but disarmament, financing for what the United Nations estimates will be $70 billion in reconstruction, and a promised international security force all remain unresolved — leaving the plan's prospects deeply uncertain.

## New Palestinian Committee

Egypt, Turkey and Qatar, which co-mediated the ceasefire alongside the United States, said the committee will be led by Ali Shaath, an engineer and former deputy minister in the Palestinian Authority in the West Bank. A U.S. official, speaking on condition of anonymity to discuss the evolving plan, said additional committee members are expected to be named within days.

"This really will be a technocratic committee," the official said. "They seem to be a group that wants to have peace."

The committee will report to the Board of Peace, a supervisory group of world leaders chaired by Trump. Nickolay Mladenov, a former Bulgarian politician and United Nations Mideast envoy, is expected to serve as the board's on-the-ground representative and has already met with Netanyahu and Palestinian leaders in the occupied West Bank.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu played down the announcement, calling the creation of the new committee a "declarative move."

## Disarmament Deadlock

Trump's plan calls for Hamas to surrender its weapons under international monitoring, with militants who disarm offered amnesty and the option to leave Gaza. Hamas has said it will dissolve its government once the committee takes office but has shown no signs it will dismantle its military wing or security forces.

Hamas has said it will not disarm until Israel ends its occupation of Palestinian territories. Bassem Naim, a senior Hamas official, told the Associated Press last month that the group is open to "freezing or storing" its weapons while a political process takes place, perhaps over many years. It is unclear whether that position would satisfy Israel.

"We're going to do our best to try and see if we can work with the Palestinian people to try something new," the U.S. official said. "It will be hard to do."

## Security Force and Reconstruction

Trump's plan calls for an International Stabilization Force to maintain security and train Palestinian police, but the force has not been formed and no deployment date has been announced. A U.S. official said there would be "important announcements in the coming weeks" but provided no details on the force's command structure or authorities.

No reconstruction plan has been announced. The United Nations estimates rebuilding Gaza — where most of the territory's 2 million people are displaced and unemployed following widespread destruction from more than two years of war — will cost $70 billion. No funding source has been identified.

## Phase 1 Incomplete

The ceasefire took effect on Oct. 10, halting more than two years of fighting between Israel and Hamas. Phase 1 also included the release of all remaining hostages held in Gaza in exchange for hundreds of Palestinians held by Israel. But Phase 1 terms have not been fully honored.

Palestinian militants continue to hold the remains of the last hostage — an Israeli police officer killed in the Hamas-led Oct. 7, 2023, attack that triggered the war. Netanyahu has said he is in no rush to advance to the next stage until the remains are returned.

Israeli fire has killed more than 400 Palestinians since the ceasefire took effect, according to Gaza health officials. Israel said it has targeted militants or responded to violations; Palestinian officials said scores of those killed were civilians. Israel retains control of just over half of Gaza, with further withdrawals tied to disarmament progress under terms still to be negotiated.

Trump's 20-point plan, approved by the U.N. Security Council, envisions a demilitarized Gaza rebuilt under international supervision, normalization of relations between Israel and Arab states, and a pathway toward Palestinian statehood. Israel rejects Palestinian statehood and opposes any role for the Palestinian Authority in postwar Gaza — conditions that U.S. officials acknowledged leave the plan's long-term prospects uncertain.
