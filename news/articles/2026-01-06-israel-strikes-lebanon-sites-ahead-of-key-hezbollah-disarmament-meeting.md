The strikes came days before Lebanon's army commander is scheduled to brief the government Thursday on disarming Hezbollah along the border — a requirement of the November 2024 ceasefire that ended a 14-month war between Israel and Hezbollah, and one Israel says is moving too slowly.

Israel's air force struck sites in southern and eastern Lebanon late Monday and into the early hours of Tuesday, including a strike that leveled a three-story commercial building in Sidon, Lebanon's third-largest coastal city, days before Lebanon's army commander is scheduled to brief the government on disarming Hezbollah.

The strike in Sidon hit around 1 a.m. Tuesday, collapsing a building in a commercial district. An Associated Press photographer at the scene described the area as containing workshops and mechanic shops; the building was uninhabited. At least one person was transported by ambulance, and rescue teams searched the site for others. No deaths were reported.

Lebanese President Joseph Aoun condemned the attacks Tuesday in a statement, calling them contrary to both international efforts to reduce hostilities and Lebanon's own effort to extend the government's authority into areas long dominated by Hezbollah and to disarm militants.

Israel's military said the strikes targeted weapons storage sites and infrastructure belonging to Hezbollah and Hamas. The military acknowledged the sites were in civilian areas but blamed the groups for operating there.

## Pre-strike warnings issued; Bekaa Valley homes hit

Nearly two hours before the strikes, Israel's military Arabic-language spokesman Avichay Adraee posted warnings on X that the military would strike targets in two villages in the eastern Bekaa Valley and two in southern Lebanon. The areas were evacuated; no casualties were reported from those strikes.

Lebanon's state-run National News Agency said a home struck in the Bekaa Valley village of Manara belonged to Sharhabil al-Sayed, a Hamas military commander killed in an Israeli drone strike in May 2024.

Earlier Monday, Lebanon's Health Ministry said a drone strike on a car in the southern village of Braikeh wounded two people. The Israeli military said that strike targeted two Hezbollah members.

Monday's strikes were in villages north of the Litani river, far from the Israeli border.

## Disarmament briefing set for Thursday

Lebanon's government is scheduled to discuss Hezbollah's disarmament at a meeting Thursday to be attended by army commander Gen. Rudolph Haikal.

The Lebanese army last year began disarming Palestinian militant groups, and the government said all areas close to the border with Israel — the south Litani area — would be clear of Hezbollah's armed presence by the end of 2025.

The strikes are the latest in near-daily Israeli military action since a ceasefire brokered by the United States ended a 14-month war between Israel and Hezbollah in November 2024. That ceasefire included a Lebanese pledge to disarm militant groups, which Israel says has not been fulfilled.

Since the ceasefire, Israel has carried out almost daily airstrikes, mainly targeting Hezbollah members but also killing at least 127 civilians, according to the office of the U.N. High Commissioner for Human Rights.

## Background

The latest Israel-Hezbollah conflict began Oct. 8, 2023, when Hezbollah fired rockets into Israel in solidarity with Hamas, one day after Hamas attacked southern Israel. Israel launched a widespread bombardment of Lebanon in September 2024 that severely weakened Hezbollah, followed by a ground invasion. Much of Hezbollah's political and military leadership was killed during the 14-month conflict before the November 2024 ceasefire.
