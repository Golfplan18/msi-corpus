President Donald Trump signed two executive orders on Friday aimed at addressing home affordability, a key issue for voters going into November’s election for control of the House and Senate, according to The Associated Press.

The AP said the first order would reduce federal housing regulatory burdens and create incentives for best practices by state and local governments, with the goal of making it easier for builders to construct more homes. The second order would reduce regulatory burdens tied to mortgages and make it easier for smaller community banks to provide home loans.

The AP reported that a draft of the first order said “Layers of unnecessary regulatory barriers, slow permitting processes, and onerous mandates at all levels of government have delayed construction, restricted development, and driven up the costs of new housing,” and that “These constraints have made housing less affordable for many Americans.”

The AP also said the executive orders were obtained exclusively by the Associated Press and that they reflect how the Trump administration is seeking to emphasize home ownership as a policy priority. It noted that housing affordability has become a political challenge for Republicans and Democrats alike, as lawmakers work on measures to respond to concerns that buying a first home has become difficult for many middle-class families.

The AP reported that on Thursday, the Senate passed a broad bipartisan housing bill that sought to adjust policies to increase construction and limit institutional ownership of home development. The AP said the bill’s status path in the House was uncertain, and that the White House said in a March 2 statement it supported passage of the measure.

It was unclear, the AP said, how quickly the federal efforts could generate new construction or reduce mortgage costs, citing that key regulatory issues on home development involve state and local government policy choices and that mortgage rates would reflect changes in financial markets. The AP said the multiyear construction shortage has kept prices high and that mortgage rates spiking after the pandemic left many renters unable to buy, while some existing owners have been unwilling to part with their current properties.

Under the first order, the AP said federal agencies would create incentives to speed up permitting times by state and local governments, including curtailing “green” building codes, reducing design and building mandates, and making it easier to deploy innovative construction methods. The order, the AP said, directed the EPA and the secretary of the Army to review and update stormwater, wetlands and other water-related permitting requirements to reduce costs and improve the ability to insure homes.

The AP reported that the order also instructed the departments of Commerce, Housing and Urban Development, and Transportation, along with the Federal Housing Finance Agency, to eliminate regulations and update programs that are reducing residential development. It said the administration also sought to eliminate environmental and energy efficiency regulations that could increase costs and restrict home construction, and that White House officials said Biden-era energy efficiency mandates in HUD and Agriculture departments’ guidelines could add up to $9,000 to housing construction costs. The AP said the officials requested anonymity to outline the details of the orders before their signing.

The AP said the first order would also have the Advisory Council on Historic Preservation simplify its guidance for historic reviews, and that the federal government would seek to align its New Markets Tax Credit program with Opportunity Zone tax breaks created during Trump’s first term. It said the order would not change state and local zoning codes, and that the administration’s aim was to preserve suburban housing rather than increase housing density. The AP reported that the White House said agencies could use best practices on housing regulations as criteria for rewarding discretionary grants, including an example of a 60-day deadline for local governments to approve building permits.

For the second order, the AP said it would direct the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau to change mortgage guidelines so that smaller banks could provide more home loans, including by updating requirements under the Home Mortgage Disclosure Act to lessen regulatory burdens for obtaining a mortgage. The AP said the White House maintained that the mortgage-regulation changes would increase competition among financial institutions, reduce the cost of borrowing for buyers, and preserve the financial safety and stability of the mortgage market, with White House officials expecting homebuyers could see impacts in a matter of months.

The AP said elevated home prices have emerged as a key issue for voters under 40 as the midterms approach. It reported that the median price of an existing home sold in February was $398,000, according to the National Association of Realtors, and that the average 30-year mortgage rate in February was 6.05%, down from 6.84% a year ago, also based on the Realtors’ sales data.

The AP said Trump has primarily sought to address affordability by directing Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, the two government-controlled mortgage companies, to purchase $200 billion in mortgage bonds. It also said Trump had called for limiting financial institutions’ ability to buy homes and for caps on credit card interest rates, and noted that Trump has previously pushed back against dramatically increasing construction, saying it could bring down home prices and reduce the net worth of existing owners.

The AP included a quote from Trump’s January Cabinet meeting in which he said: “People that own their homes, we’re going to keep them wealthy. We’re going to keep those prices up. We’re not going to destroy the value of their homes so that somebody that didn’t work very hard can buy a home.”
