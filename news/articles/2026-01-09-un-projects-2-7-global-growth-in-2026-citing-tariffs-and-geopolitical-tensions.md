The forecast signals that the global economy continues to cope with structural headwinds from trade friction and geopolitical conflict, even as some resilience has emerged. Prospects vary sharply by region, with large developing economies posting stronger gains while advanced economies face slower expansion and many low-income countries remain constrained by debt and climate-related pressures.

The United Nations on Thursday projected global economic growth of 2.7% in 2026, slightly below the estimated 2.8% for 2025, as higher U.S. tariffs, economic uncertainties, and geopolitical tensions continue to weigh on the global outlook. U.N. economists projected growth would edge up to 2.9% in 2027 — still well below the 3.2% average recorded in the decade before the COVID-19 pandemic, according to the U.N.'s World Economic Situation and Prospects report.

"A combination of economic, geopolitical and technological tensions is reshaping the global landscape," U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said Thursday in a statement, "generating new economic uncertainty and social vulnerabilities."

The report signals that the global economy continues to grapple with structural headwinds from trade friction and geopolitical conflict, even as some unexpected strength has emerged. Prospects vary sharply by region, with large developing economies posting stronger gains while advanced economies face slower expansion and many low-income countries remain constrained by debt and climate-related pressures.

Despite sharp increases in U.S. tariffs last year, U.N. economists said there was "unexpected resilience" in the global economy, supported by solid consumer spending and easing inflation. They cautioned, however, that underlying weaknesses persist.

## United States and Advanced Economies

U.S. economic growth declined from 2.8% in 2024 to an estimated 1.9% in 2025, the report said, "as strong consumer spending and AI-related investment were partly offset by weak residential and commercial construction." The U.N. projected U.S. growth would rise to 2.0% in 2026 and 2.2% in 2027.

Japan's economy is forecast to grow 0.9% in 2026 and 1.0% in 2027, below the 1.2% estimated for 2025. U.N. economists said private consumption is expected to recover gradually, while exports — particularly of automobiles — "will likely remain constrained by higher United States tariffs and policy uncertainty."

The European Union's growth is forecast to slip from 1.5% in 2025 to 1.4% in 2026 "as higher U.S. tariffs and ongoing geopolitical uncertainty dampen exports," the report said, before recovering to 1.6% in 2027.

## Developing Economies

Large developing economies, including China, India, and Indonesia, are expected to continue posting solid growth. China, the world's second-largest economy, is forecast to grow 4.6% in 2026 and 4.5% in 2027, below the estimated 4.9% expansion in 2025. East Asia as a whole is projected to grow 4.4% in 2026, down from 4.9% in 2025.

"A temporary easing of trade tensions with the United States — including targeted tariff reductions and a one-year trade truce — has helped stabilize confidence," the report said of the region.

Growth in the world's least developed countries is projected to accelerate from an estimated 3.9% in 2025 to 4.6% in 2026 and 5.0% in 2027. The U.N. singled out stronger economic performances in Bangladesh, Ethiopia, and Tanzania.

Africa is projected to record modest growth, rising from an estimated 3.9% in 2025 to 4.0% in 2026 and 4.1% in 2027. "However, high debt and climate-related shocks pose significant risks," the report said.

In Latin America and the Caribbean, growth is projected to slip slightly from an estimated 2.4% in 2025 to 2.3% in 2026 before edging up to 2.5% in 2027. The U.N. described the outlook for the region as "broadly resilient."
