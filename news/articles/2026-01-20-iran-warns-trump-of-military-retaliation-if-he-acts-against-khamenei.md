Iran warned President Donald Trump in January that the country's military would retaliate against any U.S. action targeting Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, as mass protests over Iran's economic policies prompted a violent government crackdown that killed thousands.

"Trump knows that if any hand of aggression is extended toward our leader, we not only cut that hand but also we will set fire to their world," said Gen. Abolfazl Shekarchi, a spokesman for Iran's armed forces.

The warning reflected the highest tensions between the U.S. and Iran in months, with Trump having explicitly called for regime change in Iran while the Islamic Republic faced its worst domestic unrest in years. The exchange came as the U.S. repositioned military assets in the region, signaling a readiness to respond to any Iranian action.

## The Warning

Trump's statements calling for regime change had already pushed tensions to their highest point in months. On Saturday, January 18, Trump told Politico that Khamenei was "a sick man who should run his country properly and stop killing people." He added that "it's time to look for new leadership in Iran."

Two days later, Iran responded with an explicit threat. Gen. Abolfazl Shekarchi, spokesman for Iran's armed forces, warned that any U.S. aggression toward Khamenei would prompt a severe response.

"Trump knows that if any hand of aggression is extended toward our leader, we not only cut that hand but also we will set fire to their world," Shekarchi said.

## The Crackdown

The military threat came as Iran's government continued its violent response to the largest domestic protests in decades. The unrest began on December 28 over the country's economic crisis and quickly expanded into a broader challenge to the government's authority.

By January 20, the death toll had reached at least 4,519 people, according to the U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency. The agency, which has maintained a network of activists inside Iran confirming fatalities, reported death figures that the Associated Press was unable to independently verify.

Khamenei himself had acknowledged the scale of the deaths. Speaking on Saturday, he said the protests had left "several thousand" people dead while blaming the United States for stoking the unrest.

The toll exceeded any other round of protest or unrest in Iran in decades. No protests had occurred for several days by January 20, but fears persisted that the death count would rise as information gradually emerged from a country still under a government-imposed internet shutdown that had been in effect since January 8.

## The Arrests and Threats

Government authorities had arrested more than 26,300 people in connection with the protests, according to Human Rights Activists News Agency reporting. Official statements raised concerns about the fate of those detained.

On January 19, National Police Chief Gen. Ahmad Reza Radan appeared on Iran's state television with a warning for arrested protesters.

"Those who were deceived by foreign intelligence services, and became their soldiers in practice, have a chance to turn themselves in," Radan said. "In case of surrender, definitely there will be a reduction in punishment. They have three days to turn themselves in."

Radan did not say what would happen after the deadline expired.

## The Military Dimension

The rhetorical confrontation between Trump and Iran coincided with a significant repositioning of U.S. military assets. The USS Abraham Lincoln aircraft carrier, which had been in the South China Sea, had passed through the Strait of Malacca by January 20 with three accompanying destroyers, according to ship-tracking data.

A U.S. Navy official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the carrier strike group was heading west. While defense officials stopped short of confirming the group was headed to the Middle East, its current position in the Indian Ocean meant it was only days away from entering the region.

Trump had previously drawn two explicit red lines for Iran's government. Any killing of peaceful protesters or any mass executions following the protests would, he signaled, provoke a U.S. response.

The Abraham Lincoln's rerouting would not be the first time in recent years that a carrier strike group deployed to the Pacific had been moved to the Middle East to address regional instability. The same carrier was rerouted to the Middle East in 2024, and the USS Nimitz strike group had been ordered to the region in June of the previous year.

Human rights monitors and international observers watched for how events in Iran would unfold. The combination of mass civilian casualties, mass arrests, threatened executions, and U.S. military positioning created a volatile situation with potential for rapid escalation.
