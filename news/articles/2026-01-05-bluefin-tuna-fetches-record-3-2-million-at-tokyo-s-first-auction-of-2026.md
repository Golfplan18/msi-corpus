A 243-kilogram (535-pound) bluefin tuna sold for 510 million yen — roughly $3.2 million — at the first auction of 2026 at Tokyo's Toyosu fish market Monday, setting an all-time record for the annual New Year's opening event.

The winning bidder was Kiyomura Corp., whose owner Kiyoshi Kimura runs the popular Sushi Zanmai chain. Kimura, who has claimed the top bid at the opening auction many times in the past, broke the previous record of 334 million yen ($2.1 million) — a mark he himself set in 2019.

"The price shot up before you knew it," Kimura told reporters after the auction.

## A fish from Oma

The record-setting tuna was caught off the coast of Oma in northern Japan, a region widely regarded for producing some of the country's finest fish. At the final sale price, it cost 2.1 million yen ($13,360) per kilogram, or $6,060 per pound.

At the predawn auction, hundreds of torpedo-shaped tuna — their tails cut off so bidders can inspect the meat for color, texture and fattiness — lined the floor of Toyosu. Prices are significantly higher than usual for Oma tuna at the celebratory New Year's event, according to the Associated Press.

## Good luck and good fish

Kimura said the purchase reflected both commercial judgment and seasonal tradition.

"It's in part for good luck," he said. "But when I see a good looking tuna, I cannot resist ... I haven't sampled it yet, but it's got to be delicious."

Pacific bluefin tuna was previously a threatened species due to climate change and overfishing, according to the Associated Press. Its stock has been recovering following conservation efforts.
