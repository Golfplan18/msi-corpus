Democrats in The Villages, one of the world's largest retirement communities and a longstanding conservative stronghold in central Florida, are organizing more openly during the 2024 presidential election cycle. The local Villages Democratic Club has seen its membership nearly double since the 2020 election cycle, reaching roughly 1,500 people, according to club president Diane Foley, who credited Vice President Kamala Harris' emergence as the Democratic presidential candidate with energizing residents who had long kept their political views private.

The shift illustrates how the 2024 presidential race is reshaping political dynamics in communities long regarded as safely Republican, while revealing the social pressures that have historically kept Democratic voices quiet in heavily partisan retirement enclaves.

THE VILLAGES, Fla. — Democrats in The Villages, one of the world's largest retirement communities, are organizing more openly during the 2024 presidential election cycle, ending years of self-imposed political silence in a central Florida enclave that has long been a Republican stronghold, the Associated Press reported Tuesday.

The Villages Democratic Club's membership has nearly doubled since the 2020 election cycle, reaching roughly 1,500 people, according to club president Diane Foley. Foley credited Vice President Kamala Harris' emergence as the Democratic presidential candidate with energizing residents who had previously kept quiet to preserve social harmony.

"They are beginning to realize that they have a voice and they can use it," Foley said.

As MSI previously reported, [Democrats are gaining a foothold in Florida's Villages retirement enclave](/articles/2024-09-24-democrats-gain-a-foothold-in-florida-s-villages-retirement-enclave) — a development now accelerating with Harris atop the Democratic ticket. Foley said the silence had been longstanding: "Democrats have been inclined to be quiet and to not broadcast their political preferences because we were so overwhelmed by the Republican party."

The new Democratic visibility has taken concrete forms in the community. Parades of golf carts decorated in support of Harris have rolled through the streets. Second Gentleman Doug Emhoff recently paid a visit. Democrats who have worn Harris T-shirts or put up lawn signs report that neighbors approached them with relief, saying they believed they were the only ones.

Not all supporters rallying behind Harris in The Villages came from the Democratic Party. Howard Underwood, 71, a retired lawyer, said he and his wife, Janet, 74, were Republicans for four decades until Donald Trump became the party's candidate in 2016. He said he is now unaffiliated with either major party but has volunteered with the Democratic club to support Harris.

"We've made common cause with the Democrats, even though I don't share a whole lot of Kamala's ideas on things," Underwood said.

Underwood said he chose not to put a bumper sticker on his car for fear of blowback. But he acknowledged the political atmosphere was shifting.

"Democrats in The Villages has kind of become a thing," he said.

Despite the increased activity, most Democratic residents said they continue to avoid open political discussions with neighbors. "You want to get along with your neighbors. They're nice people. So we just don't talk about it," Foley said.

The tensions have not remained entirely mild. Some residents reported receiving death threats over their voting intentions, according to the AP.

Wildwood-The Villages, the official name for the census-designated, master-planned metro area, has a population of more than 151,000 people.
