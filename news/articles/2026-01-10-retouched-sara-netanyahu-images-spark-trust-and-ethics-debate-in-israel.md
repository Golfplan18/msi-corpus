JERUSALEM — Retouched images of Sara Netanyahu, distributed by Israel’s government in official announcements, have ignited an ethics debate over trust in official imagery and the integrity of record-keeping, the Associated Press reported Friday.

The photos, described as showing Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu alongside his wife and U.S. Ambassador Mike Huckabee and Israeli soldiers as they lit Hanukkah candles at the Western Wall, appeared to include heavy retouching of Sara Netanyahu’s appearance, including smoothing skin and sharpening facial features, the report said.

Critics said the core concern was not the existence of photo-editing software itself, which is common among public figures, but the use of manipulated images within state communications and official archiving. The report said the issue raised questions about whether such edits distort reality in ways that can undermine the reliability of government records.

Shabi Gatenio, a veteran political journalist who broke the story in The Seventh Eye, said Israel’s archives contain only authentic images captured by photographers since the establishment of the state. “All the pictures to this day in the archives in Israel are authentic pictures of reality as it was captured by the lenses of photographers’ cameras since the establishment of the state,” Gatenio said. He added that inserting altered images into the database would “forever infect it with a virtual reality that never existed.”

After the manipulation was revealed, the government took what the report described as an unprecedented step of crediting Sara Netanyahu by name in releases that included manipulated images. It also said it was not clear whether the official archive would include images of her taken during the second half of last year, when Gatenio said the editing appears to have begun.

Nitzan Chen, director of Israel’s Government Press Office, told the Associated Press that images of the prime minister are never manipulated and that his office would not upload retouched photos to the official archive. Chen said his office’s lawyers were trying to determine how to handle and properly identify photos “processed by people other than GPO photographers,” and he said the Justice Ministry was examining the criteria, limitations and possibilities for edited images. Chen stressed, however, that there is nothing illegal about touching up photos, arguing that the issue is transparency when such changes are made.

Gatenio said he first noticed the image editing in July when the Netanyahu couple visited President Donald Trump in Washington, D.C., and again in September when Sara Netanyahu joined her husband on the tarmac ahead of a trip to New York for the U.N. General Assembly. The report said the prime minister’s office released a video send-off along with a photo credited to Avi Ohayon, and that digital forensics expert Hany Farid found the image had been post-processed to smooth Sara Netanyahu’s skin and remove wrinkles. Farid said: “There’s been some Photoshop editing to — let’s call it — ‘beautify,’ lighten, smooth the face.” He also said: “Is it nefarious? No. Is it a problem? Yes. This is about something bigger than, ‘she Photoshopped her face to make herself look younger.’ This is about trust. Why should I trust any official photo coming out of that administration?”

The report said photos of Sara Netanyahu meeting Vice President JD Vance and his wife, Usha, in Washington also appeared to have been retouched. It said the Times of Israel told readers it would no longer carry official state photos that appear manipulated, and that the Associated Press does not publish images that appear to have been retouched or digitally manipulated.

Beyond Photoshop-like editing, the Associated Press report described a broader pattern in the Netanyahu administration’s official communications in which Sara Netanyahu’s Instagram posted content that appears AI-edited or generated. It said one image raised suspicions in Israel because Sara Netanyahu appeared in a black dress rather than a dark red frock, and because it included fireworks and flags that Farid said were “almost certainly” generated by AI. The report said the Instagram image later carried a tag indicating it may have been altered or generated using AI, while it was unclear when the tag was added or by whom.

The prime minister’s office, the report said, maintained that Benjamin Netanyahu’s images are not edited, quoting it as saying: “No Photoshop, no corrections, no color. Nothing.” The report also cited Tehilla Shwartz Altshuler, who runs the “Democracy in the Digital Age Program” at the Israel Democracy Institute, saying the approach reflects “part of the populist playbook” and that there was “no question” Netanyahu was emulating how Trump uses the technology. Altshuler said: “This is exactly what Netanyahu and his surrounding circle have tried to do for many years,” and described a presentation of Netanyahu as a superhero and his wife as a supermodel. She said: “The question of archiving the truth, archiving history, will be one of the questions of our time.”
