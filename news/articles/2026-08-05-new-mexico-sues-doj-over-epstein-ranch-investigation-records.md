## New Mexico sues DOJ over Epstein ranch investigation records

**Subtype:** fact

- New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez filed a federal lawsuit Wednesday against the Department of Justice and Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche, alleging the DOJ stonewalled the state's criminal investigation into Jeffrey Epstein's activities at Zorro Ranch.
- The complaint asks the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia for a declaration that Blanche and the DOJ acted illegally in refusing to hand over evidence about events at the ranch, and for a court order directing the DOJ to comply with the Epstein transparency law and Touhy regulations governing federal document requests during litigation.
- The U.S. attorney's office for the district of New Mexico said it "substantively responded" to Torrez's request on June 30 by emailing 31 documents; Torrez said the papers were mostly old news clippings and heavily redacted pages of little use to state investigators.
- Zorro Ranch, a roughly 10,000-acre property about 30 miles south of Santa Fe, has been mentioned thousands of times in recent Epstein files releases but has not been subject to a confirmed federal search, according to Torrez.

## Competing claims emerge over DOJ response to state records request

The lawsuit, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia, accuses the DOJ of "stonewalling" Torrez's investigation and alleges that the federal government took steps that "actively harms victims and undermines the public interest." Torrez and his senior prosecutors "have faced sustained resistance" to the kind of information-sharing that had previously occurred between state and federal investigators, according to the Associated Press. The complaint also seeks costs and "such other and further relief as the Court deems just and proper," The Guardian reported.

At the center of the dispute are competing accounts of what the DOJ provided. Torrez said in a July 14 letter to Blanche that he had made "repeated, documented, good-faith efforts to obtain the unredacted investigative records in the DoJ's custody," and warned he would "pursue all available legal remedies without further notice" if a July 31 deadline he set passed without compliance. The U.S. attorney's office countered that it had "substantively responded" on June 30 by emailing 31 documents. Torrez characterized the response as "mostly old news clippings and heavily redacted pages of little use."

The filing also arrived the same day New Mexico released a report showing preliminary findings from its ongoing probe into Epstein and the ranch.

The lawsuit follows Torrez's reopening of the investigation in February, after Zorro Ranch was mentioned thousands of times in recent releases of the Epstein files. Those files were compelled into public release by a transparency act signed by Donald Trump in November. Democrats and critics have accused Blanche of covering up many of the files, The Guardian reported.

Torrez has said he wanted to establish what took place at the ranch, which, unlike other properties prominent in Epstein's sex-trafficking network — including locations in Palm Beach, New York, and a private Caribbean island — saw no confirmed federal search. The investigation was initially opened by Hector Balderas, Torrez's predecessor as New Mexico attorney general, in February 2019, before being mothballed at the request of federal prosecutors at the Southern District of New York, which led a previous Epstein investigation. Those same prosecutors told Torrez last month that they "will not cooperate" in the New Mexico investigation, he said in a social media post on July 14.

Epstein purchased Zorro Ranch in 1993 from the estate of former New Mexico Governor Bruce King, according to The Guardian. Numerous photographs of men and young girls taken at the property in subsequent years featured in the latest Epstein files release, and survivors gave testimony about sexual and physical abuse they were subjected to at the ranch by Epstein and others. Epstein died in a New York jail in August 2019 while awaiting trial on child sex-trafficking charges.

The Guardian has contacted the justice department for comment on Wednesday's filing.
