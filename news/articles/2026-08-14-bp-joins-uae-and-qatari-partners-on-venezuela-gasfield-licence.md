## Meg O'Neill's first major deal as BP's new chief executive

BP will develop the second phase of the Loran offshore gasfield in Venezuela alongside two oil firms that have close links to the Trump administration, according to The Guardian. The London-listed company's project is one of the first large-scale foreign investments in the country since the US removed Nicolás Maduro from power in January, the newspaper reported.

BP holds a licence to develop the field with a Qatari company owned by the Al-Khayyat brothers, the Syrian-born billionaires working with Ivanka Trump, the president's daughter, and her husband, Jared Kushner, to build a multibillion-dollar resort in Albania. BP will also work alongside the overseas investment arm of the UAE's national oil company, run by the industry minister, Sultan Al Jaber, who in 2025 promised Trump that the Gulf state would grow its investments in the US energy sector more than sixfold, to $440bn (£325bn), within a decade.

The deal makes BP one of the first large-scale international oil firms to return to Venezuela after the rival Shell secured a licence to develop the first phase of the Loran field earlier this summer. BP is one of a handful of European oil firms that have returned to the basin since Trump called for foreign oil companies to invest "at least $100bn" to help rebuild and tap Venezuela's oil resources. Caracas is also in talks over oil projects with the Italian oil company Eni, the newspaper reported.

ExxonMobil and ConocoPhillips have begun scouting opportunities in Venezuela but have been slower to play a role in its fossil fuel revival after the country nationalised the industry in 2007. Chevron remained in the country as a minority partner to Venezuela's state oil company and has continued to produce crude.

Meg O'Neill, BP's chief executive, described the licence as "an important step forward" in the company's collaboration with Caracas, which "reflects the progress we have made together." She said: "BP has a longstanding presence in the region and deep experience developing major gas resources. Together, these agreements provide a strong foundation to progress Venezuela's offshore gas potential and unlock the next phase of development."

The licence was awarded almost four months after BP agreed to work with the Venezuelan government to explore potential gas development, and was one of the first big agreements struck by O'Neill, a US-born former ExxonMobil executive, in her first month at the helm.

Venezuela is believed to hold the largest oil reserves of any country in the world, according to The Guardian. The newspaper reported the country produced more than 3.5m barrels of oil a day in the late 1990s as one of the top 10 crude producers in the world, but output fell to roughly 1m barrels a day. Since the Trump administration took control of Venezuela's oil industry, output has climbed to 1.2m barrels a day, allowing exports to US refineries to reach their highest level since 2019, the newspaper reported.
