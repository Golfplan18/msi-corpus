BANGUI, Central African Republic — The Central African Republic's Constitutional Council on Monday confirmed President Faustin-Archange Touadéra's re-election with 77.9% of the vote, granting him a third term and rejecting runner-up Anicet Georges Dologuélé's fraud appeal, the Associated Press reported. The council also dismissed Dologuélé's bid to annul the election, citing a lack of supporting documents.

The ruling cements Touadéra's hold on power in one of Africa's most fragile states, where a 2023 referendum removed presidential term limits and the main opposition coalition boycotted the election, citing what it called an unequal political environment.

The Constitutional Council's certified tally revised Touadéra's vote share upward from the 76.15% reported in provisional results earlier this month. Dologuélé's share was revised down to 13.1% from 14.66%.

## Opposition accepts ruling while vowing to continue fight

Dologuélé's party, the Union for Central African Renewal, said in a Facebook statement that it accepted the ruling in a "spirit of republican responsibility" but said its struggle would continue.

"Our fight has never been one of disorder. It is about electoral truth, the sovereignty of the people and respect for democratic rules," Dologuélé's spokesperson, Yves Sanghamy, said.

Dologuélé had contested the provisional results on Jan. 6, alleging malpractice by the National Elections Authority and widespread fraud, and had proclaimed himself the winner.

## Election context

Touadéra, 68, faced six challengers in the December vote, but the main opposition coalition did not participate after denouncing what it called an unequal political environment. Analysts said Touadéra has consolidated power within state institutions, according to the Associated Press.

The election was the first in the country's history where citizens voted simultaneously for all tiers of government, including presidential, legislative, regional and municipal seats. Approximately 2.4 million citizens were registered to vote.

## Background: Conflict and Russian security ties

The Central African Republic has been embroiled in conflict since 2013, when mostly Muslim rebels seized power and forced then-President François Bozizé from office. A 2019 peace deal between the government and 14 armed groups de-escalated the fighting, though six of the 14 groups later withdrew from the agreement.

Wagner, a Russian mercenary group, has been responsible for Touadéra's security. Tensions have grown between Touadéra and Russia, however, after Moscow demanded that Wagner be replaced with the Russian government-run Africa Corps, the Associated Press reported.

Touadéra's inauguration is scheduled for March 30.
