## BepiColombo spacecraft separate from cruiser, close in on Mercury

**Subtype:** fact

- The linked European and Japanese spacecraft of the BepiColombo mission separated from their cruiser on Thursday, beginning the final leg of a nearly decade-long voyage to Mercury.
- Ground controllers at the European Space Agency's control center in Germany waited two hours for radio confirmation of the separation, conducted more than 124 million miles (200 million kilometers) from Earth.
- Lead project scientist Geraint Jones called the maneuver "a brilliant moment" after confirmation arrived.

- BepiColombo had 2 million miles (3.3 million kilometers) remaining as of Thursday.

## November orbit insertion planned at solar system's innermost planet

"It's a brilliant moment," said lead project scientist Geraint Jones, speaking after controllers at the European Space Agency's control center in Germany received the radio signals they had been waiting for. The separation had gone ahead as planned, but the signal confirming it took two anxious hours to cross the more than 124 million miles (200 million kilometers) separating the mission from Earth.

The operation is the latest step in a nearly decade-long journey for the two probes, which have traveled together attached to a cruiser for most of the voyage. They are bound for the solar system's smallest planet and the one closest to the sun.

If all goes well, the conjoined spacecraft will enter orbit around Mercury in November. In December, the European and Japanese orbiters are to split from each other and begin mapping and studying the entire planet from different altitudes.

As of Thursday, the spacecraft still had 2 million miles (3.3 million kilometers) to go before reaching its destination.
