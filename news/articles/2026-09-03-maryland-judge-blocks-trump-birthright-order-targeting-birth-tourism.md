## Maryland judge blocks Trump birthright order targeting birth tourism

- US District Judge Deborah Boardman of Maryland blocked the Trump administration from enforcing a new executive order limiting birthright citizenship, calling the policy "almost certainly unconstitutional" in a 35-page ruling on September 3.
- The preliminary injunction followed a challenge by rights groups including We Are CASA to Trump's August 6 executive order, which the president had described as targeting "birth tourism."
- Boardman wrote that "the supreme court has spoken: Children in the certified class are 'citizens at birth,'" citing the high court's earlier rejection of Trump's bid to narrow the constitutional right.
- Shana Khader, legal director at the Maryland-based group We Are CASA, said the administration "has lost before this court, lost before the supreme court, and lost again today."
- The ruling is the latest legal setback in a series of immigration policy initiatives pursued by the Trump administration, including efforts to expel undocumented migrants and remove deportation protections from nationals of more than a dozen countries.

## Boardman ruled policy "almost certainly unconstitutional" in 35-page opinion

Trump's August 6 executive order was directed at people the president alleged enter the country under the guise of tourism while intending to give birth. In her 35-page ruling, Boardman found that the order contradicted the Supreme Court's prior holding and that the president's policy "is almost certainly unconstitutional." Rights groups, including We Are CASA, had challenged the order in court.

Shana Khader, legal director at the Maryland-based group We Are CASA, said the administration "has lost before this court, lost before the supreme court, and lost again today."

The birthright citizenship order is among several immigration policy initiatives the Trump administration has pursued. Those efforts have included expelling undocumented migrants and removing deportation protections from nationals of more than a dozen countries.
