RAQQA, Syria — A ceasefire agreement between Syria's new government and the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces appeared to collapse Monday, a day after it was announced, as fresh fighting broke out around two prisons holding Islamic State group members in the country's northeast and some IS detainees broke out of one facility.

The breakdown threatens one of the most consequential security arrangements in the region: the SDF's custody of approximately 9,000 IS fighters held without trial whose escape could rekindle the extremist group's capacity for mass violence in Syria and Iraq.

## Prison break and clashes

The Syrian army confirmed in a statement that detainees at Shaddadi Prison in the town of Shaddadeh fled amid the chaos, and said a curfew had been imposed and search operations were under way. The SDF confirmed it had lost control of the facility, which sits roughly 50 kilometers (31 miles) from the Iraqi border.

The army and the SDF traded accusations over responsibility for the escape. The government warned the SDF "not to facilitate the fleeing of Daesh detainees or opening prisons as a revenge measure or for political pressure," according to a statement carried on state media, using the Arabic acronym for the Islamic State group.

At a second prison, al-Aqtan, northeast of the city of Raqqa, the SDF said nine of its members were killed and 20 others wounded in fighting. An Associated Press reporter saw a U.S. military convoy entering the prison area, apparently to mediate between the two sides.

## SDF declares resistance

The SDF issued a statement calling for "all of our youth" to "join the ranks of the resistance," invoking the 2014 siege of the Kurdish border town of Kobani and directing its language explicitly at Turkey.

"Just as our comrades in 2014 forged a historic resistance in Kobani and turned it into a graveyard for (the Islamic State group) ... today we affirm with the same resolve that we will turn our cities ... into a graveyard for the new (IS)-minded people who are directed by Turkey," the statement said.

SDF chief commander Mazloum Abdi was said to be in Damascus to discuss the ceasefire deal reached Sunday — which ended days of fighting during which government forces captured wide areas of northeast Syria from the SDF — but Abdi issued no statement about the meeting.

## Diplomatic contacts

Syrian interim President Ahmad al-Sharaa spoke by telephone Monday with U.S. President Donald Trump, according to al-Sharaa's office. Al-Sharaa "affirmed the importance of preserving the unity and independence of Syrian territory" and "the need to guarantee the rights and protection of the Kurdish people," the office said. The two leaders also agreed to continue cooperating in the fight against IS.

Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan warned the SDF against delay in complying with the agreement. "Procrastination, resistance, and playing for time by hiding behind various excuses will benefit no one," Erdogan said. "The era of terrorism in our region has come to an end. The requirements of the ceasefire and full integration agreement must be fulfilled without delay, and no one should miscalculate again."

## Stakes for IS detainees

The SDF, the principal U.S.-backed force that fought IS across Syria, controls more than a dozen prisons in the northeast where approximately 9,000 IS members have been held for years without trial. Many of the detainees are believed to have carried out atrocities in Syria and Iraq after IS declared a caliphate over large parts of both countries in June 2014.

IS was defeated in Iraq in 2017 and in Syria in 2019, but the group's sleeper cells continue to carry out deadly attacks in both countries. The question of what to do with the imprisoned IS fighters has long remained unresolved, with no functioning international tribunal to try them and no country willing to repatriate most foreign fighters.

Turkey considers the SDF a terrorist organization because of its ties to the Kurdistan Workers' Party, or PKK, a Kurdish separatist group that has waged a long-running insurgency in Turkey. The United States has maintained relations with both the Syrian government and the SDF.
