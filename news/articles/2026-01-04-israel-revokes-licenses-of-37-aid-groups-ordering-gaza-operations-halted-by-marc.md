Israel revoked the licenses of more than 37 humanitarian organizations operating in Gaza this week, barring them from bringing supplies into the territory or deploying international staff and ordering all suspended groups to halt operations by March 1, the Associated Press reported Saturday.

The 37 organizations include some of the most prominent of more than 100 independent nongovernmental groups working in Gaza alongside United Nations agencies. Those whose licenses were revoked include Doctors Without Borders, the Norwegian Refugee Council, Oxfam and Medical Aid for Palestinians.

The ban strains aid operations already under severe pressure for Gaza's more than 2 million Palestinians more than 12 weeks into a ceasefire. The United Nations says more than a quarter of families eat only one meal a day, food prices remain out of reach for many, and more than 1 million people need better tents as winter storms sweep the territory.

In a joint statement Tuesday, the UN and leading NGOs said the organizations that remain licensed by Israel "are nowhere near the number required just to meet immediate and basic needs" in Gaza.

## Why licenses were revoked

Earlier this year, Israel introduced strict new registration requirements for aid agencies working in Gaza, most notably requiring groups to provide the names and personal details of local and international staff. The registration process is overseen by Israel's Ministry for Diaspora Affairs and Combating Antisemitism, led by a far-right member of the ruling Likud party.

Israel says the rules aim to prevent Hamas and other militants from infiltrating the groups — something it has said was occurring throughout the more than two-year-old war. The UN, which leads the massive aid program in Gaza, and independent groups deny the allegations and Israeli claims of major diversion of aid supplies by Hamas.

Aid organizations said they did not comply, in part, because they feared that handing over staff information could endanger workers. More than 500 aid workers have been killed in Gaza during the war, according to the United Nations. Israel denies targeting aid workers.

"Demanding staff lists as a condition for access to territory is an outrageous overreach," Doctors Without Borders, known by its French acronym MSF, said Friday. The group said Israeli officials had refused its attempts to find alternatives.

Groups had a week from Dec. 31 to appeal.

## The case against MSF

A December report issued by an Israeli government team recommended rejection of MSF's license. It pointed primarily to statements by the group criticizing Israel, including characterizing its campaign in Gaza as genocide and calling a monthslong ban on food entering the territory earlier in the year a "starvation tactic." The report said those statements violated neutrality and constituted "delegitimization of Israel."

The report also repeated claims that an MSF employee killed by an Israeli airstrike in 2024 was an operative with the Islamic Jihad militant group — a claim the report said suggested MSF "maintains connections with a terrorist group."

MSF on Friday denied the allegations, saying it would "never knowingly employ anyone involved in military activities." The group said the statements cited by Israel simply described what its teams witnessed in Gaza.

"The fault lies with those committing these atrocities, not with those who speak of them," MSF said.

## Medical operations at risk

Independent NGOs play a major role in sustaining Gaza's health sector, devastated by more than two years of fighting and restrictions on supplies.

MSF said Israel's decision would have a catastrophic impact on its work in Gaza, where it provides funding and international staff for six hospitals as well as running two field hospitals and eight primary health centers, clinics and medical points. It also runs two of Gaza's five stabilization centers helping children with severe malnutrition.

MSF's teams treated 100,000 trauma cases, performed surgeries on 10,000 patients and handled a third of Gaza's births, the group said. Since the ceasefire began in early October, MSF brought in about 7% of the 2,239 tons of medical supplies that Israel allowed into Gaza, according to a UN tracking dashboard — making it the largest provider of medical supplies after UN agencies and the Red Cross. The group has 60 international staffers in the West Bank and Gaza and more than 1,200 local staff, most of them medical professionals.

## Staff and supply disruptions

Aid groups said the most immediate impact would likely be the inability to send international staff into Gaza.

"Having international presence in Gaza is a morale booster for our staff who are already feeling isolated," said Shaina Low, communications adviser for the Norwegian Refugee Council, one of the main NGOs providing shelter supplies and fresh water to displaced people. The NRC has roughly 30 international staff who rotate in and out of Gaza working alongside some 70 Palestinians. The NRC said it had not been allowed to bring in supplies for 10 months.

Many of the 37 groups had already been blocked from bringing supplies into Gaza since March, said Bushra Khalidi, Oxfam's policy lead for Israel and the occupied Palestinian territories. What changes with the formal license revocation is "that these practices are now formalized, giving Israel full impunity to restrict operations and shut out organizations it disagrees with," she said.

The effects extend to private donors as well. Amed Khan, an American humanitarian philanthropist who has been privately donating medicine and emergency nutrition for children to Gaza, said the fewer NGOs that Israel approves, the harder it becomes to find one to receive and distribute supplies.

"It's death by bureaucracy," he said.
