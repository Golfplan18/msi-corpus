MILAN — Milan Fashion Week closed Monday with menswear previews for Fall-Winter 2026-27 that drew heavily on the approaching Winter Olympics and renewed debate over diversity on the runway. Designers from Dsquared2 and Ralph Lauren leaned into ski-resort aesthetics ahead of the Feb. 6-22 Games, while Dolce & Gabbana faced social media criticism for presenting an all-white cast at its Saturday menswear show.

The four days of previews featured the Winter Olympics as a recurring design touchstone while calls for diversity on Milan's runways drew commentary from designers and social media critics that organizers and brands said remain largely unaddressed.

## Olympic spirit

Dsquared2, founded by Canadian twins Dean and Dan Caten, made an overt bid for Olympic relevance with ski-inflected looks and hybrid footwear. The brand created a high heel that snaps into a ski boot at the ankle for women, with a similar design for men. Actor Hudson Williams, who stars in the series "Heated Rivalry" about a gay hockey love story, opened the show, walking down a fake-snow-covered staircase wearing a ripped double-denim jacket and a sparkly racing number.

Ralph Lauren presented ski-resort wear at a stately Milan palazzo before a celebrity crowd that included Nick Jonas, Tom Hiddleston, and Noah Schnapp. The collection featured colorful patterned knits and fleece jackets layered with puffers and flannel. The brand is preparing to outfit Team USA for the Games.

"As a designer you feel the vibrations in the world. Living in New York City and traveling around the world, you feel the vibrations, and if you are sensitive to that, you develop an ear or a feel for the clothes that you think you're going to do the next season," founder Ralph Lauren said in a social media campaign released before the show.

Emporio Armani mounted an in-store parade of Team Italia's uniforms during the week, according to the Associated Press.

## Prada's proportions

Prada co-creative directors Miuccia Prada and Raf Simons showed an ultra-slim silhouette in base-layer car coats alongside men's dress shirts that bore T-shirt necklines and buttoned down the back. The pair explored men's headwear — from berets to foldable fedoras engineered to snap flat onto the back of outerwear. Exaggeratedly long cuffs protruded from jacket sleeves and were fastened with gemstone cufflinks in lapis lazuli and tiger's eye.

"That's fashion," Miuccia Prada said of the slim silhouette. She added: "Talking about intellectual honesty, we are working for a brand that sells expensive clothes to possibly rich people, and so you have to deal with beauty, elegance, to understand what is believable."

## Sustainability and legacy

Zegna creative director Alessandro Sartori built a collection around wardrobe longevity, centering on a longer, more voluminous jacket with square shoulders that can be configured single-breasted, double-breasted, or with three horizontal buttons, using a button-reversing mechanism. The brand, which recently named co-CEOs from its fourth generation, said it controls about 60% of its supply chain — a distinction it highlighted as other Italian fashion houses face supply chain scrutiny.

"Our customers are collectors, and not just fashionistas," Sartori said. "I want people to collect pieces like watches."

Zegna displayed behind glass a nearly century-old jacket made from the company's own fabrics to underscore the brand's generational identity.

Simon Cracker, a 15-year-old upcycled fashion brand, also presented on the Milan runway. The AP identified it as among a small number of Milan houses able to make credible sustainability claims.

## Jewelry for men

Jewelry occupied increasing runway space across the men's collections. Dolce & Gabbana's evening looks featured lapel jewels, including large floral pins and gold brooches embedded with watches on long elegant chains. Giorgio Armani added a selection of subtle lapel pins to men's looks. Prada used mismatched sculptural earrings to finish its runway looks.

## Diversity debate

Ghanaian designer Victor Hart made his Milan runway debut supported by the decade-old Afrofashion Association, showing a denim collection incorporating street elements including industrial belting.

According to the AP, Milan fashion experienced a period of increased diversity following the Black Lives Matter movement in 2020, after designers including Stella Jean and Edward Buchanan and Afrofashion Association founder Michelle Ngonmo demanded action. The AP reported that the spotlight has dimmed in more recent seasons.

Dolce & Gabbana's Saturday show drew criticism on social media for presenting an all-white model lineup. The label previously faced criticism in 2018 over advertisements that were perceived as anti-Asian.

French fashion TikToker Elias Medini described the casting as "fifty shades of white." Influencer Hanan Besovic, who posts under @ideservecouture, said: "having a cast of all white models in 2026 is diabolical."
