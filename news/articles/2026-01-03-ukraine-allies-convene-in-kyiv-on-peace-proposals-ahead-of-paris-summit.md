The Paris consultations — scheduled to begin Monday with Ukrainian and European military officials and continue Tuesday with European heads of government — represent the most intensive multilateral diplomatic push yet toward a negotiated settlement, though allied governments have not yet identified specific funding sources for an $800 billion economic support package agreed to in principle.

KYIV, Ukraine — National security advisers from 18 allied nations gathered in Kyiv on Saturday to review security guarantee proposals and economic support plans for Ukraine, as President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he would travel to Paris for meetings aimed at finalizing a peace framework for the nearly four-year war with Russia.

Zelenskyy said Ukraine had shared all documents under discussion with the assembled advisers and expected subsequent negotiations to continue rapidly in allied capitals.

"We expect that this further work will now take place in the capitals of European countries as well as Canada, Japan and other representatives of the Coalition of the Willing," Zelenskyy said, referring to approximately 30 countries supporting Kyiv's effort to end the war on acceptable terms.

"We are not allocating a great deal of time for this process," he added.

## Paris meetings

The Paris consultations are scheduled to begin Monday with Ukrainian General Staff and military officials, followed Tuesday by a session of European heads of government that Zelenskyy said he hoped would finalize documents on security guarantees. Meetings with U.S. representatives in Paris are also planned.

Ukrainian negotiator Oleksandr Bevz described the proposed security architecture as a multilateral framework under which Ukrainian forces would serve as the first line of defense, European-led troops would be deployed inside Ukraine, and the United States would provide backstop support.

## Economic package

Ukraine's Deputy Prime Minister Taras Kachka said international partners have reached consensus on an economic support package of approximately $800 billion over the next decade. The package, based on calculations by the World Bank, the International Monetary Fund and the European Union, would cover damage compensation, reconstruction and economic stability, along with a $200 billion growth component tied to Ukraine's EU accession reforms.

Economy Minister Oleksii Sobolev said specific funding sources have not been identified, though roughly $500 billion is expected to come from public grants and concessional loans, with details to be worked out over the next two weeks.

## Government reshuffles

Also Saturday, Zelenskyy announced additional changes to Ukraine's government. He said he had proposed Denys Shmyhal — who served most recently as defense minister and formerly as Ukraine's longest-serving prime minister — to become energy minister and first deputy prime minister. Mykhailo Fedorov, the digital transformation minister credited with introducing drone technology into the army, was proposed to replace Shmyhal in the defense portfolio.

On Friday, Zelenskyy had appointed Gen. Kyrylo Budanov, head of Ukraine's military intelligence, as his new chief of staff, framing the move as part of an effort to sharpen focus on security, defense development and diplomacy.

## Russian attacks

Russian strikes continued through the weekend. A missile attack on the city of Kharkiv on Friday killed two people, including a 3-year-old boy, according to Kharkiv regional head Oleh Syniehubov. An overnight Russian drone attack on the Mykolaiv region struck critical infrastructure and left some communities without electricity, regional head Vitalii Kim said. Kim reported no casualties and said engineers worked through the night to restore power.
