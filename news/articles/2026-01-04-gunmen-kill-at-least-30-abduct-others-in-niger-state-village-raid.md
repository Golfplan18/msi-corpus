The attack underscores the persistent grip of armed gang violence across northern Nigeria, where dozens of criminal groups target isolated communities with impunity; it comes weeks after gunmen abducted more than 300 schoolchildren and teachers from a Catholic school in the nearby Papiri community.

Gunmen stormed Kasuwan-Daji village in Nigeria's Niger State on Saturday evening, killing at least 30 residents and abducting others — including children — in an assault that lasted up to three hours, the state police said Sunday. The raiders also burned the local market and set fire to several homes before retreating into the National Park Forest along the Kabe district, according to state police spokesman Wasiu Abiodun.

The confirmed death toll of at least 30 is contested by local accounts. At least two residents said the number of dead stood at 37 as of Sunday, and suggested it could rise further because some people remained missing. Rev. Fr. Stephen Kabirat, spokesman for the Catholic Church of Kontagora Diocese, said the gunmen killed more than 40 people and abducted several others, including children.

As of Sunday, locals said security forces had not yet arrived in the village — an account that contradicted Abiodun's statement that officers had been deployed to search for those kidnapped. Survivors said they remained too frightened to return to retrieve the bodies. "The bodies are there," one resident, who asked not to be named for fear of his safety, told the Associated Press. "If we don't see any security, how can we go there?"

## President condemns attack

Nigeria's President Bola Tinubu condemned the attack and said he had directed security officials to hunt down the gunmen and rescue those abducted. "These terrorists have tested the resolve of our country and its people," Tinubu said in a statement. "They must face the full consequences of their criminal actions. No matter who they are or what their intent is, they must be hunted down. In addition, those who aid, abet, or enable them will also be brought to justice."

## Attack followed a week of reported movement nearby

The unnamed resident said the gunmen had been moving around communities in the area for roughly a week before the raid on Kasuwan-Daji. Police said the attackers arrived from the National Park Forest along the Kabe district — pointing to what officers described as a recurring pattern in which large, largely unpoliced forest reserves serve as bases for armed gangs.

The attack is the latest in a long-running cycle of violence across northern Nigeria, Africa's most populous country. Armed gangs — often called "bandits" in local usage — have repeatedly targeted remote villages where police and government services are sparse.

## Area near November school kidnapping

Kasuwan-Daji village lies near the Papiri community, where gunmen abducted more than 300 schoolchildren and their teachers from a Catholic school in November. That incident drew widespread international attention to the security situation in Niger State.
