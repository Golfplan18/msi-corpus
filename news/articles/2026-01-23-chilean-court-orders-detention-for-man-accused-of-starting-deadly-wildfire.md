A Chilean court on Friday ordered the pretrial detention of a 39-year-old man accused of starting one of the country's most destructive wildfires in recent history. The man, arrested late Thursday, allegedly sparked the Trinitarias fire by using a defective wood-burning stove, according to prosecutors. The fire has killed at least 21 people in Chile's central and southern regions and is one of approximately 15 major blazes that have ravaged the country for nearly a week.

The detention order reflects the scale of Chile's unfolding disaster. The combined wildfires have burned more than 45,000 hectares (about 176 square miles), destroyed more than 2,300 homes, and displaced nearly 700 people into emergency shelters.

## Court Accepts Detention Request

The Guarantee Court of Concepción in the Bio Bio region, about 500 kilometers south of Santiago, accepted the prosecution's request to extend the man's detention pending formal charges. Prosecutors said they will present charges on January 26.

Prosecutor Jorge Lorca argued that video evidence from a forestry company linked the suspect to the Trinitarias fire. "We personally inspected the feasibility and distance of the actions with the Investigative Police, using an institutional helicopter," Lorca told reporters outside the courthouse.

## Fire's Deadly Spread

The fire allegedly started as sparks spread from the defective stove to surrounding forest. Over the following days, the Trinitarias fire merged with two other fires, creating a massive wall of flames that devastated the Bio Bio region and reduced several towns to ashes.

The fires have killed at least 20 people in the cities of Penco and Lirquen in Bio Bio, with one additional person killed as the flames spread to other regions. About 305 people have been injured.

## Regional Crisis

There are approximately 15 active megafires burning across the regions of Bio Bio, Ñuble, and La Araucania. Nearly 700 people remained in emergency shelters on Friday following evacuation orders.

By total area burned, the current fires already far exceed the 2024 Valparaiso and Viña del Mar fires, which burned more than 8,500 hectares and killed 131 people.
