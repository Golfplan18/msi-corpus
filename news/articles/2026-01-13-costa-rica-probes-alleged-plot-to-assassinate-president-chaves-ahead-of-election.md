Costa Rica's national security chief filed a formal complaint at the public prosecutor's office Tuesday after reporting an alleged plot to assassinate President Rodrigo Chaves, weeks before the country's Feb. 1 elections. Jorge Torres, director of the Intelligence and National Security Directorate, told journalists the information was confidential but said it concerned "the life of the president of the Republic." Security around Chaves has been reinforced, Torres said.

The alleged plot surfaced as Costa Rica prepares for presidential and legislative elections and as El Salvador's President Nayib Bukele — who has drawn international attention for his sweeping anti-gang security policies — was scheduled to visit the country.

Torres said authorities received a call from a woman who reported the supposed plot, adding that a payment had allegedly been made to a hitman. He said he wanted to preserve details for the formal complaint rather than disclose them publicly.

"What we received is confidential information that I would like to put on record in the complaint. I don't want to go into detail, but I would simply like to tell you that it concerns the life of the president of the Republic," Torres told local media.

Chaves, who has campaigned on a tough-on-crime platform, is scheduled Wednesday to lay the cornerstone of a new "mega-prison" Costa Rica plans to build, modeled on a high-security facility constructed under Bukele's government in El Salvador.

Chaves is constitutionally ineligible to seek reelection. The ruling party's Laura Fernández is among the candidates competing in the Feb. 1 presidential race.
