LONDON — The British government agreed Monday to pay a "substantial sum" to settle a lawsuit by Abu Zubaydah, a Palestinian born in Saudi Arabia who has been held at Guantanamo Bay without charge and alleged that U.K. intelligence agencies were complicit in his torture at CIA secret interrogation sites, his attorney said.

Attorney Helen Duffy said the confidential settlement was "symbolically and practically significant" for the "intolerable suffering" Zubaydah endured. She urged the British government to press for his immediate release and the release of others held without charge more than 25 years after the Sept. 11, 2001 attacks.

"This case is deeply relevant today, as some states ride roughshod over international law, and the world looks to others to respond," Duffy said. "There are critical lessons about the cost of cooperating with the U.S. or other allies flouting international norms. It is more important than ever that human rights and states international obligations are respected, and violations are met with reparation and accountability."

The U.K. Foreign Office declined comment, saying it would neither confirm nor deny intelligence matters.

## Capture and Documented Abuse

Zubaydah was captured in Pakistan in 2002 and was thought at the time to be a high-ranking al-Qaida member. He was the first person placed in the CIA's detention and interrogation program following the 9/11 attacks.

The European Court of Human Rights has established that Zubaydah was held at CIA black sites in Poland and Lithuania. A U.S. Senate report found he was waterboarded more than 80 times in a single month and confined for 11 days in a coffin-size box, among a range of other documented mistreatment. He was transferred to Guantanamo Bay in 2006.

## UK Parliamentary Findings

A British parliamentary committee determined in 2018 that U.K. security and intelligence services were aware Zubaydah was being tortured but continued providing questions for the CIA to pose to him without seeking assurances about his condition.

Dominic Grieve, a lawyer and former member of the House of Commons who chaired the parliamentary inquiry into detainee mistreatment, said the settlement payout was very unusual but that Zubaydah was clearly wronged.

"Americans were behaving in a way that should have given us cause for real concern," Grieve told the BBC. "We should have raised it with the United States and, if necessary, closed down cooperation, but we failed to do that for a considerable period of time."

## Continued Detention Without Charge

Zubaydah remains held at Guantanamo as a security risk, without charges or conviction. In 2022, the U.S. Supreme Court dismissed a separate lawsuit he had filed seeking testimony from two former CIA contractors as part of an investigation into his treatment while held in Poland. The court rejected the case on state-secrets grounds, even though much of the underlying information had been widely reported.

Duffy called on the U.K. to press for Zubaydah's release, describing the settlement as a foundation for further accountability.
