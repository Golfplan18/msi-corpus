The death of Pope Francis on April 21 elevated an American successor to lead the Catholic Church's 1.4 billion adherents for the first time in its history, while the fatal shooting of conservative activist Charlie Kirk during a Utah college appearance on September 10 prompted renewed national debate about political violence. They stood among the most consequential losses of a year that also claimed a former U.S. vice president, an Oscar-winning actor whose death became a mystery, and generations of artists who shaped popular culture.

From the Vatican to Hollywood and from boxing arenas to the halls of Congress, 2025 closed the final chapters on lives that had reshaped the institutions and fields they inhabited — in several cases leaving visible voids in those institutions behind them.

## Politics and public life

Dick Cheney, 84, the hard-charging conservative who served as one of the most powerful and polarizing vice presidents in U.S. history under President George W. Bush and was a leading advocate for the invasion of Iraq, died November 3. In the years before his death, he had become a critic and a target of President Donald Trump.

Pope Francis, whose humble style and concern for the poor had charmed much of the world while alienating conservatives with his critiques of capitalism and climate change, died April 21 at 88. He was history's first Latin American pontiff; his death set in motion the selection of the first American pope to lead the church.

Virginia Giuffre, 41, who had accused Britain's Prince Andrew and other influential men of sexually exploiting her as a teenager trafficked by financier Jeffrey Epstein, and who became an advocate for sex trafficking survivors after emerging as a central figure in Epstein's downfall, died by suicide on April 25, according to her publicist. Her death brought additional scrutiny to the ongoing investigations of Epstein.

## An actor's death — and a mystery

The deaths of Oscar-winning actor Gene Hackman, 95, and his wife Betsy Arakawa became a source of sadness and mystery after their bodies were found in their home in February. Authorities ultimately determined that Hackman, who was in the advanced stages of Alzheimer's disease, died of heart disease, likely unaware that Arakawa had died from hantavirus a week earlier.

Rob Reiner, 78 — whose films included "The Princess Bride," "When Harry Met Sally…" and "This Is Spinal Tap" — was fatally stabbed along with his wife Michele Singer Reiner in their home on December 14.

Filmmaker David Lynch, 78, died January 16, days before his 79th birthday. He was celebrated for his uniquely dark and dreamlike vision in films including "Blue Velvet" and "Mulholland Drive," and the TV series "Twin Peaks."

## Music

Ozzy Osbourne, 76, the gloomy, demon-invoking lead singer of the pioneering band Black Sabbath and the godfather of heavy metal, died July 22 — just weeks after his farewell concert.

Brian Wilson, 82, the Beach Boys' visionary and fragile leader whose genius for melody and wide-eyed self-expression inspired "Good Vibrations," "California Girls" and other summertime anthems, died June 11.

Roberta Flack, 88, the Grammy-winning singer and pianist known for her intimate vocal style and her enduring hit "Killing Me Softly With His Song," died February 24.

Sly Stone, 82, who led Sly and the Family Stone and transformed popular music in the late 1960s and 1970s with such hits as "Everyday People" and "Family Affair," died June 9.

## Sports

George Foreman, 76, the fearsome heavyweight boxer who memorably lost the "Rumble in the Jungle" to Muhammad Ali before winning a world championship again at age 45 and building a successful business career, died March 21.

## Science and advocacy

Jane Goodall, 91, the conservationist renowned for her groundbreaking chimpanzee field research and globe-spanning environmental advocacy, died October 1 of natural causes while in California on a speaking tour.

Viola Ford Fletcher, 111, one of the last survivors of the 1921 Tulsa Race Massacre in Oklahoma, who spent her later years seeking justice for the deadly attack by a white mob on the thriving Black community where she lived as a child, died November 24.

## A year of farewells

The losses stretched across every month, from singer Peter Yarrow, 86, of Peter, Paul and Mary, who died January 7, to Khaleda Zia, 80, the first woman elected prime minister of Bangladesh, who died December 30.

Among the others: Jean-Marie Le Pen, 96, the founder of France's far-right National Front, died January 7; Mario Vargas Llosa, 89, the Peruvian Nobel literature laureate and a giant of Latin American letters, died April 13; Giorgio Armani, 91, the Italian designer who turned understated elegance into a multibillion-dollar fashion empire, died September 4; and Robert Redford, 89, the Hollywood star who became an Oscar-winning director and a godfather for independent cinema, died September 16.
