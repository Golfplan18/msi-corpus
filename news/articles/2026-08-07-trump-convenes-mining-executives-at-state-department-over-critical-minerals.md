## Replenishing depleted weapons inventories could take years, officials warn

President Donald Trump will convene executives from some of the world's largest mining companies at the State Department on Friday to discuss securing critical-mineral supplies, according to The Guardian, citing Reuters. The meeting signals the administration's effort to address mineral supply concerns alongside the broader challenge of rebuilding U.S. weapons inventories after months of combat operations.

U.S. forces have burned through large numbers of precision-guided missiles and air-defense interceptors during the five-month-long Iran war, straining U.S. munitions stockpiles. Defense officials and lawmakers have warned that replenishing some weapons inventories could take years given existing production constraints, and the Trump administration has disputed reports of significant shortages, according to The Guardian.

The minerals meeting comes as the administration simultaneously confronts supply-chain dependencies that the conflict has exposed. The White House's focus on securing both domestic and allied mineral sources reflects an effort to reduce reliance on Chinese supply chains for materials critical to both civilian industry and defense production.
