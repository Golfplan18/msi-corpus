Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and UAE President Sheikh Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan met in New Delhi on Monday to finalize a series of agreements spanning trade, energy, defense and emerging technologies, with the two leaders setting a goal of doubling bilateral trade to $200 billion by 2032.

India's Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri told reporters that the meeting covered cooperation across trade, energy, defense and emerging technologies, reaffirming a comprehensive strategic partnership that has expanded over the past decade.

The summit, which produced a long-term liquefied natural gas supply pact and a preliminary strategic defense partnership framework, comes as both nations seek to deepen ties amid heightened regional tensions in West Asia, including recent developments involving Iran and the continuing conflict in Gaza.

## Trade and energy deals

India and the UAE have emerged as major economic partners since signing a comprehensive economic partnership agreement in February 2022, which reduced tariffs and expanded market access for goods and services. Bilateral trade has since surpassed $100 billion, and Monday's summit set a target of doubling that figure by 2032.

Among the agreements finalized Monday, India signed a pact to import 0.5 million metric tons of liquefied natural gas annually from the UAE for 10 years, beginning in 2028. The two sides also signed a letter of intent to conclude a framework agreement for a strategic defense partnership, Misri said.

Deals were concluded in the space and food sectors as well. The UAE pledged an unspecified investment in Modi's home state of Gujarat for a special investment region that would include infrastructure such as airports, ports and smart urban townships, Misri said.

## Regional context

The visit comes at a time of heightened diplomatic activity in West Asia. Regional tensions involving Iran and the unresolved conflict in Gaza have complicated the security landscape, and simmering disagreements between Saudi Arabia and the UAE over Yemen remain a factor.

"India-UAE relationship is warm, growing and multifaceted. In the context of turmoil in West Asia, and how Trump administration's plan for Gaza is shaping up, and the crisis with Iran, it's a moment for both nations that want stability in the region to exchange notes," said Harsh Pant, vice president of foreign policy at the Observer Research Foundation, a New Delhi-based think tank.

U.S. President Donald Trump last week invited Modi to join what Trump called a "Board of Peace" on Gaza. Indian authorities had not commented on whether the invitation had been accepted, according to the Associated Press. Modi has previously offered to help bring the war to an end.

India maintains deep economic and diaspora ties across West Asia. New Delhi has sought to maintain close engagement with key regional partners, including the UAE, as it navigates an increasingly complex geopolitical landscape, according to the AP.
