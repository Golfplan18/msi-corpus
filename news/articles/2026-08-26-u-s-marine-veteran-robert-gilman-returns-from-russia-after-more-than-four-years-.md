## Families cite medical problems among Americans still held in Russia

Robert Gilman, a U.S. Marine veteran, returned home this month after more than four years in Russian custody, the Associated Press reported. His release was the latest in a succession of prisoners freed by Russia going back years, and it has not cleared the broader population of Americans still held there.

Among those watching from the United States was Harold Jezler, whose wife, Olga, has been detained in Russia since 2022. Jezler told the AP that Gilman's gaunt and sickly appearance in pictures of his release underscored his concerns about the conditions facing those still held.

"You just look at the pictures and the guy is not in good shape," Jezler said. "It's concerning for me and for all the families, I would imagine."

"Is this what it takes for the government to really make a move and prioritize this — that the person is basically in dire straits?" Jezler said. "It was a very bittersweet thing to see that."

Family members and advocates told the AP that some Americans who remain in Russian custody are grappling with medical problems and receiving below-standard care. Olga faces her own medical problems, according to her husband.
