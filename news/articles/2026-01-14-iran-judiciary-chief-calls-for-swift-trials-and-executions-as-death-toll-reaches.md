Iran's judiciary chief called Wednesday for rapid trials and executions of protesters detained in nationwide demonstrations, even as President Donald Trump said he had been told the killings had stopped and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi urged diplomatic resolution. The competing signals emerged as Iran threatened retaliation against the United States or Israel for any intervention in the domestic unrest, and some U.S. military personnel at a key base in Qatar were advised to evacuate, the Associated Press reported.

The protest death toll reached at least 2,615, according to the U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency — a figure that exceeds any round of unrest in Iran in decades — with more than 18,400 people detained since demonstrations began Dec. 28 over the collapse of the Iranian rial.

## 'We have to do it quickly'

Gholamhossein Mohseni-Ejei, Iran's judiciary chief, said in a video shared by Iranian state television that the government should press ahead without delay.

"If we want to do a job, we should do it now. If we want to do something, we have to do it quickly," Mohseni-Ejei said. "If it becomes late, two months, three months later, it doesn't have the same effect. If we want to do something, we have to do that fast."

Activists warned that hangings of detainees could come soon. The Human Rights Activists News Agency said 2,615 of the dead were protesters and 153 were government-affiliated personnel. Thirteen children were killed, along with 14 civilians the agency said were not participating in protests. The AP said it has been unable to independently assess the toll given the communications disruptions inside Iran.

## Trump claims executions have stopped

Trump, speaking at the White House while signing executive orders, said he had been informed that planned executions had been halted.

"We've been told that the killing in Iran is stopping — it's stopped — it's stopping," Trump said. "And there's no plan for executions, or an execution, or executions — so I've been told that on good authority."

A day earlier, Trump had told protesters in Iran that "help is on the way" and that his administration would "act accordingly" in response to the Islamic Republic's killing of peaceful protesters. In an interview with CBS aired Tuesday, he said: "If they do such a thing, we will take very strong action."

Trump has repeatedly raised the prospect of U.S. military action in connection with the demonstrations, months after American forces bombed Iranian nuclear sites during a 12-day Iran-Israel war in June, the AP reported.

## Iran offers diplomacy while threatening retaliation

Araghchi sought to lower the temperature, urging the U.S. to pursue negotiated solutions. Asked by Fox News what message he would send to Trump, Araghchi said: "My message is: Between war and diplomacy, diplomacy is a better way, although we don't have any positive experience from the United States. But still diplomacy is much better than war."

Iranian officials nonetheless promised retaliation if the United States or Israel intervenes. One Arab Gulf diplomat told the AP that major Middle Eastern governments had been discouraging the Trump administration from launching a military confrontation with Iran, fearing "unprecedented consequences" for the region that could explode into a "full-blown war." The diplomat spoke on condition of anonymity because he was not authorized to speak to journalists.

## Airspace closed; internet restrictions continue

Iran shut its airspace to commercial flights early Thursday morning for several hours without explanation. A notice to pilots said the closure was estimated to end at 7:30 a.m. Flight-tracking data showed aircraft flying around Iran from north and south.

Iran's government cut off the country from the internet and international telephone calls on Jan. 8. Iran began allowing outgoing international calls via mobile phone on Tuesday, though incoming calls from outside the country remained blocked.

Activists said satellite internet service Starlink was offering free access in Iran. Mehdi Yahyanejad, a Los Angeles-based activist involved in distributing Starlink terminals, confirmed the service was functioning inside the country.

"We can confirm that the free subscription for Starlink terminals is fully functional," Yahyanejad said. "We tested it using a newly activated Starlink terminal inside Iran."

Security forces were reported to be searching apartment buildings in northern Tehran for satellite dishes, the AP said. While satellite television dishes are illegal, officials broadly gave up on enforcement in recent years.

## Protests rooted in economic collapse

The demonstrations began Dec. 28 over the collapse of the Iranian rial, as the country's economy is squeezed by international sanctions levied in part over its nuclear program.

A mass funeral was held Wednesday for approximately 100 security force members killed in the demonstrations. Tens of thousands of mourners attended, holding Iranian flags and photographs of Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. The caskets, draped in Iranian flags, were stacked at least three high.

## Witnesses describe fear

Plainclothes security forces still patrolled some neighborhoods, though anti-riot police and members of the paramilitary Revolutionary Guard's all-volunteer Basij force appeared to have been sent back to their barracks, the AP reported.

A mother of two, shopping for fruits and vegetables, told the AP she remained frightened. She spoke on condition of anonymity for fear of reprisals.

"We are very frightened because of these sounds (of gunfire) and protests," she said. "We have heard many are killed and many are injured. Now peace has been restored, but schools are closed, and I'm scared to send my children to school again."

Ahmadreza Tavakoli, 36, told the AP he witnessed a demonstration in Tehran and was shocked by the use of firearms by authorities.

"People were out to express themselves and protest, but quickly it turned into a war zone," Tavakoli said. "The people do not have guns. Only the security forces have guns."
