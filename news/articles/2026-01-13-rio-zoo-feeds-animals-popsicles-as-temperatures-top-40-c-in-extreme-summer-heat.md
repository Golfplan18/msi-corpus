Zookeepers at Rio de Janeiro's BioParque distributed frozen treats to jaguars, monkeys and other animals Tuesday as the city endured another day of extreme summer heat, with temperatures having surpassed 40 degrees Celsius over the weekend. City authorities issued a Level 3 heat alert and warned of health risks linked to prolonged exposure.

The frozen foods — including watermelon popsicles for monkeys and ground chicken popsicles for a jaguar — were prepared according to each species' nutritional needs, zoo officials said. Some animals received frozen fruit, while others were given mixtures containing frozen blood.

A jaguar attempted to fish its chicken popsicles from a tray floating in its water tank, a behavior that zoo biologist Letizia Feitoza said carried an added benefit.

"When she tries to fish out the frozen food, she ends up ingesting water as well," Feitoza said. "This is important for her hydration."

Zoo officials said the frozen foods are part of routine animal care and help provide thermal comfort during periods of extreme heat. Similar initiatives were carried out the previous summer as high temperatures affected much of Brazil's southeast.

The program has not discouraged visitors. Lorena Carvalho, a teacher at the zoo Tuesday, said the animals appeared to welcome the relief.

"I thought it was really cool," Carvalho said. "I think it brings them more comfort."
