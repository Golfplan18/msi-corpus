The disaster struck in a part of northern Mali where al-Qaida-linked militants have imposed security conditions — including prohibitions on after-dark river landings — that witnesses said directly contributed to the sinking.

A ferry boat struck rocks and sank on the Niger River at the town of Diré in Mali's Timbuktu region last Thursday, killing at least 38 people, local officials and relatives of the dead said Tuesday. Local authorities had not released an official death toll. Twenty-three people survived, according to Alkaidi Touré, a regional resident and former National Assembly deputy.

The vessel was carrying families and farmers who had just harvested rice, witnesses said. It arrived at Diré after nightfall, when docking on the river is prohibited by a security ban imposed to guard against attacks by al-Qaida-linked militants operating in the region. The boat driver chose not to wait until morning and tried to come ashore at an alternate location, where the boat struck rocks and sank, witnesses said.

"I collected the bodies that were scattered across the river, and some were already decomposing. As I speak to you now, I can still smell the bodies," Moussa Ag Almoubarek Traoré, a Diré resident, told The Associated Press in a telephone interview. Traoré said he lost 21 family members in the accident and helped retrieve and tally the dead with local officials.

Touré said the death toll had a broad impact on the surrounding population. "The entire region of Timbuktu is in mourning, because this accident has had a profound impact on the population, as many women and children drowned," he said.

The militants whose presence prompted the docking ban belong to Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin, or JNIM, an al-Qaida-backed group active in the Timbuktu region. Mali, a country of 25 million people, has battled jihadi militants for several decades, alongside neighboring Burkina Faso and Niger.

Niger River ferry accidents are not uncommon in Mali, though no reliable statistics are available on such incidents, according to the AP. Officials said the human toll from the Diré sinking was unusually high.
