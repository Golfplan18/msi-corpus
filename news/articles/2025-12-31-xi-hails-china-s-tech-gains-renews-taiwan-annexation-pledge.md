Chinese President Xi Jinping praised his country's advances in artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and space exploration in a New Year's Eve address broadcast by state media Wednesday, while reiterating Beijing's intention to annex self-ruled Taiwan.

"We sought to energize high-quality development through innovation," Xi said, thanking Chinese citizens for contributing to the country's economic growth over the past five years.

The address encapsulated Beijing's dual preoccupations heading into 2026: accelerating technological self-reliance as Washington tightens controls on high-tech exports, and pressing its claim over Taiwan, whose government said it would resist what it called China's "expansionist ambitions."

## Technology and the five-year plan

Xi's remarks touched on advances across multiple sectors, including military technology, as images of humanoid robots performing kung fu and new hydropower projects appeared on screen during the broadcast.

China plans its economic development in five-year increments and is preparing to discuss its next five-year plan at a legislative session in March, according to the Associated Press. Beijing has signaled it intends to accelerate domestic self-reliance in science and technology as the United States has imposed increasingly tight controls on Chinese access to semiconductors and other high-tech items.

Xi also cited China's rising presence on the world stage, listing high-level political events the country hosted over the past year.

## Taiwan

On Taiwan — a self-ruled democracy that China considers sovereign territory — Xi repeated a commitment to annexation he has made in previous addresses.

"We Chinese on both sides of the Taiwan Strait share a bond of blood and kinship," Xi said. "The reunification of our motherland, a trend of the times, is unstoppable."

The remarks followed two days of Chinese military exercises around Taiwan earlier in the week involving rockets, aircraft, and warships. Beijing said the drills were conducted in response to a planned U.S. arms sale to Taiwan.

Taiwan President Lai Ching-te, in his own New Year's address Thursday, pledged to resist China's "expansionist ambitions" and bolster the island's self-defense.
