## Challengers say enforcement power belongs to president, agencies

The disputes strike at a cornerstone of legal enforcement in the United States: the right of private groups, people and local governments to sue over violations of many major laws.

Legal activists and Elon Musk's artificial intelligence company, xAI, brought the sweeping challenges, according to a report from The Associated Press. Their argument, supported by the Trump administration, is that the Constitution reserves tremendous power for the president and federal agencies to decide how — or whether at all — to enforce federal law. The challengers contend Congress should never have handed that power to others through so-called citizen suits that are part of environmental, campaign finance and certain other laws.

Environmentalists say it would be devastating to lose this essential, decades-old tool, which is used to impose fines and halt lawbreaking by bad actors. Citizen suits, for example, have extracted millions from heavily polluting oil and gas operations, the AP reported.

The mechanism has also underpinned public-health enforcement. Citizen suits ensured that lead pipes in Flint, Michigan, would be removed after the city's water crisis, according to the report.
