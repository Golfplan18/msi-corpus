ADEN, Yemen — Saudi-backed National Shield Forces spread across Yemen's port city of Mukalla on Sunday after retaking it from Emirati-backed southern separatists, Yemen's internationally recognized government said. The capital of Hadramout province fell back under government-aligned control following days of Saudi airstrikes that drove the Southern Transitional Council from the oil-rich governorate.

The episode has exposed a deepening rupture between Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates — coalition partners whose competing interests in southern Yemen have put their proxies at direct odds — complicating a decade-long war against Iran-backed Houthi rebels controlling much of the country's north.

## Forces welcomed in streets

Video footage obtained by the Associated Press showed National Shield Forces being welcomed by residents as troops drove through Mukalla's streets in armed vehicles. Forces were also seen at al-Rayyan Airport outside the city.

Ahmed Samaan and Bakr al-Ketheri, residents of the Hadramout towns of al-Qatn and Seiyun, told the AP that the Southern Transitional Council, known as the STC, had withdrawn from military camps in the region.

Rashad al-Alimi, head of Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council — the ruling organ of the internationally recognized government — said Saturday on Facebook that the National Shield Forces achieved "record success" in taking back all military and security sites in Hadramout.

## How the conflict escalated

Tensions rose last month when the STC moved into the governorates of Hadramout and Mahra, seizing an oil-rich region and pushing out National Shield Forces aligned with the Saudi-led coalition.

The STC is backed by the United Arab Emirates, a member of that same coalition — an arrangement that has long put Riyadh and Abu Dhabi at cross-purposes over who controls southern Yemen's territory and resources.

Saudi Arabia responded by bombing STC positions and striking what it described as a shipment of Emirati weapons. Under Saudi pressure and an ultimatum from anti-Houthi forces, the UAE said Saturday it had withdrawn its forces from Yemen.

The STC said Saturday that Saudi warplanes carried out new airstrikes on a military camp in Mukalla and other areas where its forces were stationed.

## Command reshuffled, handover incomplete

Salem al-Khanbashi, the governor of Hadramout, was chosen on Friday by the internationally recognized government to command Saudi-led forces in the governorate.

Al-Alimi called Mohamed Ali Yasser, the governor of Mahra, on Saturday for a progress report on the handover of camps and facilities to the National Shield Forces and local authorities. It was not immediately clear whether that handover had been completed.

## Saudi Arabia proposes dialogue

Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry said Saturday it will host a conference in Riyadh to bring together all southern factions in Yemen "to discuss just solutions to the southern cause." No date or format has been announced. The ministry said the initiative came in response to a request for dialogue from al-Alimi.

The STC welcomed the invitation, saying dialogue is the "only rational means to address political issues, foremost among them the issue of the people of the south and their right to restore their state."

## Broader context

Yemen has been engulfed in civil war for more than a decade. The Iran-backed Houthis control much of the country's north, while the Saudi-led coalition has supported the internationally recognized government in the south.

Saudi Arabia and the UAE have competed over economic interests and regional influence across the Arabian Peninsula, particularly around the Red Sea, even while nominally sharing the stated coalition goal of restoring Yemen's internationally recognized government to full authority.
