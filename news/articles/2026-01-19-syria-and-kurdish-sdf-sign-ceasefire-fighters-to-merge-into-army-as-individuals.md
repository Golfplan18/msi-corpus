The deal marks a decisive turning point in post-Assad Syria. The SDF, which once controlled roughly a quarter of the country with an estimated 40,000 fighters and played the central role in the military defeat of the Islamic State group, now holds only the northeastern province of Hassakeh as interim President Ahmad al-Sharaa consolidates authority across the nation.

BEIRUT — Syria's interim government and the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces signed a ceasefire Sunday, ending weeks of fighting that gave government troops control of large territories in northeast Syria. Under the 14-point accord, SDF fighters will merge into the national army and police as individuals rather than as a unified force — a blow to SDF plans for a cohesive integration — as interim President Ahmad al-Sharaa extends Damascus's authority over the last major armed group operating outside government control.

The SDF, which once controlled roughly a quarter of Syria with an estimated 40,000 fighters, now holds only the northeastern province of Hassakeh after government forces captured much of Raqqa province along the Turkish border and the oil-rich eastern province of Deir el-Zour in recent weeks.

Al-Sharaa signed the agreement and displayed it before journalists. SDF commander Mazloum Abdi, who had been expected in Damascus, did not appear at the ceremony. Al-Sharaa told reporters that Abdi could not travel due to bad weather and was expected in the Syrian capital Monday. Abdi's signature appeared on the document, the state-run news agency SANA reported.

Under the agreement, the SDF will also hand over the names of commanders who will receive high military and managerial posts within the Syrian army and government.

## Background

The SDF was founded in 2015 with U.S. backing as a religiously and ethnically mixed fighting force that included Arab, Kurdish, Turkmen and Christian fighters, including the Syriac Military Council representing Assyrian communities in the northeast. Its core was the People's Protection Units, known by the Kurdish acronym YPG — a central tension in the alliance, given Turkey's view of the YPG as an extension of the Kurdistan Workers' Party, or PKK, which has waged an insurgency inside Turkey for decades.

The SDF was the primary ground force in the campaign against the Islamic State group in Syria, capturing the extremists' last sliver of held territory in March 2019. It subsequently administered a self-declared autonomous region in the northeast that Kurds called Rojava, or Western Kurdistan.

After the fall of former President Bashar Assad in December 2024, Abdi traveled to Damascus in March and signed a preliminary deal with al-Sharaa calling for SDF integration into the Syrian army by the end of 2025. In October, Abdi told the Associated Press the SDF had agreed in principle to merge its fighters as a cohesive group into the national army. Talks in early January produced no tangible results, according to state media.

Fighting escalated after clashes in Aleppo in January. Government forces launched a broader offensive that seized Raqqa and Deir el-Zour before al-Sharaa announced the ceasefire Sunday night.

## Kurdish language and IS prisons

Before the ceasefire announcement, al-Sharaa issued a decree declaring Kurdish an official language in Syria alongside Arabic and adopting the Kurdish new year as a national holiday.

The agreement also addresses the SDF's control over facilities that held IS fighters and their families after the group's military defeat. The SDF controls prisons holding approximately 9,000 IS members who have been detained for years without trial, as well as the al-Hol and Roj camps in Hassakeh, home to tens of thousands of people, mostly women and children linked to IS.

Under the ceasefire, those prisons and camps will come under full Syrian government control. No deadline was set for the transfer.

On Monday, the SDF said armed groups attacked Shaddadeh prison. The group later said its fighters had repelled several attacks but that the prison was out of its control.

The Syrian government affirmed in the agreement its commitment to fighting IS, with Syria now a member of the U.S.-led coalition against the group.
