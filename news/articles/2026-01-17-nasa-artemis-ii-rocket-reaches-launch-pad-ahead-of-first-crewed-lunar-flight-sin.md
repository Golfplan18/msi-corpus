Artemis II will be the first time humans have flown toward the Moon since the Apollo 17 mission ended the lunar landing program in 1972, though the crew will fly around the Moon rather than land — a 10-day mission NASA describes as the critical step before a planned lunar landing on Artemis III.

CAPE CANAVERAL, Fla. — NASA's Space Launch System rocket rolled out to the launch pad at Kennedy Space Center on Saturday, carrying four astronauts one step closer to the Moon on the first crewed lunar mission in more than five decades.

The 322-foot rocket and its Orion crew capsule began the 4-mile crawl from the Vehicle Assembly Building at dawn, moving at roughly 1 mile per hour aboard a massive crawler-transporter upgraded from the Apollo and shuttle eras to handle the stack's 11-million-pound combined weight. Thousands of space center workers and their families gathered in the pre-dawn cold to watch the rollout, which had been delayed for years.

"What a great day to be here," said Reid Wiseman, the Artemis II mission commander. "It's breathtaking."

NASA plans to conduct a fueling test of the rocket at the pad in early February before setting a launch date. Administrator Jared Isaacman told reporters the agency has "no intention of announcing an actual launch date" until that test is complete. The agency has only five available launch days in the first half of February before the window shifts to March.

## The crew

Artemis II will carry Wiseman, pilot Victor Glover, and mission specialist Christina Koch — all veteran NASA astronauts with spaceflight experience — along with Canadian Space Agency astronaut Jeremy Hansen, a former combat pilot who has not yet flown in space.

The mission is designed as a 10-day lunar flyby. The crew will fly around the Moon but will not enter lunar orbit or land. A crewed lunar landing is planned for Artemis III, the next flight in the series.

"This one feels very different, putting crew on the rocket and bringing the crew around the Moon," said John Honeycutt of NASA on the eve of the rollout.

## First crewed lunar flight since Apollo 17

Artemis II will be the first time humans have flown toward the Moon since Gene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt returned from the lunar surface aboard Apollo 17 in December 1972, closing out the Apollo landing program. Twelve astronauts walked on the Moon between Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin's first landing in July 1969 and the final Apollo mission. Only four of those moonwalkers remain alive. Aldrin, the oldest, turns 96 on Tuesday.

Wiseman said the surviving Apollo astronauts are eager to see a return. "They're very excited that we're going back to the Moon," he said. "They just want to see humans as far out from Earth as possible, discovering the unknown."

## Context: the first SLS flight and delays

The Space Launch System flew once before Artemis II — in November 2022, when Artemis I sent an uncrewed Orion capsule on a loop around the Moon. Heat shield damage and other capsule problems identified during that test flight required extensive analysis and testing, pushing the crewed mission to 2026.

The Vehicle Assembly Building, where the SLS was assembled before Saturday's rollout, was constructed in the 1960s to house the Saturn V rockets that carried 24 astronauts toward the Moon during Apollo.
