The detention order marks a formal legal step in Switzerland's criminal investigation into the bar's owners, who are suspected of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm, and involuntarily causing a fire in one of the deadliest nightlife disasters in recent European history.

A Swiss court on Monday ordered three months of pretrial detention for Jacques Moretti, a manager of the Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana, over his alleged role in a New Year's Eve fire that killed 40 people — mostly young revelers — and injured 116 others, many seriously. The court of compulsory measures in the southwestern Valais canton cited a possible "flight risk" flagged by prosecutors as grounds for the order.

The detention may be short-lived. The court said in a statement that it was inclined to lift the three months of pretrial detention if sufficient "security measures" — potentially including bail — are arranged, and that Moretti should remain in custody only until those conditions are worked out.

Lawyers for Jessica Moretti, who is listed alongside her husband in a Swiss business register as co-owner of the bar, said in a statement that the court decision would allow him "to regain freedom" once the conditions are in place. The office of Patrick Michod, Jacques Moretti's attorney, did not respond to requests for comment; his secretary said the attorney was not immediately available.

## Criminal investigation

Swiss authorities have opened a criminal investigation into the bar's owners. Jacques and Jessica Moretti are suspected of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm, and involuntarily causing a fire, according to the Associated Press.

Investigators believe sparkling candles ignited the blaze when they came too close to the ceiling, which was outfitted with soundproofing material. Authorities were examining whether the material conformed with regulations and whether the candles were permitted for use inside the bar.

Fire safety inspections at Le Constellation had not been carried out since 2019, according to the Associated Press.

## Official response

Swiss President Guy Parmelin said Friday that Switzerland was "appalled" by the tragedy, which struck during one of the busiest nights of the year at an alpine resort bar.
