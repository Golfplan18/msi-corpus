A fire swept through a packed bar in the Swiss Alpine resort of Crans-Montana during a New Year's celebration early Thursday, killing about 40 people and injuring 115 others, Valais Canton police said. Most of the injured were seriously hurt. Switzerland declared five days of national mourning.

The blaze at Le Constellation bar erupted less than two hours after midnight, turning a scene of revelry into what authorities called one of the country's worst tragedies.

"This evening should have been a moment of celebration and coming together, but it turned into a nightmare," said Mathias Reynard, head of the regional government of Valais Canton.

## Investigation underway; cause unknown

Valais Canton attorney general Beatrice Pilloud said it was too early to determine what started the fire and that investigators had not yet been able to enter the wreckage.

"At no moment is there a question of any kind of attack," Pilloud said. "An investigation has been opened, not against anyone, but to illuminate the circumstances of this dramatic fire."

She said the number of people inside the bar at the time of the blaze was "currently totally unknown," and added that the bar's maximum capacity would be part of the investigation. Asked whether anyone had been arrested, Pilloud said: "For the time being, we don't have any suspect."

Two women who told French broadcaster BFMTV they were inside the bar described seeing a male bartender lifting a female bartender on his shoulders as she held a lit candle in a bottle. The flames spread and collapsed the wooden ceiling, the women said.

A separate witness described a crowd surge as people tried to escape a basement nightclub by climbing a narrow staircase and pushing through a narrow door.

## Victims identified slowly; Italian nationals among the missing

Valais Canton police commander Frédéric Gisler said authorities did not yet have an exact count of the dead and that identifying victims would take "several days." He said the community was "devastated."

Italy's ambassador to Switzerland, Gian Lorenzo Cornado, told state-run RAI television that 13 of the wounded were Italian citizens and that six other Italian nationals were unaccounted for.

Giovanni Tamburi, an Italian, was among those missing. His mother, Carla Masielli, issued a public appeal for information, telling RAI: "We have called all the hospitals but they don't give me any news. We don't know if he's among the dead. We don't know if he's among the missing."

Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani planned to travel to the site on Friday given the significant number of Italian nationals involved.

Three of the wounded were transported from Sion hospital in Switzerland to Milan's Niguarda hospital, the Italian civil protection agency said.

## Survivor describes chaos

Axel Clavier, a 16-year-old from Paris, told the Associated Press he experienced "total chaos" inside the bar. One of his friends died and two or three others were missing, he said.

Clavier said he initially hid behind a table, then ran upstairs and used a table to break a Plexiglas window, which fell from its casing and allowed him to escape. He lost his jacket, shoes, phone and bank card while fleeing.

"I am still alive and it's just stuff," Clavier said. "I'm still in shock."

Another witness, speaking to BFMTV, described people smashing windows to escape the blaze, some gravely injured, and panicked parents rushing to the scene in cars.

## Hospitals overwhelmed

The regional hospital's intensive care unit and operating theater quickly reached full capacity, Reynard said. Authorities called on the local population to exercise caution in the days ahead to avoid accidents that could further strain medical resources already stretched past their limits.

## President addresses tragedy on first day in office

Swiss President Guy Parmelin, speaking on his first day in office, said many emergency workers had been "confronted by scenes of indescribable violence and distress."

"This Thursday must be the time of prayer, unity and dignity," Parmelin said. "Switzerland is a strong country not because it is sheltered from drama, but because it knows how to face them with courage and a spirit of mutual help."

## A celebrated resort struck by disaster

Crans-Montana is a prominent international ski and golf venue in the Valais region, with ski runs rising to roughly 3,000 meters (nearly 9,850 feet) in the snowy peaks and pine forests of the Swiss Alps. The resort is scheduled to host men's and women's World Cup downhill races ahead of the Milan-Cortina Winter Olympics in February.

The fire came 25 years after a New Year's Eve blaze in the Dutch fishing town of Volendam that killed 14 people and injured more than 200 at a café.
