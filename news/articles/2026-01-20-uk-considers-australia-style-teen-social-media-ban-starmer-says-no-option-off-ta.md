The announcement follows growing pressure within Starmer's own Labour Party: more than 60 Labour lawmakers wrote to the prime minister this week calling for a minimum social media age of 16, modeled on Australia's recently enacted law that requires platforms including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and X to bar users under 16.

The British government said Tuesday it is considering banning young teenagers from social media platforms, with Prime Minister Keir Starmer writing that "no option is off the table, including looking at what age children should be able to access social media and whether we need restrictions on things such as addictive features like infinite scrolling or streaks in apps."

The announcement, which Starmer posted on Substack, followed a letter from more than 60 lawmakers in his center-left Labour Party calling on the government to set a minimum social media age of 16. "Successive governments have done far too little to protect young people from the consequences of unregulated, addictive social media platforms," the lawmakers wrote. "We urge the government to show leadership on this issue by introducing a minimum age for social media access of 16 years old."

## Australia model under review

Government ministers said they plan to travel to Australia to study the country's recently enacted law, which requires major social media platforms — including Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, and X — to bar users under 16 from their services. The Australian approach has drawn international attention as one of the most restrictive age-based access regimes adopted by any major democracy.

The UK government said it would consult with parents, young people, and other interested parties about safe technology use before advancing any legislation. A response to the broader public consultation on online safety is expected by summer 2026, the government said.

## Concern over harmful content and addictive design

The government framed the potential restrictions around two distinct concerns: children's exposure to harmful or unregulated content, and the design features of social media platforms that critics say are engineered to maximize engagement, particularly among young users. Starmer's statement specifically named infinite scrolling and in-app streaks — mechanics that reward continuous platform use — as areas under consideration.
