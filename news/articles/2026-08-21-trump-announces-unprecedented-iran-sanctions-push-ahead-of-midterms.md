## Stockpiles of key weapons dwindle as Iran war nears six months

President Donald Trump announced this week that the United States would impose what he described as an "unprecedented" level of economic warfare and isolation on Iran, the Associated Press reported. With sparse details, the president said the measures aim to force Tehran's leadership to end its nuclear program and fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz to oil and natural gas tankers — a move he framed as an "economic D-Day."

The announcement comes as the Iran war approaches its sixth month and the administration confronts diminishing stockpiles of key weapons, the AP reported. Iran has so far withstood nearly five decades of American sanctions and ranks among the most economically penalized countries in the world, according to the AP.

The AP described the announcement as reflecting what the news organization called "the dire reality Trump faces" — an increasingly unpopular war he has been unable to end, with pivotal midterm elections months away that will decide whether his Republican Party keeps control of Congress.

The AP characterized the administration's approach as a "crushing financial campaign" against Tehran. The news organization wrote that Trump is "refocusing America's might on bringing Iran to its knees through an accelerated sanctions campaign," framing the shift as occurring "whether out of desperation or strategy."


Trump's announcement this week of "unprecedented" economic measures advances the "accelerated sanctions campaign" the AP reported the administration is pursuing as the war enters its sixth month.
