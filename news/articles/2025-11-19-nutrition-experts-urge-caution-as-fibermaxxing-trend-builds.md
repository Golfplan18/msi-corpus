Dietary fiber is emerging as the food industry's next marketing focus, with companies adding fiber to packaged products and social media users promoting high-fiber eating under the term "fibermaxxing" — but nutritionists and researchers say people should increase their fiber intake gradually to avoid digestive side effects, the Associated Press reported.

Most Americans consume well below recommended fiber levels, and experts say the strongest evidence for fiber's health benefits comes from whole foods rather than packaged products with added fiber.

## The fiber moment

The number of new products promoted with high or added fiber saw a big uptick in the United States in 2025, according to market research firm Mintel. Hundreds of social media videos celebrate fiber's benefits and share recipes for getting more of it.

Food companies are taking notice. "I think fiber will be the next protein," PepsiCo CEO Ramon Laguarta said during a recent conference call with investors. "Consumers are starting to understand that fiber is the benefit that they need."

## What fiber does

Fiber is a carbohydrate found in plants that the body cannot break down. It helps feed gut bacteria and moves food through the digestive system.

There are two main types. Soluble fiber dissolves in water and forms a gel-like material that feeds gut bacteria; it is found in foods like oats, peas, beans, apples and carrots. Insoluble fiber moves food through the digestive system and is found in whole wheat flour, popcorn, wheat bran, nuts, green beans and potatoes.

Studies have shown that fiber lowers cholesterol levels, regulates blood sugar and promotes weight loss, since high-fiber foods tend to make eaters feel more full. It may also protect against heart disease, diabetes, diverticulitis and colon cancer, according to the American Heart Association.

## How much is enough

Under U.S. Department of Agriculture guidelines, adults should aim for 14 grams of fiber for every 1,000 calories they consume — about 25 grams per day for women and 38 grams per day for men.

Debbie Petitpain, a registered dietitian nutritionist and spokeswoman for the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, said Americans generally get only about two-thirds of that amount because their diets are low in vegetables, fruits and whole grains.

Petitpain said rising use of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs may be one reason for the renewed focus on fiber, since GLP-1s naturally slow digestion and fiber can prevent constipation. She said fiber has seen similar spikes in interest when people wanted to alleviate symptoms from high-fat diets such as Atkins or keto.

## Cautions on fibermaxxing

Sander Kersten, director of the Division of Nutritional Sciences at Cornell University, said long-term studies on fiber's benefits have examined whole food consumption rather than packaged products with added fiber.

"The way it is consumed as an additive and part of a diet that doesn't contain a lot of fiber may be different than a naturally fiber-rich diet," Kersten said. "You can eat a very processed, Western diet and consume foods that are enriched, but we don't know if it confers the same benefit."

There is no defined upper limit for fiber intake, Kersten said, but increasing fiber can cause painful gas and bloating, especially if done quickly. Petitpain said people should increase their fiber intake gradually and drink plenty of water.

Petitpain added that certain populations should exercise extra caution, including people who are sensitive to gluten or allergic to soy, shellfish or psyllium husk, since some foods with added fiber contain those ingredients.

Kersten questioned the broader trend of focusing on a single nutrient. "We don't eat nutrients, we eat foods," he said. "Ultimately, what you want to be striving for is a healthy diet, and you should choose foods that are considered to be an important part of a healthy diet."
