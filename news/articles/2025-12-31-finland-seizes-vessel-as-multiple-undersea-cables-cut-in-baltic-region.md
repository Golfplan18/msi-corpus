Three undersea cables belonging to Finnish and Swedish telecommunications providers were cut over two days in the Gulf of Finland and Baltic Sea, raising fresh concern about the vulnerability of underwater infrastructure in one of Europe's busiest shipping corridors.

Finnish authorities seized a cargo vessel Wednesday and opened a criminal investigation after an undersea telecommunications cable in the Gulf of Finland was damaged, the second such incident in less than a year affecting critical infrastructure linking Finland and Estonia.

The cable belongs to Finnish telecommunications provider Elisa and is considered critical underwater infrastructure. The damage occurred in Estonia's exclusive economic zone, Helsinki police said. Finnish border guard officers seized the vessel suspected of causing the damage — named the Fitburg — after discovering it in Finland's exclusive economic zone with its anchor lowered.

Helsinki police opened an investigation into aggravated criminal damage, attempted aggravated criminal damage and aggravated interference with telecommunications.

## Vessel seized, crew detained

The Fitburg was flagged in St. Vincent and the Grenadines and had been traveling from Russia to Israel, according to authorities. Local media reported that Finnish authorities detained the ship's crew of 14, who held citizenship from Russia, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Kazakhstan.

Finnish National Police Commissioner Ilkka Koskimäki told local media that investigators are not speculating on whether a state-level actor was behind the damage. He also said the ship had been dragging its anchor for hours.

Finnish President Alexander Stubb addressed the incident on social platform X. "Finland is prepared for security challenges of various kinds, and we respond to them as necessary," Stubb wrote.

Elisa said its service was not affected by the damage.

## Two additional cables cut

Estonian officials confirmed that a second undersea cable — owned by Swedish telecommunications provider Arelion — was also damaged early Wednesday in the Gulf of Finland. It was not immediately clear whether that incident was linked to the Elisa damage.

Martin Sjögren, an Arelion spokesperson, said a third cable, running between Sweden and Estonia in the Baltic Sea, had been damaged on Tuesday.

"We are actively working with authorities in Sweden and other countries to investigate the cause of the cuts," Sjögren said in an email. "We cannot disclose any details about exact times or locations at this point with regard to the ongoing investigation."

Sjögren said the vast majority of Arelion's customers were unaffected. Repair work is expected to begin once poor weather conditions clear.

## Prior incident and broader context

The undersea cables and pipelines that cross the Gulf of Finland and Baltic Sea link Nordic, Baltic and central European countries, supporting trade, energy security and, in some cases, reduced dependence on Russian energy resources.

The incident follows a similar case from Christmas Day 2024, when the Eagle S tanker allegedly damaged undersea cables between Finland and Estonia. Finnish prosecutors charged the vessel's captain and two senior officers in August with aggravated criminal mischief and aggravated interference with communications; the defendants denied the allegations. The Eagle S was flagged in the Cook Islands but had been described by Finnish customs officials and the European Union's executive commission as part of Russia's shadow fleet — aging vessels with obscure ownership, acquired to evade Western sanctions amid the war in Ukraine and operating without Western-regulated insurance. The Kremlin previously denied involvement in that incident.

For Western governments, such incidents are believed to be part of a broader pattern of sabotage attacks in Europe linked to Russia following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, according to the Associated Press.

Estonian authorities said they are cooperating with Finnish counterparts to determine whether to pursue a separate criminal case or a joint prosecution in the Elisa matter.
