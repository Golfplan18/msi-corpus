More than two years of Israeli bombardment have left the majority of Gaza's more than 2 million residents living in tents and temporary shelters with little protection against winter storms. Aid groups say shelter materials are not reaching the territory in adequate quantities even under the ceasefire framework, now in its third winter since the war began.

Strong winter winds killed at least five people in Gaza on Tuesday, collapsing walls onto makeshift tents and sweeping shelters into the Mediterranean Sea, as aid organizations warned that materials needed to protect displaced Palestinians from winter storms remain in short supply despite a ceasefire in effect since October.

The dead included three members of the same family and a 1-year-old boy who died of hypothermia, according to Shifa Hospital and Gaza's Health Ministry. Aid groups said shelter materials are not reaching the territory in adequate quantities even under the ceasefire framework, now in its third winter since the conflict began.

## Wall collapse kills family of three

Three members of the same family — 72-year-old Mohamed Hamouda, his 15-year-old granddaughter and his daughter-in-law — were killed when an 8-meter (26-foot) wall collapsed onto their tent in a coastal area of Gaza City, Shifa Hospital said. At least five others were injured. A second woman was killed when a wall fell on her tent in the western part of the city, the hospital also said.

Relatives on Tuesday began removing rubble that had buried their loved ones and rebuilding tent shelters for survivors.

"The world has allowed us to witness death in all its forms," Bassel Hamouda said at the funeral. "It's true the bombing may have temporarily stopped, but we have witnessed every conceivable cause of death in the world in the Gaza Strip."

## Cold deaths mount

Gaza's Health Ministry said a 1-year-old boy died of hypothermia overnight in the central town of Deir al-Balah — the seventh fatality attributed to cold since winter began. Earlier in the week, the ministry announced the deaths of a seven-day-old baby and a 4-year-old girl from cold-related causes.

The ministry, part of the Hamas-run government, said more than 440 people have been killed by Israeli fire and their bodies brought to hospitals since the ceasefire took effect. U.N. agencies and independent experts consider the ministry's casualty records generally reliable.

## Tents swept into the sea

Hundreds of tents and makeshift shelters were blown away or heavily damaged, the U.N. humanitarian office reported. On the shore in southern Gaza, tents were swept into the Mediterranean, with families pulling what remained from the water.

"The sea took our mattresses, our tents, our food and everything we owned," Shaban Abu Ishaq said as he dragged part of his tent out of the sea in the Muwasi area of Khan Younis.

In the central town of Zawaida, footage showed inundated tents, with people trying to rebuild their shelters. Yasmin Shalha, displaced from the northern town of Beit Lahiya, stitched her tent back together with needle and thread after it fell on her family as they slept.

"The winds were very, very strong. The tent collapsed over us," Shalha, a mother of five, told the Associated Press. "As you can see, our situation is dire."

Mohamed al-Sawalha, a 72-year-old from the northern refugee camp of Jabaliya, said conditions for most people in Gaza are barely livable.

"It doesn't work neither in summer nor in winter," he said of the tent. "We left behind houses and buildings (with) doors that could be opened and closed. Now we live in a tent. Even sheep don't live like we do."

## Children killed since ceasefire

UNICEF spokesman James Elder said Tuesday that at least 100 children under age 18 — 60 boys and 40 girls — have been killed since the ceasefire began due to military operations, including drone strikes, airstrikes, tank shelling and the use of live ammunition. Hundreds more children have been wounded, Elder said.

Those figures reflect incidents where enough details have been compiled to warrant recording, and the actual toll is expected to be higher, Elder said from Gaza City at a U.N. briefing in Geneva.

"While bombings and shootings have slowed during the ceasefire, they have not stopped," Elder said. "So what the world now calls calm would be considered a crisis anywhere else."

Israel's military said Tuesday it exchanged fire with six people spotted near its troops in southern Gaza, killing at least two in western Rafah.

## Aid shortfalls persist under truce

The U.N. and its humanitarian partners were distributing tents, tarps, blankets and clothing as well as nutrition and hygiene items across Gaza, the U.N. Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs said. But aid groups said not enough shelter materials are entering Gaza during the truce, and residents are not able to return to their homes in Israeli-controlled areas of the territory.

The majority of Palestinians in Gaza have been living in makeshift tents since their homes were reduced to rubble during more than two years of fighting. Gaza's Health Ministry says more than 71,400 Palestinians have been killed in Israel's retaliatory offensive. The war began on Oct. 7, 2023, when Hamas militants stormed into southern Israel and killed around 1,200 people and abducted 251 others.
