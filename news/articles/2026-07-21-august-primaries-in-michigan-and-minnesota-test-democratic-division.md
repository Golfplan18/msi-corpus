## Maine Democrats set to replace Platner with progressive Troy Jackson on Saturday

Progressive and moderate Democrats are headed for August primaries in Michigan and Minnesota that will gauge the party’s direction less than four months before the 2026 midterm elections, the Associated Press reported July 21. Before those contests, Maine Democrats are expected to nominate progressive former state lawmaker Troy Jackson on Saturday to succeed Graham Platner, whose candidacy collapsed earlier this month, giving the party a chance to recover in a key Senate race.

All three states are central to the fight for Senate control, raising the stakes over which wing of the party emerges with momentum heading into the fall.

The Maine convention on Saturday is the first hurdle. Platner’s exit left an opening that progressives appear poised to fill with Jackson, who is a former state lawmaker. A Jackson victory would in effect give the party’s left wing a second chance in the state after Platner’s downfall, the AP said.

The attention then shifts to Michigan and Minnesota, where August primaries will expose the party’s internal contest between an emboldened progressive wing and more moderate rivals. The outcome in both states, the AP noted, will offer a fresh gauge of whether progressive energy can translate into wins in statewide races.

“We’re seeing the pendulum shift from right to left. That’s ultimately what this midterm election is about,” Joseph Geevarghese, who leads the Sanders-founded group Our Revolution, said in an interview. “We’re in a transitional moment in national politics.”

The August contests in Michigan and Minnesota could either extend the progressive streak or signal that it has crested, the AP reported. Both states host Senate races that will help determine control of the chamber next year.

With the Senate majority in play, the outcome of this summer’s intraparty battles will shape the map and the message Democrats take to voters in the fall.
