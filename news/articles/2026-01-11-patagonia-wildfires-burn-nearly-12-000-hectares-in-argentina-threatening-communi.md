Raging wildfires in Argentina's Patagonia region have burned through nearly 12,000 hectares of scrubland, planted forest, and native woodland in Chubut province, threatening a power plant, a school, and surrounding rural properties, firefighting authorities reported Sunday. Argentina has deployed 295 firefighters and 15 aerial resources to combat the blazes, which started roughly a week ago in the Andean foothills of Chubut and are being fueled by persistent drought and strong winds.

The fires mark the second consecutive summer of major wildfire crisis in Patagonia, following blazes last year described as the worst in decades — fires that devastated tens of thousands of hectares, destroyed dozens of homes, and killed one person.

Chubut Gov. Ignacio Torres said Thursday that evidence suggests at least one of the largest active blazes was deliberately set. He announced a reward of 50 million pesos — approximately $34,000 — for information leading to those responsible.

"The wretches who started the fire are going to end up in jail," Torres told journalists.

Jorge Aranea, a volunteer firefighter battling the flames near El Hoyo on Friday, described conditions on the ground as overwhelming.

"It's sad to see everything burning. And sometimes you do what you can and it's not enough. What's happening is terrible," Aranea said.

Manuel Adorni, chief-of-staff and spokesperson for the Argentine executive branch, said Sunday that the firefighting operation also includes fire engines and logistical support from the Armed Forces.

On Saturday, Chilean President Gabriel Boric offered help to Argentina via the social platform X, pledging assistance in combating the active fire fronts across the border.

Firefighting efforts have been further hampered by massive columns of smoke that have reduced visibility for aerial and ground crews. Wildfires are common across Patagonia during the austral summer season, which runs from December through March and typically brings high temperatures, strong winds, and dry conditions that accelerate fire spread.

In addition to the Chubut fires, the Federal Emergency Agency reported active blazes in the neighboring Patagonian province of Neuquén. Fires in Santa Cruz and Río Negro provinces have been contained, though both remain under emergency status, authorities said.
