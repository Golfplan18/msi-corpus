Valentino Garavani, the Italian couturier whose sweeping gowns in his trademark shade of red dressed generations of royalty, first ladies and film stars for nearly half a century, died Monday at his Rome residence. He was 93.

The Valentino Garavani and Giancarlo Giammetti Foundation announced his death in a social media statement but did not give a cause. "Valentino Garavani was not only a constant guide and inspiration for all of us, but a true source of light, creativity and vision," the foundation said.

Universally known by his first name alone, Valentino founded his eponymous couture house on Rome's Via Condotti in 1959 and built it into a global luxury empire before retiring in 2008, leaving an enduring imprint on high fashion and the culture of celebrity glamour.

## The Red-Carpet Couturier

Valentino was adored by Jackie Kennedy Onassis, Julia Roberts and Queen Rania of Jordan, among many others. His reputation as the king of the red carpet rested on fail-safe designs that clients wore in some of Hollywood's most photographed moments.

Roberts wore a vintage Valentino black and white column gown to the 2001 Academy Awards when she accepted her best actress Oscar. Cate Blanchett wore a one-shouldered butter-yellow silk Valentino when she won the Oscar for best supporting actress in 2005.

Valentino also designed the long-sleeved lace dress Jacqueline Kennedy wore for her 1968 wedding to Greek shipping magnate Aristotle Onassis. Kennedy and Valentino were close friends for decades, and for a spell the former U.S. first lady wore almost exclusively Valentino. He was also close to Diana, Princess of Wales.

Beyond his signature orange-tinged shade of red, Valentino's trademarks included bows, ruffles, lace and embroidery — feminine embellishments that the designer employed throughout his career.

## From Voghera to Via Condotti

Valentino was born into a well-off family in the northern Italian town of Voghera on May 11, 1932. He studied fashion in Milan and Paris, then spent much of the 1950s working for Paris-based designers Jean Desses and later Guy Laroche before striking out on his own.

From the founding of the house, Giancarlo Giammetti handled the business side while Valentino used his charm to build a client base among the world's wealthy. Early fans included Italian film stars Gina Lollobrigida and Sophia Loren, as well as Hollywood stars Elizabeth Taylor and Audrey Hepburn. Legendary American *Vogue* editor-in-chief Diana Vreeland also took the young designer under her wing.

Over the decades, Valentino expanded into ready-to-wear, menswear and accessories. He and Giammetti sold the label to an Italian holding company for an estimated $300 million in 1998, with Valentino remaining in a design role for another decade. In 2007, he celebrated his 45th anniversary in fashion with a three-day celebration in Rome, capped with a grand ball at the Villa Borghese gallery. He retired in 2008.

## Tributes and Succession

Alessandro Michele, the current creative director of the Valentino fashion house, wrote on Instagram that he continues to feel Valentino's "gaze" as he works on the next collection, which will be presented March 12 in Rome. Michele described Valentino as "a man who expanded the limits of the possible" and possessing "a rare delicacy, with a silent rigor and a limitless love for beauty."

Italian Premier Giorgia Meloni called Valentino "an indisputable maestro of eternal style and elegance of Italian high fashion." Condolences also came from Donatella Versace, who posted photographs of the designer and said "he will forever be remembered for his art." Former supermodel Cindy Crawford said she was "heartbroken" and called Valentino "a true master of his craft."

The fashion house is currently owned by Qatar's Mayhoola, which controls a 70% stake, and French luxury conglomerate Kering, which owns 30% with an option to take full control in 2028 or 2029.

A public viewing will be held at the Valentino Garavani and Giancarlo Giammetti Foundation on Wednesday and Thursday. A funeral will be held Friday at the Basilica di Santa Maria degli Angeli e dei Martiri in central Rome.
