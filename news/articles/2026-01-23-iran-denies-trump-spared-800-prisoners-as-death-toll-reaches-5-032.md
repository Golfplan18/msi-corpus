Iran's top prosecutor on Friday rejected U.S. President Donald Trump's repeated claims that he halted the executions of 800 detained protesters, calling the assertion "completely false." The death toll from the nationwide crackdown on demonstrations has reached at least 5,032, according to activists, while a comprehensive internet blackout has stretched past two weeks.

The dispute comes as the U.S. military deploys naval assets toward the Middle East, with Trump suggesting military action remains possible if Iran executes more detained protesters. Analysts say the military buildup could enable strikes despite recent diplomatic signals.

Iran's chief prosecutor on Friday called Trump's claims about preventing executions of detainees "completely false," as activists documented a mounting death toll from weeks of nationwide crackdowns.

Mohammad Movahedi said the United States president had made repeated claims that he halted the hangings of 800 detained protesters without providing a source for those claims.

"This claim is completely false; no such number exists, nor has the judiciary made any such decision," Movahedi said in remarks carried by the judiciary's Mizan news agency.

A White House official, speaking on condition of anonymity, disputed Movahedi's assertion and insisted that Trump's warnings had caused Iran to call off planned executions. The official offered no evidence or details.

## The Crackdown's Scope

The dispute emerged amid an escalating military standoff between the United States and Iran. The U.S. Navy moved the aircraft carrier USS Abraham Lincoln and associated warships toward the Middle East, with Trump suggesting military action remained possible if Iran executes more detained protesters.

The death toll from demonstrations that began in late December has reached at least 5,032, according to activists documenting the violence. Iran's government reported a lower figure of 3,117 on Wednesday but has historically undercounted casualties from unrest.

More than 27,600 people have been detained in connection with the protests, the activists said. A comprehensive internet blackout that began shortly after the demonstrations started has lasted more than two weeks, limiting the ability of independent observers to verify the scale of the crackdown.

Prosecutors have charged detained protesters with "mohareb," meaning "enemies of God," a charge that carries the death penalty. The same charge was used in mass executions in 1988 that reportedly killed at least 5,000 people.

## Military Threats and Analysis

Trump said Thursday aboard Air Force One that the U.S. was moving the naval ships toward Iran "just in case" he chose to take action. He mentioned previous negotiations over Iran's nuclear program and threatened military strikes that would make earlier U.S. bombing campaigns against Iranian uranium enrichment sites "look like peanuts."

"We have a massive fleet heading in that direction and maybe we won't have to use it," Trump said.

The Soufan Center, a New York-based think tank, said in an analysis Friday that despite Trump's recent statements suggesting a pullback from military threats, military assets continue to be moved into the region, indicating that kinetic action may still occur.

## International Concerns

Human rights officials expressed alarm at the situation. Volker Türk, the United Nations' human rights chief, said Friday that Iran "remains among the top executioner states in the world," noting that the country reportedly executed at least 1,500 people last year, a 50 percent increase over 2024.
