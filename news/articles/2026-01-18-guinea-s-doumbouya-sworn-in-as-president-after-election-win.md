Gen. Mamadi Doumbouya was sworn in Saturday as president of Guinea after he won last year’s presidential election, according to the Associated Press. The inauguration marked his transition from leading a 2021 military coup to heading the country as an elected president.

Doumbouya took the oath of office in a new 55,000-seat stadium in the suburbs of Conakry, Guinea’s capital, the AP reported. The ceremony drew an audience that included African leaders and representatives of the African Union Commission and the ECOWAS Commission.

Doumbouya told those gathered that he fully appreciated the responsibility entrusted to him after the presidential election. “I fully appreciate the immense responsibility that the people of Guinea have entrusted to me following the presidential election,” he said, adding that “This mandate that has just been given to me is not a personal honor; it is a commitment to the Guinean people. A commitment to address the various governance challenges facing our country.”

The AP said Doumbouya was sworn in after a December election held under a new constitution. The wire service reported that the new constitution revoked a ban on military leaders running for office and extended the presidential mandate from five years to seven years.

Critics, the AP said, accused Doumbouya of clamping down on opposition and dissent. Doumbouya’s new term is now set following the vote in which the AP said he won “overwhelmingly” despite an earlier promise not to present himself as a candidate.

The AP reported that Guinea has large mineral resources, but about half of its 15 million population is mired in poverty and experiences record levels of food insecurity, citing the World Food Program. The wire service said the country’s economic and social challenges have remained central to the political debate surrounding the leadership change.

The AP said the Simandou iron ore project has been the focal point of infrastructural and economic revitalization under the junta. It described Simandou as a 75% Chinese-owned mega mining project at the world’s largest iron deposit, and said production at the site began late last year after decades of delay.

The AP added that authorities are banking on the project to create thousands of jobs and to draw investment in other sectors, including education and health. It also reported that many supporters attended the ceremony, including 28-year-old law student Rokiatou Kaba, from the Kankan Prefecture, who said: “Guinea is fully back on the international stage. Economic takeoff is imminent, prosperity is guaranteed.”

Not all attendees, the AP reported, were convinced. In the corner of the stadium, 26-year-old economics student Hassmiou Baldé said, “This is all just theater,” and argued that “After driving out all the real opposition, he surrounded himself with minor, unknown rivals.” Baldé added: “It’s a charade. A power grab.”

Guinea is among several West African countries that have seen a coup or coup attempt since 2020, the AP said, as military officers have seized power amid popular discontent tied to deteriorating security, underwhelming economies, or disputed elections.
