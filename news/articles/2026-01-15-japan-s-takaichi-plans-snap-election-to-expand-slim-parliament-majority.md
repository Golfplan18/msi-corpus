Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi plans to dissolve the lower house of Parliament after it convenes Jan. 23 and call a snap election as early as Feb. 8, the Liberal Democratic Party's secretary general said Wednesday.

Shunichi Suzuki, LDP secretary general, told reporters that Takaichi informed him and other top party officials she planned to dissolve the lower house "soon" after the session opens. Suzuki said Takaichi would explain the timeline at a news conference the following Monday but did not give dates for a dissolution or an election.

An expanded lower-house majority would give Takaichi and her new coalition partner, the Japan Innovation Party, the votes needed to pass a record 122.3 trillion yen ($770 billion) budget before the April start of the fiscal year and to press ahead with accelerated military spending — the twin pillars of her governing agenda.

## First female prime minister gambling on momentum

Takaichi became Japan's first female prime minister in October. Her approval ratings have remained around 70%, a level of public support the scandal-weakened LDP has not enjoyed in years.

The party and its coalition hold a slim majority in the lower house, the more powerful of Japan's two chambers, after a 2024 election loss. The ruling bloc has since added a number of independent lawmakers to maintain that margin, but the coalition remains a minority in the upper house, constraining its capacity to move legislation.

Media reports said Takaichi plans to dissolve the house on Jan. 23, the first day of this year's ordinary parliamentary session, paving the way for a vote as early as Feb. 8.

## Shifted coalition, same conservative agenda

Komeito, the LDP's long-standing junior partner, left the ruling bloc over disagreements with Takaichi's ideological positions and her push on anti-corruption measures. The Japan Innovation Party, a more conservative grouping, filled that role.

Suzuki said Takaichi seeks the public's mandate for policies including "proactive" fiscal spending and plans to further accelerate Japan's military buildup under the new coalition.

Her Cabinet approved the 122.3 trillion yen budget in late December to fund measures aimed at fighting inflation, supporting low-income households, and fueling economic growth. That budget requires parliamentary approval before the fiscal year begins April 1.

## Opposition: self-serving and ill-timed

Opposition lawmakers criticized the plan as a move driven by the LDP's self-interest that would push back substantive parliamentary debate on a budget the country needed to pass as soon as possible.

Takaichi also faces external pressures. A remark about Taiwan in the days after she took office angered Beijing, and trade and political tensions with China have intensified since. On Wednesday she met with South Korean President Lee Jae Myung in Nara at a summit intended to deepen bilateral ties between the neighboring countries.
