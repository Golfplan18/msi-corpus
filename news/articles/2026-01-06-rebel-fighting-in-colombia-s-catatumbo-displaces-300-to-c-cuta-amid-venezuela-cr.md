More than 300 civilians have fled Colombia's Catatumbo region to seek shelter in the border city of Cúcuta after rebel groups resumed fighting in December, Colombia's Human Rights Ombudswoman Iris Marín said Monday. The displaced came from Tibu and El Tarra, two municipalities in the volatile region that straddles the border with Venezuela.

The fresh displacement arrives as Colombia braces for a potential influx of Venezuelan refugees following a U.S. raid that led to the capture of President Nicolás Maduro — compounding a humanitarian emergency in Catatumbo, one of Colombia's primary coca-producing zones, where more than 56,000 people were displaced and at least 80 killed in 2025 alone.

"Once again we call on rebel groups to cease combat and leave the civilian population out of the conflict," Marín wrote in a message posted on X on Monday night.

## A region under prolonged siege

Drug traffickers and rebel groups have fought for control of the Catatumbo region for years, drawn by its status as one of Colombia's primary coca-producing areas. In 2025, the National Liberation Army — known by its Spanish acronym ELN — waged an offensive against a rival faction known as the FARC-EMC, displacing more than 56,000 people and killing at least 80.

The scale of that violence prompted Colombia's government to suspend peace talks with the ELN that had begun in 2022.

## Border on alert after Venezuela operation

Colombia deployed tanks and troops to Cúcuta on Saturday, following a U.S. raid on neighboring Venezuela that led to the capture of Maduro, according to the Associated Press. Colombian officials said the country was preparing for a possible wave of Venezuelan refugees, though that influx had not materialized as of Tuesday.

On Tuesday, Gloria Arriero, the director of Colombia's National Immigration Service, said foot traffic along the border had not changed significantly since the operation in Venezuela.

"We feel calm, because the flow of people has not increased," Arriero said at a news conference. She said approximately 60,000 people enter Cúcuta and leave the city each day.

The situation in Venezuela appeared to have stabilized, Arriero said, with Maduro's Vice President Delcy Rodríguez sworn in as interim leader.
