Sheinbaum has worked to build a cooperative relationship with the Trump administration and framed bilateral security gains as evidence that U.S. military intervention in Mexico is unnecessary, even as Trump's Venezuela operation has put Latin American governments on alert about potential U.S. action in the region.

Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum said Friday that joint efforts to combat drug cartels and reduce northbound migration were producing "compelling results," as President Donald Trump threatened to send U.S. forces into Mexico following Washington's military operation that deposed Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro.

Sheinbaum, who has worked to build a cooperative relationship with the Trump administration, framed the bilateral security gains as evidence that U.S. military intervention in Mexico is unnecessary — even as the Venezuela operation has put Latin American governments on alert about potential U.S. action in the region.

On Thursday, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Mexican Foreign Secretary Juan Ramón de la Fuente issued a joint statement after a phone call, saying the two sides agreed "more must be done to confront shared threats."

Speaking at her Friday morning press briefing, Sheinbaum cited a steep drop in the homicide rate, lower fentanyl seizures by U.S. border authorities, and reduced migration as evidence of progress. She described the gains as the product of cooperation between Mexico and the United States.

"There are very compelling results from the joint cooperation and the work that Mexico has been doing," Sheinbaum said.

Sheinbaum also pressed Washington to act on its side of the security equation, calling for an end to arms trafficking from the United States into Mexico and urging the United States to treat drug consumption as a public health concern.

"The other side also has to do its part. This consumption crisis they have over there also has to be addressed from a public health perspective, through education campaigns," Sheinbaum said.

Trump threatened that U.S. forces "will now start hitting land" in Mexico targeting drug cartels, according to the Associated Press. The threat followed the U.S. military raid on Venezuela that deposed Maduro and brought him to the United States to face narco-trafficking charges. The AP reported that the Venezuela operation set much of Latin America on edge, fueling concern that Trump could turn American forces on other nations in the region, particularly Cuba and Mexico.

Sheinbaum and Trump had spoken by phone the previous Monday. She said she again told Trump that U.S. intervention in Mexico was unnecessary.
