Bulgaria's political deadlock — driven in part by the influence of sanctioned oligarch Delyan Peevski, whose party has repeatedly propped up the outgoing coalition — has prevented the country from forming a stable government and raises fresh questions about democratic integrity in the EU's newest eurozone member.

SOFIA, Bulgaria — Thousands of Bulgarians braved freezing temperatures in Sofia on Wednesday to demand clean elections, as the country appeared headed toward its eighth vote in five years.

The demonstration, the latest in a series of protests that erupted last month, followed the governing coalition's attempt to introduce a 2026 budget that many Bulgarians — already struggling with rising prices — feared would worsen their financial situation. The government later withdrew the budget plan, but protesters broadened their demands to include calls for the center-right coalition to resign.

Demonstrators insisted on elections free of vote buying, vote manipulation, and falsification of results, practices they alleged had marred previous campaigns, according to the Associated Press. Protesters also said the outgoing government's refusal to adopt machine voting ahead of a possible early election amounted to another attempt to influence the outcome.

## The Peevski factor

At the center of protesters' frustrations is Delyan Peevski, a Bulgarian politician and oligarch who has been placed under U.S. and U.K. sanctions. Peevski's MRF New Beginning party has repeatedly backed the outgoing coalition led by the GERB party of former Prime Minister Boyko Borissov, the AP reported.

Bulgaria, a Balkan nation of 6.4 million people, joined the European Union in 2007. Divisions within the country have fueled a succession of elections that have failed to produce a stable government.

The country did mark one milestone at the start of the year: Bulgaria adopted the euro on January 1, 2026, replacing its national currency, the lev, and becoming the eurozone's 21st member.

## What comes next

President Rumen Radev offered the two largest parliamentary groups the opportunity to form a new government; both attempts failed. Radev will now present a candidate for prime minister, and if that effort also falls short, he will appoint a caretaker Cabinet and call a new election, the AP reported.
