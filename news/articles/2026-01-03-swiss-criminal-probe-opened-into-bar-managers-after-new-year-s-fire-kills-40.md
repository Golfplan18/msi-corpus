Swiss authorities opened a criminal investigation Friday into the managers of Le Constellation bar in Crans-Montana, two days after a fire at a New Year's party killed 40 people and injured more than 120 others, the Valais region's chief prosecutor said Saturday. The two managers face suspicion of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm, and involuntarily causing a fire, according to prosecutor Beatrice Pilloud.

The blaze broke out around 1:30 a.m. Thursday at the Alpine resort town bar during New Year's celebrations. Police have said many of those killed and injured were in their teens to mid-20s.

Investigators believe sparkling candles atop Champagne bottles ignited the blaze when the candles came too close to the ceiling of the crowded venue. Authorities are examining whether sound-dampening ceiling material conformed with fire regulations, whether the candles were permitted inside the bar, and whether fire extinguishers and escape routes met safety standards.

CRANS-MONTANA, Switzerland — Swiss authorities opened a criminal investigation Friday into the managers of the bar where a fire during a New Year's party killed 40 people and injured more than 120 others, the Valais region's chief prosecutor said Saturday.

The two managers face suspicion of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm, and involuntarily causing a fire, prosecutor Beatrice Pilloud told reporters. Pilloud said the investigation was opened Friday night and would help "explore all the leads." She did not name the managers.

## Probable cause and safety inquiries

Investigators said Friday they believe sparkling candles atop Champagne bottles ignited the blaze when they came too close to the ceiling of the crowded venue. The fire broke out around 1:30 a.m. Thursday at Le Constellation bar in the Alpine resort town of Crans-Montana.

Authorities plan to examine whether sound-dampening material on the ceiling conformed with fire regulations, whether the candles were permitted for use in the bar, and whether fire extinguishers and escape routes met safety standards.

Stéphane Ganzer, the Valais region's top security official, told SRF public radio Saturday that "such a huge accident with a fire in Switzerland means that something didn't work — maybe the material, maybe the organization on the spot." He added: "Something didn't work and someone made a mistake, I am sure of that."

Nicolas Féraud, head of the Crans-Montana municipality, told RTS radio he was "convinced" that checks on the bar had not been lax, the broadcaster reported.

Asked whether the tragedy could have been avoided, Swiss Justice Minister Beat Jans said officials could not yet answer, adding that "we know that the world needs an answer on this question."

## Identifying the dead and injured

The severity of burns has complicated the identification of victims, requiring families to provide DNA samples. In some cases, wallets and identification documents were destroyed by fire.

By Saturday, regional police said four victims had been identified and their bodies returned to families: a 16-year-old boy, a 16-year-old girl, an 18-year-old man, and a 21-year-old woman, all Swiss nationals. Several injured people also remained unidentified.

Italy's ambassador to Switzerland, Gian Lorenzo Cornado, told reporters Saturday that 121 people had been injured, with five still unidentified, and that 14 Italians were being treated in hospitals. Swiss police have said the injured included more than 70 Swiss nationals and over 10 each from France and Italy, along with citizens of Serbia, Bosnia, Luxembourg, Belgium, Portugal, and Poland.

## Families await word

Laetitia Brodard, whose 16-year-old son Arthur attended the New Year's celebration at Le Constellation, told reporters Friday: "I'm looking everywhere. The body of my son is somewhere. I want to know where my child is and be by his side. Wherever that may be, be it in the intensive care unit or the morgue." On Saturday she told French broadcaster BFM TV that "we, parents, are starting to get tired ... and anger is starting to rise."

Elvira Venturella, an Italian psychologist working with the families, said: "It's a wait that destroys people's stability. And the more time passes, the more difficult it becomes to accept the uncertainty, not having information."

Ganzer, who visited the site Saturday alongside Justice Minister Jans, called the families' wait "unbearable" and said officials' top priority was providing them "the legitimate answers they are waiting for."

Mourners bearing flowers gathered at makeshift memorials outside Le Constellation, consoling one another as they shed tears. One handwritten note read: "RIP you are all our children."
