The Spain derailment comes as rail travel remains a common and relatively affordable means of transport for Europeans and tourists, with investigators in past European accidents repeatedly attributing crashes to human error, equipment failure and aging infrastructure.

A high-speed train derailment in southern Spain has left at least 39 people dead and more than 150 injured, authorities said. The crash is among the continent's deadliest rail accidents in recent years, occurring even as the European Union has reported a long-term decline in major railway accidents since 2010.

Rail travel in Europe is a common, relatively affordable and convenient mode of transport for Europeans and tourists. Major railway accidents have decreased since 2010, according to the European Union, yet the Spain crash is a reminder of how deadly they can be when they happen.

## Recent deadly European rail accidents

**September 2025 — Lisbon, Portugal**

A street car in Lisbon derailed and crashed into a building, killing 16 people and injuring 21. A preliminary report found that an underground cable acting as a counterweight between the two tram cars was unsuitable for use and broke.

**February 2023 — Northern Greece**

A passenger train carrying hundreds of people collided at high speed with an oncoming freight train in northern Greece, producing a fiery wreck that killed 57 people. An investigative report blamed human error, outdated infrastructure and major systemic failures.

**July 2016 — Puglia, Italy**

Two commuter trains collided head-on between towns in the southern Italian region of Puglia, killing 31 people and injuring scores more. An investigation found an error of communication between the stations each train had departed from.

**July 2013 — Santiago de Compostela, Spain**

A commuter train near the northwestern Spanish city of Santiago de Compostela left the tracks as it came around a bend, killing 80 and injuring 145. An investigation found the train was traveling at 179 kph (111 mph) on a stretch with an 80 kph (50 mph) speed limit when it left the tracks and struck a wall.

**February 2010 — Brussels, Belgium**

Two commuter trains outside Brussels collided during morning rush hour when one ran a red light, killing 19 people and injuring 171. It was Belgium's worst train crash.

**June 2009 — Viareggio, Italy**

A freight train carrying gas derailed at Viareggio station near the Tuscan city of Lucca and exploded, killing 32 people. Investigators blamed poorly maintained axles.

**July 2006 — Valencia, Spain**

A subway train traveling at excessive speed crashed in an underground tunnel in the eastern Spanish city of Valencia, killing 43 people. A court found four managers of the city's subway system guilty of negligent manslaughter for not taking the necessary safety measures — a verdict reached 13 years after the crash.

**January 2006 — Montenegro**

A braking system failure caused a train in Montenegro to derail and plunge into a ravine outside the capital, Podgorica, killing 45 people including five children and injuring 184. It was the worst train disaster in Montenegro's history.

**November 2000 — Kaprun, Austria**

A cable car on a funicular railway caught fire in a mountain tunnel in Kaprun, killing 155 people — skiers and snowboarders heading to the slopes of the Kitzsteinhorn mountain.

**October 1999 — London, United Kingdom**

A train departing London's Paddington station went through a red light and collided with an incoming high-speed train, killing 31 people. Around 400 people were injured.

**June 1998 — Eschede, Germany**

A high-speed train traveling at 200 kph (125 mph) collided with a bridge at Eschede, causing it to collapse. The crash killed 101 people and injured around 100 more. It was Germany's deadliest postwar rail disaster.
