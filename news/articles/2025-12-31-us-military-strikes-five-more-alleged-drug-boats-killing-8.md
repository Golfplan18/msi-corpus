The U.S. military struck five alleged drug-smuggling boats over two days, killing eight people while others jumped overboard and may have survived, U.S. Southern Command said Wednesday.

The CIA also conducted a drone strike last week at a docking area on Venezuelan soil believed to have been used by Venezuelan drug cartels, according to two people familiar with details of the operation who requested anonymity to discuss the classified matter.

The latest attacks bring the total number of known U.S. boat strikes to 35 and the death toll to at least 115 since early September, according to numbers announced by the Trump administration.

## Tuesday strikes

Southern Command said three boats were traveling in close formation in a convoy along what the military described as known narco-trafficking routes. The military said the boats "had transferred narcotics between the three vessels prior to the strikes" but did not provide evidence to back up that claim.

Three people were killed when the first boat was struck. People aboard the other two vessels jumped overboard and distanced themselves from the boats before those vessels were attacked. Southern Command said it immediately notified the U.S. Coast Guard to activate search and rescue efforts. The command did not say whether those who jumped overboard were rescued.

## Wednesday strikes

U.S. forces struck two more boats Wednesday, killing five people alleged to be traveling along known trafficking routes, Southern Command said in a separate statement. It did not provide evidence of the alleged trafficking or identify the body of water where the attacks occurred.

## Scrutiny from September incident

Notifying the Coast Guard followed scrutiny of a September incident in which U.S. forces killed the survivors of an initial boat strike with a follow-up attack on the disabled vessel. Some Democratic lawmakers and legal experts said that follow-up strike constituted a crime. The Trump administration and some Republican lawmakers said it was legal.

## Venezuela pressure campaign

Southern Command, which oversees military operations in South America, did not reveal where Tuesday's or Wednesday's strikes took place. Previous attacks have occurred in the Caribbean Sea and in the eastern Pacific Ocean.

President Trump has justified the boat-strike campaign as a necessary escalation to stem the flow of drugs into the United States. He has asserted the country is engaged in an "armed conflict" with drug cartels.

The Trump administration has also built up military forces in the region as part of an escalating pressure campaign on Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, who has been charged with narco-terrorism in the United States.

The CIA drone strike, described by two people familiar with the classified operation, was the first known direct U.S. operation on Venezuelan soil since the boat-strike campaign began in September.
