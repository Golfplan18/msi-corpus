Former Brazilian President Jair Bolsonaro left a hospital in the capital Brasilia on Thursday and was transported back to the federal police headquarters where he is serving a 27-year prison sentence for leading a coup attempt following his 2022 election defeat.

Hospital DF Star confirmed Bolsonaro's discharge after a double hernia surgery completed without complications, along with several other minor medical procedures performed during the same hospitalization. Brazil's Supreme Court had authorized the temporary departure from custody for the operation. Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes, who sentenced Bolsonaro, denied the former president's request for house arrest upon discharge.

## Conviction and custody

Bolsonaro governed Brazil from 2019 to 2022. In September 2025, a panel of Supreme Court justices convicted him and several allies of attempting to overthrow Brazil's democratic system after he lost the presidential election to incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva.

Prosecutors documented that the coup plot included plans to kill Lula, Vice President Geraldo Alckmin, and Justice de Moraes. Prosecutors also documented a plan to encourage an insurrection in early 2023. Bolsonaro was additionally convicted of leading an armed criminal organization and of attempting the violent abolition of the democratic rule of law. He has denied any wrongdoing.

Bolsonaro is held at the federal police headquarters in Brasilia in a 12-square-meter room — roughly 130 square feet — equipped with a bed, a private bathroom, air conditioning, a television, and a desk, according to the Associated Press. He has no contact with the few other inmates at the facility.

## Medical history

Thursday's surgery was not Bolsonaro's first major procedure. He has undergone several medical operations since he was stabbed in the abdomen during a campaign rally in 2018.

## Political succession

In December, Bolsonaro moved to shape the political landscape beyond his imprisonment by appointing his eldest son, Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro, as his political party's presidential candidate for the 2027 election — positioning Flávio as the standard-bearer against Lula's continued incumbency. Neither Flávio Bolsonaro nor former first lady Michelle Bolsonaro made public comments specifically about the former president's return to prison following his hospitalization.

Michelle Bolsonaro said on her social media channels that "there is a Brazil of good people which loves you and prays for you." She added: "We will beat the bad days."
