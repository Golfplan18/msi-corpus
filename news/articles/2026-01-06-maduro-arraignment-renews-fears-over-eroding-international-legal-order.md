The operation has alarmed world leaders from France to China who say it sets a destabilizing precedent for unilateral military action at a moment when Ukraine, Taiwan, Greenland and Iran are already testing the limits of international norms — while the Trump administration insists the mission was a lawful enforcement action against narco-terrorism.

NEW YORK — Governments across Europe, Asia and Latin America warned Monday that the Trump administration's military capture and arraignment of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro on narco-terrorism charges threatens the international rules-based order, prompting an emergency session of the United Nations Security Council. U.S. forces seized Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, from their home on a military base in the Venezuelan capital Caracas before Maduro appeared in a Manhattan federal courtroom on charges of participating in a narco-terrorism conspiracy.

U.N. Undersecretary-General Rosemary A. DiCarlo told the Security Council on Monday that the "maintenance of international peace and security depends on the continued commitment of all member states to adhere to all the provisions of the (U.N.) Charter."

The Trump administration has insisted the operation was legal. U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz called the military action a justified "surgical law enforcement operation." The administration had declared drug cartels operating from Venezuela to be unlawful combatants and said the United States is in an "armed conflict" with them, according to a memo obtained in October by the Associated Press. The mission fits within Trump's National Security Strategy, published in December, which identifies restoring "American preeminence in the Western Hemisphere" as a key goal of his second term.

## World leaders condemn the operation

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said Maduro's capture "runs counter to the principle of the non-use of force, which forms the basis of international law." He warned that the "increasing number of violations of this principle by nations vested with the important responsibility of permanent membership on the United Nations Security Council will have serious consequences for global security and will spare no one."

Russian Ambassador to the U.N. Vasily Nebenzya called the mission "a turn back to the era of lawlessness" and urged the Security Council to "unite and to definitively reject the methods and tools of U.S. military foreign policy."

Hungarian Prime Minister Viktor Orbán, a close Trump ally, took a contrasting view. International rules, he said, "do not govern the decisions of many great powers. This is completely obvious."

The 27-nation European Union, while maintaining that Maduro lacked political legitimacy, said in a statement that "the principles of international law and the U.N. Charter must be upheld" and that Security Council members "have a particular responsibility to uphold those principles."

## Concerns about further action

Analysts and some world leaders voiced fears that Maduro's ouster could serve as a blueprint for further military interventions.

On Sunday evening, Trump also put Venezuela's neighbor Colombia and its president, Gustavo Petro, on notice. Trump said Colombia is "run by a sick man who likes making cocaine and selling it to the United States." The Trump administration had imposed sanctions in October on Petro, his family and a member of his government over accusations of involvement in the global drug trade.

## Regional implications

The operation raised questions about several ongoing geopolitical situations, according to the Associated Press.

In Europe, Trump fanned additional concern when he speculated openly about the future of Greenland, a Danish territory. "It's so strategic right now. Greenland is covered with Russian and Chinese ships all over the place," Trump told reporters Sunday. "We need Greenland from the standpoint of national security, and Denmark is not going to be able to do it." Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said in a statement that Trump has "no right to annex" the territory and reminded him that Denmark already provides the United States broad access to Greenland through existing security agreements.

China condemned the Venezuela mission as a "blatant use of force against a sovereign state" and said Washington was acting as the "world's judge." Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said Tuesday the United States had "wantonly trampled on Venezuela's sovereignty and security." Analysts said China was unlikely to replicate the approach against Taiwan, which Beijing claims as its own territory, preferring a strategy of gradual military pressure and political influence over targeting the island's leadership directly.

In Ukraine, Volodymyr Fesenko, chairman of the board of the Penta think tank in Kyiv, said Russian President Vladimir Putin has long undermined the global order and weakened international law. "Unfortunately," Fesenko said, "Trump's actions have continued this trend."

On Friday, Trump warned Iran that if Tehran "violently kills peaceful protesters," the United States "will come to their rescue." Violence sparked by Iran's ailing economy killed at least 35 people, activists said Tuesday.
