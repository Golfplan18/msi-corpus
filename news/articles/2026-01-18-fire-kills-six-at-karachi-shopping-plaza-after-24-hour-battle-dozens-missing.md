KARACHI, Pakistan — Firefighters in Karachi extinguished a blaze Sunday at Gul Plaza, a multistory shopping complex, after a nearly 24-hour effort that left six people dead — including a firefighter — and dozens missing, officials said. A search for those still unaccounted for remained underway.

The disaster underscored Karachi's persistent vulnerability to deadly commercial building fires, which authorities have long attributed to poor safety standards and illegal construction in one of South Asia's most densely populated cities.

## The Fire

The blaze broke out late Saturday at Gul Plaza, a four-story building with a basement that housed about 1,200 shops, according to Dr. Abid Jalal Sheikh, the city's chief rescue officer. It spread rapidly through shops stocked with cosmetics, garments, and plastic goods. Parts of the structure collapsed during the blaze, and television footage showed dozens of firefighters in protective gear as thick smoke billowed from the damaged building.

Five bodies were recovered from inside the building, Sheikh said. A firefighter also died while fighting flames on the upper floors, rescue officials said.

Murad Ali Shah, the chief minister of Sindh province — of which Karachi is the capital — said the fire had been fully extinguished and that rescue workers had begun searching for the missing. Earlier, authorities had said crews needed another four to six hours to bring the blaze under control.

The cause of the fire was not immediately known. Officials said an investigation would follow.

## Government Response

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif expressed sorrow over the deaths and ordered authorities to use all available resources to prevent further losses, officials said.

Families of the missing gathered near the scene as the search continued, according to the Associated Press.

## Safety Record

Karachi has a history of deadly fires in commercial buildings, often blamed on poor safety standards and illegal construction, according to the Associated Press. A fire at a shopping mall in the city in November 2023 killed 10 people and injured 22 others.
