An Associated Press videographer came under attack Monday in Port-au-Prince while filming a police patrol in Haiti's capital, according to Pierre-Richard Luxama. Gang members set the armored vehicle's roof ablaze with Molotov cocktails, and while officers inside remained calm and returned fire, the attackers fled.

The attack underscores grave risks journalists face reporting on Haiti's gang crisis, where at least 1.4 million people have been displaced and gang violence shows no signs of abating despite efforts by police and a United Nations-backed mission.

It took about seven to ten minutes to return to the police base, Luxama recounted. Throughout the vehicle's journey back, he said he remained still and breathed slowly, intent on not inhaling the heavy smoke spreading through the compartment. "Inside the armored car, the smoke was really heavy and was spreading everywhere quickly," Luxama said.

When the vehicle arrived at base, there was immediate chaos. "Everybody was running around us. They were panicked when they saw the roof on fire," Luxama said. A group of civilians and police officers sprinted over to throw water on the roof as it burned.

## Two Decades in the Field

The attack underscores the daily hazards Luxama and his colleague face in Haiti. For nearly two decades, he and Dánica Coto have been covering Haiti's descent into gang violence, documenting the country's worsening humanitarian crisis. At least 5.7 million Haitians are at crisis level, with 1.9 million of those facing emergency levels of hunger. Gang violence has displaced 1.4 million people in recent years despite efforts by Haitian police and a U.N.-backed police mission.

## What He Witnessed

What Luxama observed during the patrol remained vivid in his account—images he said may never leave him. He saw a severed arm and leg tied to an electrical wire hanging in front of an abandoned and looted store. Streets were full of trash. Buildings had been demolished, their doors and windows removed. Neighborhoods had been emptied by residents' profound fear of the powerful gangs controlling them.

In downtown Port-au-Prince, Luxama said, "You only hear the birds singing." But the quiet masks a grave and constant threat. Viv Ansanm, a powerful gang federation, controls large areas of the capital.

## Composure Under Fire

Reflecting on the experience, Luxama drew on nearly two decades of covering conflict. "It was scary," he said of coming under fire. But he emphasized the importance of staying composed in dangerous situations. "When I'm in a difficult situation, the first thing I should do is stay calm. When you stay calm, you think about what the next situation will be, and what you should do. If you panic, it's not going to be good for you."

The attack occurred along the capital's main thoroughfare during a tactical police patrol meant to address the gang violence that has devastated the city. Police told those inside to breathe slowly as they navigated the smoke. One officer announced over the radio: "We have been attacked by a Molotov cocktail, so let's move. Let's move because we don't want the armored car to break down." During gang attacks in prior years, police officers pulled from broken-down armored vehicles had been killed, with grisly videos of the killings circulating on social media.

Journalists in Haiti face grave danger. They dodge bullets as they document the collapse of the capital and the humanitarian catastrophe unfolding across the country. Luxama's account of Monday's attack represents one moment in an ongoing crisis that shows no signs of abating.
