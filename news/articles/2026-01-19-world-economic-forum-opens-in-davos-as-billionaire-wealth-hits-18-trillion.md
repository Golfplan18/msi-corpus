Billionaire wealth grew more than 16 percent in 2025, Oxfam reported — three times faster than the prior five-year average — while Edelman's trust barometer found a broad collapse in public confidence in institutions, framing the backdrop for approximately 3,000 business and government officials assembling to address inequality, artificial intelligence, and geopolitical friction.

Corporate chiefs and government leaders, including U.S. President Donald Trump, convened Tuesday in Davos, Switzerland for the World Economic Forum's four-day annual summit as anti-poverty group Oxfam reported that global billionaire wealth surpassed $18 trillion last year. The gathering, which organizers themed "A Spirit of Dialogue," opened as an annual global trust survey found trade and recession fears at record highs and nearly 70 percent of respondents said they believe institutional leaders deliberately deceive the public.

Oxfam reported that billionaire wealth grew more than 16 percent in 2025 — three times faster than the average over the previous five years — reaching more than $18 trillion, based on Forbes magazine data tracking the world's wealthiest individuals. The $2.5 trillion added to billionaire fortunes last year would be sufficient to eliminate extreme poverty 26 times, the group said. Billionaire wealth has grown more than four-fifths since 2020, while nearly half the world's population lives in poverty, Oxfam added.

## Trump's attendance and agenda

Trump's visit marks his third appearance at Davos as president. He leads what the Associated Press described as the largest U.S. delegation to the forum to date, with approximately six Cabinet secretaries accompanying him. Trump is scheduled to address the gathering Wednesday on housing and affordability.

An announcement related to Trump's Gaza "Peace Council" is expected during the summit, the AP reported. Trump and members of his administration have planned bilateral meetings in side rooms of the Congress Center.

The two governments most prominent as potential counterweights to U.S. influence at the forum are China and the European Union, both of which held leading positions on the opening day's agenda. EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen was scheduled to address the gathering Tuesday morning, immediately followed by Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng.

Also attending are Colombian President Gustavo Petro, with whom Trump has had a fractious relationship, and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, whose country faces U.S. sanctions.

## Billionaire wealth and the inequality gap

Oxfam, the international anti-poverty group, released a report before the summit that drew on Forbes magazine data to show billionaire wealth grew to more than $18 trillion globally last year, an increase of more than 16 percent that was three times the average growth rate of the preceding five years.

The $2.5 trillion increase in one year alone would be enough to eliminate extreme poverty across the world 26 times over, Oxfam said. Billionaire wealth has risen by more than four-fifths since 2020, while nearly half of the world's population lives in poverty, the group said.

Oxfam attributed part of the acceleration to what it described as a "pro-billionaire agenda" under the Trump administration, citing tax reductions for high earners, gains in AI-linked stocks that have enriched investors, and resistance to international efforts to tax large corporations.

## Trust at record lows

Edelman, the public relations firm that has tracked global trust annually for 25 years, released its 2026 Trust Barometer at the forum, drawing on surveys of nearly 34,000 people in 28 countries. Trade and recession fears have reached their highest levels on record, the survey found. Optimism is falling most sharply in developed nations.

Two-thirds of respondents said their trust was concentrated in the CEOs of their employer companies or in their neighbors — not in governments or major institutional actors. Nearly 70 percent said they believe institutional leaders, whether in business or government, deliberately deceive the public.

Edelman CEO Richard Edelman said the results reflected a broad withdrawal from public engagement. "People are withdrawing from dialogue and engagement, choosing the safety of the familiar over the perceived risk of change," Edelman said, according to the AP. "We favor nationalism over global connection and individual benefit over collective progress. Our mindset has shifted from 'we' to 'me.'"

The 2026 barometer characterized last year's "resentment" as having broadened into a more generalized "insularity."

Mirek Dušek, the forum's programming director, said the gathering comes at a pivotal moment. "Geopolitics is changing," Dušek said, according to the AP. "Some think we are in a transition, some think we have already entered a new era. But I think it is undeniable that we are seeing a more competitive and more contested landscape."

## Forum under new leadership; AI prominent

The summit convenes without its founder, Klaus Schwab, who organized the first Davos event 55 years ago and presided over its expansion from a business-focused conference into a broader global policy gathering. Schwab retired in April. The new co-chairs are Larry Fink, chief executive of the investment firm BlackRock, and Andre Hoffman, vice chairman of the pharmaceutical company Roche.

This year marks the Davos debut of Jensen Huang, chief executive of Nvidia, among approximately 850 CEOs and company presidents attending. Artificial intelligence — its business applications, its effect on employment, and the prospects for artificial general intelligence — is among the summit's principal topics.

The presidents of Argentina, France, Indonesia, Syria, and Ukraine are among the dozens of national leaders attending.

## Protests

Hundreds of demonstrators gathered near Davos on Saturday, before the summit opened, carrying a banner opposing war profiteering and a truck bearing a sign that read "World Economic Failure," according to the AP.

Mirjam Hostetmann, president of the Swiss Young Socialists, which helped organize the protest, said she found it concerning that Swiss politicians were hosting what she described as warmongers and their beneficiaries at the forum. The gathering would never bring peace, only fuel escalation, she said.

Companies including Microsoft, India's Tata Consultancy Services, TikTok, and cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike have established spaces along Davos's main promenade alongside government pavilions from Nigeria, Qatar, Ukraine, and the United States. Davos merchants rent their storefronts to forum participants during the week.

Critics have long contended that the annual gathering generates more rhetoric than results. Some described Trump's return to the forum as a signal of the disconnect between the forum's participants and those left behind economically, the AP reported.
