## Judge suspends Ohio voter registration document rule before midterms

**Subtype:** fact

- Judge Solomon Oliver Jr. temporarily blocked Ohio’s citizenship-document requirement for voter registration.
- Frank LaRose said Ohio would immediately appeal the ruling.
- Solomon Oliver Jr. ruled that the requirement appears to conflict with the National Voter Registration Act.
- Ohio’s injunction applies before the Oct. 5 registration deadline for the fall midterm elections.
- Donald Trump is urging Congress to pass a bill requiring documentary proof of citizenship for voter registration.

## LaRose says he will appeal the ruling issued in Cleveland

The Ohio requirement applies when voters register at a motor vehicle bureau. The rule took effect for the first time this year.

Oliver issued the temporary injunction in Cleveland. His ruling said the National Voter Registration Act takes precedence over the Ohio requirement and that the state rule appears to conflict with the federal law.

The order suspends enforcement of the documentation requirement ahead of the Oct. 5 deadline for registering for the fall midterm elections.

The lawsuit was brought on behalf of the Ohio Alliance of Retired Americans and Red Wine & Blue by the Elias Law Group. LaRose, a Republican, said he would immediately appeal.

The ruling comes as President Donald Trump pushes Congress to pass a bill that would require documentary proof of citizenship to register to vote, along with other election changes.
