Days of monsoon rain burst riverbanks on Siau island early Monday, triggering flash floods that killed at least 16 people, injured 25 others, and forced more than 680 residents into emergency shelters in Indonesia's North Sulawesi province, officials said Tuesday. Fast-moving torrents of water mixed with mud, rocks, and debris swept away people and submerged villages in Siau Tagulandang Biaro District. Three residents remained missing as rescuers searched inundated neighborhoods where entire communities had been submerged.

The disaster underscores Indonesia's acute vulnerability to seasonal flooding during monsoon months, coming weeks after catastrophic floods and landslides on Sumatra killed more than 1,100 people and left tens of thousands displaced.

## Flooding and rescue operations

The flooding struck Siau, a tiny island about 130 kilometers (80 miles) off the northern tip of Sulawesi, Indonesia's fourth-largest island, said Abdul Muhari, a spokesperson for the National Disaster Management Agency. Emergency rescue personnel supported by police and the military deployed to four devastated villages. In some places, access was hampered by damaged roads and disrupted communications, Muhari said.

At least seven houses were washed away and more than 140 homes were damaged when the deluge surged down hillsides. Residents fled to temporary shelters in churches and public buildings.

As weather improved and floodwaters receded Tuesday, a North Sulawesi search and rescue office spokesman, Nuriadian Gumeleng, said rescuers had recovered 16 bodies and were still searching for three missing residents in areas where entire neighborhoods had been inundated.

## Emergency declaration

Sitaro District Chief Chyntia Ingrid Kalangit declared a 14-day emergency response period beginning Monday to speed the delivery of aid, evacuations, and infrastructure repairs. Heavy equipment and relief supplies were dispatched by the provincial government.

"We called on residents to remain alert as further rainfall could raise the risk of additional flooding or landslides," Kalangit said.

## Broader toll across Indonesia

The North Sulawesi disaster follows a series of deadly floods across Indonesia's archipelago. Catastrophic floods and landslides in December struck 52 cities and regencies on Sumatra, Indonesia's largest island. The National Disaster Management Agency said those floods left 1,178 people dead and more than 7,000 injured, with 148 residents still missing as of Tuesday.
