## US strikes Iran for 9th consecutive night as Hormuz conflict continues

**Subtype:** fact

- The U.S. military completed a ninth consecutive night of strikes against Iranian military targets on Sunday, hitting command centers, maritime capabilities, communications networks, and missile and drone launch sites, according to U.S. Central Command.
- CENTCOM said the strikes were carried out to further diminish Iran's ability to attack commercial vessels and civilian mariners transiting the Strait of Hormuz.
- A third U.S. service member has been killed since fighting resumed nearly two weeks ago, bringing the total number of American deaths in the conflict to 17 since the war began in late February, the U.S. military announced Sunday.
- Explosions were reported in the Iranian port cities of Bandar-e Mahshahr and Bandar Imam Khomeini in Khuzestan province by state-run Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting.
- The Kuwait Army said its air defenses were confronting Iranian drones, and Bahrain's Interior Ministry announced sirens were blaring while urging residents to remain calm and find shelter.
- The U.S. Embassy in Manama issued a warning that it "has information to suggest that Iran may seek to target unspecified locations in central Manama."

The U.S. military struck Iran for a ninth consecutive night on Sunday, targeting command centers, maritime capabilities and air defense sites across the country, as the conflict over the Strait of Hormuz showed no sign of abating.

U.S. Central Command said in a statement that it completed its latest wave of attacks at 10 p.m. EDT Sunday. The strikes targeted Iranian military command centers, maritime capabilities and communications networks, as well as missile and drone launch sites and air defense and coastal surveillance sites, CENTCOM said.

"The U.S. military is holding Iran accountable at the Commander in Chief's direction," CENTCOM said.

Explosions were reported in Bandar-e Mahshahr and Bandar Imam Khomeini, in Khuzestan province, by state-run Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting.

As CENTCOM announced the start of the attacks, the UK Maritime Trade Operations Center said an unspecified vessel was on fire about 8 nautical miles northwest of Kumzar, Oman, in the Strait of Hormuz. The cause was not immediately known. The UKMTO said it had been informed of the fire by military authorities.

The conflict has also drawn in neighboring Gulf states. The Kuwait Army said its air defenses were confronting Iranian drones. Bahrain's Ministry of the Interior announced sirens were blaring while urging residents to remain calm and find shelter.

The U.S. Embassy in Bahrain's capital of Manama issued a statement early Monday local time stating it "has information to suggest that Iran may seek to target unspecified locations in central Manama."

"We urge all Americans to remain vigilant, follow local authorities' instructions and review the latest guidance from the U.S. Embassy," the embassy said.

The ninth night of strikes came after the U.S. military earlier Sunday announced the death of a U.S. service member, the third to be killed since fighting resumed nearly two weeks ago and the 17th killed since the war began late February.

The broader conflict, which began in late February with the United States seeking to eliminate what it described as Iran's nuclear weapons ambitions and encourage Iranians to overthrow their government, has shifted almost entirely to the question of control over the Strait of Hormuz, a vital energy transportation route.

The United States says it is fighting to restore the prewar freedom of navigation through the chokepoint, while Iran claims authority to determine which vessels may transit the waterway. The two sides agreed to a fragile cease-fire in April and later signed an agreement intended to lay the groundwork for ending the war, but remained divided over its terms. Iran claimed that the agreement recognized its control over the strait, while the United States maintained that it required a return to the prewar status quo. The cease-fire collapsed early this month after three vessels transiting the strait were attacked, according to CENTCOM.
