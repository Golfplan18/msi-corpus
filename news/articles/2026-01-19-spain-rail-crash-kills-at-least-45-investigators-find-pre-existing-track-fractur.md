A high-speed train derailment near Adamuz, in the province of Cordoba, killed at least 45 people and injured more than 150 on Sunday evening, when the rear of a Málaga-to-Madrid passenger service jumped the tracks and slammed into an oncoming train, according to Spain's national rail operator Adif. Spain's rail accident investigating body, CIAF, said Friday that a fracture in the rail appeared to have been present before the crash, and that investigators found uniform notches on the wheel treads of the derailed train consistent with contact with a broken rail.

The collision was the deadliest on Spain's high-speed rail network since the network opened its first line in 1992. Two more crashes struck Spain's rail system later in the week — one fatal, near Barcelona — renewing scrutiny of the country's railway infrastructure and prompting a railway drivers' union to threaten a general strike.

Spain operates Europe's largest high-speed rail network, with more than 3,900 kilometers of track, but the week's three crashes exposed safety questions on both high-speed and commuter lines, as investigators said the cause of Sunday's derailment remained under investigation.

Spain's national rail operator Adif said the Málaga-to-Madrid train, carrying 289 passengers, went off the rails at 7:45 p.m. Sunday near Adamuz, a town in Cordoba province about 370 kilometers south of Madrid. Its rear section slammed into an oncoming Madrid-to-Huelva service carrying approximately 200 people, killing at least 45 and injuring more than 150.

Transport Minister Óscar Puente said the head of the second train bore the brunt of the impact, knocking its first two carriages off the track and sending them down a four-meter slope.

"The impact was so incredibly violent that we have found bodies hundreds of meters away," said Juan Manuel Moreno, president of the Andalusia regional government. Authorities said they had identified almost all victims from the Sunday crash.

## Investigation: fractured rail

Spain's rail accident investigating body CIAF said Friday that a fracture in the rail appeared to have been present before the derailment. In a preliminary report, investigators found uniform notches on several wheel treads on the right-hand side of the train that jumped the track.

"These nicks in the wheels and the deformation observed in the rail are consistent with the rail being fractured," the CIAF report said. What caused the apparent fracture remained unknown, investigators said, and they did not rule out any possibilities.

Álvaro Fernández, president of national railway company Renfe, said both trains were traveling well under the speed limit of 250 kilometers per hour and that "human error could be ruled out." The derailed train belonged to private operator Iryo, which said in a statement that the train was manufactured in 2022 and had passed its latest safety check on Jan. 15. The second train, which sustained heavier damage, belonged to Renfe.

## Two additional crashes

On Tuesday night, a retaining wall fell onto the tracks on a commuter line near Barcelona, causing a train crash that killed one person and injured at least 37, Spanish regional authorities said.

On Thursday, a commuter train in the southeastern Murcia region collided with a crane. Emergency services said six people with minor injuries were transferred to a hospital.

## Safety concerns

The Spanish Union of Railway Drivers sent a letter last August to Spain's rail operator requesting an investigation into flaws on high-speed lines across the country caused by increased traffic. The letter warned of potholes, bumps, imbalances in overhead power lines, and frequent breakdowns, according to a copy seen by the Associated Press. The union said it would call a general strike in the coming weeks to demand more safety assurances.

## Spain's rail network

Spain operates Europe's largest high-speed rail network for trains traveling more than 250 kilometers per hour, with more than 3,900 kilometers of track, according to the International Union of Railways. Officials said Sunday's collision was the first with fatalities on the high-speed network since Spain opened its first such line in 1992.

The crash was the deadliest in Spain since 2013, when a commuter train in the northern Galicia region derailed on a bend while traveling too fast, killing 80 people.
