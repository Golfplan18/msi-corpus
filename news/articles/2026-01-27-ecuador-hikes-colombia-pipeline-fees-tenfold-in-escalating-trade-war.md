Ecuador announced a tenfold increase in transportation fees for Colombian oil on Monday, raising the rate from $3 to $30 per barrel, in the latest escalation of a trade war between the two Andean neighbors. Colombia's Energy Minister Edwin Palma called the move "a new aggression against the people," as disputes over drug trafficking and commerce intensify between the nations.

Ecuador raised pipeline fees for Colombian oil tenfold on Monday in the latest escalation of a trade war between the neighboring Andean nations.

## The Pipeline Fee Surge

Ecuador announced it would charge $30 per barrel for Colombian companies using its oil pipelines, up from $3 — a move that will raise costs for Colombian oil exporters including the state-run Ecopetrol, which uses the pipeline to transport more than 12,000 barrels daily to Pacific ports.

"This is a new aggression against the people," said Colombian Energy Minister Edwin Palma.

The move caps a week of escalating trade actions between the nations. Colombia had suspended electricity sales to Ecuador over the preceding weekend—a significant blow to a nation heavily reliant on hydroelectric power—in response to Ecuador's earlier announcement of new tariffs on Colombian imports.

## Escalating Tariffs and Competing Claims

The dispute began last Thursday when Ecuador's President Daniel Noboa announced a 30% tariff on Colombian imports starting in February. Noboa, who has sought closer ties with the Trump administration, framed the tariffs as a "security tax," saying Colombia was not taking sufficient action to fight drug cartels along their shared border that send cocaine to Ecuador.

Colombian officials disputed the characterization. President Gustavo Petro's administration has achieved record cocaine seizures. Yet cocaine production continues at all-time highs, driven by increased lab efficiency and expanded cultivation.

Colombia responded by imposing its own 30% tariffs on many Ecuadorian imports—rice, sugar, and car tires among them—with officials suggesting additional tariffs on other goods remain possible.

Trade between the nations totaled approximately $2.3 billion in 2025, with Colombia accounting for roughly $1.7 billion of that. Colombia's Foreign Affairs Ministry said it would seek meetings with Ecuadorian counterparts this week to ease tensions and reverse the tariffs. "Colombia prioritizes dialogue and solutions that avoid impacting communities and businesses," the ministry said in a statement.

## Ecuador's Crisis of Violence

Ecuador has faced mounting security challenges. The nation's Interior Ministry reported a homicide rate of 50 murders per 100,000 residents in 2025, the highest in recent history. The rate has quintupled since 2020 as drug gangs from Mexico, Colombia, and elsewhere fight for control of Ecuador's ports. The once-peaceful country, home to the Galápagos Islands, has become a major transit point for cocaine produced in Colombia and Peru.
