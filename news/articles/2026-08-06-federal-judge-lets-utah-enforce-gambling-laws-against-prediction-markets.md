## Federal judge lets Utah enforce gambling laws against prediction markets

**Subtype:** fact

- A federal judge allows Utah to enforce its anti-gambling laws against prediction markets.
- Kalshi challenges Utah's gambling restrictions in a federal lawsuit filed in February.
- Utah bans proposition betting on sports under its current state law.
- State and federal courts reach different outcomes in cases involving prediction-market restrictions.

## Prediction-market rules remain split across state and federal courts

The Utah ruling came in a lawsuit brought by Kalshi, which asked a federal judge to prevent the state from enforcing its gambling restrictions against the platform. Kalshi filed the case in February as Utah approached legislation intended to limit prediction markets' ability to do business there.

Utah's state law now bans proposition betting in sports. Those wagers cover particular events within a game rather than the final outcome, and they represent a significant source of revenue for leading prediction markets.

The decision places Utah among states seeking to apply gambling laws to prediction markets. The platforms and government authorities have disputed whether the markets belong primarily under financial regulation or gambling regulation.

The broader legal landscape remains divided. Courts in some states have blocked restrictions on prediction markets, while courts in others have allowed them, according to the Associated Press report on the ruling.

The dispute involves both state governments and the federal government, which are contesting who should regulate the markets. The Utah decision gives states seeking to restrict the platforms a ruling supporting enforcement of state anti-gambling laws.

Kalshi and Polymarket are among the prediction markets affected by the regulatory fight. The Utah case addresses whether Utah may enforce its restrictions against Kalshi as the state applies its law to proposition betting on sports.
