The average U.S. 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 6.09% this week from 6.06% last week, according to Freddie Mac. The increase marks the latest shift in borrowing costs that have hovered near three-year lows despite recent upward pressure from bond markets reacting to geopolitical tensions and turbulence in Japan's financial sector.

The rate movement underscores the continued strain on housing affordability. Even modest increases affect purchasing power for homebuyers already struggling with elevated prices and limited inventory, though recent signs of refinancing activity suggest some borrowers are seeking to lock in lower rates before costs climb further.

Mortgage rates ticked higher this week as bond markets reacted to geopolitical tensions and economic uncertainty, though borrowing costs remain well below levels from a year ago.

## Current Rate Movements

The 30-year fixed mortgage rate rose to 6.09% from 6.06% the previous week, according to Freddie Mac. The 15-year rate climbed to 5.44% from 5.38%. Both remain near three-year lows — a year earlier, the 30-year averaged 6.96% and the 15-year was at 6.16%.

The moves follow a jump in the 10-year Treasury yield, which mortgage lenders use as a benchmark. The 10-year yield reached 4.26% at midday Thursday, up from 4.17% a week prior. Mortgage rates are influenced by the Federal Reserve's interest-rate policy and bond-market investors' expectations for economic growth and inflation. This week, the bond market's reaction to tariff threats from the Trump administration, pressure over Greenland, and turbulence in Japan's markets pushed yields higher.

## The Housing Affordability Bind

Even as rates climb, the U.S. housing market remains locked in a sales slump dating to 2022. The combination of elevated mortgage rates, years of soaring home prices, and a chronic shortage of homes for sale has left many prospective homebuyers priced out of the market. Sales of existing homes remained stuck last year at 30-year lows.

Some stabilization has emerged. December home sales jumped 5.1% from the previous month. Mortgage refinancing applications surged 20% in the most recent week and accounted for nearly 62% of all home-loan applications, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association. Purchase-loan applications rose 5%.

The refinancing surge reflects homeowners' efforts to lock in lower rates while they remain available. Nearly 69% of U.S. homes with an outstanding mortgage carry a fixed rate of 5% or lower, and slightly more than half have rates at or below 4%. Borrowers with these locked-in rates face a dramatic gap if they sell: refinancing at today's higher rates is economically punitive.

## Rate Expectations

Economists generally expect mortgage rates to ease further this year, though most recent forecasts show the 30-year rate remaining above 6% — roughly double what prevailed six years ago. For homeowners accustomed to pandemic-era and post-pandemic refinancing opportunities, a return to sustained rates below 5% appears unlikely without a significant shift in inflation or Federal Reserve policy.
