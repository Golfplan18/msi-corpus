The announcement came as key elements of the post-ceasefire agreement remained largely unresolved three months after fighting stopped, with the international oversight body known as the Board of Peace still lacking publicly named members, negotiations on a second phase yet to begin in earnest, and Israeli gunfire continuing to kill Palestinians in Gaza.

CAIRO — Hamas said Sunday it will dissolve its existing government in Gaza once a Palestinian technocratic leadership committee assumes control of the territory, as required under the U.S.-brokered peace plan, but the group gave no timeline for when the transition would occur.

Neither Hamas nor the Palestinian Authority, the Palestinians' internationally recognized representative body, had announced the names of the technocrats, who are required to have no political affiliations. It remained unclear whether candidates would be cleared by Israel and the United States.

Hamas spokesperson Hazem Kassem called for speeding up the committee's formation in comments posted to his Telegram channel Sunday.

## Governance framework unresolved

The ceasefire, which took effect Oct. 10, established an international oversight body called the Board of Peace to monitor Gaza's governance transition. The board is also tasked with handling the disarmament of Hamas, the deployment of an international security force, additional Israeli troop withdrawals and Gaza's reconstruction. Its members had not been announced as of Sunday, though they were expected to be named that week.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Thursday that Bulgarian diplomat Nickolay Mladenov had been selected as the board's director-general. Mladenov is a former Bulgarian defense and foreign minister who served as U.N. envoy to Iraq and as the U.N. Mideast peace envoy from 2015 to 2020, a period during which he worked to ease Israel-Hamas tensions.

An Egyptian official, speaking on condition of anonymity to discuss closed-door information, said Hamas would meet with other Palestinian factions this week to finalize the technocratic committee's formation. The official also said Hamas was sending a delegation to talks with Egyptian, Qatari and Turkish officials about moving to the ceasefire's second phase, with top Hamas negotiator Khalil al-Hayya chairing the delegation.

## Post-ceasefire violence continues

Israeli gunfire killed three Palestinians in Gaza on Sunday, according to Palestinian hospital officials. Two men were shot dead in the southern town of Bani Suhaila, according to Nasser Hospital, which received the bodies. A third was killed in the Tuffah neighborhood of Gaza City, according to Al-Ahly hospital.

In response to questions about the Tuffah incident, Israel's military said it had fired at a person it described as a "terrorist" in northern Gaza who had approached troops. In a separate statement, the military said it had killed a person it described as a "terrorist" in southern Gaza who approached troops.

Israel and Hamas have accused each other of violating the ceasefire. Continued Israeli strikes in Gaza have killed more than 400 Palestinians since the ceasefire began, according to local health officials. Israel's military has said any actions since the ceasefire took effect were in response to violations of the agreement.

The ceasefire began with a halt in fighting and the release of hostages held in Gaza in exchange for thousands of Palestinians held by Israel. Efforts were continuing Sunday to recover the remains of the final hostage left in Gaza.

## Japan signals interest in ceasefire role

Israel's Foreign Minister Gideon Saar met in Jerusalem on Sunday with Japanese Foreign Minister Toshimitsu Motegi. Saar said Israel was committed to enforcing Trump's plan. Motegi expressed Japan's willingness to play an active role in the ceasefire, according to Japan's Foreign Ministry, which said he also visited the Civil-Military Coordination Center where the ceasefire is monitored. Motegi was scheduled to meet Netanyahu and Palestinian officials in the Israeli-occupied West Bank.

## Netanyahu aide questioned in classified-leak probe

Israeli police said Sunday they were questioning a top official from Netanyahu's office over possible obstruction of an investigation into last year's leak of classified military information to German tabloid Bild.

Israeli media identified the official as Tzachi Braverman, Netanyahu's chief of staff, who is expected to become Israel's next ambassador to the United Kingdom.

Braverman is the latest official drawn into the scandal, in which Netanyahu's inner circle is accused of leaking confidential information to the tabloid to improve public perception of the prime minister following the killing of six hostages in Gaza in 2024.

Former Netanyahu spokesperson Eli Feldstein, who has been indicted in connection with the leak, described in a television interview a clandestine meeting with Braverman in an underground parking lot. Feldstein alleged that Braverman offered to "shut down" the probe into the leaked information.

Opposition leader Yair Lapid called for Braverman's removal from the ambassadorial appointment. "It is unacceptable that a person suspected of involvement in obstructing a serious security investigation should be the face of Israel in one of Europe's most important countries," Lapid wrote on X.

Foreign Minister Saar defended Braverman's appointment and said he would not be removed from it until formally charged or tried.
