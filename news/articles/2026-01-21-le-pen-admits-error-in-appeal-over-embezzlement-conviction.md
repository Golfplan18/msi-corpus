Marine Le Pen, the 57-year-old leader of France's far-right National Rally, acknowledged Wednesday that she made "an error" while defending herself in a Paris courtroom against embezzlement charges. Le Pen is seeking to overturn a March 2025 conviction that found her guilty of misappropriating €2.9 million from the European Union Parliament over a 12-year period. She denied being at the center of what prosecutors had described as a "fraudulent system," but acknowledged that some people paid as EU parliamentary assistants performed work for her party instead.

The appeal's outcome could reshape French politics before the 2027 presidential election. If the March 2025 conviction stands, Le Pen will face a five-year disqualification from holding office, eliminating her from the race. That prospect has already prompted her to designate a 30-year-old successor, Jordan Bardella, as the party's potential presidential candidate.

## The hearing

Marine Le Pen took the stand Wednesday in a Paris courtroom to defend herself against the embezzlement conviction that threatens her political future. The 57-year-old leader of France's far-right National Rally was appearing at an appeal hearing that began the previous week and is scheduled to conclude over five weeks, with a ruling expected before summer.

The trial centers on allegations that Le Pen and her party misappropriated €2.9 million—roughly $3.4 million—in funds meant for EU parliamentary staff. A Paris court in March 2025 found Le Pen guilty of diverting those funds from the European Parliament between 2004 and 2016, a period during which she served as a member of the EU parliament and, from 2011 onward, as her party's president.

During Wednesday's questioning, Le Pen acknowledged that the allocation of some staff members had been problematic. "The error lies here," she said. "Certainly there were some assistants, case by case, who should have worked in a marginal way, more substantially, or completely for the benefit of the party."

She maintained, however, that no deliberate fraud had occurred. "I would not say we did everything perfectly," Le Pen said. "We can be criticized on some aspects. But we acted with complete good faith."

## The charges and the stakes

The March 2025 conviction imposed serious penalties: a five-year ban from holding elected office, two years of house arrest with electronic monitoring, and a suspended two-year prison sentence. The Paris court found that Le Pen stood "at the heart of a fraudulent system" and characterized the misappropriation as a "shortcut to democracy" that unfairly disadvantaged her political competitors.

If the conviction survives the appeal, Le Pen will be barred from running in the 2027 presidential election. She had been widely expected to be among the leading contenders in that race. The disqualification would reshape French politics, leaving space for a new far-right candidate to emerge.

In anticipation of that possibility, Le Pen has already designated a 30-year-old protégé, Jordan Bardella, as a potential successor for the party's presidential candidacy. Bardella currently serves in a senior party role and has been groomed as the far-right movement's next standard-bearer.

## The defense

On the stand, Le Pen acknowledged specific instances in which the line between EU parliamentary duties and party work had blurred. She admitted that her personal assistant, who held a contract as an EU parliamentary assistant, "may have purchased some plane tickets for me as president of the party." She insisted, however, that these were isolated cases, not evidence of a systematic scheme.

Judge Michèle Agi, the presiding jurist, pressed Le Pen on her role in approving the contracts. "You are a lawyer; you know the law," Agi noted. "Inevitably, a signature, a contract are notions that have meaning for you."

Agi observed that Le Pen, as a member of the European Parliament since 2004 and party president since 2011, had been in a position to approve the hiring decisions that had led to the charges against her.

Le Pen responded by placing blame elsewhere. She argued that the European Union Parliament should have warned her party at the time that the employment practices in question violated EU rules. "There are not many MEPs who need protection," she said, referring to her security requirements. "One can also think that the European Parliament takes that exceptional situation into account. I believe we committed no irregularities."

## What happens next

The five-week appeal hearing will determine whether Le Pen's 2027 presidential prospects survive intact or whether Bardella emerges as the far-right movement's leading candidate. The court's timeline calls for a ruling before the summer of 2026, leaving more than a year and a half before the presidential election itself.

At stake is not only Le Pen's political career but also the composition of France's political landscape heading into one of its most consequential elections in recent years. The conviction and the appeal have already begun reshaping far-right movement dynamics, with Bardella positioning himself as a generational successor and potentially more acceptable alternative to Le Pen in the eyes of some voters.

The appeal continues, with the outcome likely to echo across France's political landscape.
