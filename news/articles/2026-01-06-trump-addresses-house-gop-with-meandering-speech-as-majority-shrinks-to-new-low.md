Republicans enter 2026 with a shrinking House margin, an imminent vote on expired health insurance subsidies, a looming potential government shutdown, and a caucus increasingly willing to break from leadership — a difficult combination as they seek to defend their majority in November.

WASHINGTON — President Donald Trump delivered a nearly 90-minute address to House Republicans at the Kennedy Center on Tuesday, spending more time relitigating past grievances than laying out a new electoral strategy, even as the party's razor-thin House majority narrowed further on one of the most consequential days of the new year.

The session was meant to unify the White House and House Republicans ahead of November midterms that will determine control of Congress and the shape of Trump's final two years in office. Instead, Trump meandered from defending his conduct around the Jan. 6, 2021, Capitol riot to joking about winning over transgender voters to an extended riff on the first lady's comments about Franklin D. Roosevelt.

"They say that when you win the presidency, you lose the midterms," Trump told the assembled lawmakers. "I wish you could explain to me what the hell is going on with the mind of the public."

The president warned that losing the House would invite retaliation: if Democrats regain control of Congress, "they'll find a way to impeach me," he said. He repeatedly recounted his 2024 campaign performance — "We won every swing state. We won the popular vote by millions. We won everything" — and claimed his administration had delivered "the most successful first year of any president in history."

He also mused about unconstitutionally seeking a third presidential term and claimed it was never reported that he urged supporters to walk "peacefully and patriotically" on Jan. 6, 2021, before the Capitol riot.

**A majority that keeps shrinking**

As lawmakers traveled to the policy forum, they learned of the sudden death of California Rep. Doug LaMalfa. The resignation of former Georgia Rep. Marjorie Taylor Greene took effect at midnight. Rep. Jim Baird, R-Ind., is recovering from what Trump described as a "bad" car accident, further reducing available votes for Speaker Mike Johnson.

Trump acknowledged the bind directly: "You can't be tough when you have a majority of three, and now, sadly, a little bit less than that."

Despite the precarious arithmetic, Trump offered no public guidance on how Republicans should handle this week's expected vote on extending health insurance subsidies that expired at year's end, or on averting a potential government shutdown just weeks away.

**Health care and the Hyde Amendment**

On health care — a persistent vulnerability for the party — Trump urged Republicans to take ownership of the issue and to show flexibility. He specifically addressed the Hyde Amendment, the decades-old federal policy barring the use of federal funds for abortion services, telling lawmakers: "You have to be a little flexible. You gotta work something. You gotta use ingenuity."

Trump also briefly touched on foreign policy, calling the capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro "brilliant, tactically," and mentioned revenue flowing into the U.S. through tariffs and direct investment, and ongoing negotiations over drug prices.

"You have so many good nuggets. You have to use them. If you can sell them, we're going to win," Trump told Republicans.

**The Kennedy Center backdrop**

The forum was held at the Kennedy Center, where a board stocked with Trump loyalists recently voted to rename the performing arts venue the Trump Kennedy Center. That renaming is being challenged in court.

House Republicans convened the policy retreat as several fault lines deepen within the caucus. Some members have grown more willing to cross party lines and join Democratic proposals, bypassing Johnson on issues including the release of Jeffrey Epstein files. Rising health care costs and Trump's foreign policy pursuits are creating additional divisions heading into a midterm cycle that will test whether the party can hold ground with a majority that has rarely been thinner.
