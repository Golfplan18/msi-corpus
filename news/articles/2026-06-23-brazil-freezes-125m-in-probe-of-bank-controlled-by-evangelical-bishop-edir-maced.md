Brazil's Federal Police launched an operation Tuesday targeting an alleged financial fraud scheme at Banco Digimais, a digital bank controlled by evangelical bishop Edir Macedo, and secured a federal court order freezing assets equivalent to about $125 million.

More than 50 federal agents executed nine search-and-seizure warrants authorized by a federal court in São Paulo as part of what authorities have named Operation Mirage, the Federal Police said. The court also authorized investigators to lift banking and tax secrecy protections for people under investigation.

According to the Federal Police, the investigation is based on reports from the Central Bank of Brazil, the country's main financial regulator, which identified what authorities described as serious irregularities in the bank's management.

Investigators suspect that bank executives manipulated financial statements and regulatory records to conceal the institution's true financial condition, projecting an appearance of solvency to supervisors and facilitating what they allege were irregular transactions, according to the Federal Police. Authorities said the scheme involved the systematic manipulation of balance sheets and accounting results to conceal losses, inflate assets, and generate artificial revenue worth hundreds of millions of Brazilian reais.

BBC News Brasil reported that investigators suspect investment funds were used as part of the scheme. According to ICL Notícias, part of the scheme involved transferring assets into funds managed by companies linked to the financial group associated with Digimais, with investigators contending that assets may have been systematically overvalued, artificial revenue generated, and the institution's financial deterioration concealed.

ICL Notícias also reported that the investigation is examining an increase in issuance of bank deposit certificates, known as CDBs, which Brazilian banks use to raise funds from investors. According to investigators, Digimais offered returns above 110% of the Interbank Deposit Certificate, or CDI, one of the main benchmark rates in Brazil's financial market.

The Federal Police also are investigating alleged irregular financial transactions that may have benefited the bank's parent company, as well as the possible insertion of false or manipulated information into official systems used by regulatory agencies.

Those under investigation could face charges including fraudulent management, the insertion of false data into financial statements, and the execution of prohibited credit operations under Brazilian legislation governing crimes against the National Financial System, according to the Federal Police.

BBC News Brasil reported that targets of the search warrants included Digimais executives, among them Bishop João Urbaneja and his son Thiago Urbaneja, as well as people linked to fund management companies associated with the institution.

According to ICL Notícias, Macedo is among those under investigation because of his status as the bank's controlling shareholder. However, news reports did not indicate that he was a target of Tuesday's searches. BBC reported that the religious leader was not included in the warrants because he currently resides outside Brazil.

Banco Digimais was founded in 1981 under the name Banco Renner in the state of Rio Grande do Sul, according to the Federal Police. The institution was restructured as a digital bank in 2020, adopted the Digimais name, and became wholly owned by Macedo.

Macedo, 81, is the founder of the Universal Church of the Kingdom of God, one of Brazil's largest and most influential evangelical denominations, and owner of the Record media conglomerate, one of the country's largest media groups.

The investigation comes two months after investment bank BTG Pactual announced the signing of an agreement to acquire Digimais, according to BBC News Brasil.
