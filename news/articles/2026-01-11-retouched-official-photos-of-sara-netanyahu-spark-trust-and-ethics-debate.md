JERUSALEM — Israeli government press releases distributed heavily retouched photographs of Sara Netanyahu, wife of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, through official state channels, veteran political journalist Shabi Gatenio revealed in The Seventh Eye. Experts who analyzed the images said they bore local digital manipulations to smooth her skin and remove wrinkles. The disclosure has ignited a debate over trust in official government photography and the integrity of Israel's permanent historical archive.

Critics say the issue is not the use of photo-editing software, which is common on the personal social media accounts of celebrities and public figures, but its circulation in official government announcements — material that may eventually enter Israel's state archive as an authentic record of events.

"All the pictures to this day in the archives in Israel are authentic pictures of reality as it was captured by the lenses of photographers' cameras since the establishment of the state," Gatenio said. "These images, if entered into the database, will forever infect it with a virtual reality that never existed."

## How the manipulation came to light

Gatenio said he first noticed the retouching in July 2025, when the Netanyahus visited President Donald Trump in Washington, D.C., and again in September 2025, when Sara Netanyahu joined her husband on the tarmac ahead of a trip to New York for the United Nations General Assembly. At that point, the prime minister's office released a photo credited to Avi Ohayon, an official government photographer.

Hany Farid, a digital forensics expert at the University of California, Berkeley, compared that photograph to raw video of the same event and said the image had been post-processed, bearing local manipulations to smooth Sara Netanyahu's skin and remove wrinkles. Farid said photos showing Sara Netanyahu meeting with Vice President JD Vance and his wife, Usha, in Washington also appeared to have been retouched.

"There's been some Photoshop editing to — let's call it — 'beautify,' lighten, smooth the face," Farid said. "Is it nefarious? No. Is it a problem? Yes. This is about something bigger than, 'she Photoshopped her face to make herself look younger.' This is about trust. Why should I trust any official photo coming out of that administration?"

Sara Netanyahu, 67, has long used photo-editing software on her personal social media account, which contains images in which her face appears heavily retouched. Her personal spokesperson did not respond to a request for comment from the Associated Press. Mrs. Netanyahu does not hold an official government position.

## Government response

Since the manipulation was revealed, the government has taken what Gatenio described as an unprecedented step, adding Sara Netanyahu's name to press releases that include manipulated images. Since November 2025, press releases showing photos of her alongside Trump, at a Miami synagogue, and at a funeral for an Israeli mayor have carried this label.

It remains unclear whether Israel's official archive will include her photographs from the second half of 2025, when the editing appears to have begun, according to Gatenio.

Nitzan Chen, director of the Government Press Office, told the Associated Press that images of the prime minister are never manipulated and that his office would not upload any retouched photos to the official archive. Chen said office lawyers are trying to determine how to handle and properly identify photos "processed by people other than GPO photographers." He added that the Justice Ministry is also examining the "criteria, limitations and possibilities" of edited images, but stressed there is nothing illegal about touching up photos. The issue, he said, is being transparent when such changes are made.

The Times of Israel said it will no longer carry official state photos that appear to have been manipulated. The Associated Press does not publish images that appear to have been retouched or digitally manipulated.

## AI-generated imagery on official accounts

The controversy extends beyond retouching. Netanyahu's official Instagram account has posted content that appears to be AI-edited or generated, including a New Year's photograph of the Netanyahus with Trump and Melania Trump in Washington. The image showed Sara Netanyahu wearing a black dress absent from other photographs of the event, where she wore a dark red dress. Farid said fireworks and American and Israeli flags appearing in the sky above the two couples in that photograph were "almost certainly" generated by AI. Instagram has since tagged the photo as possibly altered or generated using AI; it was not clear when the tag was added or by whom.

Netanyahu's official Instagram has also posted video of Trump and Netanyahu aboard a B-2 bomber that appears entirely AI-generated, captioned "on our victory lap," a reference to joint Israel-U.S. attacks on Iran.

Tehilla Shwartz Altshuler, who runs the Democracy in the Digital Age Program at the Israel Democracy Institute, a Jerusalem think tank, called the use of AI-manipulated imagery "part of the populist playbook" and said there was "no question" that Netanyahu was emulating how Trump uses the technology.

"This is exactly what Netanyahu and his surrounding circle have tried to do for many years," Shwartz Altshuler said. "Presenting himself as a superhero, his wife as a supermodel, their family as a super loyal family. Even when it wasn't the case, even at the expense of actual political work, administrative work and social work."

She said Israel has reached a critical point in official government record-keeping and communications.

"The question of archiving the truth, archiving history, will be one of the questions of our time," she said.
