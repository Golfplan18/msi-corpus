BOGOTA, Colombia — Colombia announced a 30% tariff on 20 Ecuadorian products and suspended electricity exports to its neighbor indefinitely on Thursday, escalating a trade dispute that erupted this week after Ecuadorian President Daniel Noboa imposed matching tariffs citing a more than $852 million trade deficit with Colombia.

The tit-for-tat measures put roughly $250 million in bilateral trade at immediate risk and prompted warnings from business leaders of economic fallout and potential job losses on both sides of the shared border.

## The escalating dispute

The trade row began Wednesday when Noboa announced tariffs against Colombian goods, citing Ecuador's widening trade imbalance with its neighbor. The Ecuadorian Federation of Exporters reported the deficit with Colombia reached $852 million from January to November 2025. Earlier data from Ecuador's ministry of production showed that in the same period of 2024, Ecuador exported $760 million in goods to Colombia while importing $1.8 billion — a structural gap Noboa said had driven his government to act.

Noboa also cited what his government described as a lack of reciprocity in securing their shared border, which, according to the AP report, remains a stronghold for criminal organizations and international drug trafficking.

The Colombian government expressed surprise at Ecuador's unilateral action, noting that bilateral cooperation remained active through established binational mechanisms including joint military and anti-narcotics operations.

## Colombia's response

Colombia's 30% tariff covers 20 unspecified Ecuadorian products. Authorities noted "the possibility of extending the measure to a broader group." Colombia's ministry of commerce said the targeted goods represent approximately $250 million in exports.

Commerce Minister Diana Marcela Morales described the tariffs as temporary and said Colombia remained open to a diplomatic and negotiated resolution.

## Business alarm

Business leaders on both sides of the border warned of immediate damage if the governments failed to resolve the standoff.

"Since yesterday, member companies have been contacting us in a state of high concern," said Oliva Diazgranados, executive director of the Bogotá-based Colombian-Ecuadorian Chamber of Commerce. "This situation directly impacts corporate development and sales projections; it may even threaten jobs."

Diazgranados said that while businesses were bearing the brunt of the tension, the root of the conflict lay with security agencies, not trade.

## The energy dimension

Colombia's ministry of mines and energy framed its electricity suspension as a "preventive measure" essential to safeguarding Colombia's energy sovereignty and security.

Energy Minister Edwin Palma condemned Ecuador's tariffs as "economic aggression" that undermines regional integration. Palma noted the context of the suspension: during Ecuador's severe energy crisis in late 2024, Colombia consistently provided roughly 90% of its exportable power — approximately 450 megawatts — to stabilize its neighbor's grid.
