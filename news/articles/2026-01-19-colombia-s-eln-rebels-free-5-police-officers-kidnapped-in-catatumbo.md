BOGOTÁ, Colombia — Colombia's largest remaining rebel group released five police officers on Monday that it had seized on Jan. 6 while they were off duty and traveling by bus in the northeastern Catatumbo region, according to the country's national agency for the defense of human rights.

The National Liberation Army, or ELN, shared photos on social media of fighters in camouflage releasing the officers. "We call on armed groups to immediately free all of those who have been deprived of their liberty," the Human Rights Defender's Office said on the social platform X. The officers were reported to be in good health.

The release comes as the ELN pushes for a "national accord" to address political conflicts, even as formal peace talks with the Colombian government remain suspended. Negotiations broke down after the ELN launched an offensive in the Catatumbo region that forced more than 50,000 people to flee their homes.

## Peace conditions

Last week the group said in a statement that after elections in Colombia this year, it would like to work with the nation's new government to design agreements aimed at defeating poverty, protecting ecosystems, and "overcoming" the drug trade in rural areas.

Colombian President Gustavo Petro replied that the group's leadership must give up drug trafficking and recruiting minors before peace talks can resume.

## Background

The ELN, founded in the early 1960s, has an estimated 5,000 fighters operating in Colombia and Venezuela. The U.S. State Department designated the group a foreign terrorist organization in 1997. The group has been accused of profiting from the drug trade and of blowing up oil pipelines in eastern Colombia.

## U.S. dimension

The presidents of Colombia and the United States recently discussed the possibility of joint actions against the ELN in a phone call, according to Colombia's Interior Minister Armando Benedetti, who said the conversation helped ease tensions between the two countries. The ELN said it is willing to unite with other rebel groups in Colombia to fend off potential attacks from the United States.
