Republican Rep. Doug LaMalfa of California died Tuesday at age 65, the Butte County sheriff’s office said. The sheriff’s office said LaMalfa had a medical emergency Monday night and was taken to a local hospital, where he died during a surgical procedure during the event.

Officials have not disclosed the cause of LaMalfa’s death.

LaMalfa was a California rice farmer who served seven terms in the U.S. House. His death trims Republicans’ narrow margin of control of the House to 218 seats to Democrats’ 213.

President Donald Trump said he was saddened by LaMalfa’s death in remarks to a Tuesday meeting of House Republicans. Trump said “tremendous sorrow,” and lamented what he described as the loss of a lawmaker he called an ally for his agenda. He said LaMalfa “wasn’t a 3 o’clock in the morning person” like other lawmakers he would call to lobby for votes, and added: “He voted with me 100% of the time,” and “With Doug, I never had to call.”

LaMalfa was first elected to Congress in 2012 after serving in the state Legislature, according to the report. He represented California’s 1st District, described as covering a broad swath of rural North California spanning from the Oregon border down to just north of Sacramento.

Colleagues and staff described LaMalfa as a regular presence in the House chamber, helping GOP leadership open the chamber and offering views on local and national affairs. The report said he served on the House Agriculture Committee and was chairman of a subcommittee with jurisdiction over forestry issues, and that he also served on committees dealing with transportation and natural resource issues.

LaMalfa had planned to run for reelection even after his district was redrawn under a ballot measure passed by California voters in November. The report said the measure was backed by Democrats and was designed to make it harder for LaMalfa and four other Republicans to win reelection. California Gov. Gavin Newsom’s office said Newsom must call a special election to replace LaMalfa, and that the election could happen as late as June, when California will hold its primary for the 2026 midterm.

The report said LaMalfa’s colleagues, staff members and friends emphasized his focus on rural issues and wildfire prevention. David Reade, identified as a former chief of staff for LaMalfa when he was in the state Legislature, recalled that LaMalfa would drive “literally hundreds and hundreds of miles to be there” even for small events.

LaMalfa’s current chief of staff, Mark Spannagel, said LaMalfa was “deeply funny” and “probably one of the most normal people in Congress, down to earth.” Spannagel said LaMalfa was obsessed with cars, often showing up at events with grease under his fingernails, and that he loved classic rock and the “Austin Powers” movies. In a phone interview, Spannagel also said: “You want to sit there and have an iced tea with him,” and described how LaMalfa advocated for a constituent by traveling to multiple Veterans Affairs offices in Washington while also hosting town halls and other events in small towns.

The report said Spannagel and others described LaMalfa’s approach as prioritizing constituents beyond the biggest cities. It quoted Spannagel recalling LaMalfa’s attitude as: “Just because, ‘Why not? We’re gonna go do them. We’re gonna be there. Let those people be heard, too,’” and adding that he emphasized being present in a wide range of communities.

State Assembly member James Gallagher said LaMalfa cared about wildfire mitigation and protecting victims and called him his “big brother.” The report said LaMalfa passed legislation in 2024 to exempt wildfire relief payments from federal income taxes, after parts of his district were ravaged by what the report described as California’s deadliest wildfire in state history in 2018. The report said President Joe Biden signed the law.

The report said LaMalfa also called for increased water storage and for more forest management to reduce wildfire threat. It also noted that some voters had become frustrated in recent years with LaMalfa’s unwavering support for Trump, including with yelling and booing at town halls last year.

National Republican Congressional Committee Chairman Richard Hudson called LaMalfa “a principled conservative and a tireless advocate for the people of Northern California,” saying he “was never afraid to fight for rural communities, farmers, and working families.” Hudson added that “Doug brought grit, authenticity, and conviction to everything he did in public service.” C-SPAN, the report said, compiled that LaMalfa gave remarks for the record on 81 days in 2025, with only two other lawmakers speaking on the House floor more frequently.

House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries called for a moment of silence in honor of LaMalfa at the start of a Capitol panel commemorating the fifth anniversary of the Jan. 6, 2021, attack, the report said.
