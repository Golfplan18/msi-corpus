Iraq requested the transfer of nearly 9,000 Islamic State prisoners held in Syrian detention camps controlled by Kurdish forces, citing security concerns about escapes during recent military clashes. The U.S. military began the transfers on Wednesday with the first 150 detainees, moving them from Syria's Hassakeh province to secure facilities in Iraq, according to American and Iraqi officials.

The prisoner transfers follow a broader military campaign in which Syrian government forces have seized control of detention camps from the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) over the past two weeks, raising humanitarian concerns in areas still under Kurdish control.

"Iraq offered proactively" to take the detainees rather than the United States requesting it of them, a senior U.S. military official confirmed to the Associated Press. An Iraqi security official said the transfer decision reflected concerns that prisoners might become a danger to Iraq's security if they escaped amid the chaos of the military operations.

U.S. Central Command said that up to 7,000 detainees could be transferred to Iraqi-controlled facilities. The SDF currently holds approximately 9,000 Islamic State detainees in more than a dozen detention centers across northeast Syria.

## Syrian forces seize detention facilities

The transfers began as Syrian government forces took control of the sprawling al-Hol camp from the SDF following a ceasefire agreement last week. The camp houses thousands of mostly women and children. Government troops also seized a prison in the northeastern town of Shaddadeh, where some IS detainees escaped before being recaptured, state media reported.

At al-Aqtan prison near the northern Syrian city of Raqqa, government forces surrounded the facility with tanks and shelled it with heavy weapons while negotiations proceeded on its future. Early Friday, Syria's defense ministry announced that an "internationally sponsored agreement" had been reached for the withdrawal of approximately 800 SDF fighters from inside the prison, with the facility to be handed over to the Syrian army.

On Thursday, U.S. envoy to Syria Tom Barrack met with SDF leader Mazloum Abdi in Irbil, the capital of Iraq's semi-autonomous Kurdish region.

U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio applauded Iraq's "initiative to detain ISIS terrorists in secure facilities" following the recent instability, adding that non-Iraqi prisoners would be held temporarily and urging other countries to repatriate their citizens.

## Humanitarian toll escalates

Iraq has beefed up patrols along its border with Syria in response. On Thursday, tanks lined up along the frontier in the northern province of Sinjar, where members of the Yazidi minority expressed particular concern about the military operations. During a 2014 Islamic State occupation of the region, IS militants killed Yazidi men and boys and sold women into sexual slavery or forced them to convert and marry militants.

The humanitarian toll is mounting in areas still under Kurdish control. In the besieged northern town of Kobani, which has become a symbol of resistance against the Islamic State, residents face deteriorating conditions. "There is no electricity or running water in the town," said Nesrin Abdullah, a commander with the Kurdish Women's Protection Units, speaking from inside Kobani during an online news conference. "The people here are facing a genocide. We have many people in hospitals, and hospitals cannot continue if there is no electricity."

Abdullah warned that if fighting around Kobani continues, thousands of people "will be massacred." The SDF accused the Syrian government of violating a four-day truce declared earlier this week, saying government forces pounded the southern outskirts of Kobani with artillery.

## Islamic State's enduring threat

The Islamic State declared a caliphate in 2014 across large parts of Syria and Iraq, attracting large numbers of fighters from around the world. The militant group was defeated in Iraq in 2017 and in Syria two years later, but IS sleeper cells continue to carry out deadly attacks in both countries. The SDF played a key role in defeating the group and has since held thousands of detainees in northeast Syria.

Under a peace process, the SDF is expected to eventually hand over its prison facilities to Syrian government control and merge with government forces, according to officials. The current prisoner transfers represent a first step in that transition.

U.N. Assistant Secretary-General Khaled Khiari told the U.N. Security Council on Thursday that clashes were taking place in parts of Hassakeh province and on the outskirts of Kobani, and that the situation elsewhere on the ground remained "very tense."
