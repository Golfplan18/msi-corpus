Hundreds of mourners marched in silence Sunday through the Swiss Alpine resort of Crans-Montana to honor the 40 people killed in a New Year's Eve fire at Le Constellation bar, as Swiss authorities completed identification of all victims and advanced a criminal investigation into the bar's two managers.

The blaze, which broke out around 1:30 a.m. on New Year's Day and injured 119 others, has prompted criminal charges and calls for tighter nightlife safety standards. Swiss President Guy Parmelin has declared Jan. 9 a national day of mourning, and dozens of the most severely burned survivors have been transferred to hospitals in five European countries.

CRANS-MONTANA, Switzerland — Hundreds of mourners filed out of a chapel to organ music Sunday and marched in silence up a hill to the ruins of Le Constellation bar, where 40 people died and 119 were injured in a fire that broke out around 1:30 a.m. on New Year's Day. By Sunday evening, Swiss authorities said they had identified all of the dead, completing a process made difficult by the severity of burns that required DNA samples.

The silent procession wound through the Alpine resort town from the Chapelle Saint-Christophe, past shuttered stores in bright winter sunlight, toward the bar, which remained largely screened from view by white barriers. At the entrance, the swelling crowd stood in near total silence before breaking into sustained applause for the rescue teams and police who had rushed to the scene. Mourners deposited flowers, stuffed animals and other tributes at a makeshift memorial outside.

"Through this tragic event, I believe we must all remember that we are all brothers and sisters in humanity," said Véronique Barras, a local resident who knows grieving families. "It's important to support each other, to hug each other, and to move forward towards light."

Cathy Premer, whose daughter had been celebrating her 17th birthday at the bar and called in the early hours to say she was stuck because Le Constellation was cordoned off, described the difficulty of making sense of the deaths. "For the young — but even for adults — it's hard to understand things that seem inexplicable," she said. "They went there to party, it's a destination for Dec. 31, it's very festive, there were people of many nationalities ... and it all turned into a tragedy."

Paola Ponti Greppi, an 80-year-old Italian with a house in Crans-Montana, called for stricter oversight of nightlife venues. "We need more safety in these places because it's not the only place like this," she said. "Why didn't the town do the proper checks? For me that's terrible."

## The Mass

The hourlong Mass at the Chapelle Saint-Christophe preceded the march. Readings were delivered in German, French and Italian. The Rev. Gilles Cavin addressed the grief directly. "There are no words strong enough to express the dismay, anguish, and anger of those who are affected in their lives today," he said. "And yet, we are here, gathered because silence alone is not enough."

Cavin spoke of the "terrible uncertainty" still facing some families unsure whether their loved ones were among the dead or in hospital. Police had said many of the victims were in their teens to mid-20s.

## A grieving family

One victim was 16-year-old Arthur Brodard. His mother, Laetitia Brodard, announced his death Saturday after a desperate search, posting a video message on Facebook. "Our Arthur has now left to party in paradise," she said. "We can start our mourning, knowing that he is in peace and in the light."

Brodard had submitted a DNA sample to help Swiss authorities identify her son, a step the government said was necessary given the extent of the burns.

## Criminal investigation

Swiss authorities have opened a criminal investigation into Le Constellation's two managers. Beatrice Pilloud, the Valais region's chief prosecutor, said Saturday that the pair are suspected of involuntary homicide, involuntary bodily harm and involuntarily causing a fire. The announcement did not name the managers. Regional police said Sunday there were no legal grounds to hold them pending the investigation and they had not been deemed a flight risk.

Investigators believe festive sparkling candles placed atop Champagne bottles ignited the blaze when they came too close to the bar's ceiling. Authorities are examining whether sound-dampening material on the ceiling complied with regulations, whether the candles were permitted inside the venue, and whether fire extinguishers, escape routes and previous construction work met code requirements.

"Initial witness accounts cited a fire that spread quickly, generating a lot of smoke and a huge wave of heat," police said Sunday. "Everything happened very fast."

## National mourning, international response

Swiss President Guy Parmelin announced a national day of mourning for Jan. 9.

France's Health Minister Stéphanie Rist said 17 patients received care in France, part of a total of 35 transferred from Switzerland to hospitals across five European countries. Additional patients were planned for transfer to Germany, Italy and Belgium.
