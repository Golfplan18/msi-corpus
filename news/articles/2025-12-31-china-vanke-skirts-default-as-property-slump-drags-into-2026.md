State-backed developer China Vanke narrowly avoided defaulting on a 2 billion yuan ($284 million) bond last week, exposing the fragility of a Chinese housing market that has failed to recover more than four years after its downturn began. The Shenzhen-based company also sought to delay repayment of a separate 3.7 billion yuan ($530 million) of onshore debt due Dec. 28, with bondholders agreeing to push that deadline to February.

China Vanke, once China's largest homebuilder by sales, narrowly avoided defaulting on a 2 billion yuan ($284 million) bond last week, exposing the fragility of a housing market that has failed to recover more than four years after its downturn began.

The Shenzhen-based company also sought to delay repayment of a separate 3.7 billion yuan ($530 million) of onshore debt due Dec. 28, with bondholders agreeing to push that deadline to February.

Vanke's liquidity crisis illustrates how China's property sector — once responsible for more than a quarter of total economic activity — has become a drag on growth rather than an engine of it, with millions of homeowners holding apartments worth far less than they paid for them and analysts warning that prices are unlikely to rebound before 2027.

### A sector still in the doldrums

More than four years after China's property downturn began, home prices have fallen 20% or more from their 2021 peak. New home sales fell 11.2% by value year-on-year in the first 11 months of 2025, according to official statistics. Property investments fell nearly 16% from a year earlier.

The slump has caused massive layoffs, hurting overall consumer confidence and spending, according to the Associated Press.

"The continued slide in the property market remains one of the most significant risks to China's efforts to shift to a domestically demand-driven growth model," said Lynn Song, chief economist for Greater China at ING Bank.

Economists at Morgan Stanley said restoring confidence in the property sector may take years. Economists at Morningstar said home prices are unlikely to rebound until 2027, citing excess supply, despite repeated pledges by regulators to stabilize the market.

### Vanke's financial position

About a third owned by Shenzhen Metro, a state-owned railway, Vanke's revenue fell 27% year-on-year in the July-September 2025 quarter, and several of its onshore bonds were suspended from trading after prices plunged.

The developer owes more than $50 billion in total — far less than the more than $300 billion in debt accumulated by China Evergrande before it defaulted in 2021 and was eventually forced into liquidation. But Vanke had long been considered one of China's financially sounder developers, making its distress a significant signal to markets.

By the end of September 2025, Vanke reported 60 billion yuan ($8 billion) in cash against short-term debts of about 151 billion yuan ($21 billion), according to Fitch Ratings. Both S&P Global and Fitch have now downgraded the company — S&P to "selective default," describing the bond extension as a distressed debt restructuring "tantamount to a default," and Fitch to "restricted default."

"This is one of the most significant, quasi state-backed developers that may be defaulting (on) their repayment," said Foreky Wong, a founding partner at Fortune Ark Restructuring.

### State support falling short

Shenzhen Metro Group, which is controlled by the Shenzhen government, has provided more than 29 billion yuan ($4 billion) in shareholder loans to Vanke this year to help with debt repayments, according to S&P Global. That support has not covered the company's full obligations.

Vanke still faces more than 9.4 billion yuan in bonds maturing over the next six months, according to S&P. The company employed more than 120,000 people as of last year.

A default by Vanke could ripple through the wider real estate sector, making it more difficult for non-state-owned developers to obtain assistance, said Jeff Zhang, an analyst at Morningstar.

"Without a strong commitment by the Shenzhen government on the bailout, we think Vanke's liquidity profile should remain fragile," Zhang said.
