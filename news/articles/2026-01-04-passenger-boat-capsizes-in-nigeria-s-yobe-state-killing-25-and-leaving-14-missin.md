The disaster is the latest in a recurring toll from waterway accidents across Nigeria, where hundreds die annually aboard overloaded and poorly maintained vessels in areas where water transport remains a primary route for commerce and daily life.

A passenger boat carrying 52 people capsized Saturday night on the Yobe River near Garbi town in northern Nigeria's Yobe state, killing at least 25 people and leaving 14 others missing, the state emergency management agency said Sunday.

Thirteen survivors were rescued from the river and were receiving medical treatment, the agency said in a statement. The boat was carrying residents who had visited a local market as well as people involved in fishing and farming when it overturned mid-journey, according to the agency, which provided no additional details on what caused the capsize.

"Search and rescue operations are ongoing, with security agencies, emergency responders, and local community volunteers working tirelessly to locate the missing passengers and recover bodies," the agency said.

Yobe Governor Mai Mala Buni offered condolences to the families of those killed and directed that medical and logistical support be provided immediately to survivors receiving treatment, according to a government statement.

## A Recurring Toll on Nigerian Waterways

Boat accidents kill hundreds of people annually across Nigeria, particularly in remote areas where rivers serve as a primary means of transport for commerce and daily travel, and where road infrastructure is limited. At least 30 passengers were reported killed when a boat capsized in September, while 25 others died in a similar accident in July, according to the Associated Press.

Past accidents have frequently been attributed to overloaded and poorly maintained vessels, many of them operating without life jackets aboard.
