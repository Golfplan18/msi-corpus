JAKARTA, Indonesia — Indonesian rescuers recovered a third body Tuesday from a tour boat that sank on Dec. 26 during a Christmas holiday trip near Indonesia's Komodo National Park, officials said, as two sons of a Spanish soccer coach remained missing more than 10 days after the vessel went down following engine failure.

The body was found near the coast of Pede beach after fishermen spotted wreckage that had drifted nearly 14 kilometers (8.6 miles) from where the boat sank, said Fathur Rahman, head of the Maumere Search and Rescue Office.

The sinking has killed at least three members of a Spanish family who had traveled to one of Indonesia's most popular national parks for the holidays. The disaster adds to a persistent record of maritime accidents in an archipelago nation of more than 17,000 islands where boats are the dominant form of regional transport and safety standards remain inconsistently enforced.

## The sinking

The boat was carrying Fernando Martín, 44, a coach for Valencia CF Women's B, his wife, their four children, four crew members, and a local guide when it sank on the evening of Dec. 26 after suffering engine failure in the Komodo National Park area.

Martín's wife and one of the four children, along with all four crew members and the guide, were rescued in the hours after the boat went down. Two of Martín's sons remained missing as of Tuesday.

## Bodies recovered and cremated

The bodies of Martín and his daughter had already been recovered and cremated in Bali on Monday at relatives' request, said Ariasandy, a spokesperson for the East Nusa Tenggara Police. Ariasandy, who goes by a single name as is common in Indonesia, said the cremations proceeded because of limited space at a mortuary in Labuan Bajo.

The third body recovered Tuesday brings the confirmed death toll from the sinking to three.

## Safety context

Indonesia is an archipelago with more than 17,000 islands where boats serve as a primary mode of transportation between communities. Maritime accidents occur frequently, officials have said, citing lax safety standards and persistent problems with overcrowding on passenger vessels.
