A federal nutrition advisory panel on Tuesday released draft recommendations for the 2025-2030 Dietary Guidelines for Americans, calling for people to eat more beans, peas and lentils and cut back on red and processed meats — guidance that will shape federal food programs and policy for the next five years.

The 20-member panel, which deliberated for nearly two years, also recommended continuing to limit added sugars, sodium and saturated fat. The panel's report now goes to the U.S. Departments of Health and Human Services and Agriculture, which will draft final guidelines for release next year.

The recommendations largely track decades of federal nutritional guidance while introducing a "health equity lens" for the first time — accounting for how income, race, ethnicity and culture shape Americans' diets. But the panel declined to address ultraprocessed foods or revise alcohol consumption recommendations, drawing criticism from food policy experts and setting up a potential clash with the incoming administration's health agenda.

## What a healthy diet looks like, per the panel

The panel concluded that a healthy diet for people aged 2 and older is higher in vegetables, fruits, legumes, nuts, whole grains, fish and vegetable oils rich in unsaturated fat. It is lower in red and processed meats, sugar-sweetened foods and beverages, refined grains and saturated fat.

The group was the first to frame its work through what it called a "health equity lens" — considering factors such as household income, race, ethnicity and culture when issuing dietary recommendations.

"It will help ensure that the guidance reflects and includes various population groups," Dr. Fatima Cody Stanford, a Massachusetts General Hospital obesity expert who served on the panel, said in an email.

Food policy expert Marion Nestle said the new guidance offered little that was new.

"This looks like every other set of dietary guidelines since 1980: eat your veggies and reduce consumption of foods high in salt, sugar and saturated fat," Nestle said in an email. "This particular statement says nothing about balancing calories, when overconsumption of calories, especially from ultra-processed foods, is the biggest challenge to the health of Americans."

## Panel passes on ultraprocessed foods and alcohol

The panel considered more than 40 studies on ultraprocessed foods — the snacks, sugary cereals and frozen meals that make up about 60% of the American diet — including several that showed links to overweight and obesity. But the experts said concerns about research quality left the evidence too limited to support recommendations.

That decision is likely to conflict with the stated priorities of Robert F. Kennedy Jr., the nominee to lead HHS, who has questioned potential conflicts of interest among panel members and vowed to crack down on ultraprocessed foods.

The panel also left alcohol recommendations unchanged, continuing to advise limiting intake to two drinks or less per day for men and one drink or less per day for women. In 2020, the government rejected the advice of scientific advisers to recommend less alcohol consumption.

Two groups — the National Academies of Science, Engineering and Medicine and a committee of the government agency that oversees substance abuse — are expected to release reports in the coming months on the effects of moderate alcohol use to help inform the final guidelines.

## A persistent gap between guidance and diet

The advisory panel acknowledged that most Americans' diets do not meet current guidelines. More than half of all U.S. adults have one or more diet-related chronic health conditions, and 18 million U.S. households lack secure sources of food, according to the report.

"Nutrition-related chronic health conditions and their precursors continue to threaten health through the lifespan," the report states. "Which does not bode well for the future of health in the United States."

Dr. Peter Lurie, president of the advocacy group Center for Science in the Public Interest, said the recommendations were on solid ground.

"Broadly, I think these are well-formulated recommendations that the incoming administration would do well to adopt," Lurie said.

## What comes next

A 60-day public comment period on the recommendations opened Wednesday. HHS and USDA officials will hold a public meeting Jan. 16 to discuss the panel's report.

The incoming Trump administration will finalize the guidelines, which are updated every five years and serve as the cornerstone of federal food programs including school meals and nutrition assistance.
