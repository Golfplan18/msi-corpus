LJUBLJANA, Slovenia — Slovenia's President Natasa Pirc Musar on Tuesday announced a March 22 parliamentary election, scheduling a vote widely seen as a test for Prime Minister Robert Golob's liberal coalition government as it faces a challenge from populist right-wing parties.

"I want the election to be conducted fairly, and I will pay attention to disinformation and manipulation, including from abroad," Pirc Musar said.

The vote will fill all 90 seats in Slovenia's national assembly in a campaign Pirc Musar said she will monitor for disinformation and foreign manipulation — a signal of the pressures confronting a small EU member state whose government has drawn opposition fire over its recognition of a Palestinian state and its handling of the economy.

Under Slovenian law, parliament must convene within 20 days of the election. The president then has an additional 30 days to appoint a prime minister-designate who commands a majority of lawmakers.

Golob's coalition came to power in 2022, defeating his populist predecessor Janez Jansa, who was seen as pushing the traditionally moderate Alpine nation to the right. During Golob's tenure, Slovenia recognized a Palestinian state, a move the opposition criticized alongside broader accusations that the government slowed economic growth.

The election comes months after a setback for the Golob government: Slovenians in November rejected a referendum bill that would have allowed terminally ill patients to end their lives through assisted dying.

Pirc Musar called Tuesday for "respectful, responsible and tolerant communication during the election campaign."

Slovenia, a country of approximately 2 million people, declared independence from the communist-run former Yugoslavia in 1991 and joined both NATO and the European Union in 2004.
