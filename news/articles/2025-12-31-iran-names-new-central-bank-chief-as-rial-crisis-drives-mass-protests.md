The appointment places Hemmati at the center of one of Iran's deepest economic crises in years, with a currency that has lost more than 97% of its value since 2015, an inflation rate experts put at 40%, and street demonstrations that officials have warned will face a strong security response if they turn violent.

TEHRAN — Iran's government appointed Abdolnasser Hemmati, a former economics minister, as the new governor of the Central Bank of the Islamic Republic of Iran on Wednesday, as mass protests over a record-low currency continued and merchants kept shops shuttered in major bazaars across the country.

President Masoud Pezeshkian's Cabinet announced the appointment, the official IRNA news agency reported. Hemmati, 68, succeeds Mohammad Reza Farzin, who resigned Monday — the day after protests erupted in Tehran and other cities as the rial fell to a record low of 1.38 million to the U.S. dollar.

The protests are among the largest Iran has seen in three years, according to the Associated Press. MSI previously reported that Hemmati was named to the post as demonstrations spread across the country — [read the earlier report](/articles/2025-12-31-iran-names-new-central-bank-governor-as-rial-protests-spread).

## A fractured mandate

Hemmati's appointment carries its own complications. In March, parliament dismissed him from his previous role as minister of economic and financial affairs — also under President Pezeshkian — citing alleged mismanagement and accusations that his policies had weakened the rial against hard currencies.

The government's spokeswoman, Fatemeh Mohajerani, wrote on X that Hemmati's agenda would include controlling inflation, strengthening the currency and addressing mismanagement in the banking sector.

Experts say a 40% inflation rate has fueled the public discontent driving the demonstrations. The rial stood at 430,000 to the dollar when Farzin took office in 2022; it now trades at more than three times that rate against the U.S. currency. A combination of the currency's depreciation and inflationary pressure has pushed up the prices of food and other daily necessities, straining household budgets. Inflation is expected to worsen following a gasoline price change introduced in recent weeks, according to the Associated Press.

## Protests continue; officials warn of crackdown

Merchants and traders kept their shops closed Wednesday in the main bazaars of Tehran, as well as in the southern city of Shiraz and the western city of Kermanshah, witnesses said.

Prosecutor General Mohammad Movahedi Azad warned that any effort to turn protests over economic issues "to insecurity, damage to public properties or carrying out foreign scenarios, will face...strong reaction," according to the Mizanonline news outlet.

In the southern city of Fasa, a local judiciary official denied that a young man had been killed during a protest, Mizanonline reported. Hamed Ostevar, head of Fasa's justice department, said protests had turned violent after crowds broke into the governor's office, injuring three policemen. Four protesters were arrested, he said.

## A currency in long-term decline

Iran's currency has shed more than 97% of its value against the dollar since the 2015 nuclear accord, when it traded at 32,000 rials to the dollar. That agreement, which lifted international sanctions in exchange for tight controls on Iran's nuclear program, unraveled after President Donald Trump unilaterally withdrew the United States from it in 2018, during his first term. Western sanctions, reimposed and expanded since then, have restricted Iran's access to foreign exchange and compounded inflationary pressure on household budgets already strained by years of economic isolation.
