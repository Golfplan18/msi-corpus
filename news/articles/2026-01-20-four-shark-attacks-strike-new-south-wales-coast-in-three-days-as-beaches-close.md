A surfer suffered minor injuries Tuesday when a shark attacked his board at Point Plomer on Australia's New South Wales coast, the fourth shark attack along the state's coastline in three days. Authorities closed beaches along the northern New South Wales coast and northern Sydney for at least 48 hours and deployed electronic drumlines offshore as officials warned that recent rainfall had left coastal waters murky and unusually conducive to bull shark activity.

The cluster of attacks — capped by a Sunday mauling of a 12-year-old boy at Sydney Harbour that news media reported cost him both legs — has prompted emergency beach closures and a public warning from surf lifesaving officials urging residents to use swimming pools rather than the ocean while conditions remain dangerous.

## Tuesday's attack at Point Plomer

The shark attacked the 39-year-old man's surfboard at Point Plomer, 460 kilometres (290 miles) north of the New South Wales state capital, around 9 a.m., officials said. Bystanders drove the man to hospital, where he was later discharged after being treated for minor cuts.

Matt Worrall, captain of the Kempsey-Crescent Head Surf Life Saving Club, said the board had absorbed most of the force of the attack. "The board seemed to take most of the impact," Worrall told the Australian Broadcasting Corp. "He made his own way into shore where he was assisted by locals."

The isolated location had no shark netting, officials said.

## Sunday and Monday attacks in and around Sydney

The most serious injuries came Sunday, when a 12-year-old boy was attacked after jumping from a six-metre (20-foot) ledge known as Jump Rock near Shark Beach inside Sydney Harbour. Police credited the boy's friends with saving his life by jumping from the cliff during the attack and dragging him back to shore. News media have reported that the boy lost both legs.

"Those actions of those young men are brave under the circumstances and very confronting injuries for those boys to see," said Supt. Joseph McNulty.

On Monday, a shark bit a chunk from an 11-year-old boy's surfboard at Dee Why Beach, an ocean beach north of Manly. The boy was not injured. Later that afternoon, at approximately 6:20 p.m., a surfer in his 20s was bitten on the leg at North Steyne Beach in the northern Sydney suburb of Manly. Bystanders pulled him from the water; an ambulance transported him to hospital in critical condition.

All three Sydney beaches involved in the weekend attacks have some form of shark protection netting, officials said. It was not immediately clear whether the attacks occurred within the netted zones.

## Officials cite murky water and bull shark conditions

Steve Pearce, chief executive of Surf Life Saving NSW, said recent rainfall had left the water off Sydney's northern beaches murky — conditions that authorities said are associated with elevated bull shark activity. Bull sharks are responsible for most shark attacks around Sydney, according to officials.

"If anyone's thinking of heading into the surf this morning anywhere along the northern beaches, think again. We have such poor water quality that's really conducive to some bull shark activity," Pearce said. "If you're thinking about going for a swim, just go to a local pool because at this stage, we're advising that beaches are unsafe."

Authorities deployed electronic drumlines — devices that send alerts to officials when a large shark takes bait — off the Sydney coast alongside the beach closures.

## Recent fatal attacks in the area

The current cluster follows two fatal shark encounters along the New South Wales coast in recent months. A 57-year-old surfer was killed by a suspected white shark near Dee Why Beach last September — the same stretch of ocean where Monday's surfboard attack on the 11-year-old occurred. In November, a 25-year-old Swiss tourist was killed and her partner was seriously injured as they swam off a national park north of Sydney.
