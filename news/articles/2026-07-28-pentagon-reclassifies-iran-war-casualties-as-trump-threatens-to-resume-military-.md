## New 'Overseas Operations' category draws accusations of disrespect for war dead

The Pentagon introduced a new casualty category in its Defense Casualty Analysis System (DCAS) that reclassifies military personnel killed in the Iran war starting July 7 as deaths in "Overseas Operations," splitting the conflict into two separate campaigns and drawing sharp criticism from veterans who accused the Department of Defense of disrespecting the war dead.

"It's such a huge disrespect for the families," said retired Rear Adm. Michael E. Smith, who completed combat tours in Iraq, Bosnia and Kosovo. "To claim that they weren't killed as a result of the war – oh, it was just overseas operations – is just ludicrous."

The reclassification came as President Trump warned the U.S. would resume "very strong military action" against Iran if diplomatic negotiations fail to produce a rapid resolution. "We are in very deep talks with Iran. If they don't work out, we will go back to very strong military action," Trump told Axios on Monday. Asked how long he would allow the talks to proceed, he answered: "Not much time. Either it goes fast or not at all."

The Trump administration maintains the new category follows its interpretation that the April ceasefire paused the 60-day clock required under the 1973 War Powers Act for military operations without congressional authorization. The separate casualty count beginning July 7 reflects that interpretation.
