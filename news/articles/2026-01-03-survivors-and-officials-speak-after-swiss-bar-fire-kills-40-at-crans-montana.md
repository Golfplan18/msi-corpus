CRANS-MONTANA, Switzerland — Survivors, bystanders and officials on Friday described scenes of chaos and grief in the aftermath of a fire that killed 40 people during New Year's celebrations at the Le Constellation bar in the Swiss Alpine ski resort of Crans-Montana.

More than 100 people were injured as the blaze ripped through the crowded venue early Thursday, authorities said. Investigators said Friday they believe sparkling candles atop Champagne bottles ignited the fatal fire when they came too close to the ceiling of the crowded bar. The fire is among the deadliest tragedies in Switzerland's history, the Associated Press reported.

## A mother's search

Laetitia Brodard told reporters Friday in Crans-Montana that she was searching for her son Arthur, 16, and did not know where to find him.

"I'm looking everywhere. The body of my son is somewhere," Brodard said. "I want to know where my child is and be by his side. Wherever that may be, be it in the intensive care unit or the morgue."

## Young volunteers

Marc-Antoine Chavanon, 14, told the Associated Press on Friday that he rushed to the bar to help the injured.

"We were bringing people out, people were collapsing. We were doing everything we could to save them, we helped as many as we could. We saw people screaming, running," Chavanon said. "There was one of our friends: She was struggling to get out, she was all burned. You can't imagine the pain I saw."

Gianni Campolo, a Swiss 19-year-old who was in Crans-Montana on vacation, said he too rushed to assist first responders.

"I have seen horror, and I don't know what else would be worse than this," Campolo told France's TF1 television.

His father, Paolo Campolo, described finding an emergency exit behind the bar and working with another volunteer to open a glass door.

"Despite the heavy smoke, I could see bodies pressed against the glass," Paolo Campolo told French broadcaster BFM TV. "That's when several bodies fell. We immediately pulled them out, one after the other. I think we had to pull out about ten bodies in total," he said.

## Hospital and legal authorities

Eric Bonvin, general director of the regional hospital in Sion, which received dozens of injured people, said the disaster weighed on the entire region.

"It was hard to live through for everyone. Also, probably because everyone was asking themselves, 'Was my child, my cousin, someone from the region at this party?'" Bonvin told AP on Friday. "This place was very well known as somewhere to celebrate the new year. Also, seeing young people arrive — that's always traumatic."

Beatrice Pilloud, the Valais region's attorney general, addressed reporters Friday at a news conference in Sion.

"The priority today is truly placed on identification, in order to allow the families to begin their mourning," Pilloud said.

## Official responses

Mathias Reynard, cantonal head of government, said residents had responded with solidarity in the immediate aftermath.

"We have numerous accounts of heroic actions, one could say of very strong solidarity in the moment," Reynard told RTS radio Friday. "In the first minutes, it was citizens — and in large part young people — who saved lives with their courage."

Pope Leo said in a telegram Friday to the bishop of Sion that he "wishes to express his compassion and concern to the relatives of the victims. He prays that the Lord will welcome the deceased into His abode of peace and light, and will sustain the courage of those who suffer in their hearts or in their bodies."

Swiss President Guy Parmelin, speaking on his first day in the annually-rotating position, addressed the disaster Thursday.

"Switzerland is a strong country not because it is sheltered from drama, but because it knows how to face them with courage and a spirit of mutual help," Parmelin said.
