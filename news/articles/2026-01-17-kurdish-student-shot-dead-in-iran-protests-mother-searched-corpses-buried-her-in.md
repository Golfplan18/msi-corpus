Iranian security forces shot and killed Robina Aminian, a Kurdish college student studying fashion in Tehran, on the night of Jan. 8, according to her relatives and the Oslo-based Iran Human Rights organization, which verified a shooting near her campus. Her mother, Amina Norei, rushed overnight from western Iran to the capital, spent hours searching through body bags in a morgue to find her daughter, then buried the young woman in an unmarked roadside pit while security forces surrounded their home.

Three of Aminian's relatives, all based in Oslo, spoke to the Associated Press and corroborated details Norei had relayed in a brief phone call on Jan. 10 — the last contact the overseas family has had with relatives in Iran. The AP said it was unable to independently confirm the family's account due to Iran's severe communications restrictions. The Iranian mission to the United Nations in New York declined to comment.

Aminian's case reflects a pattern documented by human rights organizations: beyond the killings, Iranian security forces have compounded families' grief by withholding bodies, demanding payment for their return, and pressuring relatives to bury the dead in secret.

"She wanted a bright future for herself," her uncle, Nezar Minoei, said from Oslo. "But unfortunately, the future has been stolen from her."

## The shooting

Aminian's relatives say her friends called her mother shortly after she was killed on the night of Jan. 8. According to the friends' account — relayed through Norei to relatives in Oslo — Aminian and her friends had been walking away from the Shariati Technical and Vocational College for Girls in Tehran, where she was enrolled, when they encountered a protest nearby and joined it. A bullet fired by security forces struck the back of her head, the friends told her mother.

Iran Human Rights said it independently verified a shooting on the night of Jan. 8 near the campus, based on witness testimony. Social media videos verified by the AP, along with accounts from rights groups, doctors, and survivors, document Iranian security agents using rifles and shotguns to disperse protesters across the country. Authorities have alleged that some demonstrators were armed; there are no allegations that anyone near Aminian was armed at the time of her death. Her relatives said she was not an activist and was not involved in politics.

Iran's government has increasingly referred to demonstrators as "terrorists."

## The search

Norei was in Kermanshah, a city in the Kurdish region of western Iran roughly 460 kilometers from Tehran, when she learned of her daughter's death. She traveled to Tehran through the night and began searching the morgue.

"She looked through so many beautiful faces, trying to find her girl," said Hali Norei, Aminian's aunt, speaking from Oslo. "And what is so horrifying for me is imagining what my sister feels as she searches for her daughter."

When Norei found her daughter's body, she was joined by her husband, her oldest daughter, and her son, and the family rushed out of the building.

"She actually stole the body," Minoei said, describing how his sister took the body without official authorization.

The family feared that security forces would demand payment in exchange for the official release of the corpse. The New York City-based Center for Human Rights in Iran said in a statement to the AP that it had received multiple accounts of intelligence forces demanding money from families for the return of protesters' bodies. The center called the practice "a well-known, standard practice" used to scare families into not publicly mourning their dead. Other families told the center they were required to sign papers falsely declaring that their dead relatives had been members of the security forces in order to retrieve the bodies.

Iranian state television aired a statement saying mortuary and burial services were free, after the allegations drew widespread attention.

## The burial

Minoei said his sister told him she and her oldest daughter spent the seven-hour drive back to Kermanshah clutching Aminian's body in the backseat, blood and tears staining their clothes. When the family arrived home, security forces had surrounded the house.

Left with no other option, the family drove out of town and dug a pit on the side of the road. They placed Aminian's body inside and drove away. More than a week after her death, no funeral had been held, her relatives said. She is still believed to be buried there, in the unmarked grave.

## Broader toll

The U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency, which relies on a network of activists inside Iran and has been accurate during previous periods of unrest, said at least 3,090 people have been killed in the nationwide protests. Iran's government has not released any overall casualty figures.

Amnesty International has reported that bodies of protesters have piled up in trucks, freight containers, and warehouses, with many other families searching overflowing morgues.
