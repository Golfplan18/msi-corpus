The troop movements mark a fresh advance by Syria's post-Assad government into territory long held by the U.S.-backed SDF, deepening a contest over northern Syria that has drawn in American military officials, Iraqi Kurdish mediators, and a new presidential decree extending language and cultural rights to Syria's Kurds — a concession the SDF's governing authority said falls short without constitutional guarantees.

DEIR HAFER, Syria — Syrian government forces entered the northern towns of Deir Hafer and Maskana on Saturday after the Kurdish-led Syrian Democratic Forces announced they would evacuate the area, according to an Associated Press reporter who witnessed armored columns move in after bulldozers cleared barriers at Deir Hafer's edge.

Two soldiers were killed and others wounded in a clash as troops entered Maskana, state media reported. The Syrian military said its forces were in full control of Deir Hafer, had captured the Jarrah air base to the east, and were clearing mines and explosives left behind.

More than 11,000 civilians fled Deir Hafer and Maskana over the two preceding days by side roads to government-controlled areas after Damascus announced an offensive to retake the towns.

## Disputed terms of withdrawal

Each side accused the other of violating a withdrawal agreement. The SDF said in a statement that "Damascus violated the terms of the agreement and entered the towns before our fighters had fully withdrawn, creating a highly dangerous situation with potentially serious repercussions."

Syrian state agency SANA reported that SDF fighters "violated the agreement" by targeting an army patrol near Maskana, leaving two soldiers dead and others wounded. SANA added that government forces continued east, reaching two villages in the northern province of Raqqa.

Residents in Deir Hafer welcomed the troops. "Our happiness is beyond expression. It is the happiness of liberation," said Hussein Mustafa. Another resident, Mohammed al-Jaber, said: "The Syrian Arab Army came here and liberated us from this terrorist organization."

## U.S. urges restraint, backs mediation

The easing of hostilities came after U.S. military officials visited Deir Hafer on Friday and held talks with SDF officials. Adm. Brad Cooper, commander of U.S. Central Command, welcomed ongoing efforts by all parties to prevent escalation and said in a statement Saturday that the United States urged Syrian government forces "to cease any offensive actions in areas between Aleppo" and the town of Tabqa to the east.

SDF commander Mazloum Abdi, who announced on X late Friday night that his fighters would relocate east of the Euphrates River starting at 7 a.m. Saturday, was scheduled to meet U.S. special envoy to Syria Tom Barrack in the northern Iraqi city of Irbil on Saturday.

An official with Iraq's Kurdistan Democratic Party said KDP leader Masoud Barzani had communicated with interim Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa, who asked Barzani to serve as a mediator — a role Abdi also accepted, according to the official, who spoke on condition of anonymity because he was not authorized to speak with the media. The official said Barzani's initiative led to the Irbil meeting, aimed at keeping SDF forces east of the Euphrates and preventing a return to fighting.

## Kurdish decree draws qualified SDF response

The SDF's withdrawal decision came after al-Sharaa issued a decree on Friday that recognized Kurdish as a national language alongside Arabic and adopted Newroz, a traditional spring festival observed by Kurds across the region, as an official holiday.

The Kurdish-led authority in northeast Syria said Saturday that the rights of Kurds should not be protected by "temporary decrees" but by constitutional inclusion, adding that a decree "does not form a real guarantee for rights of Syria's ethnic groups."

## Background

The SDF has been the principal U.S. partner in Syria in operations against the Islamic State. Ankara regards the SDF as a terrorist organization because of its ties to Kurdish separatist insurgents in Turkey; several factions now comprising the Syrian army were formerly Turkish-backed groups with a history of conflict with Kurdish forces.

The confrontation in Deir Hafer and Maskana followed fighting earlier this month in Aleppo, Syria's largest city, that ended with the evacuation of SDF fighters from three neighborhoods taken over by government forces. Syria's Kurds, who made up roughly 10 percent of the country's prewar population of 23 million, faced decades of marginalization under the Baath Party government that fell when President Bashar Assad was ousted in December 2024.
