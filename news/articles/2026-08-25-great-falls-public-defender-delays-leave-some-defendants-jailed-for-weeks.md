## Great Falls public defender delays leave some defendants jailed for weeks
**Subtype:** fact

- Great Falls city court Judge Mark Dunn told city commissioners on July 21 that the Montana Office of the State Public Defender is delaying assignment of counsel to indigent defendants by 30 to 60 days.
- Dunn said cases are being continued one to two times before an attorney is assigned, leaving some defendants in jail without representation.
- The OPD operates 20 offices across Montana and supplements its staff with private-practice contract attorneys to cover its workload.
- The OPD acknowledged challenges including difficulty filling vacant positions, a shortage of contract attorneys, and growing caseloads.

## OPD acknowledges staffing vacancies, attorney shortages, and growing caseloads

At a budget meeting on July 21, Great Falls city court judges identified the public defender delay as a key challenge facing the local court system, a problem judges said has created scheduling headaches, case delays, and has left some people waiting in jail without an attorney.

"I would say the bigger problem is the delay with the public defender's office," Dunn told the city commissioners. "And I have found there have been cases 30 to sometimes 60 days out where someone does not have an appointed attorney. And those cases are being continued one to two times before an attorney is even assigned."

The Montana Office of the State Public Defender (OPD) is the state agency responsible for providing defense attorneys to people who cannot afford private representation. The agency operates 20 offices across the state and also contracts with private-practice attorneys to cover its workload.

In statements to Montana Free Press and in agency reports, the OPD acknowledged several challenges, including difficulty filling vacant positions, a shortage of contract attorneys, and growing caseloads.
