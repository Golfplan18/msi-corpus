The surge reflects intensive Pakistani counterterrorism operations against the Pakistani Taliban, also known as Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan or TTP, alongside an escalation in the group's capabilities — partly attributed to U.S. military equipment that reached militant groups after American forces withdrew from Afghanistan in 2021. The violence has sharpened an already fraught relationship between Pakistan and Afghanistan, where all border crossings have remained closed since October following clashes that killed dozens and wounded hundreds.

Pakistan recorded its deadliest year in more than a decade in 2025, with violence killing 3,413 people — a 74% increase from 1,950 deaths in 2024 — according to a report released Thursday by the Pakistan Institute for Conflict and Security Studies.

Militants accounted for the largest share of the toll. The Islamabad-based think tank recorded 2,138 militant deaths, a 124% increase from 2024 that reflects intensive counterterrorism operations against the Pakistani Taliban, also known as Tehrik-e-Taliban Pakistan or TTP. The TTP is distinct from Afghanistan's Taliban government.

Security personnel suffered 667 deaths in 2025 — a 26% increase and the highest annual figure for security forces since 2011, according to Abdullah Khan, managing director of PICSS. Civilian deaths reached 580, the highest annual toll since 2015. An additional 28 members of pro-government peace committees were also reported dead.

## Suicide bombings and captured equipment drove the toll higher

Khan attributed the elevated death toll in part to a rise in suicide bombings and to the militants' use of U.S. military equipment left behind when American forces withdrew from Afghanistan in 2021. That equipment reached the TTP and other groups, increasing their operational capabilities, Khan said.

PICSS recorded at least 1,066 militant attacks in 2025. Suicide attacks rose 53%, with 26 incidents reported during the year. Security forces arrested approximately 500 militants during intelligence-based operations — nearly double the 272 arrests recorded in 2024. The TTP, along with other militant groups, claimed most of the attacks, according to the report.

Pakistan's military offered a parallel accounting of the campaign. Lt. Gen. Ahmad Sharif Chaudhry, the military's spokesman, said security forces conducted 67,023 intelligence-based operations in 2025, killing 1,873 militants, including 136 Afghan nationals.

## Pakistan-Afghanistan tensions remain unresolved

The rising violence has deepened pressure on Pakistan's already fraught relationship with Afghanistan. Border clashes in October, following explosions in Kabul that Afghanistan's Taliban government blamed on Pakistan — a claim Islamabad has denied — led to the closure of all border crossings between the two countries. The crossings have remained shut since then, halting bilateral trade and the movement of people.

A Qatar-mediated ceasefire has largely held since the October clashes, though the two sides failed to reach a broader agreement despite three rounds of talks in Istanbul in November.

Pakistan's newly appointed armed forces chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, sharpened Islamabad's public posture in December, calling on Afghanistan's Taliban government to choose between maintaining ties with Pakistan or continuing to support the Pakistani Taliban.

Foreign Ministry spokesperson Tahir Andrabi said Thursday that the reopening of border crossings is contingent on written assurances from Kabul that it will not allow Afghan soil to be used for attacks against Pakistan. Andrabi said Pakistan had permitted the United Nations to deliver relief supplies to Afghanistan, but that trucks carrying aid remained stranded on the Pakistani side because Afghanistan had not opened the gates on its end.

"A country in need of humanitarian assistance is unwilling to receive it. This is unprecedented — a country facing a humanitarian crisis is blocking humanitarian supplies," Andrabi told reporters.

There was no response from Kabul to the claim.
