## Ukrainian drones strike Iranian military supply line to Russia in Caspian Sea

**Subtype:** fact

- Ukrainian drones struck military cargo vessels and a warship in the Caspian Sea, targeting supply routes connecting Russia and Iran.
- President Volodymyr Zelensky claimed responsibility, saying the strikes damaged vessels used for military shipments involving Iran and a warship.
- Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said a sailor was killed on an Iranian commercial vessel and warned the action could not go unanswered.
- The Caspian route allows Iran to supply Russia with Shahed attack drones and other military goods while circumventing a U.S. naval blockade of its southern ports.
- Analysts said the strikes aim to disrupt Russia’s access to Iranian weapons and signal Ukraine’s alignment with U.S. objectives against Iran.

## Iran says Ukrainian strike killed a sailor and cannot go unanswered

Zelensky said in a tweet on Saturday that Ukraine achieved “very strong results” with long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea, including against vessels used for military cargo shipments involving Iran and a warship. The Security Service of Ukraine, an intelligence agency, said the drone strikes targeted a Russian oil field, a Russian missile boat and ships used to transport Iranian drones to Russia.

The Caspian Sea trade route has become vital for Iran to supply Russia with military goods, including Shahed one-way attack drones, while circumventing a U.S. naval blockade of its Persian Gulf and northern Arabian Sea ports. Nicole Grajewski, an assistant professor at Sciences Po and author of a book on the Russia-Iran partnership, said the sea “allows Iran a route to Russia and onward to Central Asia that is somewhat unfettered by any kind of interdiction by the United States or other partners.”

Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi blamed Ukraine for a strike that he said killed a sailor on an Iranian commercial vessel. He said in a social-media post that he phoned Russian and European Union leaders to stress that Kyiv’s actions could not go unanswered, and Iran summoned the Ukrainian chargé d’affaires in Tehran. Ebrahim Azizi, head of the Iranian parliament’s national-security commission, warned on social media that “Ukraine, too, may soon come to understand that Iran doesn’t let actions go unanswered.”

The strikes mark a new front in the expanding drone war. Ukraine has used long-range drones to hit oil refineries deep inside Russia, contributing to an energy crisis, and the Caspian operations extend that reach more than 430 miles from Ukraine’s nearest border. Hanna Notte, director of the Eurasia Nonproliferation Program at the James Martin Center for Nonproliferation Studies, said the attacks also serve a political purpose. “For Ukraine, it is linking the theaters more closely in the European and perhaps American reading, and I think this is part of the intention here,” she said. “The more that Zelensky can signal to Trump that he’s on Trump’s side against Iran, the better.”

Russia has used the Caspian corridor to import Iranian Shahed drones since the fall of 2022, firing waves of the cheap, hard-to-intercept aircraft at Ukrainian cities and energy infrastructure. It later began manufacturing its own version, the Geran, at a factory in southern Russia—a facility Ukraine targeted last year. Since February, when a U.S. and Israeli bombing campaign against Iran began, Tehran has fired Shaheds at commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz, civilian airports, Gulf oil infrastructure and American military bases, exploiting the same drones’ ability to overwhelm air defenses.

Analysts said Iran now views itself as encircled. Hamidreza Azizi, a visiting fellow at the German Institute for International and Security Affairs, said, “Iran sees this in the context of this bigger picture of being under blockades from both the south and the north.” The strikes come as President Trump paused U.S. strikes on Iran and deliberated whether to escalate attacks aimed at breaking Iran’s grip on the Strait of Hormuz—making the Caspian route even more important. Israel also struck a naval outpost on Iran’s Caspian coastline in March in an attempt to disrupt the weapons trade, and Ukraine’s new strikes add another layer of pressure on a route that also supports sanctioned economies.

Iran has promised a response but did not specify what form it would take. Security analysts note that Ukraine is within range of some Iranian missiles, and the direct confrontation long feared may be drawing closer.
