Nebraska lawmakers on Wednesday were asked to consider a motion to expel state Sen. Dan McKeon, setting up what would be the first expulsion in the Nebraska Legislature’s history if the action succeeds.

The consideration came on the first day of the 2026 legislative session, after a complaint from a legislative staffer alleging that McKeon made a sexually charged comment to her and touched her inappropriately during a session-end party last year, according to an Associated Press report. If the Legislature votes to expel him next week, the AP reported it would mark the first time the body has ever expelled one of its own members.

The complaint prompted an outside law firm report compiled at the request of the Legislature’s Executive Board and released on Wednesday. The report said McKeon has “a reputation for making jokes” that some were unprofessional and/or inappropriate for the workplace. It concluded that McKeon’s conduct did not rise to a level of sexual harassment or retaliation actionable under state or federal discrimination law, but it said the conduct violated the Nebraska Legislature’s workplace harassment policy.

The report said lawmakers “may, in their discretion, censure, reprimand or expel the senator for his conduct and comments.” It described an episode at a Lincoln Country Club party attended by state lawmakers, staffers and lobbyists. The report said the staffer and other people had been discussing vacation plans at a May 29 event when McKeon asked whether she was “going to Hawaii to get laid.”

The report said the complainant was not vacationing in Hawaii and that the comment was inconsistent with the discussion of vacation plans. It also said the staffer alleged that McKeon patted her behind, and it said McKeon initially denied touching her but later said he may have touched her back or lower back “or even rear end,” while insisting the conduct was not intended to be sexual.

After the complaint, the report said Sen. Ben Hansen, who chaired the Legislature’s Executive Board, instructed McKeon on June 2 not to attend social gatherings where staffers would be present. The report said McKeon nevertheless attended another party that night that was attended by staffers, including the woman who filed the complaint.

The report also described what McKeon wrote to the staffer after she filed the complaint, saying nearly a month later he sent a note urging her to forgive him and linking the message to biblical instruction. It further said that in August McKeon texted another staffer who shares an office with the woman, writing that she “seems to be difficult to work with.”

Separate from the workplace complaint process, the AP reported that a Nebraska State Patrol investigation led to McKeon being charged with a misdemeanor count of disturbing the peace. The report said McKeon has pleaded not guilty.

McKeon attended Wednesday’s opening session and declined to comment on the report itself. The AP reported that when asked about the allegations, his remarks relied on religious references, saying his name Daniel means “just” in Hebrew and remarking “we’re all sinners.”

McKeon said he has no plans to step down, despite calls from leaders in his own party, including Republican Gov. Jim Pillen, the AP reported. He also suggested he could be forced out by the expulsion vote, telling reporters he expects the vote in the full Legislature to be “pretty close,” and saying, “It is what it is. I’m not going to cry about it or anything.”

The Legislature’s Executive Board will hold a hearing Monday on the resolution to expel McKeon. If the resolution is voted out of committee, the full Legislature could debate it as soon as Tuesday and would need 33 votes to pass, according to the AP report.

The AP also noted that if McKeon is expelled, he would be the nation’s 57th state lawmaker accused of sexual misconduct to leave office via expulsion or resignation since 2017.
