Officials said 325 homes have been destroyed and another 1,140 damaged. The outbreak is one of Chile's deadliest wildfire events in recent years, drawing emergency firefighters and supplies from Mexico, Argentina, and Brazil.

Firefighters in Chile battled 15 active wildfires Monday that have killed at least 19 people and forced 50,000 residents to evacuate their homes, according to Chile's National Service for the Prevention of Disasters. The fires, which broke out Sunday across the center and south of the country, have burned through at least 30,000 hectares — roughly five times the size of Manhattan Island — destroying homes, vehicles, infrastructure, and forests.

President Gabriel Boric declared a state of catastrophe in the central Biobío and neighboring Ñuble regions on Sunday, an emergency designation that allows greater military coordination in fighting the blazes. Chilean officials said 325 homes have been destroyed and another 1,140 damaged.

## Scale and conditions

"Controlling fires of this magnitude, with unfavorable weather conditions like high temperature, wind, and a lack of humidity is a tremendously difficult job," Boric said at a press conference Monday.

Wildfires are common in Chile during the summer months due to high temperatures and dry weather. The current outbreak across central and southern Chile is one of the deadliest in the country in recent years.

## International response

Boric thanked Mexico, Argentina, and Brazil for sending firefighters and supplies to assist in the emergency response.

## Investigation

Chilean police are investigating the causes of the current outbreak. Boric said Monday that in "99% of cases" wildfires in Chile are the result of human behavior.

## Historical context

The outbreak follows one of Chile's worst wildfire disasters on record. In 2024, massive fires ripping across the country's central coastline killed at least 130 people, making it Chile's deadliest natural disaster since a devastating 2010 earthquake.
