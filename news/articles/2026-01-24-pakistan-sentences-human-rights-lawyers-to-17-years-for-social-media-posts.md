A Pakistani court on Saturday sentenced two human rights lawyers to 17 years in prison each over social media posts that authorities claimed were hostile to the state and its security institutions. Judge Afzal Majoka delivered the verdict against Zainab Mazari and her husband Hadi Ali Chattha one day after their arrest in Islamabad. International human rights groups immediately condemned the convictions as part of a sustained campaign of judicial harassment against dissenting voices.

The sentences represent an escalation in Pakistan's crackdown on human rights advocates, lawyers, and journalists who document government abuses and represent political prisoners and activists detained by security forces.

## The Charges and Verdict

The court said Mazari had posted multiple tweets in recent years that "portrayed the agenda" of the outlawed Baloch separatist group and the Pakistani Taliban. Prosecutors alleged the couple used social media to "malign the state and its security institutions," with the tweets violating the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act, a law parliament passed in 2025 to restrict disinformation and hate speech.

The case originated from a complaint filed in August 2025 with the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency. The couple were formally indicted in October 2025 and repeatedly refused to appear in court. On Saturday, they appeared briefly via video link but boycotted the hearing, prompting the court to conclude the trial and deliver the verdict.

In his verdict, Judge Majoka said the couple's "overall conduct unmistakably reflects a deliberate, wilful and persistent strategy aimed at derailing, delaying and obstructing the trial process." He added that they had "undermined the dignity of the court, wilfully obstructed due process, and systematically sabotaged the administration of justice."

According to Amnesty International, eyewitnesses reported that police used excessive force when arresting the couple on Friday as they headed to a court hearing. Authorities provided no reasons for the arrests at the time.

## Background

Mazari is the daughter of Shireen Mazari, Pakistan's former human rights minister who served under imprisoned former Prime Minister Imran Khan. Mazari denounced the verdict on X as "totally illegal." The couple frequently represented journalists, political figures, and human rights activists who had been detained by security forces.

## Responses

Amnesty International said in a statement that the couple's detention marked "the latest escalation in a sustained campaign of judicial harassment and intimidation by the Pakistani authorities." The organization called for their immediate release.

Pakistan's Information Minister Attaullah Tarar praised the verdict. "As you sow, so shall you reap!" he wrote on X, using the occasion to declare that the couple had been lawfully sentenced under the country's cyber laws.

## A Broader Crackdown

Human rights activists in Pakistan have increasingly come under pressure from the government, which has been cracking down on criticism and dissent. Mazari and Chattha were among the more visible advocates representing those detained without formal charges or court appearances.

The couple denied all the charges against them.
