## Gilberto Cárdenas, Chicano movement chronicler and scholar, dies at 79

**Subtype:** fact

- Gilberto Cárdenas, a sociologist and photographer who documented the Chicano civil rights movement, died Sunday at his home in Austin, Texas, after a long illness, according to a statement released by his family. He was 79.
- Born in San Gabriel, California, Cárdenas became deeply involved in the Chicano Movement of the 1960s and 1970s, using his camera to photograph marches, meetings, and community gatherings.
- Over his career, he assembled a significant collection of Chicano artwork, often trading his photographs for posters, prints, and other pieces, and rescuing materials that were at risk of being discarded.
- He was widely regarded as a key figure in advancing Latino and Chicano identity through both academia and art.

## A scholar's commitment to documenting a movement

Gilberto Cárdenas was born and raised in San Gabriel, California. He came of age during the Chicano Movement of the 1960s and 1970s, a period when Mexican Americans mobilized for equality and civil rights under the banner of a distinct cultural and political identity.

Cárdenas engaged with the movement as a photographer, capturing marches, meetings, and community gatherings. His images served as both personal witness and historical record. He also channeled his interest into collecting Chicano art, frequently exchanging his photographs for posters, prints, and other works by artists active in the movement. On multiple occasions, according to those who knew him, he saved posters and other materials from being thrown away, citing their cultural significance.

Beyond his photographic and collecting work, Cárdenas was a highly respected sociologist and was considered by many a leading advocate for elevating Latino and Chicano identity through academic institutions and artistic preservation.
