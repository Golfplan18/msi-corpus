The consulate opening marks Canada's most concrete diplomatic move yet in support of Greenland's sovereignty, as Trump's annexation rhetoric has drawn a coordinated rebuke from seven NATO allies who issued a joint statement Tuesday affirming the Arctic island belongs to its own people. Analysts say Carney faces competing pressures — asserting Arctic solidarity with Denmark while avoiding a confrontation with Washington as a major trade agreement between the two countries is renegotiated in 2026.

TORONTO — Canada's governor general and foreign minister will travel to Greenland in early February to open a consulate in its capital, Prime Minister Mark Carney announced Tuesday, as U.S. President Donald Trump renewed his push to take control of the Arctic island territory.

Canadian Foreign Minister Anita Anand and Governor General Mary Simon, who is of Inuk descent, are expected to open the consulate in Nuuk, Carney said. He made the announcement while meeting with Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen at Canada's embassy in Paris.

"The future of Greenland and Denmark are decided solely by the people of Denmark," Carney said.

Anand said she will travel to Nuuk in the coming weeks to officially open the consulate and "mark a concrete step in strengthening our engagement in support of Denmark's sovereignty and territorial integrity, including Greenland."

## Seven NATO allies back Denmark

The leaders of Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain, and the United Kingdom joined Frederiksen on Tuesday in issuing a statement affirming that the strategic, mineral-rich Arctic island "belongs to its people."

Frederiksen addressed Carney directly at their Paris meeting, pointing to shared priorities in the region. "You have been very clear in your statement when it comes to the respect for national sovereignty," she said. "We are both into securing the Arctic region and together with all our NATO allies we can secure the region, so hopefully everybody is willing to work together."

Carney and Frederiksen were both in Paris for "coalition of the willing" talks on Ukraine. Carney said he had made a point of meeting with Frederiksen and NATO's secretary-general ahead of those sessions.

"We are making progress within NATO but we have to do more," Carney said at an earlier press conference.

## Trump's annexation argument

Trump told reporters Sunday that the United States needs to control Greenland to ensure the security of the NATO territory against rising threats from China and Russia in the Arctic. "It's so strategic right now," he said.

Stephen Miller, the White House deputy chief of staff, said Monday that Greenland should be part of the United States, despite a warning from Frederiksen that a U.S. takeover of Greenland would amount to the end of NATO. Trump has also previously talked about making Canada the 51st state.

Greenland, 80% of which lies above the Arctic Circle, is home to about 56,000 mostly Inuit people. It operates as a self-governing territory of the Kingdom of Denmark.

## Simon's background and the visit's significance

Simon became Canada's first Indigenous governor general in 2021 and previously served as Canada's ambassador to Denmark — experience that gives the February visit particular diplomatic resonance. As governor general, Simon is the representative of Britain's King Charles III, Canada's head of state.

Simon's office said the planned visit reflects the longstanding ties between the two countries. "Our two nations share a 3,000 km (1,864 mile) maritime border, as well as deep historical and cultural connections between Inuit communities," the office said in an email.

## Carney's balancing act

Daniel Béland, a political science professor at McGill University in Montreal, said Canada has both a strategic and a principled stake in resisting Trump's Greenland push.

"It is vital for Canada partly because we are a major Arctic country and that Greenland is our neighbor, and partly because we have a strong incentive to stand for international law and against Trump-style bullying and aggression," Béland said.

At the same time, Béland said, Carney wants to avoid antagonizing Trump as the free trade agreement between the two countries is renegotiated in 2026. "It's a tough balancing act for the prime minister," Béland said.
